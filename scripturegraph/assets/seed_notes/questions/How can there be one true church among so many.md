# How can there be one true church among so many?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
Joseph Smith's first question in the grove was this one, and the
Restoration's answer is specific: that the church Christ founded had
apostles, priesthood authority and revelation; that after the apostles
died those were lost and the churches that followed kept much truth but
not that authority; and that in 1830 the authority and the apostolic
structure were restored — so "the only true and living church"
([[D&C 1]]:30) is a claim about *authority and structure*, not about other
Christians lacking truth, goodness, or the Spirit. Latter-day Saints are
taught that other churches hold much that is true and that God works
through them ([[Alma 29]]:8). The claim is still exclusive, and honesty
requires not softening it into "one path among many."
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- The New Testament church had a definite structure — apostles, prophets,
  seventies, bishops — and a definite expectation of continuing revelation
  ([[Ephesians 4]]:11–14: apostles and prophets "till we all come in the
  unity of the faith"); its absence in later Christianity is a historical
  fact, not a Latter-day Saint invention.
- The New Testament predicts a falling away ([[2 Thessalonians 2]],
  [[Acts 20]]:29–30, [[Amos 8]]:11–12) and a restoration ([[Acts 3]]:21,
  [[Revelation 14]]:6) — the Restoration reads these plainly.
- Christian history records the loss: the Reformers themselves said the
  church had gone badly astray; Roger Williams concluded no church had
  authority and awaited apostles.
- The claim is falsifiable in principle: it stands or falls with Joseph
  Smith's visions and the Book of Mormon, and it says so.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- Every exclusive church makes the same claim (Rome, Orthodoxy, many
  Protestant groups); an outsider has no way to adjudicate, and the
  Restoration's answer — pray and you will know — is what all of them say.
- If a total apostasy occurred, the promise that "the gates of hell shall
  not prevail" ([[Matthew 16]]) failed for eighteen centuries.
- The Restoration has itself fractured into dozens of churches claiming
  Joseph Smith; the argument from unity cuts against it too.
- Good, Spirit-filled Christians in other traditions are hard to reconcile
  with those traditions lacking something essential.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
Latter-day Saints answer that "the gates of hell" is about the kingdom's
ultimate victory, not uninterrupted institutional continuity, and that a
church can lose authority without God losing the war; that the
Restoration's fragmentation after 1844 is real but the Twelve's succession
was the one the majority followed and the one that fulfilled Joseph's
instructions; that the truth-claim's decidability rests on a specific
promise ([[Moroni 10]]) that can be tried; and that the goodness of other
Christians is *expected* in Restoration theology — the Light of Christ is
given to everyone ([[Moroni 7]]) and salvation for the dead means no one
is finally disadvantaged by the church they were born into.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
The claim is exclusive and should be stated as such; dressing it down is
neither honest nor what the Church teaches. But its content is narrower
than critics assume — authority and revelation, not a monopoly on truth or
grace — and its theology of other faiths is more generous than most
exclusive claims. Whether it is *true* cannot be settled by comparing
churches; it turns on the founding events, which is where the neighboring
questions live.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->
