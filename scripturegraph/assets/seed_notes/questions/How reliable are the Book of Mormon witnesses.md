# How reliable are the Book of Mormon witnesses?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
Eleven men formally testified to seeing (Three) or seeing-and-hefting
(Eight) the plates; several others (Emma Smith, Mary Whitmer) left informal
witness statements. The historically striking facts: several witnesses broke
bitterly with Joseph Smith — and none of the eleven is documented as
recanting the plates testimony, with multiple deathbed reaffirmations
(David Whitmer's 1881 proclamation; Oliver Cowdery's return; Martin Harris
late in life). The debated part is the *nature* of the Three's experience
(visionary "eye of faith" language in some reports) and the evidentiary
weight of religiously committed witnesses.
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- Persistence through estrangement: Cowdery and the Whitmers testified while
  excommunicated/alienated, when repudiation would have cost nothing.
- Multiple independent late-life reaffirmations are well documented
  (interviews with David Whitmer by dozens of visitors, 1870s–80s).
- The Eight's testimony is tactile and matter-of-fact; Emma and William
  Smith described handling the covered plates domestically.
- Two witness groups with different phenomenology make a single hallucination
  model harder.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- Reports of visionary language: Martin Harris allegedly said he saw the
  plates "with the eye of faith" (John H. Gilbert; Anthony Metcalf reports);
  critics argue the Three's experience was subjective vision, not physical.
- All witnesses were inside the movement's charged spiritual culture; some
  (Whitmers, Harris) later followed other visionary claimants (e.g. Harris
  and Strang), suggesting suggestibility.
- No disinterested outsider ever examined uncovered plates.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
Defenders answer that the formal testimony's language ("we did handle with
our hands," for the Eight) resists a purely subjective reading; that
"eye of faith" reports are secondhand and late; and that suggestibility
arguments cut against the Three but poorly against the Eight's plain
statement — while granting honestly that a devotional-visionary component in
the Three's experience is textually explicit ("shown unto us by the power of
God, and not of man").
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
As human testimony goes, this is an unusually durable witness cluster with
real interpretive openness about the Three's phenomenology. It provides
strong evidence that *the witnesses believed* and non-trivial evidence of a
physical artifact; it cannot, by nature, establish the record's ancient
origin — see [[Is the Book of Mormon an ancient historical record]].
<!-- SG:END assessment -->
