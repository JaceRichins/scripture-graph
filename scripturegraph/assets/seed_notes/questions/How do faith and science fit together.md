# How do faith and science fit together?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
Better than the popular "warfare" story suggests, and with fewer commitments
for Latter-day Saints than for some Christians. The Church has no official
position on the age of the earth, evolution, or the mechanism of creation;
it teaches that God created the earth and humankind and that Adam and Eve
were real, and it leaves the "how" open. Its scripture describes creation
as organizing existing matter over unspecified "times" ([[Abraham 4]]), and
its ninth Article of Faith expects more truth to come. The remaining
friction is real — a historical Adam and a global flood are harder to
square with the evidence than a six-day creation ever needed to be — and
honest believers hold those questions open rather than closed.
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- Restoration scripture rejects creation *ex nihilo* and describes God
  working with eternal matter and law ([[D&C 93]], [[Abraham 3]]–[[Abraham
  4]]) — a picture more hospitable to a lawful, ancient cosmos than
  classical theism's.
- The Church's 1909, 1925 and 1931 statements affirmed Adam and Eve as the
  first parents while explicitly declining to pronounce on evolution; BYU
  teaches evolutionary biology; Church leaders including James E. Talmage
  and John A. Widtsoe were working scientists.
- "The glory of God is intelligence" ([[D&C 93]]) and "seek learning, even
  by study and also by faith" ([[D&C 88]]) make inquiry a religious duty.
- Historically, modern science grew up inside a Christian conviction that
  the world is intelligible because its maker is rational.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- Genetics indicates humans never descended from a single couple; a
  literal Adam and Eve as sole ancestors of all people is not tenable, and
  the Fall as the origin of death conflicts with billions of years of death
  before humans.
- A global flood in the last several thousand years is contradicted by
  geology, genetics and history; Latter-day Saint leaders have taught it
  literally.
- Some leaders (Joseph Fielding Smith, Bruce R. McConkie) taught that
  evolution is false doctrine; members were told opposite things by
  different authorities.
- Miracles, the resurrection, and answered prayer are claims science
  cannot accommodate without special pleading.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
Believing scientists reply that Adam and Eve can be historical as the first
*covenant* humans, or as representative of humanity, without being the
only humans — readings scripture permits and the Church has not ruled out;
that "no death before the Fall" is an interpretation ([[2 Nephi 2]]:22 has
been read otherwise) and not an official doctrine; that the flood can be
read as regional, as many Christian scholars do; that individual leaders'
scientific opinions were never presented as revelation, as the First
Presidency itself clarified; and that miracles are claims about God's
action, not about the failure of natural law, so science neither confirms
nor excludes them.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
On the big picture — an old universe, an evolving biosphere — Latter-day
Saint theology has more room than its popular Sunday-school form, and the
Church has deliberately kept that room open. On the specific hard points —
a historical first couple, the origin of death, the flood — thoughtful
members hold a range of views and the Church has not required one. Faith
here does not mean choosing scripture over evidence; it means trusting that
truth from both sources will converge, which is a hope, not yet a result.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->
