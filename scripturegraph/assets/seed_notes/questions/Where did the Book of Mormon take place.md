# Where did the Book of Mormon take place?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
The Church takes no official position. The text describes a land of
promise reached by sea, a narrow neck between a land northward and a land
southward, a river Sidon, and cities like [[Zarahemla]] and [[Bountiful]] —
an internally consistent geography of a few hundred miles, not a
hemisphere. The Old World portion is better anchored: [[Nahom]] matches an
attested ancient tribal area (NHM) in Yemen on Lehi's route, and a fertile
coastal [[Bountiful (Old World)]] exists in Oman where the text places it.
For the New World, Mesoamerica is the majority view among believing
scholars; a "heartland" (Great Lakes/Mississippi) model has popular
support; no site has been identified by archaeology, and the honest answer
is that we do not know.
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- The Arabian route is the strongest geographical evidence for the book:
  a place named Nahom (Nihm) south-southeast of Jerusalem where the text
  turns east ([[1 Nephi 16]]–[[1 Nephi 17]]), with altars bearing the NHM
  name dated to Lehi's era, and a green coastal Bountiful due east of it —
  neither known to anyone in 1829.
- The internal geography is consistent across hundreds of references
  written over decades of dictation, which is harder than it sounds.
- Mesoamerica uniquely offers the text's requirements: a literate,
  city-building civilization with cement, writing, warfare, and a narrow
  isthmus in the right period.
- The text never claims the whole hemisphere; the hemispheric reading was
  the readers', which DNA and scale problems have now retired.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- No Book of Mormon city, name, or inscription has ever been found;
  nothing archaeologists have uncovered has been identified as Nephite by
  non-believing scholars.
- The text names horses, cattle, wheat, steel, chariots and silk in the
  pre-Columbian Americas; none is attested, which is a problem, not a
  puzzle.
- Joseph Smith and other early leaders placed events across the hemisphere
  (Cumorah in New York, Zarahemla in Central America, Adam-ondi-Ahman in
  Missouri); the text's author seems to have had no fixed map.
- The Nahom parallel is three consonants in a region full of tribal names.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
Believing scholars argue that anachronism lists are translation issues in
part (loan-shifting familiar words to unfamiliar animals and metals is what
ancient translators did), that the archaeological record of the relevant
period is thin and mostly undeciphered, and that the absence of a Nephite
inscription is expected if the Nephites were a small elite within a larger
culture. On Nahom, they note the direction, the timing, the burial context
("the place which was called Nahom" where Ishmael is buried) and the
eastward turn all fit, which is more than a consonant match. On the New
York Cumorah, the Church itself says the location of the final battles is
not known.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
The Old World evidence is genuinely interesting and the New World evidence
is genuinely absent. That asymmetry is the honest state of the question. A
believer can hold that the text is ancient and its American setting
unidentified; a critic can point out that a century and a half of searching
has not found it. What no one should do is present a map as if it were
settled — the Church does not, and neither should this vault.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->
