# Nahom and the NHM altars

> [!evidence] The observation
> 1 Nephi 16:34 says Ishmael was buried "in the place which was called
> [[Nahom]]" ([[1 Nephi 16#^1ne-16-34|1 Ne. 16:34]]). Votive altars from the
> Bar'an temple near Wadi Jawf, Yemen (c. 7th–6th century BC), carry the
> South-Arabian tribal name **NHM** (Nihm/Naham), and a tribal region of that
> name still exists in roughly the area where Lehi's party would turn east.

<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
**Layer 1 — Observation (solid):** the altars are real, published by
non-LDS archaeologists (Deutsches Archäologisches Institut excavations at
Ma'rib); the NHM tribal name in that region in Lehi's era is documented.

**Layer 2 — Interpretation:** identifying NHM with the text's Nahom fits the
narrative's geography (southward along the incense trail, then "nearly
eastward" toward a fertile coast — candidates for Bountiful exist in Dhofar).
The text says the place "was called" Nahom (pre-existing name), matching a
tribal territory.

**Layer 3 — Significance:** one of the stronger geographical correlations
for the Old-World portion of the narrative — a specific, falsifiable,
right-era, right-place toponym. Still **supporting** evidence only: a
three-consonant Semitic root is not unique, vocalization is uncertain, and
the correlation shows plausibility, not proof of the journey.

**Does not establish:** the historicity of Lehi's journey or the book as a
whole.

**Alternative explanations:** (1) chance similarity to a common Semitic root
(n-ḥ-m "comfort/mourn"); (2) Joseph Smith could have encountered "Nehhm" on
certain 18th–19th-century maps of Arabia (documented on e.g. Niebuhr's map;
availability in his environment is debated).
<!-- SG:END summary -->
