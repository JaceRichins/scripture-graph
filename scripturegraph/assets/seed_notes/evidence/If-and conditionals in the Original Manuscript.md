# If-and conditionals in the Original Manuscript

> [!language] The observation
> The Original Manuscript and 1830 edition contain Hebrew-like *if…and*
> conditionals that are ungrammatical in English — e.g. the original text of
> [[1 Nephi 17#^1ne-17-50|1 Ne. 17:50]]: "if he should command me that I
> should say unto this water be thou earth **and** it should be earth."
> Joseph Smith removed the *and*s in the 1837 edition. Biblical Hebrew
> regularly builds conditionals this way (*wə-* apodosis).

<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
**Observation (documented):** Royal Skousen's Critical Text Project has
catalogued the original readings; at least a dozen if-and constructions stood
in the earliest text (1 Nephi 17:50; Helaman 12:13–21 cluster; Moroni 10:4
originally, per O/P manuscripts).

**Interpretation (disputed):** believing scholars read this as translation
carry-over from a Semitic source ("a Hebraism English would never produce").
Skeptical linguists respond that *if…and* occurs marginally in Early Modern
English and in dictated vernacular speech, and that dictation artifacts can
produce run-on conjunctions.

**Significance:** among the stronger single Hebraism arguments *because* the
construction is bad English and was later edited out — i.e., it was not a
stylistic imitation the translator preserved on purpose. Still supporting,
not probative; sample size is small.

**Does not establish:** that the whole text translates a Hebrew document.

**Alternative explanations:** dictation-speech artifact; sporadic Early
Modern English parallel; hypercorrection narrative (the 1837 cleanup shows
only that the syntax was nonstandard, not where it came from).
