# Canonical Narrative Foreshadowing And Fulfillment in Exodus 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Later portions of Exodus narratively realize major forecasts in this chapter: the Egyptians give Israel silver, gold, and clothing before the departure; Israel later arrives at Sinai; and sacrifices and covenant worship occur at the mountain.

**Does not establish:** Narrative correspondence does not by itself establish the historicity of the events, the date or unity of composition, or supernatural foreknowledge.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The correspondences can be explained as deliberate literary planning within the Exodus narrative.
- They may reflect retrospective composition or editing rather than supernatural prediction.
- The mountain service develops across several episodes rather than constituting one uniquely identifiable fulfillment scene.
