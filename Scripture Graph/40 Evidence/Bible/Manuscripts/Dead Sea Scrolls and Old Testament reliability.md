# Dead Sea Scrolls and Old Testament reliability

> [!textual] The observation
> The Qumran scrolls (c. 250 BC – AD 68) include manuscripts of every Hebrew
> Bible book except Esther. The Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaᵃ, c. 125 BC)
> predates the previously oldest complete Hebrew Isaiah (Leningrad Codex,
> AD 1008) by over a millennium, yet reads substantially the same text.

<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
**Observation (uncontested):** discovered 1947–1956; published corpus (DJD
series) is standard scholarship, wholly independent of any restoration claim.

**Significance (strong, bounded):** demonstrates that the Masoretic
transmission of books like [[Isaiah 53|Isaiah]] was remarkably stable across
~1,100 years — strong evidence for careful scribal transmission of the
*consonantal text* in that window. It does **not** speak to composition
history before Qumran, and the scrolls also document real textual plurality
(e.g. Jeremiah's shorter Septuagint-type edition at Qumran), which honest
treatments must include.

**Does not establish:** inerrancy of the received text; original-autograph
wording; anything about pre-3rd-century-BC transmission.

**Study payoff:** when the Book of Mormon quotes Isaiah, variants can be
compared against 1QIsaᵃ / MT / LXX — several such comparisons are classic
study material and will be linked from the Isaiah chapters as the evidence
passes run.
<!-- SG:END summary -->
