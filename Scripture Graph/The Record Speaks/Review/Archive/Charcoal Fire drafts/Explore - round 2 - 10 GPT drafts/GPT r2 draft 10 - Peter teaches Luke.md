LUKE: That weakened the account.

PETER: Considerably.

LUKE: Were you angry?

PETER: I was hungry. The room smelled of hot bread.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: Yes, I was angry. Tirzah had gone without food the night before.

LUKE: Did you rebuke Hannah?

PETER: I asked for two baskets. We carried the loaves through the quarter.

LUKE: To Tirzah?

PETER: To Tirzah and five other houses. Hannah cried so hard at the first door that I carried both baskets.

LUKE: Did you tell the others what she had done?

PETER: She did. Badly. She kept saying “borrowed.” Tirzah corrected her from inside the house.

LUKE: What happened to Hannah afterward?

PETER: For three months she measured grain beside me.

LUKE: You placed her beside the grain?

PETER: Where I could see both hands.

LUKE: Many would have kept her away from it.

PETER: Then I would have measured every sack alone. I had nets to mend.

LUKE: Did she cheat again?

PETER: She overfilled every measure.

LUKE: Out of repentance?

PETER: Out of terror. A poor family would pay for one measure and leave with one and a half. We would be empty by noon.

LUKE: So you corrected her generosity.

PETER: I scraped the heap level. She glared at me. We did that every market day until her hand stopped shaking.

LUKE: Did Tirzah trust her again?

PETER: Tirzah began coming early to sit beside the scales.

LUKE: To watch?

PETER: At first. Later because Hannah described everyone’s sandals to her.

LUKE: They became friends?

PETER: They became women who could quarrel over cumin for half an afternoon. Call it what you wish.

LUKE: Did you tell Hannah about the fire?

PETER: She had already heard Mark’s account. She recited my last answer to me while we stacked jars.

LUKE: That must have stung.

PETER: Her memory was excellent.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: That the jar went on the lower shelf.

LUKE: Nothing more?

PETER: The next week I told her about the breakfast.

LUKE: Why that part?

PETER: She had stopped eating with us. She served everyone, then found work in another room.

LUKE: And after you told her?

PETER: She sat near the door with half a loaf. Tirzah reached across and took the larger piece.

LUKE: You remember the bread.

PETER: It was burned along one edge. Hannah ate that edge first.

LUKE: You watch people closely.

PETER: John is worse.

LUKE: There was another person?

PETER: There have been many.

LUKE: Tell me one more.

PETER: Jonas of Joppa. Not my father. A rope-maker with arms like mooring posts.

LUKE: What had he done?

PETER: Struck his apprentice with a lead weight.

LUKE: How badly?

PETER: The boy lived. Two fingers never closed again.

LUKE: Jonas came to you?

PETER: After the elders barred him from serving at table. He said they had shamed him before his wife.

LUKE: Had he confessed to striking the boy?

PETER: He called it a correction.

LUKE: With lead?

PETER: That is what I asked.

LUKE: What did you require?

PETER: First, that he stop speaking of his shame until we had seen the boy’s hand.

LUKE: You went together?

PETER: He refused. I went without him. The apprentice was called Simeon. Sixteen years old. His hand was wrapped in dirty wool.

LUKE: Was there infection?

PETER: The physician had cleaned it. Simeon’s mother had sold her bracelets to pay him.

LUKE: What did Jonas do when you told him?

PETER: Said the boy had been careless before.

LUKE: You must have wanted to strike him.

PETER: I asked Andrew to sit between us.

LUKE: Wise.

PETER: Andrew complained about that too.

LUKE: How did Jonas make amends?

PETER: He sold his share in a boat. Paid for the physician, the bracelets, and the wages Simeon had lost.

LUKE: Was that enough?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What else?

PETER: Simeon could no longer grip the wooden shuttle. Jonas spent weeks carving one that fastened to his wrist.

LUKE: Did Simeon accept it?

PETER: He threw the first one into the harbor.

LUKE: And Jonas?

PETER: Went after it.

LUKE: Into the water?

PETER: In winter. He came out blue and furious.

LUKE: Did he strike the boy again?

PETER: No. He carved another shuttle.

LUKE: Better?

PETER: Worse. The leather buckle cut Simeon’s arm.

LUKE: This is not going well.

PETER: It did not go well for a long time.

LUKE: Why did Jonas continue?

PETER: I went to his shop every morning.

LUKE: To supervise?

PETER: To sit on an upturned jar and tell him the buckle was still wrong.

LUKE: How many shuttles?

PETER: Seven.

LUKE: The seventh worked?

PETER: The sixth worked. Simeon threw the seventh at him because Jonas had decorated it.

LUKE: Decorated it how?

PETER: A fish with enormous eyes. Jonas was not a gifted carver.

LUKE: Did they work together again?

PETER: Not as master and apprentice. Simeon opened a small shop with his uncle.

LUKE: Then they separated.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Where was forgiveness in that?

PETER: One market morning, Simeon’s cord tangled. Jonas saw and began to reach for it. Then he stopped and asked permission.

LUKE: Simeon allowed him?

PETER: He held out the shuttle.

LUKE: That was all?

PETER: That was enough for that morning.

LUKE: And afterward?

PETER: Some days they spoke. Some days Simeon turned away. Jonas kept the shop’s old weight hanging where he could see it.

LUKE: You did not restore him to his former place?

PETER: Not to authority over boys.

LUKE: Yet you did not cast him out.

PETER: He ate with us. At first, people left room around him. I sat in it.

LUKE: Because the Lord had sat with you?

PETER: Because there was room.

LUKE: You will not make this easy for a writer.

PETER: I am trying to save your roll.

LUKE: From what?

PETER: From becoming thinner than the people in it.

LUKE: Mattan received lamps. Hannah received scales. Jonas received seven mornings and a badly carved fish.

PETER: Six bad fish. One was only a handle.

LUKE: You gave each of them work near the place they had failed.

PETER: I did not plan it so neatly.

LUKE: But you see the pattern.

PETER: I see Mattan trimming a wick too short. I see Hannah’s red thread. I see Simeon’s two fingers lying still.

LUKE: And when you see them, do you see the shore?

PETER: Sometimes. Sometimes I am merely tired and want everyone to go home.

LUKE: Yet you return the next morning.

PETER: Usually.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: There is work.

LUKE: Peter, I told you I came for those who ask what happened between the gate and the shore.

PETER: That was not the whole truth.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Is the man alive?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Near enough to reach?

LUKE: Two days west.

PETER: Then your journey to me was longer.

LUKE: I know.

PETER: Tell me his name.

LUKE: Caleb.

PETER: What did you do to Caleb?

LUKE: Men were searching the quarter. I had six sick people hidden in a storeroom. Caleb came to the outer door.

PETER: Pursued?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Did you open it?

LUKE: No.

PETER: Did he know you were inside?

LUKE: He called my name.

PETER: And you remained quiet.

LUKE: I had counted the lives behind me. An old woman could not stand. A child’s fever had broken only that morning.

PETER: What happened to Caleb?

LUKE: They took him. He was beaten and held for seventeen days.

PETER: He lives.

LUKE: He lives.

PETER: And you have not gone to him.

LUKE: I sent medicine.

PETER: Medicine.

LUKE: His ribs had been injured.

PETER: Did you send your name with it?

LUKE: No.

PETER: Why come to me?

LUKE: Because Christ Himself came to you. He knew the whole wound and closed it with His own questions.

PETER: You wanted me to tell you Caleb’s door was different.

LUKE: I wanted to know whether a man may be called faithful after choosing the six behind him.

PETER: You are asking about your reason.

LUKE: It was a reason.

PETER: A strong one.

LUKE: You think I should have opened the door.

PETER: I was not in your storeroom.

LUKE: Then what can you tell me?

PETER: Caleb called your name.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: You heard him.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Does he know you heard?

LUKE: I do not know.

PETER: Take him the answer.

LUKE: What answer?

PETER: The true one. Take it to his door and let him decide where you stand.

LUKE: What if he shuts it?

PETER: Then stand there awhile. Watch for donkeys.

LUKE: You make it sound like Mattan.

PETER: Mattan would tell you it felt nothing like this.

LUKE: I am afraid Caleb will forgive me too quickly.

PETER: He may.

LUKE: That would not set it right.

PETER: Then go again the next day.

LUKE: And if he does not forgive me?

PETER: Go again the next day.

LUKE: How long?

PETER: You are asking a fisherman to count days before the net is in the water.

LUKE: There. That sounds like something Mark would write down.

PETER: Then do not tell him.

LUKE: What should I bring Caleb?

PETER: What can he eat?

LUKE: Fish, if it is fresh.

PETER: We have fish.

LUKE: You would give me your catch?

PETER: Not the large one. Andrew has already claimed it.

LUKE: Of course he has.

PETER: Take the two near the basket. Wrap them in wet leaves.

LUKE: Will that be enough?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What else?

PETER: Yourself, Luke.

LUKE: That sounds dangerously close to wisdom.

PETER: Then take onions too.

LUKE: Better.

PETER: Give me the net.

LUKE: I have one corner left.

PETER: Your loops are uneven.

LUKE: They hold.

PETER: So far.

LUKE: Teach me once more.

PETER: Thumb here.

LUKE: The actual thumb.

PETER: You are learning.

LUKE: Under, around twice, then through.

PETER: Slowly.

LUKE: It is loose.

PETER: Leave it loose until the next cord is through.

LUKE: I want to tighten it now.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: The opening looks worse before it closes.

PETER: Look at your hands.

LUKE: I am looking.

PETER: Pass the shuttle back.

LUKE: Like this?

PETER: Again.

LUKE: It is taking the strain now.

PETER: Pull.

LUKE: Hard?

PETER: Hard enough to know.

LUKE: It holds.

PETER: Pull the other side.

LUKE: Still holding.

PETER: Then leave it. The fish will test the rest.

LUKE: Peter, John also kept what followed the questions.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: Do you want it read?

PETER: Yes.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not. This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

[Peter folds the repaired net across his knees.]

LUKE: Are you afraid?

PETER: Some mornings.

LUKE: And on the others?

PETER: I am busy.

LUKE: With the sheep.

PETER: With Mattan’s lamps. Hannah’s measures. Jonas arriving before sunrise. Names, Luke. Keep the names.

LUKE: And Caleb.

PETER: Especially Caleb, while his door can still be reached.

LUKE: I will go before I turn east.

PETER: East?

LUKE: I am going to find Thomas next.

PETER: Are you.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Then mend your cloak before you meet him. That shoulder is nearly through.

LUKE: I hoped you had not noticed.

PETER: John is not the only one with eyes.

LUKE: Will you show me?

PETER: After we wrap the fish.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: What?

LUKE: Thank you.

PETER: Hold the leaves open.

LUKE: Like this?

PETER: Both hands. Caleb should not receive fish covered in sand.

LUKE: Both hands.

PETER: Yes. Now do not move.
