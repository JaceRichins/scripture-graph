[The scrape of a wooden needle through netting.]

LUKE: This is Luke. I have come to gather the accounts of those who walked with the Messiah. What follows is shaped as conversation; the words preserved in scripture are unchanged. I pray their witness brings us nearer to Him. This is The Record Speaks.

PETER: Put your thumb on that knot.

LUKE: Here?

PETER: No, that is the hole. If you put your thumb there, we shall have a larger hole.

LUKE: I have stitched men with less complaint from the man holding the needle.

PETER: Men do not have to hold fish.

LUKE: You promised I could ask questions.

PETER: You can ask while your thumb is useful.

LUKE: John said you would do this.

PETER: John has been waiting years to send a physician out here and have him defeated by a net.

LUKE: He said you dislike being watched when you speak.

PETER: I dislike being watched when I work. Speaking is worse.

LUKE: Yet Mark has heard you speak often enough to fill a scroll.

PETER: Mark knows when to wait. You have been here half a morning and already moved that tablet six times.

LUKE: Five.

PETER: Six. Once when I brought the bread.

LUKE: You count everything?

PETER: Only fish, debts, and nervous men.

LUKE: Which am I?

PETER: You have not touched the bread. That rules out fish.

[Peter pulls the knot tight.]

LUKE: I came about the courtyard.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: John told you?

PETER: Your face told me. Men who come about the lake look at the water. You have looked at my hands since you sat down.

LUKE: I thought it would be better not to circle it.

PETER: Then do not circle it.

LUKE: Why did you go inside?

PETER: Inside the high priest’s court?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Because John went in. Because they had taken the Lord. Because I had run, then stopped running, then hated myself for stopping. Pick one.

LUKE: John knew people there.

PETER: He was known at the door. I was not. He came back and brought me through.

LUKE: Did you think you could help?

PETER: Of course I did. I was still full of help.

LUKE: Even after the garden?

PETER: Especially after the garden. I had slept when I should have watched. I had used a blade and done no good with it. A man can make a great heap of trouble while trying to prove he is useful.

LUKE: Mark begins it earlier, at supper.

PETER: Mark begins where I began making noise.

LUKE: You asked him to keep this?

PETER: I told it when we gathered. He wrote what I told. Once he read it back and slowed at the ugly part. I told him to keep going.

LUKE: The others also made the promise.

PETER: They did. I made mine louder.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:29-31), read by Luke: But Peter said unto him, Although all shall be offended, yet will not I.

PETER: “More vehemently.” Mark liked that word less after I made him read it aloud.

LUKE: Is it wrong?

PETER: No. That was the trouble.

LUKE: What did you believe you were saying?

PETER: The truth. I had left my boat. I had gone where He went. Men had shouted at us before. I thought courage was something I owned because I had used it once.

LUKE: You meant to die with Him.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Then your heart was not false.

PETER: My mouth made a promise the rest of me had not yet paid for.

LUKE: But intention must matter.

PETER: It mattered at supper. It did not keep me awake in the garden.

LUKE: You were exhausted. All of you were.

PETER: You say that like a physician.

LUKE: Bodies have limits.

PETER: I know. Mine showed me every one of them before sunrise.

LUKE: Mark also kept this.

Watch ye and pray, lest ye enter into temptation. The spirit truly is ready, but the flesh is weak.

PETER: I remember the ground against my cheek.

LUKE: Not His voice first?

PETER: No. The ground. Damp grit here.

[Peter touches the side of his face.]

PETER: I woke with it stuck to me. James was pushing himself up. John looked ill. I heard my name and knew I had failed at a thing no child could misunderstand: stay awake.

LUKE: Did you answer?

PETER: Not well enough for anyone to keep it.

LUKE: You slept again.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why tell me that with the courtyard? They are not the same act.

PETER: No. But when the girl spoke to me, I had already learned what my promise was worth.

LUKE: You think the denials began before you spoke?

PETER: I think I was tired, frightened, angry, and still trying to look like the man at supper. That is what walked through the door.

[Outside, a basket is set down. Peter waits until the footsteps pass.]

LUKE: Tell me about the maid.

PETER: She kept the door. Younger than I expected. Strong arms. A red cord around one wrist.

LUKE: You remember the cord?

PETER: I watched her hand lift the bar. I thought if I watched her hand, I would not have to look at her face.

LUKE: John says she asked him whether he was bringing you in.

PETER: John would remember the question. I remember that she saw me twice.

LUKE: Were there soldiers near you?

PETER: Servants. Guards. Men coming in and out. Enough iron in the place that every step made some small sound.

LUKE: You stood by a fire.

PETER: I sat first. Then stood. Then moved toward the porch. You want it orderly. It was not orderly.

LUKE: I want to see where you were.

PETER: I did not know where I was. That is nearer to it.

LUKE: Were you close enough to see the Lord?

PETER: At times. A shoulder between two men. The side of His face. Then nothing. Everyone leaned whenever there was a blow or a question.

LUKE: The maid recognized you by the firelight.

PETER: She looked as if she had been trying to place me. That frightened me more than a shout would have.

LUKE: Because she was certain?

PETER: Because she was curious. I was about to tear my life apart, and to her it was a small thing before breakfast.

LUKE: Mark’s account is severe.

PETER: Read it without helping me.

LUKE: Why did you leave in the first cockcrow?

PETER: I did not leave. I went as far as the porch and stayed.

LUKE: Mark says you went out.

PETER: Out from the fire, not out through the gate. I should have gone. I wanted to see what happened. I wanted not to be seen. So I stood between the two.

LUKE: Then the first cry warned you.

PETER: It made me cold. For a moment.

LUKE: Yet you denied Him again.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: That is the part I cannot fit.

PETER: Do not try to fit it. There was room to stop, and I did not stop.

LUKE: Were the curses meant to convince them?

PETER: At first. Then I heard myself and knew whose voice it was. Mine.

LUKE: What did you swear by?

PETER: No.

LUKE: You will not say?

PETER: I will not give those words another morning.

LUKE: Mark says enough.

PETER: I made certain he did.

LUKE: You could have told him only that fear overcame you.

PETER: Fear did not make the sounds. I did.

LUKE: In my account, I kept one thing Mark did not.

PETER: The look.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (Luke 22:61), read by Luke: And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.

PETER: I have wondered how you knew.

LUKE: I spoke with those who were there. I spoke with you.

PETER: I do not remember telling you.

LUKE: John remembered that you could see each other. You supplied the rest without noticing. Your face changed whenever I asked where He stood.

PETER: Physicians.

LUKE: Is my reading wrong?

PETER: No. He looked at me.

LUKE: Angrily?

PETER: I cannot give you an easier face.

LUKE: I did not ask for an easier one.

PETER: Yes, you did.

LUKE: Then tell me the true one.

PETER: They had struck Him. Men were pulling Him from one place to another. He turned as if there were no hands on Him at all. He found me.

LUKE: And what was in His face?

PETER: Him.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: I know that does not help your scroll. I knew that face. I had watched it bend over sick children and stubborn men and bread that would not be enough. I expected to find less of Him there. I did not.

LUKE: No accusation?

PETER: I had supplied that.

LUKE: Pity?

PETER: Do not make me choose one small word because you need a clean line.

LUKE: Forgive me.

PETER: I saw that He knew. That was enough to break me.

LUKE: Where did you go?

PETER: Down a narrow street, then another. I struck my shoulder on a wall. A woman threw water from an upper window and cursed me for being there.

LUKE: You remember that.

PETER: I remember laughing.

LUKE: Laughing?

PETER: Once. One sound. I had just sworn I did not know Him, and now a stranger was angry because I could not even run straight.

LUKE: Where did John find you?

PETER: Behind a dyer’s yard. Blue water under the door. I sat beside a jar with no bottom.

LUKE: Did he speak?

PETER: Not for a long while. He sat too close. John always sits too close when a man wants to disappear.

LUKE: Then what?

PETER: He put his cloak around me. I told him to go. He stayed.

LUKE: Did you return to the others?

PETER: Before noon. I stood outside the room until Andrew saw me.

LUKE: Your brother let you in?

PETER: He moved aside.

LUKE: Did anyone ask what happened?

PETER: They had heard enough. News runs faster when it is cruel.

LUKE: What did you say to them?

PETER: Nothing worth writing. I drank water and brought it back up. Mary gave me a cloth. John cleaned the blood from my hand.

LUKE: Your blood?

PETER: From where I had struck the wall. You see? Even then I had found a way to make somebody tend another wound of mine.

LUKE: Did they blame you?

PETER: Some would not look at me. That was worse. Andrew kept setting food beside me. That was worse too.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because I could refuse anger. Bread was harder.

[Peter cuts a frayed strand from the net.]

LUKE: I came expecting you to say that sorrow changed you.

PETER: It made me sick. It made me quiet for perhaps half a day. Then it made me sharp with anyone who tried to help.

LUKE: But you repented.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: When?

PETER: You want the hour?

LUKE: If there was one.

PETER: There was an hour when I ran out. There was another when I went back into the room. There were many mornings after that when I still chose the far place at the table.

LUKE: How long?

PETER: Long enough for John to stop leaving the near place empty.

LUKE: He noticed.

PETER: John notices everything. He never said a word. He simply began sitting where I had been avoiding.

LUKE: Did you pray?

PETER: Poorly.

LUKE: What did you ask for?

PETER: Sometimes nothing. I said His name and stopped.

LUKE: Did you believe He could hear you?

PETER: I believed it and wished I did not. Then I wished He would. Then I did not know what I wished.

LUKE: You speak of those days as if you were still waiting for judgment.

PETER: We were waiting beside a sealed tomb. What other word would you use?

LUKE: Mark’s writing does not soften the courtyard. Was that your choice or his?

PETER: Mine first. His after he argued with me.

LUKE: Mark argued?

PETER: He said the account had already carried enough sorrow. He asked whether every sound from my mouth was needed.

LUKE: And you said it was.

PETER: Men knew I stood among the Twelve. They needed to know what sort of man had been standing there.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because one day a man would come through a door with the same look I had behind the dyer’s yard.

LUKE: Did one?

PETER: Many.

LUKE: That answer sounds prepared.

PETER: It has had years to become true.

LUKE: Before the years, tell me about the morning by the lake.

PETER: There were mornings before it.

LUKE: I know.

PETER: No, you know the order. That is not the same. We saw Him living, and still I could not tell whether I ought to stand near Him.

LUKE: Why would you doubt that after He had appeared?

PETER: Because He was alive. A dead man cannot ask what you did while he was being condemned.

LUKE: Did He ask?

PETER: Not then.

LUKE: Yet you feared He would.

PETER: I had answers ready. That should tell you how afraid I was.

LUKE: What answers?

PETER: That I stayed longer than the others. That I entered the court. That there were armed men. All true. All useless.

LUKE: John says it was you who proposed fishing.

PETER: I could repair a boat without anyone asking whether I loved him.

LUKE: John set it down very simply.

PETER: Entirely.

PETER: For the first hour. By dawn we were seven tired men arguing over rope.

LUKE: Then you saw someone on the shore.

PETER: We saw a shape. John knew first.

PETER: He usually said it softly, as if the rest of us might need time to catch up.

LUKE: You went into the water.

PETER: I did.

PETER: I have never claimed patience among my virtues.

LUKE: Were you running toward Him or away from the others?

PETER: I was wet enough that the difference did not matter.

LUKE: What did you see when you reached the shallows?

PETER: His hands first. He was tending breakfast.

LUKE: Not waiting to question you.

PETER: No.

LUKE: John kept the whole meal.

PETER: Waited. Gave us food. Watched Andrew recover from his own greed.

PETER: I did not say it did.

PETER: Read John. Slowly.

LUKE: You were grieved at the third.

PETER: John saw it.

LUKE: Had you understood before then?

PETER: I understood enough at the first to answer too quickly. At the second I could hear everyone else being silent. At the last, there was nowhere left to hide in the answer.

LUKE: “Thou knowest all things.”

PETER: I had once spoken as if I knew myself better than He did. I could not do that again.

LUKE: And still you said you loved Him.

PETER: What else could I say?

LUKE: A guilty man might say nothing.

PETER: I had tried that in the room after the burial.

LUKE: You might have said you were unworthy.

PETER: I was. He had asked another question.

LUKE: You might have asked His forgiveness.

PETER: I wanted to. He gave me sheep.

LUKE: That sounds almost like a complaint.

PETER: It was work. Sheep bite. People also bite, though fewer admit it.

LUKE: Did you feel restored?

PETER: I felt cold water drying under my clothes. I felt sand in my sandal. I knew He had heard every answer, and I knew what I was to do that afternoon.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Helped mend the net James had torn.

LUKE: That is all?

PETER: We needed the net.

LUKE: I mean, after such a charge—

PETER: The net was in front of me.

LUKE: And later?

PETER: Later came one person at a time.

LUKE: Tell me of one.

PETER: Ezra came first. Not the first person I served—the first who came because he had heard about the courtyard.

LUKE: What had he done?

PETER: During a search, he showed the officers which storeroom his cousin used for gatherings. He thought they would take the scrolls. His cousin was inside.

LUKE: Was the cousin killed?

PETER: Beaten. His right leg never straightened again. Two others were taken. Ezra kept repeating that he had not known they were there.

LUKE: Did you believe him?

PETER: Parts of him.

LUKE: What did he ask of you?

PETER: He wanted me to tell the gathering he was safe to return.

LUKE: Were they afraid of him?

PETER: They were furious. Mattan had lost his shop because Ezra named the owner of the storeroom. Mattan wanted him put outside and kept outside.

LUKE: What did you decide?

PETER: Nothing that night. Ezra had walked two days and had blood in one sandal. I washed the foot. He mistook that for an answer and began thanking me.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: I told him to stop talking because I was trying to find the splinter.

LUKE: And the next morning?

PETER: I took him to Mattan’s shop. The shutters were gone. Someone had pulled half the roof apart looking for hidden writings.

LUKE: You brought them face to face?

PETER: Not wisely. Mattan struck him before I had shut the door.

LUKE: What did Ezra do?

PETER: Fell over a stool. Then he stayed on the floor and said Mattan could finish.

LUKE: Did you allow it?

PETER: No. I caught Mattan around the middle. He bit my wrist.

LUKE: You said people bite.

PETER: Mattan did. I still have the mark when it is cold.

LUKE: How did it end?

PETER: That day? Badly. Mattan left. Ezra vomited in a basket of wool.

LUKE: Yet you let Ezra return?

PETER: Not to the gathering. Not yet. I gave him roof tiles.

LUKE: Roof tiles?

PETER: Mattan needed a roof. Ezra had hands. I told him to repair what he had helped uncover.

LUKE: Did Mattan agree?

PETER: No. Ezra worked while Mattan shouted from the street. On the third day Mattan stopped shouting. On the sixth he held the ladder.

LUKE: Did they reconcile?

PETER: Do not run to the end. For months Mattan checked every door when Ezra entered. Ezra never complained about it. Then one winter morning the roof leaked over Mattan’s new wool, and both men climbed up in the rain.

LUKE: Together?

PETER: They argued the whole time. But Mattan handed him the good hammer.

LUKE: Is that when you knew?

PETER: I knew Mattan guarded that hammer from his own sons.

LUKE: What became of Ezra?

PETER: He carried food to households that had reason not to open their doors to him. Sometimes they refused it. He left the basket and came back the next day.

LUKE: Because you told him your story?

PETER: I told him before I touched his foot.

LUKE: All of it?

PETER: Enough that he stopped thanking me.

[Peter begins another tear in the net.]

LUKE: You said there were many.

PETER: Damaris came with three coins tied in a cloth.

LUKE: What had she done?

PETER: Taken money from the common box. Small amounts. Oil one week, barley the next. She kept the tally herself, which was how we found it.

LUKE: Why did she steal?

PETER: Her husband had left debts and a man was threatening to take her daughter as payment. She was frightened. She also liked good oil. Both things were true.

LUKE: Did she confess?

PETER: After I accused the wrong widow.

LUKE: You accused someone innocent?

PETER: In front of six people. Hannah was deaf in one ear and made me repeat it.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Apologized in front of the same six. Louder the second time.

LUKE: And Damaris?

PETER: She came after dark. Put the coins on the floor because she would not put them in my hand.

LUKE: Did you return them to the box?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did you keep her from serving?

PETER: For a time. She had been measuring grain for the widows. I took the measure from her.

LUKE: That seems prudent.

PETER: It was. It also pleased Hannah too much, which should have warned me.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: Damaris stopped coming. Her daughter came for bread alone and lied about where her mother was.

LUKE: How did you know?

PETER: Children lie with their whole bodies. She pointed toward the market while her feet turned home.

LUKE: You went to Damaris?

PETER: John came with me. She had sold the sleeping mat and was sitting on bare boards, repairing sandals by a lamp with almost no oil.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: I asked whether she had eaten. She said that was not why I had come. I said I knew, but I had brought lentils.

LUKE: Did you give her the measure again?

PETER: Not that night. We ate.

LUKE: When did she return?

PETER: The next week, but she sat by the door. The week after that, nearer the table. Hannah moved away from her each time.

LUKE: Did you rebuke Hannah?

PETER: Twice. The second time she heard me.

LUKE: And the money?

PETER: Damaris repaid it in small coins. Then she kept bringing coins after the debt was gone.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: She had counted wrong on purpose.

LUKE: What did you do with the extra?

PETER: Put it in the box.

LUKE: That allowed her to punish herself.

PETER: Yes. John said the same.

LUKE: So you returned it?

PETER: I bought oil from her with it.

LUKE: From her?

PETER: She had begun pressing olives with her brother. The oil was not as good as she claimed, but no one died.

LUKE: Did she serve again?

PETER: I asked her to count the loaves before we sent them out.

LUKE: You put her near the stores.

PETER: She stared at me until I thought she might throw the counting board.

LUKE: Was that wise?

PETER: Ask me after you taste her brother’s oil.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: She counted well. Years later, when a young man took a cup from a house where we met, the owner wanted his hand broken. Damaris sat beside him until morning.

LUKE: What did she tell him?

PETER: I do not know. She shut the door in my face.

LUKE: You let her?

PETER: It was her room.

LUKE: Did the young man return the cup?

PETER: He returned seven cups. We never found where he obtained the other six.

[Luke laughs once, then grows quiet.]

LUKE: You remember them with great tenderness.

PETER: I remember being wrong about both women.

LUKE: Both?

PETER: Hannah and Damaris. Hannah had gone hungry for three days because I accused her. She thought accepting bread would make her look guilty.

LUKE: How did you repair that?

PETER: I brought bread every morning and ate the first piece on her step. On the fourth day she took the loaf before I could sit down.

LUKE: Did she forgive you?

PETER: She let me repair her shutter. It still stuck afterward, so perhaps not entirely.

LUKE: You tell these stories as if the work mattered more than the declaration.

PETER: It mattered that they ate.

LUKE: And if Damaris had stolen again?

PETER: We would have opened the tally.

LUKE: You would still have kept her?

PETER: I do not know what I would have done. It did not happen. You keep asking me to judge people who are not in the room.

LUKE: I am trying to find the boundary.

PETER: For whom?

LUKE: For the account.

PETER: Your account has a face now.

LUKE: We are speaking of your face.

PETER: You have asked four times whether work must come before a man may sit at the table.

LUKE: It is a fair question.

PETER: It is. Whose chair is empty?

[Luke closes the tablet.]

LUKE: Tell me another.

PETER: Silas.

LUKE: What did he do?

PETER: He left a fever house.

LUKE: Was he a physician?

PETER: No. He was strong, willing, and very proud of being willing. We sent him with water to a family near the south gate.

LUKE: How many were ill?

PETER: Father, mother, two boys. The youngest was five. Silas heard the boy struggling to breathe and put the jar down outside.

LUKE: He left them?

PETER: He ran to his sister’s farm. The mother crawled to the jar and brought it inside herself.

LUKE: Did they live?

PETER: The mother and older boy. Not the father. Not the little one.

LUKE: When did Silas return?

PETER: After the burials.

LUKE: What did the mother do?

PETER: Would not see him. He lay outside her wall until the neighbors complained.

LUKE: Did you make him leave?

PETER: I took him to my room. He had not eaten. He would not eat then either.

LUKE: What did he say?

PETER: That he had heard the child call from inside. That he had lifted the latch, then smelled the sickness and dropped it.

LUKE: He could have gone in.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: He knew they needed him.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And two died.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: How did you answer him?

PETER: At first, badly. I told him about the courtyard.

LUKE: Why was that bad?

PETER: I told it too quickly. He thought I was saying our deeds were the same, as if matching his failure to mine would close the graves.

LUKE: What did you do instead?

PETER: Sat with him.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Through the night. He slept near dawn with his head against a flour jar.

LUKE: Did you pray with him?

PETER: He could not bear it. I prayed while he slept.

LUKE: Then what?

PETER: In the morning a girl came from another fever house asking for water.

LUKE: You sent Silas.

PETER: I asked him to lift the jar. He could not touch it.

LUKE: So you went.

PETER: We both went. I carried the jar. He carried cloths.

LUKE: Did he enter?

PETER: He reached the door and turned white. I went inside. After a while I heard him set the cloths down behind me.

LUKE: He followed?

PETER: Only to the first room. Then he was sick in the yard.

LUKE: But he returned?

PETER: The next day. He stood farther inside. On the third, he changed the bedding under an old man who cursed him without pause.

LUKE: Did the old man live?

PETER: Yes, and remained ungrateful for years.

LUKE: What of the mother whose family Silas left?

PETER: Miriam. She would not see him for eleven months.

LUKE: He kept asking?

PETER: No. I told him to stop troubling her door. He repaired water jars for the houses with sickness. Left them at the gate.

LUKE: Did Miriam ever receive him?

PETER: Her surviving boy broke an arm falling from a wall. Silas carried him to a physician.

LUKE: Was that arranged?

PETER: The boy fell. We are not clever enough to arrange everything.

LUKE: And Miriam?

PETER: She saw Silas holding the boy still while the bone was set. Afterward she gave him the broken water jar from her house.

LUKE: To repair.

PETER: It took him four attempts. It leaked from a different place each time.

LUKE: Did she forgive him?

PETER: She filled it and kept it by her door.

LUKE: That is not quite an answer.

PETER: It is the answer I have.

LUKE: What became of Silas?

PETER: He was the first man sent when fever came. He still vomited sometimes. He went anyway.

LUKE: Did he ever stop hearing the child?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then he was not healed.

PETER: He carried water.

LUKE: You keep doing that.

PETER: Doing what?

LUKE: Refusing the word I offer and giving me a jar, a roof, a loaf.

PETER: Those were the things in front of us.

LUKE: They do not settle the debt.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then what settles it?

PETER: I have been waiting for you to tell me the man’s name.

[Long pause.]

LUKE: Theon.

PETER: The fever house?

LUKE: You knew.

PETER: I knew there was a door.

LUKE: I had only begun treating the sick. Theon worked at the eastern warehouses. They brought him after sunset because he had collapsed among the grain sacks.

PETER: Who brought him?

LUKE: His daughter. Lydia. She was perhaps twelve.

PETER: What did you do?

LUKE: I examined him in the yard. His skin was hot. There was blood when he coughed.

PETER: And?

LUKE: My teacher had warned me about that fever. I had no wife then, no child. No one depended upon me. I was simply afraid.

PETER: Did you send him home?

LUKE: I told Lydia there was no room.

PETER: Was there?

LUKE: Two empty beds.

PETER: Did she know?

LUKE: She could see them through the door.

PETER: Go on.

LUKE: She brought him around to the back. I heard her knocking while I washed my hands.

PETER: Did you open it?

LUKE: No.

PETER: How long did she knock?

LUKE: I do not know.

PETER: You know.

LUKE: Until after the lamps were lit.

PETER: Did Theon die there?

LUKE: Not at my door. She got him to a storehouse. Another physician found him in the morning. He died before noon.

PETER: And Lydia?

LUKE: Gone before I looked for her.

PETER: When did you look?

LUKE: Three days later.

PETER: Why three?

LUKE: The fever spread. People came. I worked without sleeping and told myself that was why.

PETER: Was it?

LUKE: No.

PETER: Have you sought her since?

LUKE: In every place I knew to ask. I found an uncle who believed she went north. Nothing more.

PETER: Have you told anyone?

LUKE: My teacher knew I refused a patient. He did not know about the knocking.

PETER: Why tell me?

LUKE: Because I have written mercy with the same hand that held that door shut.

PETER: Show me your hand.

LUKE: What?

PETER: Give it here.

[Peter turns Luke’s hand palm upward.]

PETER: No mark.

LUKE: There would not be.

PETER: I struck a wall and bled. For years I preferred that wound. People could clean it.

LUKE: I cannot find Lydia.

PETER: Perhaps you will not.

LUKE: I cannot give Theon what I withheld.

PETER: No.

LUKE: I thought you would tell me how to make the account level.

PETER: I told you about Silas.

LUKE: Silas could carry water to another house. It did not bring the child back.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then it is not enough.

PETER: Who told you it was enough?

LUKE: What am I to do with that?

PETER: Tomorrow, who will knock?

LUKE: That is not payment for Theon.

PETER: No. It is tomorrow’s door.

LUKE: And tonight?

PETER: Tonight you eat.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: You have refused my bread since morning. I have tried courtesy. Now I shall be a fisherman.

[PETER breaks the loaf.]

LUKE: I do not know how to receive this.

PETER: Use both hands. The lentils drip.

LUKE: You make light of it.

PETER: I am trying to keep your dinner off my net.

LUKE: I thought sorrow proved repentance.

PETER: Then Silas was a holy man the night he slept against my flour jar.

LUKE: That is not what I meant.

PETER: I know. Eat.

[Luke takes the bread.]

LUKE: Did the Lord’s questions release you from the courtyard?

PETER: They brought me to it with Him there.

LUKE: And after?

PETER: After, Andrew needed help with the boat. Then Ezra needed a splinter taken out. Damaris needed someone to admit he had accused Hannah. Silas needed me to stop talking.

LUKE: Did you ever believe the work repaid Him?

PETER: No. Look at this net.

LUKE: I am looking.

PETER: There are knots here from before you were born. Some hold. Some slip. No fish has ever thanked the knot.

LUKE: That is dangerously near a proverb.

PETER: Then forget it. I have been working on this net too long.

LUKE: You knew I had come carrying something.

PETER: You asked about the maid’s hand before you asked about the Lord’s face.

LUKE: Lydia had ink on her fingers. She must have kept tallies for her father.

PETER: There she is.

LUKE: I do not want only to remember her at the door.

PETER: Then do not.

LUKE: How?

PETER: Find someone who keeps tallies. Ask whether they know her. Find the road north. Carry medicine.

LUKE: And if she is dead?

PETER: Then speak her name before God. Speak Theon’s. Do not polish what you did.

LUKE: Will you pray with me?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Not yet.

PETER: All right.

LUKE: There is something I kept until now.

PETER: About the tomb?

LUKE: About you.

PETER: That is less promising.

LUKE: Mary Magdalene entrusted me with the words as she received them that morning. I nearly gave them to you when we began.

PETER: Why did you not?

LUKE: I thought they would answer too soon.

PETER: Read them now.

LUKE: Mark kept the message like this.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[Peter sets down the needle.]

LUKE: Mary remembered your name standing apart from the others.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Had you heard it that way before?

PETER: I heard it through a door crowded with people. Everyone was speaking at once. I thought I had mistaken it.

LUKE: You had not.

PETER: No.

LUKE: “And Peter.”

PETER: Leave it as Mark left it.

[Silence.]

LUKE: You were expected in Galilee.

PETER: There was breakfast waiting.

LUKE: Before the questions.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Before you had done anything to repair it.

PETER: I swam badly and arrived wet. That was the whole of my preparation.

LUKE: I have made a hard rule from the order I thought things should happen.

PETER: Most hard rules begin that way.

LUKE: Another proverb.

PETER: That one may be embroidered if you wish. Make it large enough for John to read.

LUKE: He would notice the stitching.

PETER: He notices everything.

[Luke unfolds the tablet again.]

LUKE: John kept more after the questions.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.  
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

LUKE: Were you afraid again?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did that shame you?

PETER: Less than before. I had stopped promising what fear could never make me do.

LUKE: And the last words?

PETER: Those I knew how to obey before I knew how they would end.

LUKE: Peter, when I find Lydia—if I find her—I do not know what I will say.

PETER: Begin with her name. Let her decide how long you stay.

LUKE: Will you give me the names of the households Silas served? I may find someone who remembers the road his sister took.

PETER: I will. After we pray.

LUKE: Now?

PETER: Now.

[They pray quietly. Only the water and the small sounds of the net are heard.]

LUKE: Thank you.

PETER: Take that loaf for the road.

LUKE: I am not leaving until morning.

PETER: Then take it before Andrew finds it.

LUKE: Where will you go?

PETER: To ask Mattan why my good net has his bad cord in it.

LUKE: Will he bite you?

PETER: He is older now. He hires younger men for that.

LUKE: I should pack my medicines.

PETER: And your tablet.

LUKE: Yes. I am going to find Thomas next.

PETER: Take two loaves.
