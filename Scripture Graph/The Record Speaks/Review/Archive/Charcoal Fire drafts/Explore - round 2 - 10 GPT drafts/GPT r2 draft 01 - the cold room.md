These conversations are shaped for the telling. The scripture is not changed. My hope is that, through their witness, you come closer to Him. This is The Record Speaks.

[A door closes. Wind worries the shutters.]

PETER: Before you set your things down—put out the brazier.

LUKE: It is cold in here.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: We could move it into the passage.

PETER: Put it out.

[Luke lifts the clay lid.]

LUKE: Water?

PETER: Sand. There, in the jar.

[Sand pours over coals. A faint hiss.]

PETER: More.

[Luke pours again.]

PETER: That will do.

LUKE: Shall I open the shutter?

PETER: No. Then we shall be cold and wet.

LUKE: You chose a cheerful day for this.

PETER: You chose the day.

LUKE: You told me to come before the rains.

PETER: I was wrong.

LUKE: I have written that down.

PETER: Then at least one thing in your bundle is true.

LUKE: May I sit?

PETER: You came all this way. Sit.

[Wood scrapes against stone.]

LUKE: Is it the smoke?

PETER: The charcoal.

LUKE: I thought so.

PETER: My jaw locks. Here.

[Peter touches beneath his ear.]

PETER: Like a hook has caught inside it. Foolish thing. Years, and still a handful of black stones can do that.

LUKE: It is not foolish.

PETER: It is when a man asks his host to freeze.

LUKE: I have stayed in colder rooms.

PETER: You physicians say that as if cold were a medicine.

LUKE: Sometimes it is.

PETER: And sometimes you charge money for saying so.

LUKE: Not today.

PETER: Good. I have fish, but no money.

LUKE: I did not come to collect either.

PETER: No. You came for the courtyard.

LUKE: I came for what happened there.

PETER: Mark has it.

LUKE: Mark has what you gave him.

PETER: That should be enough.

LUKE: It would be, if ink breathed.

PETER: Ink behaves better than breath.

LUKE: Does it?

PETER: Usually.

LUKE: Mark says you spoke vehemently before they took Him.

PETER: Mark cleaned that word.

LUKE: Cleaned it?

PETER: I was loud. I leaned across the table. I struck it with my hand and made the cups jump. John caught one before it fell.

LUKE: Why were you angry?

PETER: Because the Lord knew me.

LUKE: And you did not wish to be known?

PETER: Not that part. I wanted Him to know the man I meant to be. The brave one. The man who had already settled what he would do.

LUKE: Had you settled it?

PETER: Completely. That is what made me so loud.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

PETER: Yes.

PETER: That I was afraid.

LUKE: You kept it?

PETER: I had nowhere to put it.

LUKE: You could have thrown it away.

PETER: It was a good sword.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: I know. I know.

[Wind pushes rain against the shutter.]

PETER: There were six or seven around the coals. One guard had taken off a sandal and held his foot toward them. Another kept rubbing his palms together.

LUKE: Angry?

PETER: I had walked all night. I was cold. I thought I deserved the stool.

PETER: Yes.

PETER: Keep the stool.

LUKE: Mark does not have it.

PETER: Mark had only so much parchment.

PETER: I noticed everyone.

LUKE: What did she look like?

PETER: Young. Younger than people make her when they tell it. One front tooth crossed the other. Red wool around her shoulders.

LUKE: Did she accuse you?

PETER: She recognized me.

LUKE: Mark’s account sounds like an accusation.

PETER: It felt like one because I wanted it hidden. She was curious. Perhaps pleased to know something before the men knew it.

LUKE: What did you think she could do to you?

PETER: Call the guards. Shut the gate. Point to the sword. I gave her a whole army before she finished looking at me.

LUKE: You answered at once?

PETER: Too fast.

LUKE: Did anyone believe you?

PETER: I do not know. The first time, no one cared much. That should have stopped me.

LUKE: Why did it not?

PETER: Because I had heard myself say it.

LUKE: And?

PETER: Now I had that lie to protect as well as my skin.

LUKE: I want to read Mark’s whole account.

PETER: You have read it.

LUKE: Not here.

PETER: The room will not improve it.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Read.

PETER: Both. One returned with a bad leg. The other did not return.

LUKE: Neri came to you.

PETER: He sat behind the house after everyone had gone. I found him turning a roof tile over and over in his hands.

LUKE: What did he want?

PETER: He wanted me to say he had been frightened.

LUKE: Was he?

PETER: Of course.

LUKE: Did you say it?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did it help?

PETER: No. He already knew.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: I asked whether the brothers’ mother had food.

LUKE: Did she?

PETER: I did not know. Neither did he. We filled a basket and carried it to her.

LUKE: Together?

PETER: Neri walked ten paces behind me.

LUKE: Did she receive him?

PETER: She saw him at the gate and struck the basket from my hands.

LUKE: What did he do?

PETER: Bent down and gathered lentils from the dirt.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Gathered with him. I told him he had missed one near his sandal.

LUKE: Did you return?

PETER: The next week.

LUKE: With another basket?

PETER: A smaller one. I learn slowly.

LUKE: Did she strike it down?

PETER: She shut the gate before we reached it.

LUKE: And after that?

PETER: Rain came. Her roof failed over the sleeping room. Neri heard and brought tiles from his yard.

LUKE: Did she let him repair it?

PETER: She let me repair it.

LUKE: Could you?

PETER: Badly. Neri stood below making noises each time I set a tile wrong.

LUKE: What sort of noises?

PETER: The sort a fuller makes when an apostle is ruining a roof.

LUKE: You let him help.

PETER: I climbed down and gave him the hammer.

LUKE: Did the mother forgive him?

PETER: She let him finish the roof.

LUKE: That is not what I asked.

PETER: It is what happened.

LUKE: Did he remain among you?

PETER: At first he stood by the door. If anyone looked at him, he left.

LUKE: Did you go after him?

PETER: Usually. Once I did not.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: I was tired of him leaving.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: John went.

LUKE: Of course.

PETER: Of course.

LUKE: How long before Neri sat inside?

PETER: Through one winter. Near spring, the brother with the bad leg moved his stool aside. There was room beside him.

LUKE: Did they speak?

PETER: Not that day.

LUKE: But Neri sat.

PETER: Until dark.

LUKE: Is that what you would do with Nicanor?

PETER: I have not met Nicanor.

LUKE: Tell me another.

PETER: There was Tamar.

LUKE: What had she done?

PETER: Kept the money we had gathered for widows.

LUKE: All of it?

PETER: Twelve silver pieces. She said mice had carried them.

LUKE: Mice.

PETER: She was frightened and not gifted at lying.

LUKE: Why did she take it?

PETER: Her son owed money. The lender had two men waiting outside her door.

LUKE: Did she confess?

PETER: After we found four pieces sewn into her sleeping mat.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Told her to repay all twelve before the next moon.

LUKE: Could she?

PETER: No.

LUKE: You knew that.

PETER: I knew it the next morning. That night I was angry.

LUKE: What changed your mind?

PETER: She sold her lamp.

LUKE: To begin repayment?

PETER: And the blanket from her bed. John found her sleeping under a grain sack.

LUKE: What did he say to you?

PETER: He brought me the lamp and put it in my hands.

LUKE: Nothing else?

PETER: John can make silence very unpleasant.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Took the lamp back. Gave her the blanket. Sat with the women whose money she had taken.

LUKE: Were they willing to wait?

PETER: Two were. One was not.

LUKE: Then what?

PETER: Tamar spun wool. I carried it to the market because the merchant cheated her when she went alone.

LUKE: Did he not cheat you?

PETER: He tried once.

LUKE: How long did repayment take?

PETER: Eleven months. She marked every piece on a board with charcoal.

LUKE: Charcoal.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Was that difficult for you?

PETER: The first time I saw the board, I walked outside and was sick near the wall.

LUKE: Did Tamar know why?

PETER: No. I went back in.

LUKE: Did she repay everything?

PETER: The final piece belonged to an old widow named Keren. Tamar laid it on her palm and could not look up.

LUKE: What did Keren do?

PETER: Counted the whole purse. Twice.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: Gave Tamar the key to the storeroom.

LUKE: That seems unwise.

PETER: I said so.

LUKE: What did Keren say?

PETER: That was between Keren and me.

LUKE: Did Tamar keep the accounts again?

PETER: For six years. No mice troubled us.

LUKE: You are smiling.

PETER: She counted lentils as if each one owed her money.

LUKE: Was it easy to trust her?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: Keren did first. I followed.

LUKE: Another.

PETER: You ask like a man choosing fish.

LUKE: I am trying to understand the work He gave you.

PETER: Then hear about Joram.

LUKE: Who was he?

PETER: A maker of rope. He came to my door before dawn with blood on his sleeve.

LUKE: Whose blood?

PETER: His wife’s. Damaris. He had struck her with the wooden bar from a loom.

LUKE: Was she alive?

PETER: Yes. Her cheek was broken. One eye closed. Their little boy had seen it.

LUKE: What did Joram ask?

PETER: He wanted me to pray and send him home clean.

LUKE: Did you pray?

PETER: Later.

LUKE: Did you send him home?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Where did he go?

PETER: To a boat shed with two men who could stop him if he tried to leave.

LUKE: And Damaris?

PETER: Two women stayed with her. A physician set her cheek.

LUKE: You kept them apart.

PETER: She shook when his sandals sounded in the lane.

LUKE: Some would say a wife must receive a husband who repents.

PETER: They did not see her shake.

LUKE: How long was he kept away?

PETER: Until she chose otherwise.

LUKE: What did repentance look like for him?

PETER: At first, loud weeping.

LUKE: At first?

PETER: Then anger because no one admired the weeping. Then three days of refusing food. Then he ate.

LUKE: And after that?

PETER: He worked. His wages went to Damaris. He slept in the shed. He met with me and two older men each evening.

LUKE: What did you speak about?

PETER: That day. The day before it. Other days he had frightened her. He liked to tell only about the blow.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: One blow can be mourned quickly. A house full of fear takes longer to describe.

LUKE: Did he change?

PETER: Some days.

LUKE: And the others?

PETER: Some days he blamed the wine. Some days the loom. Once he blamed Damaris for ducking the wrong way.

LUKE: What did you do then?

PETER: Sent him back to the shed before I struck him myself.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: I said the work was not finished in me.

LUKE: Did Damaris ever receive him?

PETER: After six months she let him sit inside the outer gate.

LUKE: Alone with her?

PETER: Never. Not then.

LUKE: Did she forgive him?

PETER: Ask her.

LUKE: I cannot.

PETER: Then do not make an answer for her.

LUKE: How did it go?

PETER: Slowly. He learned to leave when his voice rose. He learned to give her his wages without asking what she bought.

PETER: One evening their boy knocked over a lamp. Oil went across the floor.

LUKE: Were you there?

PETER: In the courtyard. Joram stood so quickly the stool fell behind him.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: The boy covered his head.

LUKE: And Joram?

PETER: He saw the boy do it.

LUKE: Then?

PETER: He got on his knees and wiped up the oil.

LUKE: Nothing else?

PETER: Nothing else.

LUKE: Did Damaris see?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: What did she do?

PETER: She picked up the stool and set it upright.

LUKE: Did he return to the house?

PETER: Some months later. With rules she chose. If he broke one, he left.

LUKE: Did he break them?

PETER: Twice.

LUKE: And leave?

PETER: The first time, no. We removed him. The second time, he took his blanket and went before anyone came.

LUKE: Was that enough?

PETER: It was different.

LUKE: Where are they now?

PETER: In the same house. Damaris has white in her hair. Joram’s hands are bent from rope work.

PETER: When fever took her last winter, he washed her hands because she could not lift them. He did it gently. I watched.

LUKE: You stayed with them all those years.

PETER: Not every day.

LUKE: But you returned.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Even when there was no clean ending.

PETER: Most days end because it gets dark.

LUKE: What should I do with Nicanor?

PETER: Find him.

LUKE: And say what?

PETER: You have said enough from a ship.

LUKE: Sit until morning.

PETER: If he still asks it.

LUKE: And the three people he named?

PETER: Learn their names. Learn who waits for the man who did not return. Do not take Nicanor to their doors without asking them.

LUKE: If the gathering will not receive him?

PETER: Begin outside.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Bring a thicker cloak than this one.

LUKE: You will not give me a rule.

PETER: I have given you a stool, food, a roof, twelve silver pieces, a boat shed, and six years of lentils. What else do you want?

LUKE: A sentence I can remember.

PETER: Remember Neri’s purple nails.

[Luke closes the folded sheet.]

LUKE: Is that why you made Mark keep the courtyard?

PETER: Neri heard it before he came to me.

LUKE: Your denial?

PETER: All of it. The maid. The swearing. The bird.

LUKE: He knew the worst before he knocked.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Tamar too?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Joram?

PETER: I made him listen again.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: He thought a man appointed to care for others would not understand him.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: Enough to be afraid of myself. Enough not to leave him alone with his promises.

LUKE: When you preach the courtyard, how do you finish it?

PETER: I do not finish in the courtyard.

LUKE: You go to the shore.

PETER: I go to the bread first.

LUKE: Why the bread?

PETER: Because He gave it to me.

LUKE: Before the questions.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You want that order kept.

PETER: John kept it.

LUKE: You have not answered why the charcoal still troubles you.

PETER: My body is slower than John’s ink.

LUKE: Does it trouble you every time?

PETER: No. Sometimes I can cook over it. Sometimes I wake before dawn with that girl’s red wool in front of me.

LUKE: What do you do then?

PETER: Get up.

LUKE: And?

PETER: There is always something. A net torn. Someone ill. A letter waiting. An old man who needs help down the steps.

LUKE: Work.

PETER: The work He put there.

LUKE: Does it silence the memory?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then what does?

PETER: Some nights, nothing.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: Do not be frightened of that.

LUKE: I am not.

PETER: You are a little.

LUKE: A little.

PETER: I remember the court. I remember the shore. They are both mine.

LUKE: And when you stand beside someone like Neri?

PETER: I know where to put the stool.

[Rain softens against the shutter.]

LUKE: Your hands are blue.

PETER: Yours are worse.

LUKE: We buried the brazier thoroughly.

PETER: There is dry wood beneath the bench.

LUKE: You kept that from me.

PETER: You were asking questions.

[Peter kneels beside the bench.]

LUKE: Shall I open the shutter for the smoke?

PETER: Only a hand’s width.

[The shutter opens. Cold air moves through the room.]

PETER: Small pieces first. Not that one. It is damp.

LUKE: I know how to make a fire.

PETER: Physicians know everything when no one is bleeding.

[Luke breaks kindling.]

PETER: Where will you go from here?

LUKE: South first.

PETER: To Nicanor?

LUKE: When I can reach him.

PETER: And before that?

LUKE: There is one more man I have promised to find.

PETER: Who?

LUKE: Thomas.

[Peter places two pieces of dry wood across the kindling.]

[Luke shields the spark with both hands.]

[The first flame catches.]
