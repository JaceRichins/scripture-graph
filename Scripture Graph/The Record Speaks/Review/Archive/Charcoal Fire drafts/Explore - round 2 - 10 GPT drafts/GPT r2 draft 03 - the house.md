PETER: I know.

PETER: The maid’s face was thinner than he says.

PETER: It is an answer I have used before. You looked disappointed, so I confess it.

LUKE: I am not disappointed.

PETER: Good. Mark kept asking. Not with words every time. He would sit with the reed above the page and wait.

PETER: Yes.

PETER: Yes.

PETER: My hands were cold.

PETER: Yes.

PETER: At that last question, I understood why I hurt.

PETER: The girl. The second maid. The men by the fire. My mouth had gone to work each time.

PETER: There was nowhere left to hide and no need to instruct Him.

PETER: No. I am trying to keep you from making the work shine.

PETER: A miller’s clerk from the south quarter. Thin beard. Left foot turned inward. He could add measures in his head faster than I could with stones.

LUKE: What did he do?

PETER: He kept the grain account for nine households during a poor winter. He shaved a little from each measure and sold it.

LUKE: For himself?

PETER: His wife was ill at first. Medicine cost money. Then she recovered and he continued.

LUKE: Was much taken?

PETER: Enough that two widows ate watered broth near the end of the month.

PETER: He blamed Rhoda, the servant who carried the sacks. Rhoda was fifteen. Her master beat her.

LUKE: And Hanan came to you?

PETER: After the third night. He came after everyone had gone and put six silver pieces on that table.

LUKE: This table?

PETER: The leg was straighter then.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: I was very pleased with myself.

PETER: I believed I knew this sort of man. I told him we would bring Rhoda in at dawn, he would admit it, she would forgive him, and we would all eat.

LUKE: Did she?

PETER: She threw a scoop of barley at his face.

LUKE: Ah.

PETER: Then she threw the scoop at mine.

LUKE: Did it hurt?

PETER: It was olive wood.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: Too much. I tried to make the morning obey the shape I had planned.

PETER: Rhoda left. Hanan began to cry. My wife brought me the broom and set it against my chair.

LUKE: Without a word.

PETER: She had used the words before.

LUKE: What happened to Hanan?

PETER: We counted what he owed. Not only silver—the missing flour, the beating Rhoda had borne, the names he had allowed to darken.

PETER: He sold his good cloak. He worked at the mill without pay on every sixth day. I went with him to each house because twice he turned aside before the door.

LUKE: And Rhoda?

PETER: She would not see him.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Two winters.

LUKE: Did you press her?

PETER: Once. That was another mistake.

LUKE: Did she ever forgive him?

PETER: I do not know everything that passed in her heart.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: She put her jar down. He filled it. He did not heap the measure, and he did not make it small. She took it home.

LUKE: That was all?

PETER: The next week she came again.

PETER: Not where I heard.

PETER: I had learned something about arranging other people’s feasts.

LUKE: How did it go with Hanan after that?

PETER: Badly, then better, then badly. He lied about a broken scale. He told the truth before supper. We began again.

PETER: Three years later I put him back over the measures.

LUKE: You trusted him with grain?

PETER: Rhoda kept the tally.

LUKE: Was that your decision?

PETER: It was hers.

[The smell of onions reaches the room.]

LUKE: That is beginning to burn.

PETER: It is beginning to become supper.

LUKE: There is a difference?

PETER: In this house, a small one.

LUKE: You said names and faces were placed before you. Who else?

PETER: Nicanor.

LUKE: What had he done?

PETER: He gave three names to men who came searching a gathering.

LUKE: Under threat?

PETER: They held his oldest son.

LUKE: Then he was compelled.

PETER: Tell that to Azor’s damaged hand.

LUKE: Azor was one of the names?

PETER: He was taken and beaten. Two fingers never opened again.

LUKE: Did Nicanor’s son live?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: That matters.

PETER: It mattered to Nicanor. It mattered to Azor too. That was part of the trouble.

LUKE: Nicanor came to you afterward?

PETER: At night. People with certain things to tell prefer night.

PETER: He sat where you are. He gave me the names, then gave them again. Each time he reached Azor, he struck his own knee.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Sat with him.

LUKE: Until morning?

PETER: Until the little lamp went out. Then we sat in the dark because neither of us wished to stand and find oil.

LUKE: Did you tell him about the courtyard?

PETER: He knew it already. I had told it in the gathering.

LUKE: In preaching?

PETER: Wherever Mark had his reed. Wherever someone began speaking as if courage were a possession he had stored.

LUKE: Did your story comfort Nicanor?

PETER: No. He hated it.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: He thought I had used my wrong to take his away.

LUKE: Had you?

PETER: A little.

PETER: I wanted him to stop striking his knee. I wanted him to eat. I wanted morning to make him easier to look at.

LUKE: What did he need?

PETER: To go to Azor’s house.

LUKE: Did you take him?

PETER: The next day.

LUKE: Was he received?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What did Azor do?

PETER: Closed the door.

LUKE: Did you return?

PETER: A month later.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: The door closed again.

LUKE: Again?

PETER: After the rains.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: We had business there. Azor’s roof had lost tiles.

LUKE: Nicanor repaired them?

PETER: Azor would not let him enter, so Nicanor climbed from the neighbor’s roof. He replaced twelve tiles and came down the same way.

LUKE: Did Azor know?

PETER: He watched from the lane.

LUKE: Did that change anything?

PETER: Not that day.

PETER: Later, Azor began coming to the gathering again. He sat with his damaged hand under his cloak. Nicanor sat near the door.

LUKE: Far apart.

PETER: Eight floor stones. I counted.

LUKE: Of course you did.

PETER: One evening the oil lamp smoked. Azor could not trim it with his hand. Nicanor rose, trimmed the wick, and returned to his place.

LUKE: Did they speak?

PETER: You want them to speak.

LUKE: I do.

PETER: So did I.

LUKE: Did they?

PETER: Months later. Not kindly.

LUKE: What was said?

PETER: It belongs to them.

LUKE: How did it go?

PETER: They never became easy with each other. When Azor’s wife died, Nicanor dug beside him until the grave was deep enough.

PETER: When Nicanor’s son married, Azor came late and stood outside the courtyard.

LUKE: Did he enter?

PETER: For the meal. He left before the singing.

LUKE: Is that enough?

PETER: It is what happened.

LUKE: Did Nicanor continue in the gatherings?

PETER: Yes. He became the man who checked the door twice and then sat beside it. For years he could not hear boots in the lane without rising.

LUKE: And you?

PETER: For years I chose a place where I could see the fire but not feel much heat.

LUKE: Nobody said anything?

PETER: Nobody.

LUKE: John noticed.

PETER: John notices everything. He never said.

LUKE: Did you move nearer in time?

PETER: A boy moved my stool.

LUKE: A boy?

PETER: Micah. Twelve years old, perhaps. He wanted the wall and said my knees were too large for that corner.

LUKE: So you sat near the fire.

PETER: I sat where he put me.

LUKE: That was the third person?

PETER: No. Micah had stolen a knife, but every boy has stolen a knife.

LUKE: I did not.

PETER: You became a physician. You stole other things.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: Mara was the third.

LUKE: What did Mara do?

PETER: She left her father during a fever.

LUKE: Left the house?

PETER: Left the town. She took his purse and went with a caravan. Her brothers found him dead two days later.

LUKE: Why did she leave?

PETER: She feared the sickness. She also wanted the money. She would not let us clean one part away with the other.

LUKE: How long afterward did she come to you?

PETER: Six years.

LUKE: Six?

PETER: She had lived in three cities. She married, buried her husband, sold dyed cloth, lost the money. Then she came home.

LUKE: Did her brothers receive her?

PETER: One would not look at her. The other spat near her feet.

LUKE: What did she ask of you?

PETER: A place at the meal.

LUKE: Only that?

PETER: At first.

PETER: I let her sit near the door. My wife put lentils before her. Mara ate too quickly and was sick in the yard.

LUKE: Did she remain here?

PETER: Three nights. On the fourth morning a small bronze cup was missing.

LUKE: She took it?

PETER: We found it wrapped in her bedding.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: I shouted.

LUKE: You?

PETER: I can shout.

LUKE: I am aware.

PETER: I told her she had entered a house that made room for her and had stolen from the children.

LUKE: Was that true?

PETER: Entirely.

LUKE: But?

PETER: My wife picked up the bedding and carried it back inside.

LUKE: What did you understand from that?

PETER: That Mara was staying.

LUKE: Did you agree?

PETER: Not at once.

PETER: I went to mend nets until my hands stopped wanting to throw her into the street.

LUKE: Then?

PETER: She returned the cup. We gave her a place in the storeroom, where there were no cups.

LUKE: What work did she do?

PETER: She cleaned lentils. Poorly. Half the stones remained.

PETER: She burned bread because she would go still whenever someone entered suddenly. She lied about small things that no one had asked.

LUKE: Why keep her?

PETER: Because she was there in the morning.

LUKE: That sounds almost like one of the sayings you dislike.

PETER: Then remove it. Write that she snored and frightened the youngest child.

LUKE: How long did she stay?

PETER: Eleven months in this house. Then she rented a room near the dyers.

LUKE: Did she make peace with her brothers?

PETER: One died without seeing her.

LUKE: And the other?

PETER: He came when she was ill. He stood in the courtyard and would not enter.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Took a stool outside.

LUKE: For him?

PETER: For me. My legs hurt. He stood for an hour.

LUKE: Did Mara know he was there?

PETER: She could see his shadow beneath the door.

LUKE: Did he go in?

PETER: At dusk.

LUKE: What passed between them?

PETER: Very little that day. He brought figs the next morning. She could not eat them, so she held one.

LUKE: Did she recover?

PETER: Yes. She complained that the figs were old.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: She keeps the food list for five households.

LUKE: You trusted a thief with the food list.

PETER: She does not touch the money.

LUKE: Does Rhoda keep that tally too?

PETER: Rhoda cannot keep every tally in the city.

LUKE: Peter, each of these took years.

PETER: They are people. They take as long as they take.

LUKE: When the Lord told you to feed His sheep, did you imagine Hanan, Nicanor, Mara?

PETER: I imagined better sheep.

LUKE: Better?

PETER: Grateful ones. Clean ones. Ones that came when called and did not steal the cup.

LUKE: Yet you kept opening the door.

PETER: Some days my wife opened it while I argued.

LUKE: Did you ever think your care for them paid what you owed?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Truly?

PETER: For a while. I kept count.

LUKE: Of what?

PETER: Meals. Nights without sleep. Journeys made for men who had lied to me. I would do some hard thing and feel better before bed.

LUKE: What changed?

PETER: I became angry when the count did not purchase peace.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: An old woman named Tirzah asked why I was making her gratitude into another debt.

LUKE: What had you done for her?

PETER: Repaired her roof badly.

LUKE: Did she say it was badly?

PETER: The rain said it.

LUKE: What did you answer her?

PETER: I had no answer fit to use.

LUKE: Do you still count?

PETER: Fish, yes. Floor stones. Sacks of grain. Not those things.

LUKE: I came believing the work was the answer.

PETER: It was an answer.

LUKE: But not payment.

PETER: On the shore, who prepared the meal?

LUKE: The Lord.

PETER: Who brought me bread?

LUKE: He did.

PETER: Before you write about what these hands did afterward, keep that first.

[The child returns. A small object is placed on the table.]

LUKE: She has brought you a shell.

PETER: That was my purse.

LUKE: Shall I stop her?

PETER: No. There is nothing in it now.

LUKE: There was something?

PETER: Your reed knife.

LUKE: Ah.

PETER: She likes you very much.

[Luke retrieves the knife. The child runs away.]

LUKE: There is something I have not told you.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: You cannot know.

PETER: You have asked me three times whether work can pay a debt.

LUKE: I did not count.

PETER: I do.

LUKE: This was before I joined the Way. I had rooms near a harbor. I treated sailors, merchants, whoever could pay enough to keep oil in the lamps.

PETER: What was his name?

LUKE: Amasa.

PETER: Go on.

LUKE: He carried cargo from the ships. A fever went through the lower streets. His wife brought him to my door after dark.

PETER: Did you see him?

LUKE: Through the opening.

PETER: How ill?

LUKE: He could not stand without her arm. His lips were dry. There were swellings beneath his jaw.

PETER: And you were afraid.

LUKE: Yes.

LUKE: There were six patients inside. Two children. I thought if I brought him in, all of them might take it.

PETER: Perhaps they would have.

LUKE: I had another room.

PETER: Did you?

LUKE: It was filled with jars and linen. I could have cleared it.

PETER: But you did not.

LUKE: I told his wife there was no room.

PETER: Did you have medicine?

LUKE: Enough to ease the pain. Perhaps no more than that.

PETER: What happened?

LUKE: He died before morning, against the wall of a storehouse.

PETER: Who told you?

LUKE: His wife came back. She had blood on her sleeve where he had bitten his mouth.

PETER: What was her name?

LUKE: I never asked.

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: I know.

PETER: No. You do not know her name. That is the next thing, not the last thing.

LUKE: It has been many years.

PETER: Is the harbor still there?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Then someone remembers Amasa.

LUKE: I have treated hundreds since. I have entered houses other physicians would not enter.

PETER: Have you told yourself that often?

LUKE: Often enough that it no longer works.

PETER: Good.

LUKE: Good?

PETER: Now you may stop using sick men to argue with a dead one.

LUKE: I thought you would tell me the Lord knows.

PETER: He does.

LUKE: I thought you would tell me to accept mercy.

PETER: Eat first.

LUKE: I am not hungry.

PETER: Neither was I on the shore.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: Find the woman’s name if she lives. Find her children if she does not. Tell them what happened at your door.

LUKE: And if they hate me?

PETER: They may.

LUKE: If they refuse what I offer?

PETER: Do not make them responsible for easing you.

LUKE: What should I offer?

PETER: Ask them.

LUKE: You make it sound harder than confession.

PETER: Hanan confessed before dawn. Rhoda still had bruises.

LUKE: And if there is no one left to find?

PETER: Then say Amasa’s name when you pray. Not patient. Not porter. Amasa.

LUKE: Will that be enough?

PETER: I do not know.

LUKE: That is not comforting.

PETER: Come nearer the fire.

[Luke’s stool moves across the floor.]

LUKE: I can see why Mark waited.

PETER: Mark would have asked the woman’s name before I did.

LUKE: You let him write the courtyard because you wanted men like Nicanor to know you had stood where they stood.

PETER: No.

LUKE: No?

PETER: That happened, but it was not why.

LUKE: Then why?

PETER: Because I did it.

LUKE: That is all?

PETER: It belonged in the account.

LUKE: You were the one telling it. You could have left yourself outside the gate.

PETER: Then they would have received a man who never existed.

LUKE: Were you not afraid they would trust you less?

PETER: They should know whom they are trusting.

LUKE: And if they use it against you?

PETER: Some have.

LUKE: What do you say?

PETER: Usually too much.

LUKE: Usually?

PETER: On my best days, nothing.

LUKE: Do you still wake with it?

PETER: Sometimes I wake before the birds.

LUKE: What do you do?

PETER: Listen to the house.

LUKE: For what?

PETER: My wife breathing. A jar cooling. Someone turning over on a mat. Ordinary sounds.

PETER: Then I remember another morning. Water against the stones. Men counting fish. Bread breaking.

LUKE: Does the courtyard become smaller?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What becomes smaller?

PETER: The room I built around it.

[The pot is carried in. Bowls are placed on the table.]

PETER: Move your pages.

LUKE: There is oil on this one.

PETER: Then Mark will believe you visited us.

LUKE: He will complain.

PETER: He has eaten my wife’s stew. He will forgive anything for another bowl.

LUKE: Is this Mara’s bread?

PETER: No, hers is better.

LUKE: You know that was nearly kind.

PETER: Do not tell her.

LUKE: Shall we read the final words John kept from the shore?

PETER: Not tonight.

LUKE: They concern what awaits you.

PETER: What awaits me will arrive without your help.

LUKE: And the last command?

PETER: I remember it.

LUKE: You do not want it read?

PETER: I want this read again.

LUKE: Which part?

PETER: The meal. Begin when they came to land.

LUKE: All of it?

PETER: Through the bread and fish.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:9-14), read by Luke: As soon then as they were come to land, they saw a fire of coals there, and fish laid thereon, and bread.
Jesus saith unto them, Bring of the fish which ye have now caught.
Simon Peter went up, and drew the net to land full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and three: and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken.
Jesus saith unto them, Come and dine. And none of the disciples durst ask him, Who art thou? knowing that it was the Lord.
Jesus then cometh, and taketh bread, and giveth them, and fish likewise.
This is now the third time that Jesus shewed himself to his disciples, after that he was risen from the dead.

[Long pause.]

PETER: Give me your bowl.

LUKE: I can reach.

PETER: You are dripping on Mark.

LUKE: Here.

PETER: More?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Good.

LUKE: Peter, when you handed Hanan his measure, when you sat outside Mara’s door, when you climbed to Azor’s roof—

PETER: I did not climb. Nicanor climbed. My knees had sense by then.

LUKE: When you went with them, did you think of that breakfast?

PETER: Not every time.

LUKE: Sometimes?

PETER: Sometimes I thought only that the lentils needed salt.

LUKE: They do.

PETER: The child went twice.

LUKE: She returned once with a beetle and once with my knife.

PETER: There is salt by your hand.

LUKE: So there is.

PETER: Pass it.

[The salt is passed.]

LUKE: I should leave before Ezra returns about the boat.

PETER: He is already outside.

LUKE: I hear nothing.

PETER: He is looking through the crack beside the hinge.

LUKE: Should I invite him in?

PETER: Then he will eat, and we will mend the boat in darkness.

LUKE: You were going to mend it after supper.

PETER: I was hoping he had forgotten the signal.

LUKE: Thank you for letting me read Mark’s account.

PETER: Do not thank me yet. You still have to tell him about the oil.

LUKE: I will keep the look.

PETER: Keep the girl at the door too.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: And the first bird.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: People hurry to the second. The first gave me time to leave.

LUKE: You want that known.

PETER: I want what happened known.

LUKE: I will look for Amasa’s family.

PETER: Ask at the harbor for his wife’s name.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: And eat before you go tomorrow.

LUKE: Another instruction?

PETER: You become solemn when hungry. It cannot be good for the sick.

LUKE: May I return?

PETER: Bring Mark.

LUKE: He will want the chair away from the pot.

PETER: He may sit where the boy put me.

LUKE: Near the fire.

PETER: Near enough.

[Luke gathers the pages. Outside, a man shifts his weight beside the door.]

PETER: Where are you going after the harbor?

LUKE: I am going to find Thomas.
