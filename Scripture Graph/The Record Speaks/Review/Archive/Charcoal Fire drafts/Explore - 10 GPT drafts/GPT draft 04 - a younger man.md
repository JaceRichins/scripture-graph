The long silences are not emptiness; they are the sound of a man discovering that mercy has outlived his shame.

LUKE: This is Luke. I have gone from place to place gathering the accounts of those who knew Jesus of Nazareth—those who walked beside Him, ate with Him, feared for Him, failed Him, and saw Him alive again. These conversations are dramatized. The scripture is not. My hope is that, through their witness, we come closer to Him. This is The Record Speaks.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because I covered myself that night. It was nearly the death of me.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

LUKE: Perhaps the message entered your judgment and named you there.

PETER: Say that again.

LUKE: You had put yourself outside. So the mercy came outside to find you.

PETER: Luke—

LUKE: Is that wrong?

PETER: I do not know.

LUKE: You were named.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Not because you had denied Him less than you believed.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Not because it did not matter.

PETER: No.

LUKE: You were named where you thought your name could no longer be spoken with the others.

PETER: “And Peter.”

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I thought it was added because I was chief among them.

LUKE: Do you still?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What do you think now?

PETER: I think a shepherd counts differently from a frightened man.

LUKE: How?

PETER: A frightened man counts those who remain. A shepherd counts the one who is missing.

LUKE: And on the shore?

PETER: He counted to three.

LUKE: The questions.

PETER: The denials.

LUKE: John set down each one.

PETER: He had to.

LUKE: Did you ask him to?

PETER: No. John knew what the third question did to me.

LUKE: He says you were grieved.

PETER: That is a small word for being opened.

LUKE: Did the first question surprise you?

PETER: The words “more than these” did.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because I had once measured my faithfulness against theirs.

LUKE: Although all should fall—

PETER: Yet I would not. I had said it before them all. On the shore I could no longer say I loved Him more.

LUKE: What were “these”?

PETER: You want the fisherman’s answer or the old man’s?

LUKE: Both.

PETER: The fisherman saw the nets, the boats, the catch piled on shore. Everything I knew how to be without Him. The old man hears the others breathing beside the fire and remembers how proudly he once placed himself above them.

LUKE: Which meaning is right?

PETER: The question found every rival.

LUKE: And your answer?

PETER: Smaller than my old promise.

LUKE: You did not compare yourself.

PETER: No.

LUKE: You appealed to what He knew.

PETER: It was all I had left.

LUKE: Was it enough?

PETER: Read it.

LUKE: John set it down like this.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.
He saith to him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my sheep.
He saith unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

[long silence]

LUKE: One.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Then two.

PETER: I knew.

LUKE: Before the third?

PETER: I hoped there would not be a third.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because by the second, I could see the courtyard.

LUKE: And at the third?

PETER: I could hear myself cursing.

LUKE: Did you think He was rebuking you?

PETER: For a moment.

LUKE: Was He?

PETER: He was not doing what I had feared.

LUKE: What had you feared?

PETER: That He would make me repeat the denial.

LUKE: Why would He?

PETER: Shame expects cruelty even from God.

LUKE: Instead He asked for your love.

PETER: Three times.

LUKE: Because you had denied Him three times.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did each answer repair a denial?

PETER: I could not repair them.

LUKE: Then what happened?

PETER: He went with me into each one.

LUKE: Into the memory?

PETER: Into the place I had sealed. I had kept the denials as proof that my love was false. He did not ask whether I had failed. He knew. He asked what remained beneath the failure.

LUKE: Love remained.

PETER: Bruised. Frightened. Ashamed. But yes.

LUKE: Was it difficult to say?

PETER: The first time, I answered quickly. I was still trying to stand.

LUKE: The second?

PETER: I felt the ground going.

LUKE: The third?

PETER: I stopped telling Him who I was.

LUKE: “Thou knowest all things.”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You surrendered the judgment to Him.

PETER: At last.

LUKE: And His judgment was to give you work.

PETER: That is what broke me.

LUKE: Not the third question?

PETER: The sheep.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: A rebuke would have kept the matter between Him and me. Punishment might have let me finish paying and walk away. But He placed others in my care.

LUKE: He trusted you.

PETER: Do not say it gently.

LUKE: How should I say it?

PETER: As the impossible thing it was.

LUKE: He trusted you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: After the courtyard.

PETER: Knowing the courtyard better than I did.

LUKE: With lambs.

PETER: The small ones.

LUKE: With sheep.

PETER: The wandering ones. The stubborn. The frightened. The ones who promise and flee. The ones who warm themselves at a wrong fire because the night is cold.

LUKE: You knew how to find them.

PETER: I knew where shame hides them.

LUKE: Is that why He chose you?

PETER: I do not know why He chose me.

LUKE: You have had years to consider it.

PETER: Years teach a man which questions are too large for him.

LUKE: But the work itself—did your failure prepare you for it?

PETER: Failure does not make a shepherd. Mercy may.

LUKE: How?

PETER: Before that night, I would have fed the strong.

LUKE: Those most like the man you wanted to be.

PETER: Yes. The bold. The certain. Men who stood when others sat. Men who spoke first.

LUKE: Men like Peter.

PETER: The Peter I had invented.

LUKE: And after?

PETER: I began to recognize Simon in everyone.

LUKE: The old name.

PETER: The sleeping man. The sinking man. The man who means what he promises and discovers that meaning is not strength. The man who loves Christ and still becomes afraid.

LUKE: What do you tell such a man?

PETER: At first I told him to be stronger.

LUKE: At first?

PETER: Old habits preach loudly.

LUKE: Did it help him?

PETER: It made him better at hiding.

LUKE: Then what did you learn to tell him?

PETER: Come to the fire.

LUKE: That sounds dangerous.

PETER: The right fire.

LUKE: How does he know the difference?

PETER: One fire asks whether he belongs and leaves him to save himself. The other already has bread on it.

LUKE: And asks whether he loves Christ.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Is love enough?

PETER: Love receives a command.

LUKE: Feed.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Then forgiven is not the same as finished.

PETER: No. Forgiveness was where the work began.

LUKE: Did the work silence the old accusation?

PETER: For a while, I tried to use it that way.

LUKE: To prove you were worthy?

PETER: Each person helped, each journey endured, each faithful day—I laid them against the three denials as though enough obedience might change what had happened.

LUKE: Did it?

PETER: Nothing changes what happened.

LUKE: Then how did you put it down?

PETER: I did not put it down all at once.

LUKE: The long repentance.

PETER: You physicians want a moment of healing. The fever breaks. The swelling falls. The man rises.

LUKE: Not every healing happens in a moment.

PETER: No. Some mornings I woke knowing I was forgiven. By evening I had begun another trial in my own heart.

LUKE: With yourself as the accused.

PETER: And accuser.

LUKE: Who judged?

PETER: That was the trouble. I had already heard His judgment at the shore, but I kept appealing it.

LUKE: Appealing mercy.

PETER: Mercy felt too final for the little court I had built.

LUKE: What ended the trials?

PETER: The sheep.

LUKE: The work again.

PETER: Not my success in it. Their wounds. A young man would come ashamed of what he had done. He would tell me he had ruined everything. I could hear in him the courtyard. I could smell the coals.

LUKE: What would you say?

PETER: Sometimes nothing at first.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because shame expects a quick sentence. Silence lets a man discover he has not been cast away.

LUKE: Then?

PETER: I would ask him if he loved Christ.

LUKE: Three times?

PETER: No. Those three were mine.

LUKE: And if he said yes?

PETER: I would give him something living to care for.

LUKE: As Christ did with you.

PETER: A widow needing bread. A child needing patience. A frightened brother who could not pray alone. Something he could not tend while staring forever at himself.

LUKE: Did that forgive him?

PETER: No. Christ forgives. But work received from Christ teaches a forgiven man to walk forward.

LUKE: Even when he still feels ashamed?

PETER: Feeling is sometimes the last disciple to believe.

LUKE: That sounds like something learned over years.

PETER: It cost years.

LUKE: What could you not put down?

PETER: The sound of my own voice.

LUKE: More than the cock crow?

PETER: Much more. People imagine the bird tormented me. The bird only told the truth. My voice had lied.

LUKE: Did you fear your own speech after that?

PETER: For a long time.

LUKE: Yet you were sent to speak.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Was that part of the mercy?

PETER: It was terror.

LUKE: At first.

PETER: A mouth that had denied Him was to strengthen others. Do you understand what that felt like?

LUKE: No.

PETER: Good.

LUKE: Good?

PETER: Do not pretend to know a wound merely to comfort the wounded.

LUKE: Then tell me.

PETER: Every time I spoke His name before men, I heard the night I would not speak it. Every time I said I had known Him, the servant’s face returned. Every time someone believed because of my witness, part of me wanted to stop and confess before they trusted me too far.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: Often.

LUKE: You told them about the denial.

PETER: They needed to know the witness was not the Savior.

LUKE: Was that why you gave Mark the worst detail?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The cursing.

PETER: Especially that.

LUKE: You wanted no one to make you clean at Christ’s expense.

PETER: Say more.

LUKE: If they softened your failure, they might think the restoration was earned by a better man than you were.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And then a worse man might believe there was no shore for him.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: No breakfast.

PETER: No bread.

LUKE: No work.

PETER: Only the courtyard, forever.

LUKE: But the courtyard was not forever.

PETER: It felt forever while I stood there.

LUKE: The shore came.

PETER: The shore came.

LUKE: Did you ever thank the woman who recognized you?

PETER: I never found her.

LUKE: Did you wish to?

PETER: For years I imagined what I would say.

LUKE: What would you say now?

PETER: I would not ask her to forgive me. I did not wrong her.

LUKE: Then what?

PETER: I would tell her she had seen truly. I was one of His.

LUKE: You would give her the answer you could not give that night.

PETER: Not to mend the night. It cannot be mended by returning in thought.

LUKE: Why, then?

PETER: Because it is true.

LUKE: You were one of His.

PETER: I am one of His.

LUKE: Even then?

PETER: Especially then.

LUKE: Explain.

PETER: I thought denying Him would make it untrue. I believed my faithlessness could undo His choosing. But there in the denial—there in the very act of fleeing Him—I was still the man He had warned, still the man He looked upon, still the man He named for Galilee.

LUKE: “And Peter.”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did you belong because your love held?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Because His did?

PETER: Do not make a phrase of it.

LUKE: I am not.

PETER: Then yes.

[long pause]

LUKE: You told me not to call you Peter at the beginning.

PETER: I did.

LUKE: May I now?

PETER: Not yet.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: There is one more saying at the fire.

LUKE: About your death.

PETER: About following.

LUKE: John joins them.

PETER: As they were joined for me.

LUKE: Did the words frighten you?

PETER: Once, I had offered to die.

LUKE: Vehemently.

PETER: Loudly. Young men speak of death as if courage can choose its hour.

LUKE: But you were told another would carry you where you would not choose.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did it feel like punishment?

PETER: At first it sounded like the promise I had broken being returned to me.

LUKE: A second chance to die faithfully.

PETER: I thought so.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: Now I think the greater gift was all the days before it.

LUKE: The feeding.

PETER: The lambs do not eat once. Sheep do not cease wandering because the shepherd has repented. Love became morning work. Evening work. Work when I felt forgiven and work when I did not. Work when my hands were strong and work when another had to gird me.

LUKE: You were not restored only to one brave ending.

PETER: I was restored to a life I could not boast my way through.

LUKE: Shall I read it?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: John set it down like this.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

[long silence]

LUKE: “Follow me.”

PETER: The first mercy and the last.

LUKE: You had already followed Him.

PETER: I had followed my idea of myself.

LUKE: And after the fire?

PETER: I followed knowing what followed.

LUKE: Failure?

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: Fear?

PETER: Often.

LUKE: Christ?

PETER: Always ahead.

LUKE: Even when you could not see Him?

PETER: The sheep were before me.

LUKE: Was that enough?

PETER: It had to be.

LUKE: Simon—

PETER: Peter.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: When you remember the courtyard now, where does the memory end?

PETER: It used to end with the look.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: With bread in my hand.

LUKE: Not the questions?

PETER: After them.

LUKE: Not the command?

PETER: After it.

LUKE: Where?

PETER: Years later. A young man is sitting before me. He has failed at the thing he believed proved his devotion. He cannot lift his eyes. He is waiting for me to tell him he has become what he did.

LUKE: What do you do?

PETER: I make a fire if it is cold.

LUKE: A fire of coals?

PETER: If I can.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: I feed him.

LUKE: Before he explains?

PETER: Before.

LUKE: Then you ask whether he loves Christ.

PETER: When he is ready.

LUKE: And if he cannot answer?

PETER: I wait.

LUKE: How long?

PETER: I was waited for.

LUKE: And if he answers yes?

PETER: I find him someone hungry.

LUKE: You give him work instead of a sentence.

PETER: The sentence has already been given.

LUKE: Feed.

PETER: Follow.

LUKE: Is that how the wound closed?

PETER: It closed each time I stopped touching it to see whether it was closed.

LUKE: Do you still grieve?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Then it is not gone.

PETER: Grief is not the same as shame.

LUKE: What is the difference?

PETER: Shame says I must hide from Him. Grief says I wish I had remained.

LUKE: And mercy?

PETER: Mercy says He remained for me.

[long pause]

LUKE: I think I understand why there were three questions.

PETER: Tell me.

LUKE: Not to make you pay three times.

PETER: No.

LUKE: To come and find you in all three places.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And when He found you, He did not leave you there.

PETER: No.

LUKE: He gave your love somewhere to go.

PETER: Into the flock.

LUKE: Into years.

PETER: Into mornings I did not think I would live to see.

LUKE: Into the man sitting before you now.

PETER: Perhaps.

LUKE: Would you tell him anything else?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: What?

PETER: The wound in your mouth is not deeper than the word that calls you back.

LUKE: And if he says he has already failed three times?

PETER: Then I will tell him there is bread at the fire.

LUKE: If he says he is not finished repenting?

PETER: He may be right.

LUKE: Forgiven, but not finished.

PETER: None of us is finished.

LUKE: Does that trouble you?

PETER: It comforts me now.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because unfinished men can still be given sheep.

LUKE: And names.

PETER: And names.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Thank you.

PETER: Where will you go?

LUKE: To find Thomas.

PETER: Be patient with him.

LUKE: You think he will be difficult?

PETER: I think he will want to touch the truth before he trusts his life to it.

LUKE: Is that wrong?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What should I ask him?

PETER: Ask him what it felt like when the wound he demanded to see was offered to him.

LUKE: And if he will not speak?

PETER: Sit with him.

LUKE: How long?

PETER: Long enough for him to know you have not come to shame him.

LUKE: As John sat with you.

PETER: As Christ waited by the fire.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: Yes?

PETER: When you see Thomas—

LUKE: Yes?

PETER: Tell him Peter remembers.

LUKE: What do you remember?

PETER: That the Lord does not return merely to prove He lives.

LUKE: What does He return to do?

PETER: To find His own.
