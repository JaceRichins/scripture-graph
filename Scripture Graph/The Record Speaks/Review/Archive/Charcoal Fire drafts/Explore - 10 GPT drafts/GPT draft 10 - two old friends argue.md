PETER: Meals to prepare. Fearful men to steady. Proud men to answer. My own temper to repent of. Forgiven is not the same as finished, Luke.

LUKE: No.

PETER: I wanted forgiveness to be a door closing behind me. It was a road opening.

LUKE: With sheep upon it.

PETER: Always sheep.

LUKE: Do you ever wish He had given you something easier?

PETER: Sheep do not become easier because the shepherd complains.

LUKE: That is not an answer.

PETER: Yes. I have wished it.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: Then I remember the fire. He did not return me to usefulness because I was beyond failure. He gave me work that would keep teaching me how much I needed the mercy I was asked to show.

LUKE: So when you meet a man who has denied what he loves—

PETER: I do not tell him the denial was nothing.

LUKE: What do you tell him?

PETER: I ask whether he is hungry.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: It matters.

LUKE: And after he eats?

PETER: I help him tell the truth.

LUKE: Then?

PETER: We wait for the work mercy gives him.

LUKE: You make it sound patient.

PETER: I have not always been patient.

LUKE: But now?

PETER: Now I know a man can answer rightly and still need years to understand his answer.

LUKE: “Thou knowest that I love thee.”

PETER: Those were my words.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: He knew the love more truly than I did.

LUKE: Even in the courtyard?

PETER: That was hardest to believe.

LUKE: Do you believe it now?

PETER: I believe my fear was real. I believe my denial was mine. I believe the love was real too. Sin does not prove love never existed. It proves love in us is not yet whole.

LUKE: And repentance?

PETER: Returning to the One who already knows which part is broken.

LUKE: You returned by swimming.

PETER: That once.

LUKE: And afterward?

PETER: More slowly.

LUKE: Over years.

PETER: Over every morning He gave me.

LUKE: There is something I have not read.

PETER: You have brought a great many parchments for one fire.

LUKE: This is not from John.

PETER: Mark?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: You have already read his worst.

LUKE: Not all of what came before the shore.

PETER: What are you holding back?

LUKE: Something Mary Magdalene carried out of the garden on the morning the stone was found moved.

PETER: Mary.

LUKE: I have kept it until now because I thought it belonged after your account of the fire.

PETER: It came before.

LUKE: In time, yes.

PETER: Then why keep it?

LUKE: Because when I first heard it, I thought it meant only that you were being summoned. Now I think I hear more.

PETER: Luke, read it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[Long silence.]

PETER: And Peter.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Mary carried that.

LUKE: From the garden.

PETER: I heard it then. I have spoken it since. Yet—

LUKE: What?

PETER: Read only the beginning again.

LUKE: I should not divide the scripture carelessly.

PETER: Then say what Mark kept.

LUKE: He kept your name.

PETER: After the disciples.

LUKE: Not outside them.

PETER: No. But named.

LUKE: Why do you think?

PETER: For years I thought it was because I had fallen farthest.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: Perhaps because I would have heard “his disciples” and hidden outside the words.

LUKE: So your name was added to bring you in.

PETER: There are mercies meant for everyone that a wounded man cannot receive until he hears his own name.

LUKE: Mary knew to carry it.

PETER: She had seen the empty place. She knew absence could become a message.

LUKE: You did not yet know there would be a fire in Galilee.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Only that He had gone before you.

PETER: Before I began the journey, He had already chosen the meeting place.

LUKE: Before your net was empty.

PETER: Before the morning.

LUKE: Before you smelled the coals.

PETER: The fire may already have been burning.

LUKE: And Peter.

PETER: [voice unsteady] Yes.

LUKE: I am sorry I kept it so long.

PETER: No. I had not heard it this way.

LUKE: Nor had I.

PETER: Mary carried my name out of a garden, and He carried my shame to a shore.

LUKE: You said I was making the fire into a judgment.

PETER: You were.

LUKE: I think I understand now.

PETER: Tell me.

LUKE: The questions were not asked to discover whether you loved Him.

PETER: No.

LUKE: He knew.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: They let you stand where you had fallen and answer without boasting.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Each answer was followed by a future.

PETER: By someone to care for.

LUKE: So the third question was not a third accusation.

PETER: It still hurt.

LUKE: I know.

PETER: Do you?

LUKE: No. Not as you do.

PETER: It hurt because He would not leave the wound unnamed merely to spare me pain. But it was not cruelty. He opened room where each denial had been. Then He did not ask me to live in that room. He filled it with His sheep.

LUKE: That is not a rebuke.

PETER: Not merely.

LUKE: You were right.

PETER: Do not sound so disappointed.

LUKE: I am not.

PETER: A little.

LUKE: I came prepared to argue.

PETER: And I have deprived you of it?

LUKE: No. You argued very well.

PETER: Mark should leave that out.

LUKE: Mark is not here.

PETER: Then you leave it out.

LUKE: I do not think I will.

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: The years should be heard.

PETER: The years are mostly less noble than you imagine.

LUKE: Tell me one.

PETER: One what?

LUKE: One moment when the shore changed what you did.

PETER: There was a man who had fled when his household was threatened. Others wanted him kept at a distance. He had returned, but every gathering made him remember the door he had run through.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: I gave him bread.

LUKE: And after?

PETER: I gave him work beside a man who did not know his shame.

LUKE: Why someone who did not know?

PETER: Because sometimes a repentant man needs one pair of eyes that does not search his face for the old failure.

LUKE: Did he remain faithful?

PETER: That is not mine to measure.

LUKE: You trusted him.

PETER: I remembered being trusted.

LUKE: Another.

PETER: You asked for one.

LUKE: I changed my mind.

PETER: There was a woman whose anger had made her cruel. She confessed it plainly, then confessed it again each time anyone showed her kindness. She thought repeating the wrong was the only honest thing she could offer.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: Nothing at first.

LUKE: You?

PETER: I am capable of silence.

LUKE: I have heard reports to the contrary.

PETER: I sat beside her meal. When she began the confession again, I asked her to carry food to another table.

LUKE: Work.

PETER: A small one.

LUKE: Did it help?

PETER: She returned for another bowl.

LUKE: Another.

PETER: You understand there were years of this?

LUKE: That is why I ask.

PETER: A young man once told me he could never fail as I had.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: I wanted to seize him by the shoulders.

LUKE: But?

PETER: I asked him to stay awake with a frightened brother for one night.

LUKE: Did he?

PETER: Part of it.

LUKE: And when he slept?

PETER: We fed him in the morning.

LUKE: The fire again.

PETER: Always the fire.

LUKE: You have made a ministry of breakfast.

PETER: Hungry men hear condemnation in everything. Feed them first.

LUKE: Is that what He taught you there?

PETER: It is what He did.

LUKE: You are careful not to put words in His mouth.

PETER: His acts have borne the weight of many words I was tempted to add.

LUKE: John gives one more part.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: It does not sound gentle.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.  
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

LUKE: You are old now.

PETER: Older.

LUKE: Do those words frighten you?

PETER: Some mornings.

LUKE: Only some?

PETER: Some mornings I am too occupied to be afraid.

LUKE: And on the others?

PETER: I remember the young man with the sword. He thought faithfulness meant choosing a glorious death before fear could speak.

LUKE: The old man thinks differently?

PETER: The old man knows courage may be given only when the hour arrives. It cannot be stored in boasts.

LUKE: You do not promise yourself anymore.

PETER: I place the knowing elsewhere.

LUKE: As you did at the fire.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The final command returned you to the first one.

PETER: It returned me to Him.

LUKE: Not to confidence in yourself.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Is that why you can face what may come?

PETER: I do not face it all at once. Today I tend what has been placed before me.

LUKE: And tomorrow?

PETER: If morning is given, there will be someone hungry.

LUKE: You make holiness sound like carrying breakfast.

PETER: Sometimes it is.

LUKE: And death?

PETER: That belongs to its hour.

LUKE: You have not escaped fear.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then what has changed since the courtyard?

PETER: I no longer believe fear tells the whole truth about my love.

LUKE: Could you fail again?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You answer quickly.

PETER: I know myself better.

LUKE: And Him?

PETER: Enough to return.

LUKE: Is returning the whole of repentance?

PETER: No. A man returns, tells the truth, receives what he cannot earn, and tends what love puts into his hands. Then he returns again.

LUKE: A long repentance.

PETER: A life.

LUKE: You began tonight with the denial.

PETER: I wondered whether you would flinch.

LUKE: Did I?

PETER: A little.

LUKE: I am sorry.

PETER: Do not be. It is an ugly sentence.

LUKE: Is it still the sentence you use for yourself?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What replaced it?

PETER: Not a sentence.

LUKE: What, then?

PETER: A task.

LUKE: Feed them.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Because you were forgiven.

PETER: Because I am loved.

LUKE: You speak of it in the present.

PETER: How else could I keep working?

LUKE: And when the cock crows?

PETER: I hear it.

LUKE: Then?

PETER: Sometimes I weep.

LUKE: Still?

PETER: Tears do not always mean a wound is open. Sometimes they mean it has become tender instead of hard.

LUKE: And after?

PETER: I rise.

LUKE: You find the flock.

PETER: Or the flock finds me.

LUKE: You feed them.

PETER: As I can.

LUKE: Then you are finished?

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: I know. I wanted to hear you say it.

PETER: Forgiven is not finished.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Forgiven is how the unfinished man keeps following.

LUKE: You told me the work began at the fire. But the record carries me farther back—to the first shore, the nets, your brother beside you.

PETER: Andrew was listening before I knew there was anything to hear.

LUKE: I will need that account.

PETER: Go to the beginning, then. Do not make my years start with my failure.

LUKE: I will not.

PETER: Who are you going to find first?

LUKE: Thomas.

PETER: He was at the breakfast.

LUKE: I know.

PETER: Ask him what he saw.

LUKE: I intend to.

PETER: And Luke—

LUKE: Yes?

PETER: If he begins with his wound, let him finish.
