PETER: “More than these.”

LUKE: Your old comparison.

PETER: He touched the boast before He touched the denials.

LUKE: You no longer claimed a love greater than theirs.

PETER: How could I? I could only place what I knew within what He knew.

LUKE: “Thou knowest.”

PETER: I had spent the whole night proving that I did not know myself.

LUKE: But you trusted Him to know your love.

PETER: At last.

LUKE: Even after your conduct denied it.

PETER: That was the terror of the answer. I loved Him. I had failed Him. Both were true.

LUKE: Can love fail so completely and remain love?

PETER: Mine did.

LUKE: That troubles me too.

PETER: You want love to be proved by never failing.

LUKE: Should it not be?

PETER: His was.

LUKE: And ours?

PETER: Ours must be proved by returning to Him with no lie left.

LUKE: Is that enough?

PETER: Not if returning ends at sorrow.

LUKE: The work.

PETER: The lambs. The sheep. Faces, not penance.

LUKE: Explain.

PETER: I thought repentance meant I would spend my life staring faithfully at the worst thing I had done. He turned my face toward those He loved.

LUKE: Without dismissing what you had done.

PETER: Without dismissing any part of it.

LUKE: Then forgiveness was not forgetting.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Not pretending the denials had not happened.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Not deciding they did not matter.

PETER: They mattered enough for Him to answer each one.

LUKE: With a question.

PETER: And a trust.

LUKE: Feed them.

PETER: Every time.

LUKE: He placed the vulnerable into the hands of the man who had failed Him.

PETER: Do not make that sound safe.

LUKE: Was it not?

PETER: Not because I became incapable of harm. It was safe because they remained His.

LUKE: You could forget that.

PETER: I did, at times. A shepherd can begin to love being followed. He can mistake another’s hunger for admiration. He can guard his place more carefully than the flock.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: The charcoal fire.

LUKE: You returned to it again.

PETER: Not as punishment. As truth. I had no right to possess what He asked me to feed.

LUKE: The memory humbled you.

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: What did it do at other times?

PETER: Made me gentle.

LUKE: Those are not the same?

PETER: A humbled man may still be occupied with himself. A gentle man has room for another’s weakness.

LUKE: You became patient with those who failed.

PETER: I knew how a promise could be sincere and still break before morning.

LUKE: Did you trust their promises?

PETER: Less.

LUKE: Did you trust them?

PETER: More carefully.

LUKE: That sounds colder.

PETER: It was warmer. I stopped demanding that frightened men become certain before they came near the fire.

LUKE: Because you had not been certain.

PETER: Because He had fed me there.

LUKE: You said the work did not repay the debt.

PETER: It could not.

LUKE: Yet each command follows your answer.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Love, then feed. Love, then feed.

PETER: Not grieve, then pay.

LUKE: Is that the difference?

PETER: One of them.

LUKE: What is another?

PETER: Payment ends when the account is balanced. Love does not finish.

LUKE: Forgiven is not finished.

PETER: No.

LUKE: That is difficult news.

PETER: It is tender news, if you are tired of measuring.

LUKE: And hard news, if you wish to rest.

PETER: Sheep wander while shepherds sleep.

LUKE: You did sleep once when He asked you to watch.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: Does every task return you to a failure?

PETER: For a long while. Then, slowly, some returned me to Him.

LUKE: What changed?

PETER: I stopped asking the memory to save me.

LUKE: You had made remorse a savior.

PETER: A small and hungry one. It demanded everything and forgave nothing.

LUKE: Why cling to it?

PETER: Because remorse kept the matter in my hands. If I could hurt enough, remember enough, work enough, then perhaps I could close the wound I had opened.

LUKE: And you could not.

PETER: No.

LUKE: He closed it.

PETER: Personally.

LUKE: At the same kind of fire.

PETER: Personally.

LUKE: With three questions.

PETER: He left no part of it to chance.

LUKE: And gave you work instead of a rebuke.

PETER: The work was the shape mercy took when it stood me back upon my feet.

LUKE: Did you feel forgiven when you left the shore?

PETER: I felt known. I felt grieved. I felt entrusted. Forgiveness was larger than any one feeling.

LUKE: But did you know?

PETER: Enough for the next distance.

LUKE: Again, one distance at a time.

PETER: The shore did not give me all my years at once. It gave me the next step.

LUKE: John kept what came after.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The cost of the work.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not. This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

LUKE: Follow me.

PETER: The first road and the last.

LUKE: You had once promised to die with Him.

PETER: Vehemently.

LUKE: Now He showed you that faithfulness would cost what you had offered too easily before.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Were you afraid?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Are you afraid now?

PETER: Some days.

LUKE: After all these years.

PETER: Especially after all these years. A young man thinks courage belongs to the moment. An old man knows the moment approaches through a thousand mornings.

LUKE: How do you bear that?

PETER: I do not bear tomorrow. I feed whom He places before me today.

LUKE: And when tomorrow becomes today?

PETER: I ask for bread.

LUKE: You make it sound simple.

PETER: Simple things can require the whole life.

LUKE: Did His words feel like judgment?

PETER: They felt like the return of a future.

LUKE: A future that ended in death.

PETER: Every man’s does.

LUKE: Not every man is told its shape.

PETER: No.

LUKE: You call that a future?

PETER: Before the shore, I thought my truest act was behind me: three denials, finished and unchangeable. Then He spoke of faithfulness that was still ahead.

LUKE: You had not disqualified yourself from courage.

PETER: Courage would no longer be mine to announce.

LUKE: It would be given when needed.

PETER: As the fish were given. As the bread was given. As the work was given.

LUKE: As forgiveness was given.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You could not earn any of it.

PETER: No.

LUKE: I still resist that.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: Part of me wants the fire on the shore to be hotter.

PETER: It was hot enough.

LUKE: I want something in you to burn.

PETER: Something did.

LUKE: Shame?

PETER: The lie that shame was the same as faithfulness.

LUKE: That is not what I meant.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: You are not offended?

PETER: You did not cross the sea to flatter me.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Then say the rest.

LUKE: I have sat with people who were wounded by men who later spoke beautifully of grace. The wounded carried consequences. The men who caused them carried testimonies.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: So when you tell me of bread and fish and work entrusted, I think of those still bleeding.

PETER: You should.

LUKE: Then what am I missing?

PETER: Mercy to the sinner is not permission to abandon the wounded.

LUKE: But if he is forgiven—

PETER: Forgiven is not finished.

LUKE: Again.

PETER: Until you hear it. I was given sheep, not a clean reputation. Lambs are easily injured. Feeding them required me to face what my fear could do to someone who depended upon my courage.

LUKE: The work carried consequence.

PETER: And gift.

LUKE: Both.

PETER: Always both.

LUKE: Did you make amends to Him?

PETER: How?

LUKE: That is the question.

PETER: I could not return the night to Him unbroken. I could not stand in the courtyard again and choose differently. I could not remove the sound of my denial from the air.

LUKE: Then the wound remained His to bear.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And yours?

PETER: Mine became a scar. His became salvation.

LUKE: [quietly] There is the part I cannot make sense of.

PETER: Nor I.

LUKE: Yet you trust it.

PETER: I trust Him.

LUKE: Is that different?

PETER: Entirely. If I had waited to understand mercy before receiving it, I would still be in the boat arguing with the shore.

LUKE: Instead you jumped.

PETER: I have always been better at jumping than understanding.

LUKE: There you are almost making light of it.

PETER: Almost.

LUKE: Why now?

PETER: Because joy returned too.

LUKE: When?

PETER: Not all at once. At first I could speak of the courtyard only with my eyes upon the ground. Then I could look at the one who needed the account. Much later I could smell coals and remember breakfast before accusation.

LUKE: Which fire do you smell now?

PETER: Both.

LUKE: Does one overcome the other?

PETER: No. The second holds the first.

LUKE: Say more.

PETER: The courtyard is inside the shore, but it is no longer the whole horizon. I can remember the hands that sought warmth among His enemies. I can also remember those hands taking bread from Him after He rose. Same hands.

LUKE: Not made innocent.

PETER: Made useful.

LUKE: You prefer useful to innocent?

PETER: Innocence was no longer mine to offer.

LUKE: Purity, then?

PETER: His.

LUKE: Courage?

PETER: His when I have it.

LUKE: Love?

PETER: He knew.

LUKE: You rest heavily on that.

PETER: “Thou knowest.”

LUKE: Even when you did not.

PETER: Especially then.

LUKE: Peter, I have kept you from something.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: I told myself I was waiting for the right place.

PETER: Were you?

LUKE: At first. Then I think I wanted to see whether you could convince me without it.

PETER: Have I?

LUKE: No.

PETER: Good.

LUKE: Why good?

PETER: Because I am not the message.

LUKE: Mary gave it to me.

PETER: Mary.

LUKE: From that morning. From the garden, when the stone had been moved and grief was changing shape faster than any of them could understand.

PETER: What did she ask you to carry?

LUKE: Not her words. The words she carried.

PETER: You have held them all this time?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Why?

LUKE: Because if I gave them to you at the beginning, you would have hidden inside them.

PETER: Perhaps.

LUKE: And I would have used them to solve you.

PETER: More likely.

LUKE: Mark kept the message this way.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[long pause] ...

PETER: And Peter.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Mary remembered that?

LUKE: She did.

PETER: After all these years.

LUKE: She wanted you to have it again.

PETER: [barely above a whisper] And Peter.

LUKE: You were named before the shore.

PETER: I was named before I knew what to do with my name.

LUKE: Why separate you from the disciples?

PETER: Because I had separated myself.

LUKE: Or because you were no longer one of them?

PETER: That is what I feared.

LUKE: What do you believe now?

PETER: The message did not say the door had closed.

LUKE: It called you toward Galilee.

PETER: Toward the fire.

LUKE: You thought you returned by your own stubbornness.

PETER: We tell ourselves that because sometimes we can see only our feet, not the voice ahead of them.

LUKE: “And Peter.”

PETER: Not because I was greater.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Not because I had failed less.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Because He knew which man would hear “his disciples” and believe it meant everyone but him.

LUKE: That was the beginning of forgiveness.

PETER: It was the road to the place where He would close the wound.

LUKE: Personally.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: I spent the journey here thinking I had brought you a kindness.

PETER: You did.

LUKE: But you already knew it.

PETER: Knowing once is not the same as never needing to hear again.

LUKE: Then Mary was right to send it.

PETER: Mary has always understood that a message can be true before the hearer is able to live inside it.

LUKE: You could not live inside it that morning.

PETER: I could run toward it.

LUKE: One distance at a time.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Tomb. Room. Boat. Shore.

PETER: Fire.

LUKE: Questions.

PETER: Work.

LUKE: Years.

PETER: Still work.

LUKE: Still forgiven?

PETER: Still following.

LUKE: That is not the same answer.

PETER: For me, it is the answer I can give without turning grace into something I possess.

LUKE: You fear possession.

PETER: I know what my hands do when they think something belongs to them.

LUKE: They grip.

PETER: They strike. They boast. They warm themselves wherever heat is offered.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: Now I open them as often as I remember.

LUKE: To receive?

PETER: To receive. To feed. Sometimes to be led where I would not choose.

LUKE: The same hands.

PETER: The same hands.

LUKE: Peter, when you see Him again—

PETER: I have thought of that.

LUKE: What will you say?

PETER: Must I say anything?

LUKE: You usually do.

PETER: [a small breath of laughter] Yes.

LUKE: So?

PETER: I spent years preparing speeches. Explanations. Gratitude. Confession made perfect by long rehearsal.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: I think I will know Him.

LUKE: As He knew you.

PETER: That would be enough.

LUKE: No defense?

PETER: None.

LUKE: No accounting of the work?

PETER: He knows.

LUKE: No final proof of love?

PETER: He knows.

LUKE: You trust that fully.

PETER: Some mornings.

LUKE: And on the others?

PETER: I remember breakfast.

LUKE: Bread and fish.

PETER: And a fire He was not ashamed to sit beside with me.

LUKE: [softly] I think I understand why you kept the worst of it.

PETER: Tell me.

LUKE: Not so men would remember how far you fell.

PETER: No?

LUKE: So the man who is still outside weeping will know that the story continues.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The tears are true, but they are not the end.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The empty tomb is true, but a man may still need his own name spoken.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The meal is given, and then the work.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Mercy does not return him to the moment before he failed.

PETER: No.

LUKE: It brings him through the failure with his hands open.

PETER: You are closer.

LUKE: Only closer?

PETER: One distance at a time, Luke.

LUKE: [after a pause] I am going to find Thomas next.
