DIRECTOR’S NOTE: I built this around the terrible intimacy of being known at one fire and restored at another—the smell returning before the memory does.
The hands keep mending a net throughout: Peter cannot undo the place where it tore, but he can learn to join what was broken and still put it to work.

LUKE: This is Luke. I have gone from place to place gathering the accounts of those who knew the Messiah—what they saw, what they remembered, and what they understood only afterward. These conversations are dramatized. The scripture is not. My hope is that through their witness, we may come closer to Him. This is The Record Speaks.

PETER: The third one came easiest.

LUKE: The third knot?

PETER: Denial.

LUKE: Oh.

PETER: Hand me that weight.

LUKE: This one?

PETER: The round one. Yes. Lay it there.

SCRIPTURE (Luke 22:61), read by Luke: And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.

[Silence.]

LUKE: You saw His face.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: What was in it?

PETER: Do not ask me to reduce His face to a word.

LUKE: I am sorry.

PETER: No. I know why you ask. I have asked myself.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Years.

LUKE: What answers did you give yourself?

PETER: Anger, at first.

LUKE: You thought He was angry.

PETER: I needed Him to be.

LUKE: Needed?

PETER: Anger would have made the world orderly again. I had failed. He condemned me. The account closed.

LUKE: But you did not see anger.

PETER: I could put it there if I remembered badly enough.

LUKE: What else?

PETER: Grief.

LUKE: His grief?

PETER: That was nearer. But even grief was something I could use against myself.

LUKE: How?

PETER: A man can make an idol of the pain he caused. He can bow before it every morning. He can offer it every good thing he might still do.

LUKE: You thought sorrow was repentance.

PETER: I thought if I suffered enough inside myself, I could prove that I understood the weight of what I had done.

LUKE: Did it work?

PETER: Suffering can prove many things. It cannot purchase yesterday.

LUKE: Then what was in His face?

PETER: He knew me.

LUKE: That is all?

PETER: Is it little?

LUKE: No.

PETER: He knew the promise at the table. He knew the sleep in the garden. He knew the first denial, the second, the words still wet on my mouth. He knew the man I had wanted to be and the man I was proving myself to be. Nothing in me was hidden from Him.

LUKE: And still He looked.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You wept.

PETER: I went out and broke open.

LUKE: Where did you go?

PETER: Away.

LUKE: Alone?

PETER: As alone as a man can be while another man’s face follows him.

LUKE: Did the others find you?

PETER: Eventually.

LUKE: What did you tell them?

PETER: Enough.

LUKE: Did they know?

PETER: John knew some. Others knew by degrees. Shame imagines it can preserve a little kingdom by deciding who may enter.

LUKE: And you let them enter?

PETER: Sometimes. Then I would rebuild the wall.

LUKE: What did you do through the rest of that night?

PETER: Remembered.

LUKE: The courtyard?

PETER: Everything before it. That is the cruelty of it. Failure does not remember only itself. It reaches backward and stains the days that were clean.

LUKE: What days?

PETER: The first shore. The nets. His hand when the water took me. Bread passed from hand to hand. Roads. Dust. Questions. Laughter from the others when I had spoken too quickly. The places where I had been sure I belonged near Him.

LUKE: And after the denial?

PETER: I no longer knew what belonged to me.

LUKE: You had not stopped loving Him.

PETER: No.

LUKE: That made it worse.

PETER: Much worse.

LUKE: Because if love remained—

PETER: Then I had betrayed what I loved.

LUKE: Did you believe He still loved you?

PETER: I knew the things He had done. I knew what His mercy had looked like when it crossed a room toward another man.

LUKE: But not toward you.

PETER: A man can believe mercy fills the whole earth and still find one place where he is certain it cannot enter.

LUKE: Himself.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Is that humility?

PETER: It wears humility’s clothes. Underneath, it is pride with ashes on its head.

LUKE: Pride?

PETER: I made my sin the one thing His mercy could not govern.

LUKE: Did you know that then?

PETER: No. Then I called it honesty.

LUKE: What would honesty have said?

PETER: That I had denied Him.

LUKE: You did.

PETER: And that I was not the judge of what He would do with me.

LUKE: You wanted to pronounce the sentence first.

PETER: Before He could.

LUKE: To control the ending.

PETER: Even in shame, Simon wished to lead.

LUKE: You speak of Simon as though he were still here.

PETER: He is holding this net.

LUKE: I thought Peter was.

PETER: That is mercy. Not that Simon vanished. That Christ put His work into Simon’s hands and called him by a name he could not yet carry.

LUKE: What happened when they killed Him?

PETER: The world became a room with no door.

LUKE: You had heard what He said would happen.

PETER: I had heard many things.

LUKE: But you did not understand.

PETER: Understanding is not a jar where words wait unchanged until you need them. Fear gets into it. Grief. The sight of blood. The sound of stone.

LUKE: You thought He was gone.

PETER: I thought the last thing between us would always be that look.

LUKE: You had no chance to repair it.

PETER: Repair.

LUKE: Is that the wrong word?

PETER: It was the word I used.

LUKE: What did you imagine doing?

PETER: Saying the right thing three times. Dying bravely. Going back to the courtyard and announcing myself to people who were no longer there. Men dream foolish labors when they cannot bear to receive a gift.

LUKE: You could not return to that night.

PETER: I returned every night.

LUKE: In memory.

PETER: Memory does not feel like memory when the coals are red again.

LUKE: The smell?

PETER: Charcoal has a clean, bitter smoke. It clings low. Wood smoke wanders. Charcoal waits.

LUKE: You remembered the smell.

PETER: Before I remembered the words.

LUKE: Every fire?

PETER: For a time. I would be speaking and lose the next thought. I would wake before dawn because a bird called from some wall. I would see a servant girl turn her head in the market and feel the whole courtyard rise around me.

LUKE: Did you avoid fires?

PETER: You cannot be a fisherman and fear every fire.

LUKE: So you endured them.

PETER: I sat farther away.

LUKE: Did anyone notice?

PETER: John notices what a man hopes he does not.

PETER: No.

PETER: At first.

PETER: That was kindness too.

LUKE: No.

LUKE: When I left her.

PETER: I returned to something my hands could answer.

PETER: To close the distance before courage changed its mind.

PETER: No. That is one mercy of cold water. It asks only for the next breath.

PETER: I smelled the fire.

LUKE: Charcoal.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Before you saw it?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: What happened in you?

PETER: The water was shallow enough to stand, but for a moment my feet could not find the earth.

LUKE: You were afraid.

PETER: My body remembered before my mind consented.

LUKE: Did you want to turn back?

PETER: The boat was behind me. He was before me.

LUKE: So you kept walking.

PETER: He had built the fire Himself—or it was there by His purpose. I do not know. I only know He was beside it, and there was bread, and fish laid upon the coals.

LUKE: Another charcoal fire.

PETER: There are only two in John’s account.

LUKE: The courtyard and the shore.

PETER: John did not choose that word carelessly.

PETER: No. I knew only that He had prepared breakfast.

LUKE: That seems almost too ordinary.

PETER: That is what undid me.

PETER: The tomb was terror and wonder. This was hunger. Wet clothes. Smoke in my eyes. His hands passing bread.

PETER: For men who had caught nothing.

LUKE: And for you.

PETER: And Peter.

PETER: Every one of them.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because we counted them.

LUKE: I know. Why did John keep it?

PETER: Ask John.

LUKE: I intend to.

PETER: He may give you a better answer than I can.

LUKE: What is yours?

PETER: Mercy does not always come as mist and mystery. Sometimes it tears your shoulders with the weight of a net. Sometimes it lies shining on the shore where everyone can count it.

LUKE: May I read?

PETER: Read until the meal is over.

LUKE: You remember their hands.

PETER: I remember everyone trying not to stare.

LUKE: At Him?

PETER: At Him. At one another. At me.

LUKE: Why at you?

PETER: They knew.

LUKE: About the denials.

PETER: Enough.

PETER: Shame had followed me into the water. It came out dripping.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did He serve you?

PETER: John kept it.

LUKE: Bread and fish.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: At the same kind of fire.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Before He questioned you.

PETER: Do not move past that.

LUKE: I will not.

PETER: Before He asked anything of me, He fed me.

LUKE: You did not have to answer correctly first.

PETER: I had no correct answer large enough.

PETER: I did not have that word. I only knew that I was sitting near Him and He had not sent me away.

PETER: Eventually.

PETER: It was the best thing I could not taste.

PETER: The silence was full.

PETER: Wonder. Uncertainty. Joy that had not yet learned how to stand in its new body.

PETER: We knew.

LUKE: But no one asked.

PETER: What could the question have added?

LUKE: Sometimes nothing.

PETER: You know this from medicine?

LUKE: From deathbeds. There are moments when a question is only a place to hide from knowing.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: When the meal ended, did He take you aside?

PETER: Near enough that His words were mine. Not so far that the others could pretend they had not heard.

LUKE: Did that trouble you?

PETER: I had denied Him before witnesses.

LUKE: You needed witnesses to the healing?

PETER: I did not know I needed them. He knew.

LUKE: John gives three questions.

PETER: Three.

LUKE: You knew after the first?

PETER: I knew what He asked.

LUKE: Not why?

PETER: I had many hopes for why.

LUKE: And fears?

PETER: More.

LUKE: He used your old name.

PETER: Simon, son of Jonas.

LUKE: How did it sound?

PETER: Like the courtyard and the first shore met in one place.

LUKE: Did you hear rebuke?

PETER: I heard truth.

LUKE: In your name?

PETER: In His knowing who answered.

LUKE: His first question included the others.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You had once set yourself above them.

PETER: Though all the others failed, I had promised I would not.

LUKE: But in your answer on the shore, you did not compare yourself.

PETER: What comparison survived that night?

LUKE: None?

PETER: John stayed nearer. Others fled sooner. One betrayed. One entered the tomb after I did. I could arrange us from morning until dark and never make one soul clean.

LUKE: So you answered only for your love.

PETER: I placed even that inside His knowledge.

LUKE: You did not say you were stronger now.

PETER: I was through telling Him what I would never do.

LUKE: You did not promise to prove yourself.

PETER: He had not asked for proof.

LUKE: He asked for love.

PETER: And I could not offer love as something He had not already seen fail.

LUKE: Yet you answered yes.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: How?

PETER: Because failure had not killed the love. It had killed my boasting.

LUKE: Then He gave you work.

PETER: After the first question.

LUKE: Immediately.

PETER: That frightened me more than the question.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: I expected a sentence about the past. He placed lives in my future.

LUKE: His lambs.

PETER: Not mine.

LUKE: What did you feel?

PETER: Unworthy.

LUKE: That word can mean many things.

PETER: I mean I knew the work was beyond the man receiving it.

LUKE: Did that make you refuse?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Why not?

PETER: Because the command did not rest on my worthiness. It rested on His choosing.

LUKE: Then the second question.

PETER: The fire snapped.

LUKE: You remember that?

PETER: I remember the smallest things around the largest ones. Grease on my fingers. A coal collapsing. Water running from the net into the sand.

LUKE: Did you understand by then?

PETER: I began to.

LUKE: Two denials. Two questions.

PETER: I knew a third was coming.

LUKE: Before He asked it?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And you could not stop it.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did you want Him to stop at two?

PETER: With everything in me.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because two could be mercy without memory.

LUKE: And three?

PETER: Three meant He had entered the courtyard with me.

LUKE: Not to leave you there.

PETER: I did not know that until He finished.

LUKE: Shall I read it?

PETER: Wait.

LUKE: All right.

PETER: People hear three questions and think they are the same.

LUKE: They are not?

PETER: I was not the same.

LUKE: What changed?

PETER: The first answer still had a little defense in it. Not in the words. Under them. I wanted Him to know I had always loved Him, as though love could stand apart from what I had done.

LUKE: And the second?

PETER: Less defense. More fear.

LUKE: Because the number was rising.

PETER: Because He was not allowing me to heal vaguely.

LUKE: Vaguely?

PETER: I wanted a mercy broad enough to shelter me without touching the exact wound. A general pardon. A sunrise over everyone. Something beautiful I could praise without opening my mouth at the place where I had poisoned it.

LUKE: But He asked you directly.

PETER: By name.

LUKE: Twice.

PETER: Then three times.

LUKE: John says you were grieved.

PETER: I was.

LUKE: Because He did not believe you?

PETER: That was my fear.

LUKE: Was it true?

PETER: He knew all things.

LUKE: Those were your words.

PETER: At last.

LUKE: At last?

PETER: I stopped explaining myself to the One who knew me.

LUKE: What remained?

PETER: Love. Grief. Need. All of it uncovered.

LUKE: And trust?

PETER: Enough to answer again.

LUKE: Read it?

PETER: Yes.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.
He saith to him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my sheep.
He saith unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

[Long silence.]

PETER: The third answer came hardest.

LUKE: Unlike the denials.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The third denial came easiest.

PETER: And the third answer stripped me bare.

LUKE: “Thou knowest all things.”

PETER: The thing I had feared in the courtyard became my only hope.

LUKE: To be fully known.

PETER: To be fully known and still entrusted.

LUKE: Is that when you knew you were forgiven?

PETER: I knew He had not come to humiliate me.

LUKE: He returned you to the wound.

PETER: He returned the wound to me without the lie that it was still in command.

LUKE: Three questions for three denials.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did each answer undo one denial?

PETER: No.

LUKE: No?

PETER: I could not undo them. Do not make repentance a rope trick, Luke. I did not pull three clean words through my mouth and make the ugly words vanish.

LUKE: Then what happened?

PETER: He met me three times where I had fled three times.

LUKE: What is the difference?

PETER: Everything. If my answers undid the denials, then I saved myself by speaking better. But He prepared the place. He asked. He received. He commanded. The mercy was His before the answer was mine.

LUKE: Yet you had to answer.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Three times.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: It hurt.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Could He have spared you the third?

PETER: He spared me from living forever with only two.

LUKE: [softly] I see.

PETER: Do you?

LUKE: I think so. If He had stopped before the place of your deepest shame, you might have believed that place remained untouched.

PETER: I would have built a house around it.

LUKE: And called the locked room humility.

PETER: You learn quickly.

LUKE: I have a good witness.

PETER: You have a difficult one.

LUKE: Both may be true.

PETER: This knot is wrong.

LUKE: The one I tied?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Can it be saved?

PETER: Most knots can, if they have not drawn too tight.

LUKE: And if they have?

PETER: Cut the cord. Join it again.

LUKE: There will be a scar in the net.

PETER: A joining.

LUKE: You prefer that word.

PETER: A scar is what flesh calls it. A net has no shame about where it was mended.

LUKE: You have spent years thinking about this.

PETER: I have spent years obeying it.

LUKE: Feeding His sheep.

PETER: Learning what He placed in those words.

LUKE: What did you think at first?

PETER: That I must become impossible to accuse.

LUKE: Perfect?

PETER: Beyond reproach, beyond error, beyond weakness. I thought He had given me one final chance to become the man I had promised to be.

LUKE: Was that the work?

PETER: It was another form of hiding.

LUKE: From what?

PETER: Need. Dependence. The humiliating daily mercy of being carried by the grace you preach.

LUKE: What changed?

PETER: Sheep.

LUKE: Sheep?

PETER: They wander. They bite. They fear the shepherd and the wolf in the same hour. They press near when they are hungry and scatter when the path narrows. You cannot love them long and preserve your theories about deserving.

LUKE: You saw yourself in them.

PETER: Constantly.

LUKE: Did that make you gentler?

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: Only sometimes?

PETER: Do you want a witness or a statue?

LUKE: A witness.

PETER: Then sometimes. Mercy had to reach me more than once.

LUKE: But the shore was complete.

PETER: His forgiveness was complete. My learning to live forgiven was not.

LUKE: That took years.

PETER: It is taking this afternoon.

LUKE: What could you not put down?

PETER: The wish to pay Him back.

LUKE: Through service?

PETER: Through pain, through labor, through danger—anything that would let me place my suffering beside His gift and pretend the scales had moved.

LUKE: Did they?

PETER: The cross has no scales.

LUKE: What did you tell yourself when you failed afterward?

PETER: That the shore had been for a better future version of me.

LUKE: Not for the man who sat there?

PETER: Shame is patient. If it cannot deny mercy, it postpones it.

LUKE: So you returned in memory.

PETER: Again and again.

LUKE: To the questions?

PETER: To the meal before them.

LUKE: Why the meal?

PETER: Because work can become payment in a frightened man’s hands. The bread would not allow that.

LUKE: You were fed before you were sent.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Loved before you proved your love.

PETER: He knew it before I spoke.

LUKE: Then why ask?

PETER: I have wondered.

LUKE: What do you believe now?

PETER: That He was not gathering information.

LUKE: No.

PETER: He was giving my love a road out of shame.

LUKE: Toward His sheep.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Your repentance became care for others.

PETER: Or it became another performance. I have known both.

LUKE: How can you tell the difference?

PETER: Performance keeps looking back to see who noticed. Love watches the sheep.

LUKE: You said sincerity failed you once.

PETER: It did.

LUKE: Then what do you trust now?

PETER: Not the heat of one promise. The next act of obedience. Then the next. When I fail, the next return.

LUKE: A long repentance.

PETER: All repentance is longer than the tears.

LUKE: Longer than the courtyard.

PETER: Longer than the shore.

LUKE: But it begins somewhere.

PETER: Where truth and mercy meet without turning their faces away.

LUKE: At the fire.

PETER: At His fire.

LUKE: Not yours?

PETER: I had stood at another man’s coals and said I did not know Him. On the shore, He was the host. The fire belonged to Him. The bread belonged to Him. The question belonged to Him. Even the fish we brought had come into a net He filled.

LUKE: There was nothing you could claim.

PETER: Only that I loved Him.

LUKE: And even that, you placed within His knowledge.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: “Thou knowest all things.”

PETER: Do you know what freedom there is in that, Luke?

LUKE: Tell me.

PETER: I no longer have to make the worst thing smaller so He will keep me. I no longer have to make my love larger so He will believe me.

LUKE: Both are known.

PETER: Completely.

LUKE: And the work remains.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Forgiven, but not finished.

PETER: Forgiven is why the work can begin.

LUKE: John records more after the three questions.

PETER: He does.

LUKE: A hard saying.

PETER: A gift.

LUKE: You call it that?

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

LUKE: You had promised to die with Him.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Then failed before the threat reached you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And on the shore—

PETER: He placed even my death beyond my boasting.

LUKE: You would not choose its manner.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Or its hour.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did the saying frighten you?

PETER: It sobered me.

LUKE: That is not the same.

PETER: Fear asks whether I will be strong enough when the hour comes. Sobriety remembers the strength will not be mine.

LUKE: You believe you will be carried through it.

PETER: I have been carried through every true thing I ever did.

LUKE: Even when it felt like walking?

PETER: Especially then.

LUKE: And the last command?

PETER: The first command, given again.

LUKE: Follow.

PETER: Not return to the courtyard. Not repay the meal. Not become a man who never trembles.

LUKE: Follow.

PETER: One step behind Him. That is where Peter belongs.

LUKE: You sound certain.

PETER: Certainty is different now.

LUKE: How?

PETER: Once, certainty meant I knew what I would do. Now it means I know whom I follow.

LUKE: And if you stumble?

PETER: He already knows the whole man.

LUKE: Simon too.

PETER: Simon most of all.

LUKE: Does the charcoal still bring it back?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The courtyard?

PETER: First.

LUKE: First?

PETER: Memory wakes in the order it was wounded. Then I remember the shore.

LUKE: Does one erase the other?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Which fire is stronger?

PETER: The one He built.

LUKE: Even after all these years?

PETER: There are mornings I must choose which fire tells me who I am.

LUKE: How do you choose?

PETER: I cannot change where I stood. I can remember where He came to meet me.

LUKE: Is that what you give the sheep?

PETER: When I remember.

LUKE: And when you do not?

PETER: They remind me by needing mercy I do not feel able to give.

LUKE: Then what?

PETER: I smell smoke. I see bread. I hear my name.

LUKE: Simon.

PETER: Not as rejection.

LUKE: As recognition.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The man at the first fire believed recognition would destroy him.

PETER: The man at the second learned it was the beginning of being made whole.

LUKE: Hold this?

PETER: There.

LUKE: The cords do not meet.

PETER: Pull gently.

LUKE: I thought nets required strength.

PETER: Everything requires strength until strength becomes the problem.

LUKE: Like your promise.

PETER: Like my promise.

LUKE: There. Is that right?

PETER: Nearly. Pass the loose end underneath.

LUKE: This way?

PETER: Yes. Now draw it.

LUKE: It holds.

PETER: It may.

LUKE: That is not confidence.

PETER: It is a net. We will know when the weight comes.

LUKE: Is that how you think of faithfulness?

PETER: Less by what it says beside the table. More by what holds when something living pulls against it.

LUKE: But the net may tear again.

PETER: Then we mend it again.

LUKE: You make repentance sound ordinary.

PETER: Most of it is.

LUKE: Not the shore.

PETER: Even the shore had breakfast.

LUKE: Bread, fish, coals.

PETER: The holy does not fear ordinary things.

LUKE: Nor ordinary men?

PETER: He came back for one.

LUKE: “And Peter.”

PETER: [softly] And Peter.

LUKE: I understand now why Mary wanted you to hear it.

PETER: I am glad you waited.

LUKE: You were angry.

PETER: I was hungry.

LUKE: For the message?

PETER: For my name to be spoken from that morning again.

LUKE: Do you need me to read it once more?

PETER: No.

LUKE: You have it?

PETER: I have it.

LUKE: Good.

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: Yes?

PETER: When you write the courtyard, keep the cursing.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: Keep the oath. Keep the tears.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: But do not end there.

LUKE: I will not.

PETER: Men know how to end a story at the worst thing they have done.

LUKE: Where should I end it?

PETER: With work still in the hands.

LUKE: The sheep?

PETER: The net. The sheep. The next step. Whatever shows that mercy did not merely bring the man back into the room. It trusted him with something that could be loved.

LUKE: And if someone thinks that trust means the wound was small?

PETER: Then tell it again.

LUKE: From the promise?

PETER: From the third denial.

LUKE: The one that came easiest.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Through the look.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The opened tomb.

PETER: And Peter.

LUKE: The empty net.

PETER: The full one.

LUKE: The charcoal fire.

PETER: Both fires.

LUKE: Three questions.

PETER: Three answers.

LUKE: And then the work.

PETER: And then the work.

[The repaired net is lifted and settles with a low rustle.]

LUKE: Will it hold?

PETER: Put it in the water.

LUKE: That is your answer to everything.

PETER: Not everything.

LUKE: Most things.

PETER: Enough things.

LUKE: I should go before the light changes.

PETER: Where?

LUKE: I am going to find Thomas.

PETER: Then do not ask him only about doubt.

LUKE: What should I ask?

PETER: Ask him what it cost to want the wounds.

LUKE: Will he speak with me?

PETER: Stay when he tells you to leave.

LUKE: Is that what I did here?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What did I do here?

PETER: You held the line.

LUKE: And you mended it.

PETER: No, Luke.

LUKE: No?

PETER: I only learned where to place my hands.
