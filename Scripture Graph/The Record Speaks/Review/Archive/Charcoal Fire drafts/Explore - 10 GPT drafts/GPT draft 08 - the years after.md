DIRECTOR’S NOTE: I built the conversation around the thing Peter could never take back: his own voice saying he did not know the man he loved.
The charcoal fire becomes both wound and mercy—the place where Christ gives him a future without taking away the truth of his past.

LUKE: This is Luke. I have set myself to gather the accounts of those who knew the Messiah—what they saw, what they remembered, and what they still carry. These conversations are dramatized. The scripture is not. My hope is that, through their witness, we may come closer to Him. This is The Record Speaks.

PETER: I said I did not know him.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: That is where you should begin.

LUKE: I had thought we might begin at the lake.

PETER: Everyone begins at the lake. The nets. The boats. Fishermen leaving one life for another. It makes a clean beginning.

LUKE: And this does not.

PETER: No.

LUKE: You have had years to decide how to tell it.

PETER: Years do not make it cleaner. They only show you which parts will not wash away.

LUKE: Then tell it as you remember it.

PETER: I remember my own mouth.

LUKE: More than the courtyard?

PETER: More than the faces. More than the cold. More than the servants passing in and out. I remember the feeling of the words forming before I had agreed to speak them. As though fear had put its hand inside me and found my tongue.

LUKE: You knew what you were saying.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You could have stopped after the first denial.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Or the second.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: I am sorry.

PETER: Do not be sorry for asking. I have hidden behind gentleness before. A man can use the kindness of his friends to keep from telling the whole truth.

LUKE: And the whole truth is?

PETER: I wanted to live.

LUKE: That is human.

PETER: It was also betrayal.

LUKE: Both can be true.

PETER: They were both true in me.

LUKE: You had been willing to fight in the garden.

PETER: That was different.

LUKE: How?

PETER: There was something for my hands to do.

LUKE: And in the courtyard?

PETER: Nothing. Wait. Watch. Be known. I have always been better when there is a rope to pull, a net to mend, a blade to reach for. Give me weight and I will lean against it. Give me silence and I begin to hear what is weak in me.

LUKE: You had promised otherwise.

PETER: I had promised everything.

LUKE: Mark kept the promise.

PETER: Mark kept all of it.

LUKE: Even the way you insisted.

PETER: Especially that.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because I did.

LUKE: You could have told him to leave it out.

PETER: Then every time I spoke of mercy, someone might think I had earned the right to speak of it.

LUKE: Did Mark ask you whether you were certain?

PETER: More than once.

LUKE: And you were?

PETER: I was not certain of many things about that night. Men ran. Torches moved. Everyone shouted at once. Memory tears at the edges when terror enters it. But I knew what I had promised, and I knew what I had done.

LUKE: Let me read the promise first.

PETER: Read it. Let the boast stand beside the failure.

PETER: I believed myself.

LUKE: That may be the saddest part.

PETER: I thought love had made me incapable of cowardice.

LUKE: Had you never been afraid before?

PETER: Many times. Storms. Deep water. Empty nets when people needed food. But fear had always announced itself. That night it dressed itself as loyalty.

LUKE: What do you mean?

PETER: I spoke as though I were defending him. Listen to me in Mark’s account. All shall fail—yet not I. Others may be weak—yet not I. I made faithfulness into a contest, and I placed myself above my brothers.

LUKE: They made the same promise.

PETER: After I made it easy.

LUKE: So you blame yourself for their words too?

PETER: For years I blamed myself for anything that would let me avoid the one thing that was mine.

PETER: I denied him.

PETER: They return to me.

LUKE: Even now?

PETER: Not as they once did.

PETER: Like an accusation that knew my name. I would be speaking before people, telling them what I had seen, and suddenly I would hear that other voice underneath my own. Not another man’s voice. Mine. I would say his name with love, and remember saying I did not know him.

LUKE: Did that make you stop speaking?

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: But not for long.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Why not?

PETER: Because silence can also be a denial.

LUKE: That sounds like something learned slowly.

PETER: Almost everything worth learning is.

LUKE: You said silence exposed what was weak in you. In the garden, before the courtyard, you were asked to watch.

PETER: And I slept.

LUKE: Mark kept that too.

PETER: He had much to keep.

LUKE: Shall I?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Ready in spirit.

PETER: Weak in flesh.

PETER: It is part of it. But I learned to be careful with that saying. A man can use weakness as a soft name for choices he does not wish to confess.

PETER: I tried every escape.

PETER: I told myself I had remained closer than the others.

LUKE: Those things were true.

PETER: True things make excellent hiding places.

LUKE: And another?

PETER: I told myself I had lied so that I could remain nearby. That I had denied him in order to serve him better later.

PETER: For the length of one breath. Perhaps two.

LUKE: And beneath it?

PETER: I was afraid they would take me.

PETER: I blamed the darkness. The confusion. The arrest. I blamed the men with weapons. I blamed the servant girl for looking too closely. I blamed my own Galilean tongue for giving me away.

LUKE: But the years stripped those excuses from you.

LUKE: Mercy?

PETER: Then you have not hated yourself with enough devotion.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: I do not recommend it.

PETER: Do you?

LUKE: In my own way.

PETER: Forgive me.

LUKE: There is nothing to forgive.

PETER: There is almost always something.

LUKE: Then I forgive you.

PETER: Thank you.

PETER: It is accurate.

LUKE: He says you cursed and swore.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Some might have softened it.

PETER: I had softened it in my mind enough.

LUKE: How?

PETER: For a time I remembered the last denial only as louder than the others. It was more than loud. I wanted them to believe me. That is what I could scarcely bear.

LUKE: Not merely that you denied him.

PETER: I labored to make the denial convincing.

LUKE: You separated yourself from him.

PETER: With all the force I had used to bind myself to him hours before.

PETER: I would like not to hear it.

PETER: No. Read it.

LUKE: You are certain?

PETER: If you leave it unread because I am here, then I am still trying to change the record by standing near it.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

PETER: He did not make me a man with no past. He made me a man whose past no longer owned him. Those are not the same.

LUKE: The memory stayed.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The smell of charcoal.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Your own voice.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Then how was the wound closed?

PETER: A closed wound can leave a scar.

LUKE: And the scar?

PETER: It tells the body where mercy entered.

LUKE: Did you ever wish the memory would disappear entirely?

PETER: Many times.

LUKE: Do you still?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because men entrusted to me came carrying their own courtyards.

LUKE: Their failures.

PETER: Their worst sentences. The things spoken once and heard for years. The faces they could not forget. The promises they had broken after believing themselves incapable of breaking them.

LUKE: And you knew how to meet them.

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: At a fire?

PETER: When I was wise.

LUKE: What does that mean?

PETER: A frightened man does not always need a hall of judgment. Sometimes he needs breakfast.

LUKE: Bread and fish.

PETER: Warmth. Time enough for truth to approach without being dragged.

LUKE: Did you tell them your story?

PETER: When it would feed them.

LUKE: Not every time.

PETER: Confession can become another way of keeping attention on oneself.

LUKE: How did you know the difference?

PETER: I did not always. That is part of the unfinished work.

LUKE: What else did the work require?

PETER: Learning names. Listening past the first answer. Staying when someone’s grief repeated itself. Refusing to mistake noise for strength. Returning to those I had injured. Receiving correction from men I might once have dismissed. Tending the weak without making them ashamed of weakness.

LUKE: You make it sound ordinary.

PETER: Feeding usually is.

LUKE: You had expected something grander?

PETER: I had expected courage to feel like standing alone before danger.

LUKE: And what did it feel like?

PETER: Often, like remaining with one difficult person after everyone else had grown tired.

LUKE: Were you good at it?

PETER: Ask the difficult people.

LUKE: I am asking one now.

PETER: Then no.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: Better than I had been. Not as good as they deserved.

LUKE: They were not given to you because you would never fail them.

PETER: No.

LUKE: They were given to you because—

PETER: Careful, physician.

LUKE: I am only offering a thought.

PETER: Offer it.

LUKE: Perhaps the wound made room in you.

PETER: For what?

LUKE: For people who could not survive being loved by a flawless man.

PETER: There are no flawless men.

LUKE: Precisely.

PETER: He was.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: And his love did not crush us.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Mine sometimes did. Or what I called love. I tried to pull men where I thought they should stand. I mistook urgency for faithfulness. I feared their weakness because it reminded me of my own.

LUKE: What changed?

PETER: I began to believe that his knowledge of a person was not the end of his love.

LUKE: As it had not been the end of his love for you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You speak of feeding as though it were how you answered the questions.

PETER: At first I did.

LUKE: At first?

PETER: I treated the work as repayment.

LUKE: You tried to earn the restoration.

PETER: Not in words. I knew better in words.

LUKE: But in your body?

PETER: I would work until exhaustion and call it faithfulness. I would accept every burden because refusing one felt like another denial. I would stand where I should have rested because I had once slept when I should have watched.

LUKE: That sounds like punishment.

PETER: It was punishment wearing the clothes of service.

LUKE: How did you recognize it?

PETER: The sheep became a measure of me.

LUKE: Instead of people to love.

PETER: Yes. If they flourished, perhaps I had repaid something. If they wandered, the courtyard opened beneath my feet again.

LUKE: What freed you?

PETER: The order of that morning.

LUKE: Tell me.

PETER: He fed me before he gave me anyone to feed.

LUKE: Bread first. Then work.

PETER: Grace first. Then work.

LUKE: So the work was not the price.

PETER: It was the gift.

LUKE: That took years to learn?

PETER: I am still learning it.

LUKE: Even now.

PETER: Especially now. An old man can turn his weariness into a claim upon God if he is not watchful.

LUKE: You do not think the years entitle you to rest?

PETER: They teach me I need it.

LUKE: That was not my question.

PETER: No. I do not think obedience places God in my debt.

LUKE: But you are tired.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Your hands ache.

PETER: They do.

LUKE: You still work.

PETER: While work is given.

LUKE: And when it is taken?

PETER: Then perhaps I will learn to receive again.

LUKE: Like breakfast on a shore.

PETER: Like breakfast on a shore.

LUKE: John did not end the conversation with the sheep.

PETER: No.

LUKE: The words that follow are harder.

PETER: They were mercy too.

LUKE: You can call them that?

PETER: He did not promise me ease. He gave me the truth, and then the same summons that had drawn me from the nets.

LUKE: Did you understand what the words meant?

PETER: Not fully. John makes their meaning plain in the record.

LUKE: Are you afraid of it?

PETER: Some mornings.

LUKE: And on others?

PETER: On others I remember that I once boasted I would die with him. I no longer boast.

LUKE: But you will follow.

PETER: By grace.

LUKE: Shall I read the end?

PETER: Yes.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

[Long silence.]

LUKE: When you were young, you walked where you wished.

PETER: Usually quickly.

LUKE: Now?

PETER: Now I am learning that following is not the same as choosing the road.

LUKE: Does the end trouble you?

PETER: The end is not here today.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Today there are nets to mend.

LUKE: Actual nets?

PETER: One should not make a figure of everything. Yes, actual nets.

LUKE: I had forgotten I was in a fisherman’s house.

PETER: The smell did not remind you?

LUKE: I was being courteous.

PETER: That is why you kept your cloak on.

LUKE: It is a good cloak.

PETER: It was.

LUKE: Was?

PETER: Fish is honest. It does not pretend not to cling.

LUKE: Like charcoal smoke.

PETER: Like charcoal smoke.

LUKE: Does the smell still take you back?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: To which fire?

PETER: Both.

LUKE: At once?

PETER: At first, the courtyard always came first. Now the shore often does.

LUKE: What changed the order?

PETER: Repetition.

LUKE: More charcoal fires?

PETER: More chances not to flee from them. More meals. More cold hands held over coals. More people telling the truth in the warmth. Memory learned another road.

LUKE: Did you choose charcoal deliberately?

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: To master the memory?

PETER: No. You cannot command a wound not to ache. But you can refuse to build your house around avoiding pain.

LUKE: So you sat near the fire.

PETER: I cooked fish.

LUKE: With your brother?

PETER: Often. Andrew never asked why I watched the coals.

LUKE: Did he know?

PETER: He knew me before I thought certainty was the same as faith.

LUKE: You owe him much.

PETER: I do.

LUKE: He was beside you before the first shore changed.

PETER: Yes. If you wish to understand where the work led me, remember where it began: two brothers, a net, and hands that did not yet know what they would be asked to carry.

LUKE: Would you tell him what you have told me?

PETER: Andrew has heard what I could say. He has also heard what I could not.

LUKE: How?

PETER: Brothers learn the shape of each other’s silence.

LUKE: Peter, when you hear your denial now, what do you feel?

PETER: Grief.

LUKE: Still?

PETER: Love does not require me to call betrayal harmless.

LUKE: Shame?

PETER: Sometimes it knocks.

LUKE: Do you let it in?

PETER: I ask whether it has brought bread.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: That was nearly wit. Forgive me.

LUKE: Nearly.

PETER: The truth is, I no longer mistake shame for repentance.

LUKE: What is the difference?

PETER: Shame turns my face toward myself and says I am the final truth about what I have done. Repentance turns me toward him.

LUKE: Even when looking toward him hurts.

PETER: Especially then.

LUKE: And after turning?

PETER: There is someone to feed.

LUKE: Always?

PETER: Usually.

LUKE: You said earlier that forgiven is not the same as finished.

PETER: I did not use those words.

LUKE: No. I am offering them.

PETER: They are true.

LUKE: Forgiven is not the same as finished.

PETER: No. Forgiveness gave me back the unfinished life I had tried to throw away.

LUKE: And the years since?

PETER: Not payment. Not proof that the courtyard did not happen. They are the life beyond it.

LUKE: A long repentance.

PETER: A long following.

LUKE: Is there a difference?

PETER: Not always.

LUKE: If you could stand beside the younger man warming his hands in that courtyard, what would you say to him?

PETER: Nothing.

LUKE: Nothing?

PETER: He had been told enough. He believed knowledge would save him from weakness.

LUKE: Then what would you do?

PETER: Stand near him.

LUKE: Would that change what happened?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then why?

PETER: Because after the third denial he believed he was alone.

LUKE: And he was not.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Even there?

PETER: Even there.

LUKE: You could not have known the shore was waiting.

PETER: No.

LUKE: The fire. The bread. The questions. The work.

PETER: No.

LUKE: But they were waiting.

PETER: Mercy is often preparing breakfast while we are still in the night.

LUKE: That is something I will remember.

PETER: Then remember it when your own night comes. Not as my wisdom. I had to be fed before I understood hunger.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: And give Mary my thanks.

LUKE: I will tell her you received what she carried.

PETER: Tell her my name reached me.

LUKE: I can tell her that.

PETER: Luke?

LUKE: Yes?

PETER: When you write this, do not make me fearless at the end.

LUKE: I would not.

PETER: Men like clean endings.

LUKE: Lives rarely provide them.

PETER: Say that I was loved. Say that I was given work. Say that when the old fear returned, I had somewhere to turn.

LUKE: To him.

PETER: To him.

LUKE: And when your hands could work?

PETER: Feed them.

LUKE: And when they could not?

PETER: Hold them open.

LUKE: To receive?

PETER: Or to be led where I would not choose.

LUKE: You are not finished.

PETER: No.

LUKE: But you are forgiven.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Do you know it?

PETER: Today I do.

LUKE: Today is enough.

PETER: It was enough on the shore.

LUKE: Thank you for letting me sit beside the fire.

PETER: Thank Mary for sending my name back to me.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: Where will you go now?

LUKE: I am going to find Thomas.
