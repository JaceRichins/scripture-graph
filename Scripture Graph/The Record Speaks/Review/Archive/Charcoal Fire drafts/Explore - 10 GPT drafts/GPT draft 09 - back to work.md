PETER: I stopped asking my own heart to be the judge.

LUKE: “Thou knowest that I love thee.”

PETER: He knew what I had done. He knew what I had wanted to do. He knew the difference between them without despising either the weakness or the love.

LUKE: You were grieved because the question came a third time.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: But did you need the third?

PETER: More than I wanted it.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: One question might have comforted me. Three restored the truth.

LUKE: In front of the others?

PETER: In front of men who had heard my boasts. Men who had suffered my absence. Men who would have to trust me after He was gone from our sight.

LUKE: Was that humiliating?

PETER: Completely.

LUKE: Was it kind?

PETER: Completely.

LUKE: Both.

PETER: Mercy is not always private.

LUKE: No.

PETER: I had denied Him before strangers. He did not make me confess love before strangers. He gave me my brothers.

LUKE: Witnesses.

PETER: And sheep.

LUKE: You keep returning to the work.

PETER: Because He did.

LUKE: Did you want Him to say that the denial no longer mattered?

PETER: At first.

LUKE: He did not.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did you want an explanation of why you had failed?

PETER: I had explanations enough.

LUKE: Did He offer one?

PETER: He offered no speech that let me stand outside myself and examine Simon as though he were another man.

LUKE: Instead?

PETER: A question only I could answer, and work I could not do alone.

LUKE: “Lovest thou me?”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: “Feed my sheep.”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did that close the wound?

PETER: It closed what I could not close.

LUKE: Immediately?

PETER: The forgiveness was His to give. Receiving it occupied more than a morning.

LUKE: What did receiving it require?

PETER: Rising when shame told me to disappear. Feeding another when I wanted to study my own hunger. Speaking of Him without making my failure the center of His mercy.

LUKE: Was that difficult?

PETER: It remains difficult.

LUKE: After all these years?

PETER: Years do not turn a man into someone who no longer needs grace. They teach him where he needs it before breakfast.

LUKE: Before breakfast.

PETER: That morning taught me the hour.

LUKE: What did you do with the old fire afterward?

PETER: Which part?

LUKE: The smell. The cockcrow. The memory of His look.

PETER: I stopped trying to extinguish it.

LUKE: You let it remain?

PETER: I let the second fire stand beside it.

LUKE: Not over it?

PETER: No. If you cover the first, men like me will think mercy is for a smaller sin than ours.

LUKE: And if you speak only of the first?

PETER: Men like me will think sin is the truest thing about us.

LUKE: So you keep both fires.

PETER: I keep the One who waited beside the second.

LUKE: Did you ever wish He had restored you another way?

PETER: Many times.

LUKE: What way would you have chosen?

PETER: Something unseen. A certainty in prayer. A quiet release from shame. No questions. No grief before the others.

LUKE: Why do you no longer wish that?

PETER: Because a feeling might have faded. I could have accused myself of inventing it. But there was bread in my mouth. Wet cloth on my back. Smoke in my eyes. John watching. Three questions. Three charges.

LUKE: A memory strong enough to answer the other memory.

PETER: A fire for a fire.

LUKE: Peter, there is something I have not yet given you.

PETER: Given me?

LUKE: I carried it from Mary.

PETER: Mary Magdalene?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: You have been here all this time.

LUKE: She asked that I not leave you without it.

PETER: What is it?

LUKE: Something from the morning at the tomb. Mark kept it. I have been waiting because I did not want to use it to spare you the courtyard.

PETER: You have not spared me.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Read it.

LUKE: It is brief.

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[long silence]

PETER: Again.

LUKE: The whole line?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: The part with my name.

LUKE: “And Peter.”

PETER: ...

LUKE: Mary wanted you to have it.

PETER: “His disciples and Peter.”

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I thought the words separated me from them.

LUKE: Perhaps they found you where you had separated yourself.

PETER: ...

LUKE: I do not know. It is only a thought.

PETER: Say it again.

LUKE: “And Peter.”

PETER: Before the shore.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Before the fish. Before the fire. Before the questions.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: He had not misplaced me.

LUKE: No.

PETER: I had spent years remembering the sound of the cock. Mary carried two words all this way.

LUKE: She did.

PETER: Why did she not—

LUKE: There was much confusion in those days. Many messages. Much fear. She wanted you to hear the words as they had been preserved, with nothing hurried around them.

PETER: “And Peter.”

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Do you know what it is to hear your name returned to you?

LUKE: No.

PETER: I pray you never need to.

LUKE: I think all of us do, in one way or another.

PETER: Perhaps.

LUKE: Did He call you Peter on the shore?

PETER: The record says Simon.

LUKE: Yet the message says Peter.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: What do you make of that?

PETER: Simon was the man He questioned. Peter was the man He had not abandoned. I will not divide them now.

LUKE: You once did?

PETER: I wanted Peter to be the brave one and Simon to carry the shame. That is another kind of lie.

LUKE: Who are you now?

PETER: Both names belong to the man He knew.

LUKE: And trusted.

PETER: And commanded.

LUKE: The questions were not the last thing said beside the sea.

PETER: No.

LUKE: John kept words about what lay ahead for you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Do you want them read?

PETER: I used to hear them as a sentence.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: As a road.

LUKE: Then let us hear them.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

LUKE: “Follow me.”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: After the denial.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: After you had failed to follow.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Not merely forgiven, then.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Called forward.

PETER: A forgiven man still has somewhere to go.

LUKE: Were you frightened by what He said about your death?

PETER: I am not eager for pain, if that is what you mean.

LUKE: It is partly what I mean.

PETER: When I was young, I imagined courage would feel like fire in the blood. A great certainty. No trembling.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: Now I think courage may be allowing another to gird you while your hands shake.

LUKE: You know such an hour is coming.

PETER: I know every morning carries me toward it.

LUKE: Does the courtyard return when you think of it?

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: Do you fear you will deny Him again?

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: After the promise on the shore?

PETER: The promise was His knowledge, not my invincibility.

LUKE: Then what steadies you?

PETER: He knew what I would do in the courtyard and still went before me into Galilee.

LUKE: “And Peter.”

PETER: “And Peter.”

LUKE: Does that make you certain of yourself?

PETER: No. It makes me certain where to look when certainty in myself fails.

LUKE: Toward Him.

PETER: Toward Him.

LUKE: What would you say to the man you were beside the first fire?

PETER: Nothing.

LUKE: Nothing?

PETER: He had heard enough from himself.

LUKE: Would you warn him?

PETER: He was warned.

LUKE: Would you tell him the shore was coming?

PETER: He would use it to make the denial smaller.

LUKE: Then what would you do?

PETER: Sit near him until the cock crowed.

LUKE: And afterward?

PETER: Walk with him while he wept.

LUKE: All the way to Galilee?

PETER: If he would let me.

LUKE: Would he?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then?

PETER: I would go before him and make a fire.

LUKE: ...

PETER: That was too much.

LUKE: No.

PETER: You are kind.

LUKE: I am listening.

PETER: There is a difference.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Do not let men hear this as though the tears were unnecessary.

LUKE: I will not.

PETER: The tears told the truth.

LUKE: But not all of it.

PETER: No. Sorrow can bring a man to the road. It cannot be the place he lives.

LUKE: What is?

PETER: The next hungry person.

LUKE: The next sheep.

PETER: The next net that needs mending. The brother who thinks his worst hour has removed him from every good thing. The woman who cannot lift her eyes. The proud man speaking vehemently because he has not yet learned his weakness.

LUKE: You know that man.

PETER: I know all of them.

LUKE: Because you were all of them?

PETER: Because I am near enough still to remember.

LUKE: Is that why He gave you sheep?

PETER: You said you would not supply me with facts I lived.

LUKE: That was a question.

PETER: Then I will answer carefully. Perhaps the shepherd who has needed to be carried does not easily despise the limping sheep.

LUKE: Perhaps.

PETER: Do not write “perhaps” as though I am uncertain of mercy.

LUKE: What are you uncertain of?

PETER: Myself, often. Him, no longer.

LUKE: When you smell charcoal now, which fire comes first?

PETER: The courtyard.

LUKE: Still?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: Bread.

LUKE: Always?

PETER: Not always at once.

LUKE: But it comes.

PETER: It comes.

LUKE: The first fire says—

PETER: Do not give it a voice.

LUKE: No. You are right.

PETER: The first fire has my voice. That is enough.

LUKE: And the second?

PETER: His words are preserved.

LUKE: “Lovest thou me?”

PETER: “Feed my sheep.”

LUKE: “Follow me.”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Which one stays with you?

PETER: All of them. Love that turns only inward becomes another mirror. He turned mine outward.

LUKE: Toward work.

PETER: Toward people.

LUKE: Forgiven, but not finished.

PETER: [softly] No. Thanks be to God, not finished.

LUKE: I should let you return to your work.

PETER: There are nets in the yard that will not repair themselves.

LUKE: May I come with you?

PETER: You have a physician’s hands.

LUKE: That is not an answer.

PETER: You will slow me down.

LUKE: That is an answer.

PETER: Come, then.

LUKE: Before I go, I should tell you where I am traveling next.

PETER: Who are you looking for?

LUKE: Thomas.

PETER: Thomas.

LUKE: Do you know where I might find him?

PETER: I know where he was last headed.

LUKE: Will he speak with me?

PETER: If he believes you have come for an easy answer, no.

LUKE: I have not.

PETER: Then tell him Peter sent you.

LUKE: Which Peter?

PETER: [after a pause] Simon, son of Jonas. He will know the man.
