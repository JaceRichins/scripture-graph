LUKE: One.

PETER: I was still there.

LUKE: Two.

PETER: Still there.

LUKE: Three.

PETER: And He had not turned away.

LUKE: Then He gave you work.

PETER: Each time.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: I have asked that for years.

LUKE: What answer have you reached?

PETER: He did not only restore what I had been. He called me beyond it.

LUKE: To feed His flock.

PETER: I had thought repentance meant proving I would never be weak again. He placed the weak in my care.

LUKE: Lambs and sheep.

PETER: Lives that could be frightened. Hungry. Wandering. Lives that would not be kept by a man pretending he had never wandered.

LUKE: You think your failure prepared you?

PETER: Sin is not a holy preparation.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Do not make the wound good.

LUKE: I will not.

PETER: The wound was evil. What He did with the wounded man was good.

LUKE: That is different.

PETER: Entirely.

LUKE: Could you have fed them without remembering?

PETER: I tried, at times.

LUKE: To forget?

PETER: To become the strong man again. To speak from certainty. To drive where I should have led. To measure another man’s love by whether his courage resembled mine.

LUKE: What stopped you?

PETER: Sometimes a rooster.

LUKE: After all those years?

PETER: Especially after years. A sound can keep a shepherd gentle.

LUKE: Does it always?

PETER: No. Memory alone can make a man hard. He may hate weakness in others because it resembles what he cannot forgive in himself.

LUKE: What makes the memory gentle?

PETER: The shore.

LUKE: Not the courtyard.

PETER: Both. I cannot separate them now.

LUKE: When you smell charcoal, which fire comes first?

PETER: It depends on the day.

LUKE: And on the difficult days?

PETER: The courtyard.

LUKE: What do you do?

PETER: I stay until the shore comes.

LUKE: How long does that take?

PETER: Sometimes a breath. Sometimes all night.

LUKE: Do you still hear the questions?

PETER: I hear my answers.

LUKE: Do you trust them?

PETER: I trust the one who received them.

LUKE: You said the work had your name on it. Did that settle everything?

PETER: Work does not end repentance.

LUKE: What does?

PETER: I do not know that it ends while a man still has breath enough to turn.

LUKE: Then this has been a long repentance.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: But you were forgiven there.

PETER: I was forgiven before I understood I had been sought.

LUKE: Before the shore?

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: Is it time?

PETER: Give me what Mary carried.

LUKE: She wanted you to hear the word as it was first kept.

PETER: I am ready.

LUKE: Mark wrote that the women were told this at the tomb.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[long silence]

PETER: And Peter.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I feared those words.

LUKE: When you first heard them?

PETER: I did not know why I had been named apart.

LUKE: What did you think?

PETER: His disciples—and the one who denied Him.

LUKE: Summoned for judgment?

PETER: Perhaps. Or named because I no longer belonged inside the first word.

LUKE: But you were called to Galilee with them.

PETER: I can see that now.

LUKE: Could you not see it then?

PETER: Mercy can terrify a guilty man. He searches it for the hidden blade.

LUKE: What changed?

PETER: The fire. The bread. The questions. The work.

LUKE: Then how do you hear it now?

PETER: His disciples—and Peter.

LUKE: Still separate?

PETER: Named.

LUKE: Personally.

PETER: The lie had been personal. The seeking was personal too.

LUKE: Mary wanted you to know she remembered that word.

PETER: She has carried much faithfully.

LUKE: Why did you ask me to wait?

PETER: Because that message did not become precious to me only because it came from an empty tomb. It became precious when I learned what awaited me in Galilee.

LUKE: Not a rebuke.

PETER: A meal.

LUKE: Not dismissal.

PETER: Work.

LUKE: Not the end of your calling.

PETER: A deeper beginning.

LUKE: Did you ever ask why He did not rebuke you directly?

PETER: The questions reached deeper than rebuke.

LUKE: How?

PETER: Rebuke might have allowed me to suffer, bow my head, and believe suffering had settled the account. He asked for love and then directed that love away from my shame.

LUKE: Toward the flock.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You were not permitted to remain beside the fire examining yourself forever.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Was that difficult?

PETER: It can feel righteous to keep accusing yourself after God has given you someone to serve.

LUKE: Righteous?

PETER: A man may call it humility. Sometimes it is only refusal.

LUKE: Refusal of forgiveness?

PETER: Refusal to be useful without first becoming innocent.

LUKE: But you could not become innocent.

PETER: No.

LUKE: You could become faithful.

PETER: By grace, yes.

LUKE: Is faithfulness the proof that you were forgiven?

PETER: No. Forgiveness was not waiting for my proof.

LUKE: Then what is it?

PETER: The place where gratitude goes when it rises from its knees.

LUKE: Into work.

PETER: Into people.

LUKE: The lambs.

PETER: The frightened one. The difficult one. The one who boasts because he is afraid. The one who disappears when seen. The one who believes his worst hour has spoken his final name.

LUKE: You recognize them.

PETER: I know their speech.

LUKE: Even if they are not Galilæan.

PETER: Especially when they try to sound like no place at all.

LUKE: What do you tell such a man?

PETER: I tell him the truth about Simon.

LUKE: All of it?

PETER: Enough that he cannot mistake me for the source of mercy.

LUKE: Is that why Mark’s account is so severe?

PETER: Severe?

LUKE: It does not hide your fear. It does not hide the cursing. It does not soften the last denial.

PETER: If a man hears only that I was trusted with sheep, he may think Christ chooses men who have never fled a courtyard.

LUKE: And if he hears the denial?

PETER: He may remain long enough to smell breakfast.

LUKE: That is why you keep telling it.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Does the telling cost you?

PETER: Less than hiding did.

LUKE: But it still costs.

PETER: Truth always asks the false self to die.

LUKE: You once promised death.

PETER: I did.

LUKE: John kept what was said after the questions.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

[long pause]

LUKE: What did you feel when you heard that?

PETER: The old Simon stirred.

LUKE: The one who had promised to die?

PETER: He wanted to seize the words and make the old promise new.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: Not in the same way.

LUKE: What stopped you?

PETER: The fire between us.

LUKE: You knew yourself now.

PETER: I knew enough not to answer the future with a boast.

LUKE: Were you afraid?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Are you afraid now?

PETER: Some mornings.

LUKE: And on those mornings?

PETER: I remember that the final word was not about dying.

LUKE: What was it about?

PETER: Following.

LUKE: Is there a difference?

PETER: Dying happens once. Following asks for the next step while the body still trembles.

LUKE: You have taken many steps since that shore.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Have they carried you where you expected?

PETER: Rarely.

LUKE: But you kept going.

PETER: Not without stumbling.

LUKE: You say that quickly.

PETER: Because you want a fine ending.

LUKE: I want the truth.

PETER: The truth is that forgiveness did not make me fearless. It made fear no longer the only voice I obeyed.

LUKE: Did it make you forget the courtyard?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did it remove the grief?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then what did forgiveness change?

PETER: The courtyard ceased to be the last place where He and I stood together.

LUKE: The shore came after.

PETER: The shore comes after.

LUKE: Still?

PETER: Still.

LUKE: Every time?

PETER: Every time I return to Him.

LUKE: At the same kind of fire.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why do you think He gave you work instead of allowing you to remain there?

PETER: Because forgiven is not the same as finished.

LUKE: No.

PETER: I once believed being finished would mean never hearing the rooster again. Now I think it means the rooster can call me toward the shore.

LUKE: Toward the flock.

PETER: Toward whatever love requires before the day is gone.

LUKE: And when you fail at that?

PETER: I repent.

LUKE: Again.

PETER: Again.

LUKE: Does that not weary you?

PETER: It wearies the man who still wants to save himself. It gives life to the man willing to be found.

LUKE: Simon.

PETER: Yes?

LUKE: When He called you by that name beside the coals, did it feel as though everything since the nets had been taken from you?

PETER: For one breath.

LUKE: And after?

PETER: It felt as though He had come for the man beneath every name.

LUKE: Not only Peter.

PETER: Simon too.

LUKE: The boaster.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The sleeper.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The one who denied Him.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The one who loved Him.

PETER: He knew.

LUKE: He gave that man the sheep.

PETER: He gave that man another morning.

[long silence]

LUKE: I have kept you a long while.

PETER: You came a long way.

LUKE: I am going to find Thomas next.

PETER: Then sit with him.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: Do not hurry to fill his silence.

LUKE: I will not.

PETER: Luke—

LUKE: Yes?

PETER: Tell him I remember the room.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: And if he believes he has arrived too late, stay until he knows he has been named.

LUKE: I will.
