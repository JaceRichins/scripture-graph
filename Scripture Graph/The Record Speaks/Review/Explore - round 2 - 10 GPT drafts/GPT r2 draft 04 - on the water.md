LUKE: This is Luke. I have gone from house to house, and from road to road, gathering the accounts of those who saw the Messiah and knew Him. What follows is spoken as a conversation, but the words preserved in scripture remain as they were given. My hope is that the witness brings me—and anyone who hears it—nearer to Him. This is The Record Speaks.

[Water against the hull. A rope runs through wet hands.]

PETER: Not there. Over the side.

LUKE: I thought you said to lay it in the bottom.

PETER: The other rope.

LUKE: There are too many ropes.

PETER: There are four.

LUKE: Four is too many when the boat will not stand still.

PETER: The boat is standing exactly where a boat stands.

LUKE: It is climbing the sea and falling off it.

PETER: We have hardly left the stones.

LUKE: How far must we go?

PETER: Far enough that you cannot step ashore whenever your belly remembers dry land.

LUKE: You knew I had never fished at night.

PETER: You said you wished to see where it happened.

LUKE: I meant the shore.

PETER: It began in the boat.

LUKE: John begins in the boat.

PETER: John was in it.

LUKE: I brought his account.

PETER: Then keep it dry.

LUKE: I have wrapped it twice.

PETER: Wrap it once more.

LUKE: You are enjoying this.

PETER: A little.

LUKE: Is there anything I can do that will help?

PETER: Sit by the mast. Look at the far hills, not the water beside you. If you must be sick, lean away from the net.

LUKE: That is a physician’s advice?

PETER: It is a fisherman’s plea.

[The weighted edge of a net drops into the water.]

LUKE: You still do this often?

PETER: Not often enough to be good at it and too often to admit I am not.

LUKE: Your hands disagree.

PETER: Hands remember work after the back begins to complain of it.

LUKE: John said you chose to come out that night.

PETER: I did.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: We needed to eat.

LUKE: Only that?

PETER: Is that a small thing to you?

LUKE: No.

PETER: Good. Pull when I pull.

LUKE: Now?

PETER: No. I will say.

LUKE: I have come with a question about another night.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: You have not heard it.

PETER: Men do not cross half the world to ask me about knots.

LUKE: I may begin badly.

PETER: Most things begin badly. Nets. Journeys. First sons.

LUKE: Were you afraid of Him when He found you again?

PETER: Pull.

[They haul. The net comes up nearly empty.]

LUKE: One fish.

PETER: That is yours.

LUKE: Why mine?

PETER: It looks unwell.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: I heard the question.

LUKE: You could say no.

PETER: It would not be true.

LUKE: Then you were afraid.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Of what He would do?

PETER: Of what He would see.

LUKE: He had already seen it.

PETER: I knew that here.

[Peter taps his temple.]

PETER: The rest of me was slower.

LUKE: I have written that He turned and looked upon you.

PETER: You have.

LUKE: I need to know what was in that look.

PETER: You are asking me to name His face while men were striking Him and dragging Him from one question to another.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I cannot.

LUKE: You remember it.

PETER: I remember where I stood. I remember the wall at my shoulder. The lime had broken away in one place, and I put my thumb into the hollow. I remember a man coughing into his sleeve. I remember grease running from a lamp.

LUKE: And His face?

PETER: I remember that He found mine.

LUKE: Was He angry?

PETER: Luke, if I could answer that with one word, you would not need the journey.

LUKE: Mark kept what happened before you reached the courtyard. May I read it?

PETER: You carried it through spray and nearly fed it to your sick fish. Read it.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:29-31), read by Luke: But Peter said unto him, Although all shall be offended, yet will not I.
And Jesus saith unto him, Verily I say unto thee, That this day, even in this night, before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice.
But he spake the more vehemently, If I should die with thee, I will not deny thee in any wise. Likewise also said they all.

[Only the water for a time.]

LUKE: You told Mark to keep “vehemently.”

PETER: That is what I was.

LUKE: He might have written only that you answered.

PETER: I did not answer. I pushed. He gave me the truth, and I pushed it back across the table.

LUKE: Because you loved Him.

PETER: I loved the sound of myself loving Him.

LUKE: That is a severe reading.

PETER: You did not hear me. The others had given their word, but mine had to be louder. I made death cheap because it had not entered the room yet.

LUKE: Did the others remember it as Mark wrote it?

PETER: They remembered. For a while we each remembered what the others had said more readily than what came from our own mouths.

LUKE: Did you ask them?

PETER: Years later. We were eating lentils. Andrew had burned the bottom of the pot and insisted that scraping it made the flavor better. I asked whether I had truly spoken over everyone.

LUKE: What did he say?

PETER: He gave me the spoon and told me to scrape.

LUKE: That sounds like Andrew.

PETER: He waited until I had nearly finished. Then he said yes.

LUKE: You can hear his answer now.

PETER: I can hear the spoon against the pot.

LUKE: Why preserve it? The boast, the sleeping, the courtyard—why hand strangers the ugliest account of you?

PETER: I was not handing it to strangers. I was telling men in a room what had happened.

LUKE: You knew Mark was setting it down.

PETER: Later, yes.

LUKE: And you never asked him to make it kinder?

PETER: Once I asked whether every word was needed.

LUKE: What did Mark do?

PETER: He looked at me.

LUKE: John notices everything, you say. Mark learned from him?

PETER: Mark had his own eyes. He left the words where they were.

LUKE: Were you displeased?

PETER: For two days.

LUKE: Only two?

PETER: On the third he brought figs. Good ones. It is difficult to keep a proper anger while eating another man’s figs.

LUKE: So you agreed.

PETER: I did not say so. I ate the last one and began the story again.

[Peter gathers the net into the boat.]

PETER: Keep that weight out from under your foot. If we catch anything, it will take your ankle with it.

LUKE: Is that how you speak when you are frightened?

PETER: I speak about what my hands can reach.

LUKE: Then tell me about the garden.

PETER: We were tired.

LUKE: That is where you begin?

PETER: It was true. We had eaten. We had walked. The night was close and the ground still held the day’s warmth. I told myself I would close my eyes until He moved farther among the trees.

LUKE: Mark kept that too.

PETER: Read it.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:37-38), read by Luke: And he cometh, and findeth them sleeping, and saith unto Peter, Simon, sleepest thou? couldest not thou watch one hour?
Watch ye and pray, lest ye enter into temptation. The spirit truly is ready, but the flesh is weak.

PETER: There. That was the night, before the swords. My mouth had offered Him my life. He asked for an hour, and I slept.

LUKE: You woke when the men came.

PETER: I woke full of strength. That is the foolish part.

LUKE: You thought action would repair the sleep.

PETER: I did not think. I had steel in my hand and men before me. Thinking had very little to do with it.

LUKE: Then you followed.

PETER: At a distance.

LUKE: John was with you?

PETER: Near enough that I knew I was not alone. Far enough that I could pretend I was.

LUKE: You entered the courtyard.

PETER: Beneath it, where the servants waited. People came and went. No one knew what would happen next, so they talked too loudly. A door opened above us now and then. Every head turned.

LUKE: And the fire?

PETER: Low. More red than flame. They had raked the coals together and laid fresh pieces across them. My hands were cold from the garden. I held them out.

LUKE: You could have stayed in the dark.

PETER: I wanted the heat.

LUKE: Though it let them see you.

PETER: I did many things that night which do not fit together.

LUKE: The maid recognized you.

PETER: She studied me first. I saw her do it. I could have gone then.

LUKE: Why did you stay?

PETER: If I ran before anyone spoke, every eye would follow. That is what I told myself.

LUKE: And what was true?

PETER: I wanted to know what they were doing to Him.

LUKE: Both can be true.

PETER: They were both true. That did not help.

LUKE: May I read Mark’s account now?

PETER: All of it.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:66-72), read by Luke: And as Peter was beneath in the palace, there cometh one of the maids of the high priest:
And when she saw Peter warming himself, she looked upon him, and said, And thou also wast with Jesus of Nazareth.
But he denied, saying, I know not, neither understand I what thou sayest. And he went out into the porch; and the cock crew.
And a maid saw him again, and began to say to them that stood by, This is one of them.
And he denied it again. And a little after, they that stood by said again to Peter, Surely thou art one of them: for thou art a Galilæan, and thy speech agreeth thereto.
But he began to curse and to swear, saying, I know not this man of whom ye speak.
And the second time the cock crew. And Peter called to mind the word that Jesus said unto him, Before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice. And when he thought thereon, he wept.

[The sail shifts. Peter does not move.]

LUKE: Mark does not include the look.

PETER: No.

LUKE: I do.

PETER: Read your line.

LUKE: I kept it like this.

SCRIPTURE (Luke 22:61), read by Luke: And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.

PETER: You put the remembering after He looked.

LUKE: That is how it was given to me.

PETER: It is right.

LUKE: Then tell me what He saw.

PETER: A man beside a fire.

LUKE: More than that.

PETER: A man who had slept when he should have watched. A man who had cut when he should have waited. A man who had followed only far enough to keep sight of the danger. A man glad for another man’s coals.

LUKE: Did you think He despised you?

PETER: I did not wait to decide. My legs carried me out. I struck my shoulder on the doorway because I had turned my head back toward Him.

LUKE: Where did you go?

PETER: Through two streets. Then I missed a turning and came against a wall. I could hear people behind me, so I kept walking.

LUKE: Were they following?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Where was John?

PETER: With Him.

LUKE: Did you resent him for staying?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: That answer came quickly.

PETER: It has had years to travel.

LUKE: Did you see John afterward?

PETER: Near dawn. He had blood on the shoulder of his garment. It was not his. I asked him a question, but no sound came. My throat had closed.

LUKE: Did he know?

PETER: He took one look at me and knew enough.

LUKE: What did he do?

PETER: He sat down.

LUKE: Nothing else?

PETER: He moved so I could sit between him and the wall. People were passing. He kept them from brushing against me.

LUKE: Did you tell him then?

PETER: Not in words. I held up my hand.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Three fingers.

LUKE: And John?

PETER: He took my hand and closed it.

[Luke breathes through another rise of the boat.]

PETER: You are pale again.

LUKE: I am listening.

PETER: You are also losing your supper.

LUKE: There was very little supper.

PETER: Even so. Far side.

[Luke is sick over the rail. Peter waits.]

LUKE: I apologize.

PETER: To the fish?

LUKE: To you.

PETER: I have had worse things in this boat.

LUKE: John?

PETER: James.

LUKE: I would not have guessed.

PETER: Neither would James. He blamed the dates.

LUKE: Peter, when I wrote of that look, I believed I understood it.

PETER: What did you understand?

LUKE: That you were caught. Not by the maid or the men. By Him.

PETER: I was.

LUKE: I thought the pain came because His face stripped away your lie.

PETER: It did.

LUKE: You keep making room for another answer.

PETER: Because there is one.

LUKE: What?

PETER: I had cursed. I had made my mouth ugly so they would believe me. Then I saw Him, and He was still there.

LUKE: Under guard.

PETER: Still there.

LUKE: Looking at you.

PETER: At me. Not past me. Not toward John. Me.

LUKE: And you fled from that.

PETER: I could not stay beneath it.

LUKE: Beneath anger?

PETER: You want the one word again.

LUKE: I do.

PETER: You are a dangerous man with a reed in your hand.

LUKE: A reed can be cut away.

PETER: The mark remains.

LUKE: Then tell me what I have marked wrongly.

PETER: Not wrongly. Too soon.

LUKE: I do not understand.

PETER: Neither did I.

[Peter coils the rope twice, then begins again.]

LUKE: There is a man I have not put into my account.

PETER: A witness?

LUKE: No. A man from my own road.

PETER: What did he do?

LUKE: He traveled with us for five months. He knew where we slept and which houses received us. When men began searching, he gave them the name of one house.

PETER: Why?

LUKE: They had taken his brother. He thought one name would purchase his brother’s release.

PETER: Did it?

LUKE: No.

PETER: Who was in the house?

LUKE: A widow, her two daughters, and one of our companions. The daughters escaped over a wall. The widow could not climb. They broke her wrist pulling her through a window.

PETER: You set it?

LUKE: I did. She never held a cup properly again.

PETER: And the man?

LUKE: He came back in winter. He had walked without sandals for two days. His feet were split to the flesh.

PETER: You washed them.

LUKE: I am a physician.

PETER: That was not my question.

LUKE: Yes. I washed them.

PETER: And after?

LUKE: I dressed the wounds. I gave him food. When he asked to travel with us again, I refused.

PETER: Was he angry?

LUKE: No. That would have been easier. He thanked me for the bread.

PETER: What was his name?

LUKE: Simeon.

PETER: Where is he now?

LUKE: I do not know. He went east.

PETER: Did you ask the widow what she wanted?

LUKE: She would have taken him into her house.

PETER: Ah.

LUKE: She did not have to decide for the others he might endanger.

PETER: No.

LUKE: You think I was wrong.

PETER: I think you came into my boat with more than one question.

LUKE: I kept people safe.

PETER: Perhaps you did.

LUKE: You do not believe me.

PETER: I was not there.

LUKE: Then why say “perhaps”?

PETER: Because you still hear him thanking you for the bread.

LUKE: I hear the widow cry when I straightened her wrist.

PETER: I would hear that too.

LUKE: What would you have done?

PETER: At which moment?

LUKE: When he asked to come back.

PETER: I do not know.

LUKE: That is a poor answer.

PETER: It is the only clean one I have.

LUKE: Christ gave you charge of people.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You must have learned what to do with men who had failed them.

PETER: I learned names.

LUKE: Names?

PETER: And what they had done. And who would not sit beside them. And which door they stood outside after everyone else had gone in.

LUKE: Tell me one.

PETER: There was a young man called Joram. He had narrow shoulders and hands too large for him. He came to us first because his mother needed barley.

LUKE: Was she ill?

PETER: Her lungs had been bad since a winter fever. She could weave sitting down, but she could not carry wool from the market. Joram did that. He also threw dice behind the bathhouse.

LUKE: He gambled away the barley.

PETER: First his own money. Then hers. Then he began taking from the basket we kept for three widows.

LUKE: Did you catch him?

PETER: A child did. Little Tirzah followed him because he had promised her a honey cake. She saw him hide coins in his sandal.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: Joram denied it. Then he blamed the keeper of the basket. Then he struck the wall hard enough to split two knuckles. There was blood on the plaster. He wept over the blood, which seemed strange until I learned it was the first true thing he had seen all day.

LUKE: Did you send him away?

PETER: I took him fishing.

LUKE: As punishment?

PETER: Have you enjoyed it?

LUKE: I understand.

PETER: We went every morning for twelve days. He worked beside me. Half his share went into the widows’ basket. I gave the other half to his mother before he could lose it.

LUKE: Did the widows accept that?

PETER: One did. One refused to eat fish he had touched. The third, Anna, asked why a thief was earning a full share.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: I told her the catch did not know who pulled it up.

LUKE: That sounds nearly like a saying.

PETER: It was a large catch. She could see it.

LUKE: Did Joram stop stealing?

PETER: No.

LUKE: No?

PETER: On the eighth day he took my knife.

LUKE: After you brought him into your boat?

PETER: It was a good knife.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: I went to his house. He had hidden it in the stove ashes. His mother found it while making bread and held it out to me. Joram stood behind her.

LUKE: Did he confess?

PETER: He said he had meant to sharpen it.

LUKE: Did you believe him?

PETER: The blade still had fish scale under the handle.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: I told him to be at the beach before dawn.

LUKE: Again?

PETER: Again.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because the net was torn and I needed his hands.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: Because he had returned the knife.

LUKE: His mother returned it.

PETER: It came back from his house.

LUKE: That is generous.

PETER: It was irritating. He complained about the hour. He tied poor knots. On the tenth morning I made him cut every bad knot and tie it again.

LUKE: How long did this continue?

PETER: Seven months. Not every day. By the end he could mend faster than I could.

LUKE: Did people trust him?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: With rope. Not with money.

LUKE: Then what had changed?

PETER: One rain came hard while we were out. Anna—the widow who questioned his share—had jars of oil beneath a broken awning. Joram ran from the shore before we had tied the boat. He carried every jar inside.

LUKE: Did he know they were hers?

PETER: Yes. He had stolen two of them the year before. She knew it too.

LUKE: Did she thank him?

PETER: She shut the door.

LUKE: That must have hurt him.

PETER: He came back angry. He said he was finished helping those who would always call him thief.

LUKE: What did you tell him?

PETER: I told him the boat was drifting because he had left the stern line loose.

LUKE: Nothing about Anna?

PETER: Not then. We pulled the boat in. We covered the net. We stood in the rain until he stopped shouting.

LUKE: And later?

PETER: Three days later Anna came to the shore. She had my knife wrapped in cloth.

LUKE: Your knife?

PETER: Joram had taken it again.

LUKE: You left that out.

PETER: I had not reached it.

LUKE: What did Anna do?

PETER: She gave the knife to him, not me. She asked him to clean fish for her sister’s household.

LUKE: Did he go?

PETER: Every sixth day for two years. Anna counted the knives when he arrived and when he left. He knew she counted.

LUKE: Was that forgiveness?

PETER: It was Tuesday.

LUKE: You evade me.

PETER: No. You want to know the whole thing before the next dawn. Joram needed many Tuesdays.

[The net falls again.]

LUKE: Did he ever steal from you afterward?

PETER: Not from me.

LUKE: From someone else?

PETER: A belt from a fuller in Joppa. He walked back and returned it.

LUKE: On his own?

PETER: He came to my door first.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Walked with him as far as the fuller’s lane.

LUKE: Not to the door?

PETER: He asked me to wait at the corner.

LUKE: Did the fuller take the belt?

PETER: He took the belt. He also struck Joram in the mouth.

LUKE: And you permitted that?

PETER: I had not been invited to the door. When the fuller lifted his hand again, I became invited.

LUKE: What became of Joram?

PETER: He married Tirzah.

LUKE: The child who caught him?

PETER: She did not remain a child.

LUKE: Did she trust him with money?

PETER: Better than that. She knew when not to.

LUKE: Are they still together?

PETER: They have four children and one good knife between them. He sends me the dull ones.

LUKE: You did not declare him safe. You gave him work you could watch.

PETER: At first.

LUKE: Then you gave him room to walk to a door alone.

PETER: When he asked for it.

LUKE: Simeon asked me to travel with us.

PETER: Joram did not know the houses where men slept.

LUKE: So you might have refused him too.

PETER: I might have refused the road and found him another net.

LUKE: I did not look for one.

PETER: No.

LUKE: I gave him bread.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You make that sound small.

PETER: Bread is not small. It is only eaten quickly.

LUKE: There was no shore where I could keep him near and everyone else safe.

PETER: There is always work near someone.

LUKE: You cannot know that.

PETER: No. I cannot.

LUKE: That is where our argument ends?

PETER: Is it an argument?

LUKE: I believe I am losing it.

PETER: Good. Then we can fish.

[They haul. Three small fish strike the boards.]

PETER: There. You are improving.

LUKE: I did nothing.

PETER: You did not fall over. In this boat, that counts.

LUKE: Tell me another name.

PETER: You will not release me now.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Damaris. She sold dyed cloth near the eastern gate. Purple when she could afford the shell, blue when she could not.

LUKE: What had she done?

PETER: Her husband died owing silver to three men. She kept the loom, but they took the wool. She began putting stones in folded cloth to make the bundles weigh properly.

LUKE: That is theft.

PETER: It was clever theft. Buyers did not open the bundles until they reached home. She used smooth stones and wrapped them well.

LUKE: How was she discovered?

PETER: She sold cloth to a man whose donkey stumbled outside her stall. The bundle split. Six stones rolled down the street.

LUKE: Six?

PETER: I told you she was clever, not restrained.

LUKE: Why did she come to you?

PETER: She did not. I went to her after two women asked that she be put out from our table.

LUKE: Did she admit it?

PETER: She said every merchant cheats the scale.

LUKE: What did you answer?

PETER: I asked whether every merchant used six stones.

LUKE: Did she laugh?

PETER: No. I did.

LUKE: That was unkind.

PETER: It was. Her face closed. I had to return the next day.

LUKE: You apologized?

PETER: I brought the smallest stone.

LUKE: Why had you kept it?

PETER: It was round. I thought one of my sons might use it in a sling.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: I told her I had laughed because I had been pleased not to be the one caught that morning.

LUKE: Was that true?

PETER: By the next day it was.

LUKE: What did she say?

PETER: Nothing. She moved aside so I could enter.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: We laid every debt on the floor. Three tablets, two torn bits of leather, one promise remembered differently by both men. Damaris knew the numbers better than any of us.

LUKE: Could she repay them?

PETER: Not if they took the loom. So I asked the three men to leave it with her and take cloth each month.

LUKE: They agreed?

PETER: Two did. The third wanted the frame sold.

LUKE: What changed his mind?

PETER: His wife.

LUKE: What did she do?

PETER: She bought the first length of honest cloth.

LUKE: Was Damaris restored to the table?

PETER: One woman welcomed her. The other kept her cup on the far side.

LUKE: Did you correct the woman?

PETER: Once.

LUKE: Once?

PETER: Damaris asked me to stop.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: She said she wished to move the cup herself when the woman was ready.

LUKE: How long did that take?

PETER: Until the woman’s daughter married. Damaris dyed the wedding cloth. No stones.

LUKE: And afterward?

PETER: The cup stayed between them.

LUKE: What work did you give her?

PETER: The accounts for the bread basket.

LUKE: You entrusted a cheat with the accounts?

PETER: Not at once. First she counted loaves beside another woman. Then she weighed grain while two watched. Then one watched. After a year, she kept the tablet.

LUKE: Was no one else qualified?

PETER: Several were. None could find a false weight as quickly.

LUKE: That is almost funny.

PETER: It was very funny. Damaris laughed hardest.

LUKE: Did she ever cheat again?

PETER: Once she marked a poor woman’s debt paid when it was not.

LUKE: That was mercy.

PETER: It was not her grain.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Made her replace it.

LUKE: Did she?

PETER: She sold a blue shawl.

LUKE: And the poor woman?

PETER: Damaris told her what she had done. The woman brought back half a measure each market day until the grain was repaid.

LUKE: You did not let kindness excuse deceit.

PETER: The widows still needed bread.

LUKE: Did Damaris remain with you?

PETER: She buried me twice in her mind.

LUKE: What does that mean?

PETER: When word came that I had been taken, she assumed I would not return. Both times she gave away my cloak.

LUKE: The same cloak?

PETER: The second was better.

LUKE: Did you ask for it back?

PETER: The first time.

LUKE: Not the second?

PETER: The second had gone to a man with no arms.

LUKE: I see the difficulty.

PETER: Damaris did not.

LUKE: You trusted her enough to be inconvenienced by her.

PETER: That is one way of saying it.

LUKE: And she knew the people she had cheated would see her at the table.

PETER: Every week.

LUKE: Was that difficult for her?

PETER: At first she arrived after the prayer and left before the cups were gathered. Then one evening a child spilled oil over her garment. She had to stay while we washed it. After that she came on time.

LUKE: A child accomplished what teaching did not.

PETER: Children do that often.

[Wind presses the sail. Peter lowers it.]

LUKE: Is the wind failing?

PETER: Turning. We will work back toward shore.

LUKE: Dawn is not near.

PETER: It is nearer than when we left.

LUKE: You said names. Joram. Damaris. Who is the third?

PETER: Eli.

LUKE: What did Eli do?

PETER: He barred a door.

LUKE: Against whom?

PETER: His sister, Mara. Men were chasing her.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: She had carried food to a family they wanted driven out. Eli heard the shouting. He had his wife and two small boys inside. Mara reached his courtyard first and struck the door.

LUKE: He did not open it.

PETER: No.

LUKE: What happened to her?

PETER: They caught her in the lane. Broke two fingers. Cut her hair. Dragged her through mud. She lived.

LUKE: And Eli came to you.

PETER: Not for six years.

LUKE: Six?

PETER: He moved his family to the coast. Told them Mara had chosen another road and he could not find her.

LUKE: Did they believe him?

PETER: His wife did not. But she had heard the men outside too. She never asked in front of the boys.

LUKE: How did you meet him?

PETER: His eldest son came to hear us. A strong boy named Benjamin. He spoke of courage as if he owned a storehouse of it.

LUKE: Like a young man.

PETER: Like me.

LUKE: Did Eli tell you himself?

PETER: He waited until Benjamin had brought Mara to the gathering.

LUKE: The son found her?

PETER: He had been searching. She lived three days away, with a hand that would not close and hair gone gray at the temples. Benjamin led her into the room. Eli dropped a jar.

LUKE: What did Mara do?

PETER: Sat by the door.

LUKE: Did she accuse him?

PETER: She did not need to.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: I finished breaking the bread.

LUKE: How could you continue?

PETER: Thirty people had not eaten.

LUKE: And afterward?

PETER: Mara left. Eli followed her. Benjamin followed Eli. Then Eli’s younger son followed Benjamin because no one had told him to stay. Half the room went into the street.

LUKE: Did Mara speak to Eli?

PETER: She looked at him. He fell to his knees in the mud.

LUKE: What did he say?

PETER: His own words. They belong to her.

LUKE: Did she forgive him?

PETER: Not in the street.

LUKE: Did she ever?

PETER: She came to my lodging the next morning and asked me to tell Eli never to kneel before her again.

LUKE: Was that forgiveness?

PETER: It was a request about his knees.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: You keep asking me to decide what occurred in another heart.

LUKE: Then tell me what you saw.

PETER: I saw Mara refuse his house. I saw her accept bread from his wife. I saw Benjamin sit outside his father’s door for three nights.

LUKE: Why outside?

PETER: He was ashamed to enter and too angry to leave.

LUKE: Did you sit with him?

PETER: The second night.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: Nothing for a long while. Then I told him his father was not the deed at the door.

LUKE: That is dangerously close to embroidery.

PETER: Benjamin said the same thing with less courtesy.

LUKE: What happened on the third night?

PETER: Rain. Eli came out with two coverings. Benjamin took one. Neither went inside.

LUKE: And Mara?

PETER: She had a dye shed with a leaking roof. Eli repaired roofs.

LUKE: She let him work?

PETER: She left the broken tiles in a pile where he would see them.

LUKE: Did she ask him?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did he go?

PETER: Before sunrise. Benjamin went too. Mara watched from a neighbor’s window.

LUKE: How long did the roof take?

PETER: Four days, because Eli removed sound tiles and laid them again whenever he became afraid to finish.

LUKE: Did Mara speak to him then?

PETER: She brought water on the second day. Set it on the ground. On the fourth she held the ladder while he came down.

LUKE: Did he return to his family?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did Benjamin enter the house?

PETER: After the rain began.

LUKE: And Mara?

PETER: Not for another year.

LUKE: What brought her back?

PETER: Eli’s younger boy cut his leg on a broken pot. Mara knew how to sew a wound.

LUKE: She entered to heal the son of the brother who shut her out.

PETER: She complained that the lamp was poor, sent Benjamin for clean water, and told Eli to stop breathing over her shoulder.

LUKE: You were there?

PETER: I held the boy’s leg.

LUKE: Did she stay afterward?

PETER: For lentils. Eli burned the bottom.

LUKE: Another bad pot.

PETER: Andrew would have approved.

LUKE: Did they speak of the door?

PETER: Many times. Badly at first. Eli wanted her to say it was finished. Mara wanted him to listen while she named each house where she had begged for shelter that night.

LUKE: Did he listen?

PETER: The first time, until she spoke of his sons. Then he left.

LUKE: And the next?

PETER: He stayed longer.

LUKE: How many times?

PETER: I do not know. Eight. Twelve. Enough that she no longer began at the lane each time.

LUKE: Were you present?

PETER: Once. Mara told me to go away.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Eli kept looking at me to see whether he was listening correctly.

LUKE: Did you go?

PETER: I waited in the yard.

LUKE: That sounds like something you learned.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: From whom?

PETER: A man who stood on a shore and did not help me answer.

[The boat turns. Water knocks more softly against the boards.]

LUKE: We have come back to my first question.

PETER: We never left it.

LUKE: Were you afraid when He found you?

PETER: I had seen Him alive. I had been glad beyond sense. I had touched Him. I had eaten near Him. And still, when the first gladness quieted, the courtyard was waiting inside me.

LUKE: So you returned to fishing.

PETER: We were fishermen.

LUKE: John kept your words.

PETER: Read them.

LUKE: John set it down like this.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:3), read by Luke: Simon Peter saith unto them, I go a fishing. They say unto him, We also go with thee. They went forth, and entered into a ship immediately; and that night they caught nothing.

PETER: He might have left out the last part.

LUKE: An hundred and fifty and three fish later, he may have thought the empty night mattered.

PETER: We counted because fishermen count.

LUKE: Why did the others come with you?

PETER: Perhaps they feared I would sink the boat alone.

LUKE: Did you tell them what you meant to do?

PETER: I said I was fishing.

LUKE: Nothing else?

PETER: I was tired of words. Mine especially.

LUKE: Was it like this night?

PETER: Warmer. Less wind. James sat there. John there. Nathanael complained about the net until he was made to cast it.

LUKE: Did anyone speak of the courtyard?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did you wish they would?

PETER: No. Yes. I do not know.

LUKE: You knew He was alive.

PETER: Knowing did not tell me where to put my hands.

LUKE: So you put them on a net.

PETER: The net came back empty. Again and again. By morning I was angry at the sea, which had done nothing to me.

LUKE: Then you saw the shore.

PETER: John saw first.

LUKE: Of course.

PETER: Of course.

LUKE: What did you see?

PETER: A man near the water. A small fire. Something laid over it. John knew before I did.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: Then I was in the water.

LUKE: You left the fish and the boat.

PETER: There were no fish when I left it.

LUKE: John’s account disagrees by a moment.

PETER: John may come out tonight and argue the order.

LUKE: He says you drew the full net ashore.

PETER: I did. After I had reached Him. After the others had brought the boat. I went back into the water and dragged because that was what needed doing.

LUKE: Shall I read it?

PETER: Begin when we came to land.

LUKE: John set it down like this.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:9-14), read by Luke: As soon then as they were come to land, they saw a fire of coals there, and fish laid thereon, and bread.
Jesus saith unto them, Bring of the fish which ye have now caught.
Simon Peter went up, and drew the net to land full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and three: and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken.
Jesus saith unto them, Come and dine. And none of the disciples durst ask him, Who art thou? knowing that it was the Lord.
Jesus then cometh, and taketh bread, and giveth them, and fish likewise.
This is now the third time that Jesus shewed himself to his disciples, after that he was risen from the dead.

[Peter lets out the sail a little.]

LUKE: What did you expect to find on that shore?

PETER: Not breakfast.

LUKE: A rebuke?

PETER: Something. I had prepared for anger. I had prepared to be dismissed. I had prepared words for both.

LUKE: What words?

PETER: I cannot remember. They were likely loud.

LUKE: But He fed you.

PETER: Fish and bread. The fish was crisp at the tail. I burned my finger and put it in my mouth. Nathanael saw and laughed, then pretended he had coughed.

LUKE: Were you able to eat?

PETER: Two pieces.

LUKE: Only two?

PETER: I counted those too.

LUKE: Was the fire like the one in the courtyard?

PETER: Coals. Red beneath. Gray at the edges.

LUKE: Did you notice at once?

PETER: I noticed everything at once. The fire. His hands. The sound of John chewing. Water running from my garment into the sand.

LUKE: Did He look at you while you ate?

PETER: He gave me bread.

LUKE: That is not an answer.

PETER: It was enough to keep me seated.

LUKE: And after you had eaten?

PETER: John wrote it. My own telling always catches there.

LUKE: Would you rather read it?

PETER: No. You read.

LUKE: John set it down like this.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.
He saith to him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my sheep.
He saith unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

[Long silence.]

PETER: The first answer still had too much of the old night in it.

LUKE: “Yea, Lord.”

PETER: Quick. Ready.

LUKE: The second is the same.

PETER: Less ready.

LUKE: The third—

PETER: I heard the girl then.

LUKE: At the fire?

PETER: At both fires. Her voice, the men, my own mouth. I thought the question had opened everything I had tried to hold shut.

LUKE: You were grieved.

PETER: John chose a small word.

LUKE: Did you understand why He continued?

PETER: Not when He began.

LUKE: When did you understand?

PETER: When I had no answer left that depended on me.

LUKE: “Thou knowest all things.”

PETER: I could not tell Him what sort of man I would be tomorrow. I had done that once.

LUKE: So you told Him what He knew.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And each time He gave you people.

PETER: Lambs. Sheep. Hungry, troublesome, frightened people who steal knives and weight cloth and close doors.

LUKE: Did that surprise you?

PETER: I had expected to lose my place near Him. I did not expect Him to place anyone near me.

LUKE: Was the work a test?

PETER: Joram was not a test. Damaris was not a test. Mara and Eli were not pieces set out for my improvement.

LUKE: No.

PETER: They needed food. They needed roofs. They needed someone at the corner while they walked to a door.

LUKE: You knew how it felt to stand outside one.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Is that why you stayed with them?

PETER: Sometimes. Sometimes I stayed because He had given them to us and no one else wanted the trouble. Sometimes because I liked them. Sometimes because leaving would have shown that my fine words had returned.

LUKE: You still feared your own words.

PETER: I do.

LUKE: Yet you preach.

PETER: Then I mend nets. It helps.

LUKE: There is something I have held back from you.

PETER: I wondered when you would spend it.

LUKE: You knew?

PETER: Mary Magdalene found me before you left.

LUKE: She asked me not to give it to you until we had spoken of the shore.

PETER: That sounds like Mary.

LUKE: She said Mark preserved words from the garden morning which you seldom allow anyone to read in your hearing.

PETER: He preserved them.

LUKE: May I?

PETER: Now.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[Peter’s grip loosens on the rope.]

PETER: She remembers that?

LUKE: She remembers carrying it.

PETER: I thought only of the haste. The running. The stone. John and I went as if the whole city were burning behind us.

LUKE: Mary remembers your face when she gave you the message.

PETER: I did not ask her what she saw.

LUKE: She knows.

PETER: “And Peter.”

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I had put myself outside the word “disciples.”

LUKE: The message did not.

PETER: No.

LUKE: You heard your name before you saw Him alive.

PETER: I heard it and did not know what to do with it.

LUKE: You ran.

PETER: I ran.

LUKE: To the tomb.

PETER: To an empty place I did not understand.

LUKE: And later to the water.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why did Mary hold that until now?

PETER: Because she knew I would speak around it.

LUKE: You have done that before?

PETER: For years.

LUKE: Does she permit it?

PETER: Mary does not wait for permission.

[The first bird calls from shore.]

LUKE: Dawn.

PETER: Just the edge of it.

LUKE: I can see the beach.

PETER: Take the stern rope.

LUKE: The other rope?

PETER: You have learned.

LUKE: I have learned four ropes.

PETER: Do not become vehement about it.

LUKE: I deserved that.

PETER: A little.

[The keel touches gravel. They step into shallow water.]

LUKE: You have not answered what I should have done with Simeon.

PETER: Find him.

LUKE: It may be years too late.

PETER: Find the widow first.

LUKE: To ask whether I may bring him near?

PETER: To ask about her wrist.

LUKE: I know about her wrist.

PETER: You know how it healed beneath your bandage. Ask how she lifts a cup now.

LUKE: And if she still wants him in her house?

PETER: Then listen to why.

LUKE: And if she does not?

PETER: Listen to that too.

LUKE: Then find Simeon.

PETER: If you can.

LUKE: And give him work.

PETER: Ask what work his hands know.

LUKE: He copied accounts badly.

PETER: Then do not give him accounts.

LUKE: He could carry remedies.

PETER: Not the names of the houses?

LUKE: No.

PETER: There. You have begun.

LUKE: I may never find him.

PETER: That is possible.

LUKE: You are not troubled by how little certainty you offer.

PETER: I have certainty enough for the next thing.

[Peter drags the boat above the waterline.]

LUKE: Joram had a net. Damaris had a scale. Eli had a roof. What did you have?

PETER: Wet clothes.

LUKE: After the shore.

PETER: A net heavy with fish. Men who had seen the worst of me. Bread in my belly. Work I had not earned.

LUKE: And the look in the courtyard?

PETER: Still with me.

LUKE: Has it changed?

PETER: His face has not.

LUKE: Your memory of it?

PETER: I stayed long enough to see the shore.

LUKE: That is not the one word I wanted.

PETER: No.

[PETER gathers dry grass, small twigs, and three pieces of charcoal from a covered pot.]

LUKE: Let me.

PETER: You may clean the fish.

LUKE: I am a physician.

PETER: Then you know where to cut.

LUKE: You keep charcoal here?

PETER: It takes a spark well when the wood is damp.

LUKE: Your hands are shaking.

PETER: I am old.

LUKE: That is not why.

PETER: No.

[Flint strikes. A thread of flame catches in the grass.]

PETER: Sit nearer. Your clothes are wet.

LUKE: Here?

PETER: Nearer than that.

[Luke sits. Peter lays the first fish over the coals.]

LUKE: Did you ever sit near such a fire without hearing the courtyard?

PETER: Once.

LUKE: Only once?

PETER: The first morning.

LUKE: And afterward?

PETER: I heard the courtyard. I also heard John chewing.

LUKE: Both remained.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Peter, when He looked at you, what was the other answer?

PETER: He knew where I was.

LUKE: Beneath the palace.

PETER: There too.

LUKE: Is that what you saw at the shore?

PETER: I saw that He had come where I was.

LUKE: And after all these years?

PETER: Luke, pass me the bread.

LUKE: It is damp.

PETER: We will warm it.

LUKE: I understand why Mary made me wait.

PETER: Do not tell her that.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: I will hear of it every time she sees me.

LUKE: She already knows.

PETER: Yes. She usually does.

[The fire settles. Dawn reaches the boat.]

PETER: Who will you find when you leave here?

LUKE: Thomas.
