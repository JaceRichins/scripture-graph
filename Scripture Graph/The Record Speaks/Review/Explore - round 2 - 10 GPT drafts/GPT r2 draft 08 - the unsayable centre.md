LUKE: What became of the family?

PETER: Her husband returned before winter. The boy grew broad and lost the broom. The younger child could not remember Kezia’s face, which wounded Judith all over again.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: She began telling him.

LUKE: Telling the younger child about his sister.

PETER: Every night. The cup, the beans, the two-note song. She asked me once whether she was speaking too much of the dead.

LUKE: What did you answer?

PETER: I asked whether the boy was listening.

LUKE: Was he?

PETER: He could sing the song.

LUKE: You fed that household for nine days.

PETER: Judith fed me on the tenth. Burned the bread.

LUKE: Did you eat it?

PETER: Every black part.

LUKE: Why did you not tell me this before?

PETER: You asked about a face.

LUKE: And this is part of your answer?

PETER: It is what came after the shore.

LUKE: There were others.

PETER: Many.

LUKE: Tell me one you did not help.

PETER: You think there was only one?

LUKE: Tell me one you still remember.

PETER: Malchus.

LUKE: The servant from the garden?

PETER: Not him. A stonecutter from Ptolemais. Everyone called him Malchus because no one could pronounce his other name.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: He drank. Beat his wife. Then wept and promised. Then drank and beat her again.

LUKE: Did she come to you?

PETER: Her sister did. She had a tooth wrapped in cloth.

LUKE: Whose tooth?

PETER: The wife’s.

LUKE: Why bring it?

PETER: She thought I would believe a tooth.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: I believed Malchus first.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: He cried.

LUKE: And she did not?

PETER: She stood very still. Her cheek was the size of an apple. I asked them both to come back the next day.

LUKE: You sent her home with him?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: I know.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: He broke two of her fingers that night.

LUKE: Because she had spoken?

PETER: Because he knew she had.

LUKE: What did you do then?

PETER: Took her to her sister’s house. Sat outside with a staff in my lap.

LUKE: Did he come?

PETER: Near midnight. Brought three men from the quarry.

LUKE: Were they armed?

PETER: Hammers. They did not lift them. I knew one of the men.

LUKE: Who?

PETER: Hananias.

LUKE: The tile maker.

PETER: He stood behind Malchus in the dark, wearing both sandals.

LUKE: What did he do?

PETER: When Malchus stepped toward the door, Hananias caught his belt and pulled him backward.

LUKE: Did they fight?

PETER: Hananias took one blow. Lost part of a tooth.

LUKE: Another tooth.

PETER: I spent too much time with teeth that year.

LUKE: What happened to Malchus?

PETER: We kept him from the house. Two men stayed with him in a storeroom by the harbor.

LUKE: To guard him?

PETER: To keep him sober. To keep others safe.

LUKE: Did his wife return to him?

PETER: Not for a long time. It was her choice.

LUKE: Did he change?

PETER: Some. Then not at all. Then some again.

LUKE: That is not much of an ending.

PETER: It was not finished when I last saw him.

LUKE: And his wife?

PETER: Her fingers healed crooked. She learned to cut letters into soft clay with the other hand. Children liked her letters because every line leaned left.

LUKE: What did you learn from sending her home?

PETER: I learned her name.

LUKE: You did not know it?

PETER: I had called her the stonecutter’s wife.

LUKE: What was her name?

PETER: Damaris.

LUKE: You remember it now.

PETER: I remember it before his.

LUKE: Did she forgive you?

PETER: She brought me a clay cup.

LUKE: Was that forgiveness?

PETER: It was a cup. She said I could drink from it.

LUKE: You have answered every question with something that must be touched.

PETER: A foot. A mat. A tooth. A cup. Those are the things people bring.

LUKE: And what did you bring to the shore?

PETER: Nothing He needed.

LUKE: You brought yourself.

PETER: So did Judas.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: Do not make a fine phrase of me.

LUKE: I am trying to see why you could receive the work He gave you.

PETER: I received it because He gave it.

LUKE: Even after what you had done.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: When Hananias stood outside, did you see yourself?

PETER: No. I saw a man with one sandal and a cut foot.

LUKE: When Judith struck you?

PETER: I saw that she had not eaten.

LUKE: When Damaris showed you the tooth?

PETER: I should have seen her. I saw his tears.

LUKE: You do not trust ideas about people.

PETER: I have trusted too many.

LUKE: Yet you trust Christ.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: You are back at the face.

LUKE: I never left it.

PETER: I know.

LUKE: There is more in John’s account.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: After the charge, He spoke of where your own road would lead.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.  
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

LUKE: How did you hear that beside the lake?

PETER: I looked at my hands.

LUKE: Were you frightened?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did the fear remain?

PETER: It comes when it likes.

LUKE: What do you do then?

PETER: Mend something. Visit someone. Eat if there is food.

LUKE: Sharpen a knife.

PETER: Four times.

LUKE: You told Mark the worst of the court. You let John keep the questions on the shore. But you will not tell me what was in His face.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Is it because words would lessen it?

PETER: Perhaps.

LUKE: Is it because you are guarding something?

PETER: Perhaps.

LUKE: From me?

PETER: From the face you brought with you.

LUKE: Dositheus.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: I left his brother’s eyes open.

PETER: Then that is where you begin.

LUKE: He may shut the door.

PETER: He may.

LUKE: He may tell me I should have known.

PETER: You did know.

LUKE: Not soon enough.

PETER: Then do not defend yourself.

LUKE: What if he asks why I crossed the street?

PETER: Tell him.

LUKE: You make it sound possible.

PETER: It is a street.

LUKE: You know it is more than that.

PETER: I know there will be stones. Carts. Men who will not move aside. Watch your feet.

LUKE: And if I reach him?

PETER: Use his name.

LUKE: Dositheus.

PETER: Not to me.

LUKE: No.

[Luke closes the tablet.]

LUKE: I have carried something else. I was told not to give it to you until we had spoken.

PETER: By whom?

LUKE: Mary Magdalene placed it in my care when I asked her about the morning at the tomb.

PETER: Mary remembers every step of that morning.

LUKE: She wanted this read to you as Mark kept it.

PETER: Why now?

LUKE: She did not tell me. Only that I would know when.

PETER: Read.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[Long silence.]

PETER: She carried that all these years?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I heard it then. I knew the words.

LUKE: She knew that.

PETER: And still she sent it.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: She would.

LUKE: Why do you say it that way?

PETER: Because she saw the empty place before I did.

LUKE: Peter, when He turned in the court—

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: I will not write it.

PETER: You may.

LUKE: Tell me.

PETER: I had made myself a man He did not know.

LUKE: And His face?

PETER: He knew me.

LUKE: Is that all you can say?

PETER: He did not look away.

[The knife is set down.]

PETER: Where will you go from here?

LUKE: To find Thomas.
