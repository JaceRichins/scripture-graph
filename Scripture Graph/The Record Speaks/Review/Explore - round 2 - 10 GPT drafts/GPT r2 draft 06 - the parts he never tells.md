LUKE: Was it the same as the courtyard?

PETER: No.

LUKE: How was it different?

PETER: He was near enough to hand me bread.

LUKE: Then, after they had eaten—

PETER: Read it slowly.

LUKE: You have heard it often.

PETER: I asked you to read it slowly.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs. He saith to him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my sheep. He saith unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

[The room is still.]

PETER: I hear how sure the first answer sounds.

LUKE: Were you sure?

PETER: Of my love? Yes. Of myself? No.

LUKE: The first question reached back to your claim that you would remain when others fell.

PETER: I knew where it reached.

LUKE: You did not answer that you loved Him more than they did.

PETER: I had stopped weighing my heart against theirs.

LUKE: When did you understand what He was doing?

PETER: Not at the first question.

LUKE: At the next?

PETER: I began to fear it.

LUKE: At the last?

PETER: I could hear the girl again.

LUKE: At the gate?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Could you see the other men?

PETER: Fennel seed in one man’s teeth. Oil on the sleeve of another. The place on the stones where I had stood.

LUKE: Yet you were on the shore.

PETER: With daylight on the water. Fish grease on my fingers. His face before me.

LUKE: Did you understand the work He gave you?

PETER: I understood sheep poorly. I understood hunger.

LUKE: Was it a command or forgiveness?

PETER: Luke, I was not sorting words while He sat there.

LUKE: You have sorted them since.

PETER: Too much.

LUKE: What did you think He wanted?

PETER: People fed.

LUKE: By you.

PETER: That was the part I could scarcely take in.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: I would not have trusted me with anyone.

LUKE: He did.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did you feel free when He finished?

PETER: I felt tired.

LUKE: Tired?

PETER: I had held my body tight for days. My shoulders dropped. Then I began to shake.

LUKE: Did the others see?

PETER: Of course.

LUKE: Were you ashamed?

PETER: Some.

LUKE: What did they do?

PETER: Andrew gave me his cloak, though I already had one. John poked the coals apart. No one watched me kindly.

LUKE: Kindly?

PETER: There is a kind watching that is hard to bear. They spared me that.

LUKE: You said nothing more?

PETER: Not that John kept.

LUKE: And afterward, you fed them.

PETER: Badly at first.

LUKE: That is where you usually stop.

PETER: It is enough for most tellings.

LUKE: Not for mine.

PETER: No. I see that.

LUKE: Who was first?

PETER: First what?

LUKE: The first person He put in front of you who had done something you could not excuse.

PETER: Jacob.

LUKE: Tell me about him.

PETER: He owned half a boat near Magdala. His brother owned the other half. In a storm, Jacob cut their towline to keep the boat from being pulled onto rocks.

LUKE: What was on the line?

PETER: His brother’s smaller boat. His brother, two hired men, and the brother’s oldest boy.

LUKE: Did they live?

PETER: The brother and one man reached shore. The other man and the boy did not.

LUKE: Jacob saved his own boat.

PETER: And six men aboard it. That was how he told the matter.

LUKE: Was he wrong to cut the line?

PETER: I was not in that water.

LUKE: What brought him to you?

PETER: His brother would not speak to him. The dead man’s wife spat when Jacob passed. Jacob began sleeping in the boat.

LUKE: Did he confess?

PETER: For three mornings.

LUKE: Three?

PETER: Each time he changed the story.

LUKE: How?

PETER: First, the rope tore. Then another man cut it. Then Jacob had cut it but only after everyone shouted at him.

LUKE: And the last morning?

PETER: He said his brother begged him to wait. Jacob cut it while his brother’s hand was still on the line.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: I told him to go home.

LUKE: That was all?

PETER: I was angry. He wanted me to say something over him and make the village stop looking.

LUKE: So you dismissed him.

PETER: He walked as far as the road.

LUKE: Then?

PETER: I followed. Without sandals.

LUKE: Why without sandals?

PETER: I had taken them off near the water. I forgot them.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: I asked where the widow’s roof leaked.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Rain was coming. Her boy had been the one who patched it.

LUKE: Did Jacob repair it?

PETER: He tried. She threw lentils at him.

LUKE: Cooked?

PETER: Dry. They hurt.

LUKE: You were there?

PETER: I had climbed the ladder first. I took most of them.

LUKE: What did Jacob do?

PETER: He picked them from the mud.

LUKE: All of them?

PETER: Most. We never found every lentil.

LUKE: Did she forgive him?

PETER: Not that day.

LUKE: Did you make her receive him?

PETER: How could I?

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: He repaired the roof. Then part of it fell in because he had laid the reeds poorly.

LUKE: Oh, Jacob.

PETER: That is what I said.

LUKE: Did she throw more lentils?

PETER: A cup.

LUKE: At you?

PETER: At Jacob. It missed and broke near my foot.

LUKE: Did he return?

PETER: The next morning. He brought a man who knew roofs.

LUKE: How long did this go on?

PETER: Months. He carried grain. Replaced a stool. Took the younger children to gather driftwood.

LUKE: And the brother?

PETER: Would not speak to him.

LUKE: Never?

PETER: Not while I was there.

LUKE: Then how did it end?

PETER: It did not end for my convenience.

LUKE: You still counted Jacob among the flock?

PETER: I ate with him.

LUKE: Even while the brother refused?

PETER: I ate with the brother too.

LUKE: At the same table?

PETER: Once. It went badly.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: Jacob reached for the salt. His brother stood and left.

LUKE: Did you follow?

PETER: No. That time I stayed with Jacob.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: He had put the salt down and could not lift his hand.

LUKE: What became of him?

PETER: He sold his share of the boat to provide for the widow’s children. Not at once. Do not make him too quick.

LUKE: Did you ask him to sell it?

PETER: I asked whether the sight of it kept him awake.

LUKE: And later?

PETER: Years later, the brother let Jacob mend a torn net beside him. They did not speak. They worked until dark.

LUKE: Were you there?

PETER: Across the beach. I kept my back turned so they would continue.

LUKE: That is one.

PETER: You are counting?

LUKE: I want to know what the shore became.

PETER: It became many meals eaten with men who did not deserve pleasant company.

LUKE: That is close to a line for a cushion.

PETER: You are learning cruelty from me.

LUKE: Another person.

PETER: Keziah.

LUKE: What had she done?

PETER: She kept the common purse for a small gathering inland. Her son owed money to a dyer. Men had threatened to take his tools.

LUKE: She stole from the purse.

PETER: Six silver pieces.

LUKE: Did she admit it?

PETER: After accusing two other women.

LUKE: That made it worse.

PETER: Much worse.

LUKE: Who came to you?

PETER: The women she accused. One was her sister’s daughter.

LUKE: What did they want?

PETER: Keziah put out.

LUKE: What did you want?

PETER: The same.

LUKE: What stopped you?

PETER: She was sitting outside.

LUKE: Like Leontes.

PETER: Not like him. She had a basket of onions and kept sorting them by size.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: She had brought them for the meal. No one would take them.

LUKE: Did you let her in?

PETER: I sat outside first.

LUKE: What did she say?

PETER: That the large onions would spoil sooner.

LUKE: Did you discuss the money?

PETER: Not until we had sorted the basket.

LUKE: Then?

PETER: She lied again.

LUKE: While confessing?

PETER: Yes. People do.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Asked her niece to bring the account tablet.

LUKE: Was the niece willing?

PETER: No. She brought it because I asked. She set it down hard enough to crack the wax.

LUKE: Did Keziah repay the silver?

PETER: Her son repaid two pieces. Keziah worked in the dye yard for the rest.

LUKE: Was she restored to the purse?

PETER: Not alone.

LUKE: But she handled it again?

PETER: After a year. Beside her niece.

LUKE: Was that wise?

PETER: The first week, no one slept.

LUKE: Did she steal again?

PETER: A copper.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: She returned it before dusk.

LUKE: How did the niece respond?

PETER: She screamed at her in the lane.

LUKE: And you?

PETER: I was inside eating a fig.

LUKE: You did not intervene?

PETER: By the time I reached them, Keziah had opened both hands and the copper was on her palm. Her niece took it. Then they both cried. I stood there holding half a fig.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: Nothing useful.

LUKE: Did Keziah remain?

PETER: Yes. She never kept the purse alone. She brought onions every week until no one remembered why.

LUKE: Did you trust her?

PETER: Enough to give her something to carry. Not enough to pretend the copper had not been there.

LUKE: You make room for both.

PETER: I try to keep accounts as they are.

LUKE: Another.

PETER: You came prepared to stay.

LUKE: I brought two tablets.

PETER: Dositheus, then.

LUKE: What did he do?

PETER: He gave names to men with rods.

LUKE: Like Leontes.

PETER: Perhaps.

LUKE: What happened to those he named?

PETER: One family escaped. One man was taken for nine days. Another, Asa, was held longer. His left hand never closed properly afterward.

LUKE: Why did Dositheus come to you?

PETER: His mother sent him.

LUKE: He did not come willingly?

PETER: He stood outside my door until rain soaked him. That was willingness enough for the first night.

LUKE: Did you let him in?

PETER: I asked whether he had eaten.

LUKE: Had he?

PETER: Not since the day before.

LUKE: You gave him food.

PETER: Two bowls. He brought the first one back clean and asked for more without lifting his eyes.

LUKE: Then he confessed?

PETER: After he ate.

LUKE: Would food not make confession too easy?

PETER: Have you confessed hungry?

LUKE: No.

PETER: Neither had I.

LUKE: What did you do after he told you?

PETER: Took him to Asa.

LUKE: At once?

PETER: The next morning.

LUKE: Did Asa receive him?

PETER: Asa saw him through the door and told me to take him away.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Where?

PETER: To the second house he had named.

LUKE: And there?

PETER: The woman of the house washed his feet. Her husband would not enter the room.

LUKE: Did that family forgive him?

PETER: The woman gave him a sleeping mat. Let us not call every mat forgiveness.

LUKE: How long did he stay?

PETER: Four nights. Then the husband put the mat outside.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Dositheus had asked whether they would speak for him to Asa. He asked too soon.

LUKE: Did he return to you?

PETER: Angry. He said he had confessed and nothing had changed.

LUKE: What did you tell him?

PETER: To help me carry a basket.

LUKE: Where?

PETER: To Asa’s house.

LUKE: Asa had refused him.

PETER: The basket was for Asa.

LUKE: What was in it?

PETER: Barley, lamp oil, dried fish, and salve for his hand.

LUKE: Did Asa accept it?

PETER: He accepted the barley and oil. Sent back the fish. Kept the salve after pretending he had not.

LUKE: And Dositheus?

PETER: Waited around the corner.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Until I returned.

LUKE: What did you tell him?

PETER: Exactly what Asa kept.

LUKE: Why the details?

PETER: He needed to know the door had opened the width of a jar of salve.

LUKE: Did it open farther?

PETER: Slowly.

LUKE: How slowly?

PETER: Dositheus left a bundle of kindling each sixth day. Asa burned it. That went on through winter.

LUKE: Did they meet?

PETER: In spring, Asa’s roof beam cracked. Dositheus was a carpenter.

LUKE: Asa let him repair it?

PETER: Asa sat outside and watched every peg.

LUKE: Did they speak?

PETER: About the beam.

LUKE: Nothing else?

PETER: Not that day.

LUKE: Later?

PETER: Dositheus began carrying Asa’s nets because Asa could not grip them well. Months after that, they ate at the same table.

LUKE: Did Asa forgive him?

PETER: He gave Dositheus the larger piece of fish.

LUKE: That was your answer?

PETER: It was Asa’s. I did not ask for another.

LUKE: Were all the stories like that?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Tell me one that failed.

PETER: A man named Hiram struck his wife and came to me weeping.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Believed his tears too quickly.

LUKE: He struck her again?

PETER: Three days later.

LUKE: What then?

PETER: She and the children slept in another house. Hiram said I had turned them against him.

LUKE: Had you?

PETER: I had told them where they could sleep.

LUKE: Did he remain among you?

PETER: At the edge.

LUKE: Did you trust him?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did you feed him?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Years.

LUKE: Did he change?

PETER: Some. Not enough for her to return.

LUKE: Did you ask her to?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did he accuse you of withholding pardon?

PETER: Often. He liked that word when it got him near her door.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: That the children were sleeping.

LUKE: Nothing more?

PETER: Once I argued until the lamp went out. It did no good.

LUKE: What did you do instead?

PETER: Met him in the morning. Put him to work unloading grain with men who knew why he was there.

LUKE: Did you still call him brother?

PETER: Some days with difficulty.

LUKE: Why not send him away?

PETER: Because being fed did not give him his wife back. He had confused those things. I would not.

LUKE: And her?

PETER: We repaired her oven. Hiram did not come.

LUKE: Who paid for it?

PETER: Keziah kept the account.

LUKE: The woman who stole from the purse.

PETER: She wrote every copper down.

LUKE: Did Hiram ever dine with his family again?

PETER: Once, when the oldest girl was married. He sat near the door. His wife chose the place.

LUKE: How did it go?

PETER: He left before sweet cakes. That was wise.

LUKE: Did you sit with him?

PETER: Outside. We shared one cake someone wrapped in cloth.

LUKE: You have remembered many meals.

PETER: People speak differently after they have eaten.

LUKE: Is that what you learned at the shore?

PETER: I told you, I was not learning sentences there.

LUKE: Yet when Leontes stood outside—

PETER: You went inside to decide whether he belonged.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: He was bleeding.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Start with that.

LUKE: And after I wash him?

PETER: Ask whose names he gave.

LUKE: Then take him to them?

PETER: If they permit it. Do not arrive at a wounded family with their betrayer and call it mercy.

LUKE: What if they refuse him?

PETER: Find where he sleeps.

LUKE: What if he lies?

PETER: He will.

LUKE: You sound certain.

PETER: I lied while a girl looked straight at me.

LUKE: What if he betrays them again?

PETER: Keep him away from names until his hands stop shaking.

LUKE: And still feed him?

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: You know the answer.

LUKE: I wanted you to say it.

PETER: Why?

LUKE: Because I did not give him bread.

PETER: Then take bread when you find him.

LUKE: If I find him.

PETER: You are good at finding men. You found me.

LUKE: You were not hiding.

PETER: I complained from the moment you arrived.

LUKE: That made the house easier to locate.

PETER: Mark gave you that answer.

LUKE: No. That one was mine.

PETER: Keep it. It may be worth half a fig.

LUKE: There is something else.

PETER: Your second tablet.

LUKE: A message.

PETER: From whom?

LUKE: Mary Magdalene.

[Peter does not answer.]

LUKE: She knew I was coming.

PETER: How is she?

LUKE: Strong. Tired. She made me promise not to spend this at the beginning.

PETER: She said that?

LUKE: Not in those words.

PETER: Good. For a moment she sounded like you.

LUKE: She remembers the morning at the tomb in pieces. The path. The stone. Her own breath. She said there was one piece you hurry past.

PETER: I know the morning.

LUKE: Mark kept the words.

PETER: Luke—

LUKE: Let me read them.

PETER: Why now?

LUKE: Because I nearly left a man outside.

PETER: That is your reason.

LUKE: Mary had hers.

PETER: Did she tell you what it was?

LUKE: She said you would know.

[Long pause.]

PETER: Read.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[Peter draws a slow breath.]

PETER: Mark kept that.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I told him to.

LUKE: But you hurry over your name.

PETER: It slows me.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: I was in the room with the others.

LUKE: Yet you were named.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: When did Mary give you the message?

PETER: In the confusion. I heard the Galilee part. The room was full of feet and questions.

LUKE: And your name?

PETER: I heard it.

LUKE: Did you believe it was meant for you?

PETER: Not at first.

LUKE: Who else could it mean?

PETER: I thought perhaps she added it because she was kind.

LUKE: Did you ask her?

PETER: Much later.

LUKE: What did she tell you?

PETER: She had not added it.

LUKE: That is why she sent it again.

PETER: She remembers too much.

LUKE: You sound grateful.

PETER: I am trying not to weep in front of your two tablets.

LUKE: I can turn them over.

PETER: No. Leave them.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: I spent those days thinking I had placed myself outside the others.

LUKE: But your name was carried to you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Before the shore.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Before the questions.

PETER: Yes, Luke.

LUKE: Before you had repaired anything.

PETER: I had repaired nothing.

[The bread cloth is unfolded.]

PETER: You may have the bread now.

LUKE: I thought we were going to eat after.

PETER: It is after.

LUKE: There is one more passage.

PETER: John’s last words on the shore.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Read them. Then eat.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not. This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

PETER: The bread is hard.

LUKE: I have good teeth.

PETER: Physician’s pride.

LUKE: Fisherman’s bread.

PETER: Dip it in the oil.

LUKE: I will go back through Joppa.

PETER: Find Leontes first.

LUKE: Before I speak with the households?

PETER: Before you decide what he is.

LUKE: Water, cloth, bread.

PETER: Two loaves. He may be hungry enough to lie about that too.

LUKE: And if he is gone from Joppa?

PETER: Ask at the dye yards. Men with bruised faces find work where questions are few.

LUKE: You have known many of them.

PETER: Enough to keep bread near the door.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: Yes?

LUKE: Thank you for the parts you do not tell.

PETER: I told too many.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Mark will want them.

LUKE: I will hide the tablets.

PETER: He will find them.

LUKE: Then I will make him ask.

PETER: Make him bring fresh bread.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: Who are you going to find after Leontes?

LUKE: Thomas.
