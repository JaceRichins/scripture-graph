LUKE: I am Luke. I have gone from house to house gathering the accounts of Jesus the Messiah from those who saw Him.

LUKE: These conversations are shaped for the telling. The words preserved in scripture are not changed.

LUKE: I hope that, through the witness of those who knew Him, we may draw nearer to Him.

[The scrape of a bowl across a wooden table.]

PETER: Take that piece. The dark one.

LUKE: You burned it.

PETER: A little.

LUKE: Entirely.

PETER: Then leave it for me.

LUKE: You invited me to dine.

PETER: I did not promise good bread.

LUKE: Whose is the third bowl?

PETER: Nobody’s. I set three without thinking.

LUKE: You often do that?

PETER: More often than I used to.

LUKE: Expecting someone?

PETER: Somebody generally comes.

LUKE: At this hour?

PETER: Especially at this hour.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: In daylight a man can tell himself he only happened to be passing. At night he must admit he came to the door.

LUKE: You have had many such visitors.

PETER: Enough that I keep bread even after I ruin it.

LUKE: Mark told me you insisted on keeping the whole account of the courtyard.

PETER: Mark says I insisted?

LUKE: He said you would correct him if he softened it.

PETER: Ah.

LUKE: Is that not true?

PETER: It is. I dislike hearing that it is.

LUKE: I thought you might have wanted the account to stand against you.

PETER: Against me?

LUKE: As a judgment. So no one could make you larger than you were.

PETER: You came a long way to call me vain.

LUKE: That was not—

PETER: I know. Eat the middle. It escaped the flame.

LUKE: I believed you kept the worst of it because you did not trust yourself to be forgiven too easily.

PETER: Easily.

LUKE: That was my reading.

PETER: Mark had one too. John had another. You writers arrive with clean tablets and dirty questions.

LUKE: I can put the tablet away.

PETER: No. Then you will remember it incorrectly and blame me.

LUKE: Why did you keep it?

PETER: A man named Joel came here with an empty belly and a split lip.

LUKE: That is your answer?

PETER: It is where the answer begins.

LUKE: Who was Joel?

PETER: A fisherman from the western shore. Broad hands. Very poor knots. He tied everything as though he expected the rope to apologize and hold itself.

LUKE: How old was he?

PETER: Twenty-five, perhaps. He said twenty-three. Men with older brothers steal two years whenever they can.

LUKE: What had happened to his lip?

PETER: His brother struck him.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Joel had left him on the water.

LUKE: Left him?

PETER: There was a squall. Two boats, one net between them. It had twisted around the steering oar of Joel’s boat.

PETER: His brother Abner shouted for him to wait. Joel cut the net and turned for land.

LUKE: Was Abner’s boat lost?

PETER: Nearly. It filled. Two men from Gennesaret reached him before it went under.

LUKE: Then Joel had saved his own boat.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And perhaps his life.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: But he had not waited.

PETER: No.

LUKE: What did he want from you?

PETER: At first, supper.

LUKE: And after supper?

PETER: He wanted me to say Abner would have cut the net too.

LUKE: Would he?

PETER: I had never met Abner.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: I asked Joel whether he had called back before he turned.

LUKE: Had he?

PETER: Three times he answered a different question. Then he shook his head.

LUKE: Did he believe his brother would drown?

PETER: He believed it long enough to reach the beach. Then he learned Abner had been brought in alive.

LUKE: What did Joel do?

PETER: Hid the knife. Went home. Told their father the net had parted.

LUKE: So the brother struck him for the lie.

PETER: For the cutting. For the lie. For standing at the door while Abner told their father the truth.

LUKE: Why come to you?

PETER: Somebody had told him I understood leaving a man alone.

LUKE: Did that anger you?

PETER: Very much.

LUKE: At Joel?

PETER: At whoever sent him.

LUKE: Yet you let him in.

PETER: He was bleeding on the lintel. I did not want it on the wood.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: I let him in.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Gave him bread. Not this black. Better bread. He swallowed too fast and was sick in that corner.

LUKE: Here?

PETER: John was sitting where you are. Lift your feet if the memory troubles you.

PETER: For part of it. Joel would not look at either of us.

LUKE: Why ask that?

PETER: A cut net can be mended.

LUKE: You went after it?

PETER: At dawn. Joel and I walked the mud for half a day. We found perhaps a third of it.

LUKE: And Abner?

PETER: I told Joel to carry the wet cords to his brother.

LUKE: He agreed?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What did he do?

PETER: Sat in the mud.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Long enough for the flies to find us.

LUKE: Did you persuade him?

PETER: I told him I was going to Abner’s house and that he could follow or lose me at the bend.

LUKE: He followed.

PETER: At a great distance. Brave enough to keep me in sight.

LUKE: What happened at the house?

PETER: Abner opened the door, saw Joel, and shut it.

LUKE: And you?

PETER: Knocked again.

LUKE: Did Abner know who you were?

PETER: His wife did. She called through the door and said fame had not improved my manners.

LUKE: She sounds sensible.

PETER: Very. Abner opened again because she made him.

LUKE: What did Joel say?

PETER: He told the whole thing badly. He explained the wind. The oar. The weight of water. Everything except his brother’s face.

LUKE: Did Abner forgive him?

PETER: Not that day.

LUKE: Then how did it go?

PETER: I put the cords between them and asked whether either knew how to splice without making a lump.

LUKE: You gave them work.

PETER: Abner told me to leave.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: Eventually.

LUKE: Eventually?

PETER: His wife brought stools. We sat outside. Abner stood inside where we could see one sandal.

PETER: Joel began separating good cord from rotten. Near midday Abner came out and took the other end.

LUKE: They spoke?

PETER: About the net.

LUKE: Nothing else?

PETER: Not while I was there.

LUKE: Did they remain together?

PETER: They repaired boats together for six years. Some days they quarreled so loudly people crossed the road.

LUKE: And the cut net?

PETER: Too little remained for fishing. Abner made it into carrying bags.

LUKE: Did Joel keep one?

PETER: He brought grain here in it whenever he came.

LUKE: So when someone told him you understood—

PETER: He knew the courtyard story.

LUKE: And that was why you allowed Mark to keep it?

PETER: That was one reason.

LUKE: You could have told Joel privately.

PETER: I had never seen him before.

LUKE: Someone else told him.

PETER: Someone who had heard me tell it in a crowded house.

LUKE: You preached your own disgrace.

PETER: I told what happened.

LUKE: Repeatedly.

PETER: There were always new faces.

LUKE: Did the telling grow easier?

PETER: No. I grew more exact.

LUKE: Mark kept something before the courtyard. May I read it?

PETER: You will whether I permit you or not.

LUKE: I would rather have your permission.

PETER: Read.

PETER: He remembered the number.

LUKE: You did not?

PETER: I remembered that the net was too heavy and that my back hurt for two days.

LUKE: John says one hundred and fifty and three.

PETER: John counted anything that remained still.

LUKE: May I read it?

PETER: Begin with the boat.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:3), read by Luke: Simon Peter saith unto them, I go a fishing. They say unto him, We also go with thee. They went forth, and entered into a ship immediately; and that night they caught nothing.

LUKE: Why did you go?

PETER: The boat was there.

LUKE: You had already seen the risen Lord.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You knew death had not held Him.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Yet you returned to the nets.

PETER: We needed food. My hands needed something they knew. The others were staring at me.

LUKE: Waiting for you to lead?

PETER: Waiting for me to stop walking from room to room.

LUKE: Did you intend to resume fishing for good?

PETER: I intended to get through the night.

LUKE: And you caught nothing.

PETER: Not even weed worth scraping from the mesh.

LUKE: John has more.

PETER: Read.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:9-14), read by Luke: As soon then as they were come to land, they saw a fire of coals there, and fish laid thereon, and bread. Jesus saith unto them, Bring of the fish which ye have now caught. Simon Peter went up, and drew the net to land full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and three: and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken. Jesus saith unto them, Come and dine. And none of the disciples durst ask him, Who art thou? knowing that it was the Lord. Jesus then cometh, and taketh bread, and giveth them, and fish likewise. This is now the third time that Jesus shewed himself to his disciples, after that he was risen from the dead.

[Peter exhales.]

LUKE: What do you see?

PETER: His hands breaking bread.

LUKE: Not the fire?

PETER: I saw it.

LUKE: Did you think of the courtyard at once?

PETER: Before my feet touched the bottom.

LUKE: What did you expect?

PETER: To hear the night named.

LUKE: It was not?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did that comfort you?

PETER: It frightened me.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: If He had rebuked me, I could have lowered my head and received it.

LUKE: You wanted a sentence.

PETER: I wanted to know where to stand.

LUKE: Instead there was breakfast.

PETER: Fish, bread, wet clothes, all of us trying not to stare.

LUKE: Did anyone speak?

PETER: The record gives what matters.

LUKE: What did the bread taste like?

PETER: Bread.

LUKE: You remember Reuben’s broth and Joel being sick, but not this?

PETER: I remember holding it. My fingers would not close.

LUKE: Did He place it in your hand?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And you ate?

PETER: Eventually.

LUKE: What was difficult about eating?

PETER: The kindness of it.

LUKE: Because you had not earned a place there?

PETER: Because it was a real meal. Not a sign in the clouds. There was grease on my thumb.

LUKE: You have served food to every person in the stories you told me.

PETER: People arrive hungry.

LUKE: Joel first.

PETER: He had walked all night.

LUKE: Damaris and her boys.

PETER: The flour was for them.

LUKE: Reuben.

PETER: Fever eats a man.

LUKE: Did you know you were repeating that morning?

PETER: Not the first time.

LUKE: When did you notice?

PETER: Kezia complained that I gave bread away before asking why anyone had come.

LUKE: What did you tell her?

PETER: To bake more.

LUKE: That did not answer her.

PETER: It solved the bread.

LUKE: John next records the questions.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: I have read them many times.

PETER: Then read slowly.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.

[Silence.]

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: He saith to him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

[Long pause.]

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: He saith unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

[The legs of Peter’s chair settle against the floor.]

LUKE: You were grieved.

PETER: John saw that.

LUKE: What changed at the last question?

PETER: I had nothing left to offer as proof.

LUKE: You did not appeal to what you would do.

PETER: I had done that before.

LUKE: You placed the knowing with Him.

PETER: Where else could I put it?

LUKE: And then He entrusted people to you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Immediately.

PETER: The fish was still before us.

LUKE: Did you feel restored?

PETER: I felt tired.

LUKE: Only tired?

PETER: Relieved. Afraid. Hungry again. John was crying and pretending smoke had entered his eyes.

LUKE: Did you understand the work?

PETER: I thought I did.

LUKE: What did you imagine?

PETER: Crowds listening. Men changing their lives before I finished speaking. Perhaps fewer grain tallies.

LUKE: Instead Joel sat in the mud.

PETER: And Damaris stole barley.

LUKE: Reuben doubted your face.

PETER: Kezia frightened everyone.

LUKE: Did the work disappoint you?

PETER: No. It surprised me by having names.

LUKE: You still speak of them.

PETER: I still have Joel’s grain bag. Damaris still argues over measures. Reuben’s bowl will not sit flat.

LUKE: These are the reasons you kept the courtyard in Mark’s account.

PETER: A man who had cut a net needed to know I had run. A woman who had taken bread needed to know I had used my mouth for worse.

LUKE: And Reuben?

PETER: He had seen me at the fire. If I removed the cursing, he would know I had lied again.

LUKE: Mark’s severity was not against you.

PETER: It was true. That was enough.

LUKE: I wanted the shore to cancel the courtyard.

PETER: It did not.

LUKE: No.

PETER: I remember both.

LUKE: Yet you no longer sit far from the fire.

PETER: Sometimes I do.

LUKE: Still?

PETER: Last winter Reuben found me on the other side of the room. He moved my stool without asking.

LUKE: Did you object?

PETER: Naturally.

LUKE: Where did he put it?

PETER: Beside his.

LUKE: And you stayed?

PETER: The room was cold.

LUKE: Of course.

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: Yes?

PETER: Find Nicanor.

LUKE: I do not know where he went.

PETER: Ask the people whose accounts he altered.

LUKE: They may not know.

PETER: Ask.

LUKE: And if I find him?

PETER: Take bread.

LUKE: Then what?

PETER: You are the physician.

LUKE: He may lie again.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: He may damage the record.

PETER: Do not give him the record.

LUKE: Bowls?

PETER: You have very few ideas for a writer.

LUKE: I can think of work.

PETER: Good.

LUKE: I am not certain I can welcome him.

PETER: Take the third bowl.

LUKE: The one you set without thinking.

PETER: I am beginning to think I set it for you.

LUKE: There is one more thing.

PETER: There always is with you.

LUKE: I kept it until now because Mary Magdalene asked that the morning be told with care.

PETER: Go on.

LUKE: Mark preserved the message given at the tomb. There is a part she would not let me pass over.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[Peter draws one breath, then another.]

PETER: Read the beginning again.

LUKE: “But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter—”

PETER: No. The scripture as Mark kept it. All of it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[Long silence.]

PETER: Mary kept that?

LUKE: Every word.

PETER: I knew the message. I knew we were to go.

LUKE: But?

PETER: In the telling, with everyone speaking at once, I thought perhaps my name had been added for my sake afterward.

LUKE: Mary was certain.

PETER: She would be.

LUKE: Mark was certain too.

PETER: He asked me once why I stopped him whenever he reached that line.

LUKE: What did you tell him?

PETER: That his reading was poor.

LUKE: Was it?

PETER: Very poor.

LUKE: And mine?

PETER: Worse.

LUKE: Shall I read it once more?

PETER: Not now.

[Peter turns the uneven bowl beneath his hand.]

PETER: Leave it there.

LUKE: The tablet?

PETER: The bowl.

LUKE: You expect someone.

PETER: Somebody generally comes.

LUKE: I should go before it grows later.

PETER: Where will you go?

LUKE: First, to ask after Nicanor.

PETER: Good.

LUKE: Then I continue gathering the witnesses.

PETER: Who is next?

LUKE: Thomas.
