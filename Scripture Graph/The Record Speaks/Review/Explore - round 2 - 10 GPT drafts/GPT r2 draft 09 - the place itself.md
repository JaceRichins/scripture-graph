PETER: She fed him lentils when the roof was done. She did not call him son. Not then.

LUKE: Did Joazar return to the gathering?

PETER: He stood by the open door for months. First outside, then with one foot across, then at the back. His wife always sat in front.

LUKE: And later?

PETER: When another family was threatened, Joazar hid them beneath stacks of unfired clay. He stayed when the soldiers searched.

LUKE: Because you had restored him.

PETER: Because the Lord had not finished with him. I only found him roof work.

LUKE: Did you tell him about this shore?

PETER: Not until years later. He laughed at the part where my garment tangled my legs. Then he wept into his beard and denied it afterward.

LUKE: That is one man.

PETER: You want another.

LUKE: I want to know what feeding them looked like after the breakfast was gone.

PETER: Usually like an argument over money.

LUKE: Truly?

PETER: Truly. There was Tirzah. She kept the common purse for four households of widows.

LUKE: Was she a widow?

PETER: No. Young. Quick with figures. Faster with an excuse.

LUKE: What did she do?

PETER: Took silver to pay her father’s debt. When the sum was found short, she said a servant girl named Kezia had taken it.

LUKE: Was Kezia punished?

PETER: Put out of the house. She slept two nights beneath a grain stall before someone found her.

LUKE: Did Tirzah confess?

PETER: After the merchant came asking why she had paid only half.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Called everyone together. Made Tirzah stand before them. Made Kezia stand there too.

LUKE: That sounds cruel.

PETER: It was.

LUKE: You thought public wrong required public confession.

PETER: I had a rule ready. Rules are comforting when another person must bleed under them.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: Tirzah confessed. Kezia listened. Then Kezia walked out.

LUKE: You expected her to forgive.

PETER: I expected the matter to be finished because the right words had been spoken in the right room.

LUKE: Did you follow her?

PETER: Not until morning.

LUKE: Why not?

PETER: I was angry with her.

LUKE: With Kezia?

PETER: She had ruined my fine correction by leaving.

LUKE: What made you go?

PETER: Her blanket was still in the corner.

LUKE: Where did you find her?

PETER: At the dye pits. She was scraping purple mud from the stones for a crust of bread. Her hands were stained to the wrist.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: I told her I had seen Tirzah’s sin and missed mine.

LUKE: Did she return?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What did you do then?

PETER: Sat beside the pit.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Until she told me to stop frightening away the workers.

LUKE: Then?

PETER: I found her a place with Mara, who sold linen and trusted no one. They suited each other badly enough to become close.

LUKE: And Tirzah?

PETER: She sold her bracelets. Her father sold a mule. They repaid the silver.

LUKE: Was that enough for Kezia?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did you make them meet again?

PETER: Not for a year. This time I asked Kezia first.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: Tirzah came carrying the purse she had once kept. She placed it on the ground between them and could not look up. Kezia picked it up, counted the coins, and said one was clipped.

LUKE: Was it?

PETER: Yes. Tirzah began to laugh. Then Kezia did. I had not known whether to breathe.

LUKE: Did Kezia forgive her?

PETER: She let Tirzah walk home beside her.

LUKE: You keep answering with what people did.

PETER: What else have I?

LUKE: Words.

PETER: Words told me I would die before I left Him. My feet took me out of the courtyard.

LUKE: Yet words can confess.

PETER: Yes. Then the body must wake tomorrow.

LUKE: Tell me another.

PETER: You are greedy.

LUKE: I came with three rolls.

PETER: Very well. Rufus the potter.

LUKE: What had he done?

PETER: His brother Benjamin was seized in the market. Rufus was close enough to touch him. When a guard asked whether they were kin, Rufus shook his head.

LUKE: Did Benjamin see?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Was he released?

PETER: After eight months.

LUKE: And Rufus came to you then?

PETER: Before. He came the same evening, while Benjamin was still gone. Rufus wanted me to go to their mother and tell her he had tried to intervene.

LUKE: Had he?

PETER: No.

LUKE: What did you tell him?

PETER: That I would go with him while he told her the truth.

LUKE: Did he?

PETER: He vomited beside her gate.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: You asked how it went.

LUKE: Did he enter?

PETER: His mother opened the gate, saw us both, and closed it again.

LUKE: Then what?

PETER: We sat in the lane. Rufus kept saying he had only shaken his head once.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: Nothing useful. I drew lines in the dust with a potsherd.

LUKE: Did his mother let him in?

PETER: At dusk she pushed out a loaf. One loaf. I broke it. Rufus would not eat.

LUKE: What work did you give him?

PETER: None at first. I told him to stop coming to me and go each week to the place Benjamin was held.

LUKE: Was he allowed inside?

PETER: No. He stood across the road where Benjamin could see him through a high opening.

LUKE: Every week?

PETER: Every market day. Rain twice. Heat most days. Once Benjamin turned his back.

LUKE: Did Rufus remain?

PETER: Until the guards drove him away.

LUKE: When Benjamin returned, did he receive his brother?

PETER: He walked past him.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Seven months.

LUKE: Seven months in the same town?

PETER: Rufus left bread at his mother’s gate. Benjamin ate from the same oven and would not sit at the same mat.

LUKE: Could you not bring them together?

PETER: I tried after the second month. Benjamin asked whether I had ever heard a brother deny his own blood.

LUKE: What did you answer?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did you tell him about the courtyard?

PETER: All of it.

LUKE: Did that move him?

PETER: He said my failure did not make Rufus’s smaller.

LUKE: He was right.

PETER: He was.

LUKE: Then why tell him?

PETER: Because I had entered his house as a man with an answer. I needed to sit there as a man who knew the sound of one word too many.

LUKE: When did the brothers speak?

PETER: Their mother fell from a stool reaching for a jar. Broke her hip. I set it badly.

LUKE: You set it?

PETER: We had no physician with three rolls.

LUKE: What happened?

PETER: Benjamin held her shoulders. Rufus pulled the leg. She cursed both sons with great fairness.

LUKE: And afterward?

PETER: They carried her bed between them into the courtyard. Benjamin told Rufus the left side was too low.

LUKE: That was the first thing he said?

PETER: In seven months.

LUKE: Did Rufus apologize again?

PETER: Many times. Too many. Benjamin finally threw a cup at him.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: He said every apology forced him back into the market.

LUKE: So Rufus stopped.

PETER: For a while. He repaired the cup.

LUKE: Were they as they had been?

PETER: No.

LUKE: But they were brothers.

PETER: They were learning how.

LUKE: And you call that feeding sheep.

PETER: I call it Rufus and Benjamin and their mother’s crooked hip.

LUKE: You did not heal everything.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did that trouble you?

PETER: Every day.

LUKE: I thought the work given here would have made you certain.

PETER: Certain of what?

LUKE: That you were fit to do it.

PETER: Look at the shore. I could not even bring breakfast.

LUKE: Yet He chose you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Ask Him.

LUKE: I am asking the man He asked.

PETER: Then hear the answer I gave. I loved Him. I had nothing better than that.

LUKE: Was love enough?

PETER: It got me out of the boat.

LUKE: It also drew your sword.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: It made you boast.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Then how did it become care for Joazar, Tirzah, Kezia, Rufus, Benjamin?

PETER: Slowly. Badly some days. There—you wanted the years, and I nearly hid them in two words.

LUKE: Do not.

PETER: All right.

LUKE: What happened on a bad day?

PETER: I refused a man named Amos at the door because he had lied twice about money. His daughter came back after dark and set his sandals before me. The soles were worn through. He had walked two days to confess.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Took the sandals to him.

LUKE: And?

PETER: Apologized outside his lodging while he made me wait.

LUKE: Another?

PETER: I spoke harshly to a widow who would not share grain. Found out the jar held enough for one meal. I had seen a full jar from the doorway.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Brought grain. Ate the first bowl so she would not call it alms.

LUKE: Did she know that was why?

PETER: She was not a fool.

LUKE: Another?

PETER: I promised a sick boy I would return before sleep. A quarrel detained me. When I arrived, he was awake and furious. He had saved me half a fig and then ate it while I watched.

LUKE: Did you stay?

PETER: Until morning.

LUKE: Was he healed?

PETER: No. He was still ill when I left.

LUKE: You went back again?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Because of the promise.

PETER: Because his name was Jacob and he liked figs.

LUKE: Nicanor liked olives.

PETER: Did he?

LUKE: The bitter ones. I brought sweet olives once. He complained through the whole examination.

PETER: Tell his sister that.

LUKE: The olives?

PETER: Tell her something true before you tell her the worst.

LUKE: Will that make it easier?

PETER: For whom?

LUKE: Neither of us, perhaps.

PETER: Good.

LUKE: You really will not release me from going.

PETER: I cannot.

LUKE: Even after all I have told you?

PETER: Especially after.

LUKE: If she closes the door?

PETER: You know how to sit in a lane.

LUKE: And if she never opens it?

PETER: Bring bread the next week.

LUKE: You make everything sound like work.

PETER: You wanted to know what happened after this shore.

LUKE: Did you ever stop hearing the maid?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Never?

PETER: Her voice is not always near. Some years it was. Once, a girl at a gate recognized me and called my name. I could not answer her.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Walked past. Then returned and asked her name.

LUKE: What was it?

PETER: Salome.

LUKE: Did you tell her why?

PETER: No. I remembered her name.

LUKE: Were you ever again beside a fire like the one in the courtyard?

PETER: Hundreds.

LUKE: Was it difficult?

PETER: At first I found reasons to sit beyond the warmth. John saw. He never spoke of it.

LUKE: Until?

PETER: One winter an old man named Haggai had a cough. There was one place near the coals. I sat there so he could lean against me.

LUKE: And nothing happened.

PETER: His nose ran onto my sleeve.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: You asked.

LUKE: I expected—

PETER: Thunder? A voice? It was a wet sleeve. The fire was warm.

LUKE: Did you think of this breakfast?

PETER: Later, washing the cloth.

LUKE: Is that why you still come here?

PETER: I come because my nets are here.

LUKE: Only that?

PETER: No.

LUKE: How often?

PETER: When I can. At dawn if the road allows.

LUKE: Do you stand where He stood?

PETER: Sometimes. Sometimes I mend the net.

LUKE: Do you say the answers again?

PETER: I do not need to summon that morning. It has enough strength of its own.

LUKE: Yet you remember each question.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And the work after each answer.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Lambs. Sheep. Sheep.

PETER: People with names, Luke.

LUKE: Joazar.

PETER: Who still makes poor tiles when he hurries.

LUKE: Tirzah.

PETER: Who counts every coin twice.

LUKE: Kezia.

PETER: Who counts after her.

LUKE: Rufus and Benjamin.

PETER: Who argue over their mother’s cups.

LUKE: Amos. Jacob. Haggai.

PETER: There were more.

LUKE: I know.

PETER: No, I mean there are always more.

[Peter lifts a coil of wet rope.]

LUKE: Is that yours?

PETER: It will be if no one claims it.

LUKE: You steal rope?

PETER: I rescue neglected rope.

LUKE: Is that what you call it?

PETER: A fisherman would.

LUKE: May I ask about the last words John kept from that morning?

PETER: You have been waiting.

LUKE: He wrote them after the questions.

PETER: Read.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.  
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

LUKE: Were you afraid when you heard it?

PETER: I looked at my hands.

LUKE: These hands?

PETER: They were younger.

LUKE: Did you understand?

PETER: Enough to know the road was not mine to choose.

LUKE: Yet you followed.

PETER: Not perfectly.

LUKE: Must you add that every time?

PETER: Usually.

LUKE: He did not withdraw the word.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Even knowing what you had done.

PETER: He knew before I did it.

LUKE: That is the part I cannot hold.

PETER: Neither can I.

LUKE: Then what do you hold?

PETER: The next thing.

LUKE: A roof.

PETER: Or a purse.

LUKE: A broken cup.

PETER: A sick boy’s hand.

LUKE: A sister’s door.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: I may reach her before the winter rains.

PETER: You will reach her sooner if you leave one roll here.

LUKE: They are not heavy.

PETER: You slipped twice coming down.

LUKE: Once.

PETER: The first was very small.

LUKE: I will tell Nicanor’s sister.

PETER: All of it?

LUKE: I turned in the street. I waited until dawn. I let him think I had been taken.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: I do not know what she will do with that.

PETER: Nor do I.

LUKE: I had hoped you would tell me I was still useful.

PETER: You are a physician. Did your hands forget their work?

LUKE: No.

PETER: Then carry medicine.

LUKE: That is not permission.

PETER: You came seeking permission from a fisherman who jumped into deep water wearing his clothes.

LUKE: When you say it that way—

PETER: Say it exactly that way.

LUKE: Are you hungry?

PETER: There is bread in my bag.

LUKE: And fish?

PETER: You arrived too late for fish.

LUKE: I thought fishermen always had fish.

PETER: John wrote down one good morning and has harmed us ever since.

[Peter breaks the bread.]

LUKE: Which piece is mine?

PETER: The larger.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: You have farther to go.

LUKE: Thank you.

PETER: Take it before I repent of generosity.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: What?

LUKE: When Mary spoke your name that morning—did you know then that you would one day tell Mark everything?

PETER: No. I knew only that I had been called to Galilee.

LUKE: And when you saw the coals?

PETER: I wanted to run back into the lake.

LUKE: Why did you stay?

PETER: He was there.

LUKE: That was enough?

PETER: My feet stayed. The rest of me took longer.

[They eat. Water moves among the stones.]

PETER: Will you write about Nicanor?

LUKE: I do not know.

PETER: Learn his sister’s account first.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: And do not make yourself better in the telling.

LUKE: I learned that from Mark.

PETER: Mark learned it from a difficult old fisherman.

LUKE: You are not old.

PETER: Leave one roll and I will remain young.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Then at least leave the ink.

LUKE: I need it for the next man.

PETER: You have chosen him?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Far from here?

LUKE: Not far, if the road is open.

PETER: Does he know you are coming?

LUKE: He knows someone has questions.

PETER: That could describe every man you meet.

LUKE: I think he has carried his answer long enough.

PETER: Who?

LUKE: Thomas.
