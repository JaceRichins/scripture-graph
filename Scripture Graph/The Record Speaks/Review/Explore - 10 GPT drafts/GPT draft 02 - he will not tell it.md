LUKE: You knew then that you were forgiven?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Not yet?

PETER: Mercy was all around me, and I still searched for the blow.

LUKE: Simon—

PETER: You asked for the truth.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I expected Him to uncover the courtyard before the others. I expected the precise sentence from my mouth to be returned to me. I had returned it to myself a thousand times.

LUKE: But He did not ask about the denial.

PETER: No.

LUKE: He asked about love.

PETER: Read John. If I tell it before you read it, I will try to protect myself from the third question.

LUKE: John set down the questions and your answers like this.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.
He saith to him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my sheep.
He saith unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

[long pause]

PETER: The first time, I heard my old boast inside the question.

LUKE: “More than these.”

PETER: I had once measured myself against the others. That morning I could not. Love was no longer something I could prove by promising more than my brothers.

LUKE: So you appealed to His knowledge.

PETER: What else was left? My knowledge of myself had failed. My promises had failed. His knowledge had not.

LUKE: The first answer was followed by work.

PETER: I almost missed that. I was listening for judgment.

LUKE: What did you hear instead?

PETER: Trust.

LUKE: Immediately?

PETER: No. At first I thought the work might be penance. Perhaps if I served long enough, I could pay against the debt.

LUKE: Do you believe that now?

PETER: No. The work was not a price. It was a gift too large for me to recognize.

LUKE: The care of what belonged to Him.

PETER: Placed into the hands of the man who had denied belonging to Him.

LUKE: Then the second question.

PETER: By then, the others had gone silent in another way. They knew what was happening before I allowed myself to know.

LUKE: Two questions.

PETER: Two denials standing answered.

LUKE: And the third.

PETER: There are mercies that feel like wounds when they enter.

LUKE: John says you were grieved.

PETER: I was. For an instant I thought the repetition meant my answer was not believed.

LUKE: Did you fear He doubted your love?

PETER: I feared my denial had made love impossible to credit. Even in my own mouth.

LUKE: Yet you answered.

PETER: Not with a promise about what I would do. Not with a comparison. Not with my strength.

LUKE: With what He knew.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: “Thou knowest all things.”

PETER: Including the courtyard. Including the oath. Including the fear. I did not ask Him to look away from any of it so He could see my love. I placed the love inside His knowledge of all of it.

LUKE: And He gave you the work again.

PETER: For each denial, a question. For each answer, someone to tend.

LUKE: Did you understand that as forgiveness?

PETER: Not as a word spoken over the past. As a future returned.

LUKE: Say more.

PETER: Shame had told me my life would forever bend backward toward that courtyard. Every road would lead there. Every morning would prove only that I had lived one more day as the man who denied Him.

LUKE: And the work turned you forward.

PETER: There were lambs who would hunger tomorrow. Sheep who would wander tomorrow. Brothers who would be afraid tomorrow. He gave me a tomorrow that was not built from punishment.

LUKE: You were not merely permitted to remain.

PETER: No.

LUKE: You were needed.

PETER: [unsteady] Yes.

LUKE: ...

PETER: That was harder to receive than pardon.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Pardon might have left me kneeling at a distance, grateful and useless. Trust required me to stand.

LUKE: And follow again.

PETER: Do not reach the end before John does.

LUKE: Forgive me.

PETER: Read the rest.

LUKE: John kept what followed like this.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

PETER: I had promised death before.

LUKE: Vehemently.

PETER: Yes. I had imagined courage as something I would seize and display in a single hour. On the shore, I was given no such hour to boast of.

LUKE: You were given a life.

PETER: A life of following. A life in which youth would leave me. Strength would not always obey me. My own hands would not always choose what girded them.

LUKE: Were you afraid when you understood?

PETER: I understood little. Enough to know that forgiveness had not made the road safe.

LUKE: Yet the final command remained.

PETER: The same road, but no longer the same man walking it.

LUKE: Peter?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You asked me to call you Simon in the courtyard.

PETER: We are no longer in the courtyard.

LUKE: No.

PETER: We are at the shore.

LUKE: Peter, was the wound closed there?

PETER: Closed is a dangerous word.

LUKE: Healed?

PETER: Better.

LUKE: But you still cannot bear the smell.

PETER: Sometimes I can.

LUKE: Not tonight.

PETER: Not tonight.

LUKE: Then what was healed?

PETER: The lie that my worst act was the truest thing about me.

LUKE: What became truer?

PETER: His knowledge of me.

LUKE: Including your love.

PETER: Including my failure. Do not divide them. He did not restore the man I had pretended to be at supper. That man had to die.

LUKE: Whom did He restore?

PETER: The man at the fire. The frightened one. The grieving one. The one who could no longer promise that his own strength would be enough.

LUKE: Is that why you keep telling the denial?

PETER: I tell it because men hide when they think Christ will receive only the person they were before they failed.

LUKE: And you know otherwise.

PETER: I know He came to the shore where I had retreated. I know He made the fire Himself. I know He fed me before I could explain myself. I know He asked for love where I expected accusation. I know He placed His own flock before a man who had proved capable of running.

LUKE: You still call yourself capable of running.

PETER: Of course I am.

LUKE: After all these years?

PETER: Forgiveness did not make me incapable of fear. It made fear no longer my lord.

LUKE: What do you do when it returns?

PETER: The next faithful thing.

LUKE: Even if it is small?

PETER: Especially then. Take the bread. Pull the net. Remain by the fire. Tend the one before you. A man can spend his life waiting to feel brave enough for obedience.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: For a time. Then I learned that courage often arrives after the feet have begun moving.

LUKE: And when shame returns?

PETER: I let it tell the truth, but not the whole truth.

LUKE: What truth may it tell?

PETER: That I denied Him. That my love did not prevent my failure. That vigilance matters. That boasting is hollow food.

LUKE: And what may it not say?

PETER: That the courtyard has greater claim on me than the shore.

LUKE: ...

PETER: Write that carefully.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: No man should hear my story and think denial is small.

LUKE: Nor think mercy is small.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Is that why Mark kept you weeping and John kept you grieved?

PETER: Tears belong in the record. Grief belongs there. Repentance is not pretending the wound never opened.

LUKE: But it is not living forever with your hand inside it.

PETER: [softly] No.

LUKE: What did you do with all the years after the shore?

PETER: The work before me.

LUKE: That sounds simple.

PETER: It was not. Some needed feeding who bit the hand near them. Some wandered because men tasked with tending had frightened them. Some could not believe grace belonged to them. I recognized those.

LUKE: Because you had been one.

PETER: Because I remain one.

LUKE: One of the sheep?

PETER: Always before I am anything else.

LUKE: Did the work help you forgive yourself?

PETER: That question places me in a judge’s seat I do not deserve.

LUKE: Then how would you say it?

PETER: I learned to stop appealing the mercy I had been given.

LUKE: Appealing it?

PETER: Each morning I brought forward the old evidence, as though the Lord of all had overlooked some fact. I argued that His judgment of me was too kind.

LUKE: Your shame called itself justice.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And what ended the argument?

PETER: It did not end all at once. There was always another hungry person. Another frightened brother. Another morning when I could choose whether to spend myself examining the old chains or use my free hands.

LUKE: Forgiven, then, but not finished.

PETER: Never finished.

LUKE: Does that weary you?

PETER: Once it did. I wanted restoration to mean the struggle had vanished.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: Now I think unfinished is another mercy.

LUKE: How?

PETER: He gave me work because there was still a life He desired from me. The scar was not the end of my usefulness. Nor was forgiveness the end of my becoming.

LUKE: The fire remains part of you.

PETER: So does the shore.

LUKE: Which do you remember more clearly?

PETER: The courtyard is louder. The shore is deeper.

LUKE: I do not understand.

PETER: The denial returns with sounds—the servant’s voice, my oath, the bird. The shore returns beneath sound. Water against stones. Nets settling. Bread in my hands. His presence where I believed I had forfeited it.

LUKE: And the three questions?

PETER: They hurt.

LUKE: Still?

PETER: Tender things hurt when they touch a wound honestly.

LUKE: But you would not have wanted fewer.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Why not?

PETER: Because there were three denials.

LUKE: Each one met.

PETER: Not erased. Met.

LUKE: Personally.

PETER: He did not send John to repair me. He did not let time blur it. He came to the same kind of fire.

LUKE: And gave you work instead of rebuke.

PETER: He gave me Himself. The work came within that.

LUKE: ...

PETER: Are you writing?

LUKE: Not this moment.

PETER: Why?

LUKE: I am staying near.

PETER: [a small breath that is almost a laugh] You are a better physician than you pretend.

LUKE: I am a tired traveler in a cold room.

PETER: The coals will be wet through.

LUKE: I can gather wood.

PETER: Wood smoke I can bear.

LUKE: Shall I?

PETER: In a moment.

LUKE: All right.

PETER: Mary truly asked that the message be brought again?

LUKE: She did.

PETER: After all this time.

LUKE: After all this time.

PETER: She understood.

LUKE: What did she understand?

PETER: That some words must arrive more than once before a man can receive them.

LUKE: “And Peter.”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Do you receive them now?

PETER: Tonight I do.

LUKE: And tomorrow?

PETER: Tomorrow I will receive them tomorrow.

LUKE: That sounds like work.

PETER: It is.

LUKE: Are you glad you told me?

PETER: Ask me when you have gone.

LUKE: Fair.

PETER: But leave the worst sentence in.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: Leave the cursing. Leave the first crow I ignored. Leave the look.

LUKE: And the breakfast.

PETER: Especially the breakfast.

LUKE: The count of fish?

PETER: Every one.

LUKE: The grief at the third question?

PETER: Do not soften it.

LUKE: And the final command.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why that order?

PETER: Because a man may hear only the denial and believe he is ruined. He may hear only the forgiveness and believe the wound was shallow. Let him smell both fires.

LUKE: ...

PETER: You have become quiet.

LUKE: I was thinking of the beginning.

PETER: Mine?

LUKE: All of yours. A shore. Nets. Your brother beside you.

PETER: Andrew.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: You should sit with him.

LUKE: I hope to.

PETER: He remembers beginnings better than I do.

LUKE: And you remember the fire.

PETER: I remember being called forward from it.

LUKE: Is that what you would have me carry?

PETER: Carry this: I was forgiven by the One I had denied. Then I was given someone to love.

LUKE: Not finished.

PETER: Not finished.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: Yes?

LUKE: Thank you.

PETER: Put another cloak around you before gratitude makes you ill.

LUKE: I will build the wood fire first.

PETER: I will help.

LUKE: You do not have to.

PETER: I know.

[The shutter opens. Wood is gathered into the brazier.]

PETER: Where will you go when you leave here?

LUKE: I leave at first light.

PETER: To find Andrew?

LUKE: Not yet.

PETER: Then whom?

LUKE: Thomas.
