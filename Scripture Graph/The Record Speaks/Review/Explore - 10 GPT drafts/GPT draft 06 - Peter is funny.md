DIRECTOR’S NOTE: I wanted the first fire to live in Peter’s mouth for years, until the second fire answers it without pretending it never happened.  
The turn is not that Peter finally finds the right apology; it is that Christ knows the whole truth, feeds him first, and still gives him something living to carry.

LUKE: This is Luke. I have set myself to gather the accounts of Jesus the Messiah from those who saw him, followed him, failed him, and found him faithful. These conversations are rendered as faithfully as I know how. The scripture is not rendered. It is given as it was kept. My hope is that, through their witness, we may come nearer to him. This is The Record Speaks.

PETER: I know not this man.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: That is where you must begin.

LUKE: I had thought to begin with the boats.

PETER: Everyone begins with the boats. Nets on the stones. My brother beside me. A clean beginning.

LUKE: Was it not?

PETER: It was. But clean beginnings are easy to tell. You can make a life look like a road if you begin early enough. One foot, then the other. Call, answer. Leave the net. Follow.

LUKE: And that is not how it feels to you now.

PETER: No.

LUKE: How does it feel?

PETER: Like those five words.

LUKE: “I know not this man.”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You said it differently before that.

PETER: I did.

LUKE: Twice.

PETER: Twice I tried to slip away from it. The third time I made certain they believed me.

LUKE: By cursing.

PETER: By cursing. By swearing. By reaching down into myself for every hard thing a frightened fisherman knows how to sound like, and throwing it between me and him.

LUKE: You have carried the exact words.

PETER: The mouth remembers what the heart would bury.

LUKE: How many years?

PETER: All of them.

LUKE: Even after—

PETER: All of them, Luke.

LUKE: I understand.

PETER: No. But you are kind to say it.

LUKE: Then help me understand.

PETER: Have you ever spoken a thing and known, before the last sound left your teeth, that you would give anything to pull it back?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Then you know the first breath of it. Now imagine there is nowhere in the world to put the thing. No grave deep enough. No sea broad enough. You sleep, and it lies down beside you. You wake, and it has risen first.

LUKE: Yet you told Mark to keep it.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: In its worst form.

PETER: There was no better form.

LUKE: Some men would have shortened it.

PETER: Then it would have grown longer in me.

LUKE: Some would have said only, “I was afraid.”

PETER: I was afraid. That is true, and not enough.

LUKE: “I was confused.”

PETER: Also true. Still not enough.

LUKE: “I failed him.”

PETER: True. Too clean.

LUKE: Then what is enough?

PETER: “I know not this man.”

LUKE: [quietly] The wound in your own mouth.

PETER: There. Now you have it.

LUKE: Mark kept what came before as well.

PETER: He argued with me about how much came before.

LUKE: Did he?

PETER: Not about the truth. Mark does not quarrel with truth once he has it by the collar. He wondered whether every man needed to hear how loud I was before I became a coward.

LUKE: And you said?

PETER: I said the loudness belongs beside the cowardice.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because otherwise a man might think I had always known my weakness.

LUKE: You had not.

PETER: I knew everyone’s weakness but mine.

LUKE: Shall I read it?

PETER: Read it as he kept it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:29-31), read by Luke: But Peter said unto him, Although all shall be offended, yet will not I.  
And Jesus saith unto him, Verily I say unto thee, That this day, even in this night, before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice.  
But he spake the more vehemently, If I should die with thee, I will not deny thee in any wise. Likewise also said they all.

[brief silence]

PETER: “More vehemently.”

LUKE: You asked Mark to keep that?

PETER: I did not have to ask. He had heard me preach it often enough.

LUKE: You spoke of it often?

PETER: A man should not ask others to stand where he has hidden the place he fell.

LUKE: What did you believe when you said it?

PETER: Every word.

LUKE: That you would die first.

PETER: Gladly.

LUKE: And that the others might fail, but you would not.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You looked at them?

PETER: I wish I could tell you I did not.

LUKE: But you did.

PETER: I knew their tempers. Their hesitations. Which one would begin a task and tire of it. Which one would count the danger twice. Which one needed to understand before he moved. I had measured them all.

LUKE: And yourself?

PETER: I mistook wanting to be faithful for being faithful.

LUKE: There is a difference.

PETER: The whole night lies in that difference.

LUKE: You had left much.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Followed far.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Risked your life before.

PETER: More than once.

LUKE: Then your confidence did not come from nothing.

PETER: No. That made it worse. Pride seldom builds on nothing. It takes what grace has given and scratches its own name into it.

LUKE: Did you hear the warning?

PETER: I heard a mistake about me.

LUKE: A mistake.

PETER: That was how it felt. He knew all things, but surely not this. Not Simon. Not the man who had left the nets. Not the man who drew a blade when soldiers came.

LUKE: We will come to the blade.

PETER: Must we?

LUKE: Not if it takes us away from the fire.

PETER: It does not. It is part of the fire.

LUKE: Then tell me.

PETER: The blade was another boast, only made of iron.

LUKE: You meant to defend him.

PETER: I meant to become the man I had claimed to be.

LUKE: Willing to die.

PETER: Yes. Though there is an ugly secret in courage that lasts one instant.

LUKE: What secret?

PETER: It can be easier to strike once than to remain faithful for an hour.

LUKE: You had failed to remain awake.

PETER: You have a physician’s hands, Luke.

LUKE: I do.

PETER: Still hands. Patient. Mine were made for rope. If a thing is wrong, pull it. If it resists, pull harder. If danger comes, move toward it before another man does. That is a useful way to bring in a net.

LUKE: And a poor way to pray.

PETER: A poor way to do many things.

LUKE: Mark kept that warning too.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:37-38), read by Luke: And he cometh, and findeth them sleeping, and saith unto Peter, Simon, sleepest thou? couldest not thou watch one hour?  
Watch ye and pray, lest ye enter into temptation. The spirit truly is ready, but the flesh is weak.

PETER: There I am.

LUKE: In which part?

PETER: Both.

LUKE: The ready spirit and the weak flesh.

PETER: I had spent years choosing one and denying the other. Before that night, if you asked who I was, I would have pointed to the spirit. Afterward, I would have pointed only to the flesh.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: Now I know he named both.

LUKE: You were tired.

PETER: We were all tired.

LUKE: Grieved.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did sleep feel like failure then?

PETER: Not until I woke.

LUKE: And found him standing over you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: He called you Simon.

PETER: He did.

LUKE: Not Peter.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did that hurt?

PETER: Not then. Later, I turned every word into a knife.

LUKE: Even your own name.

PETER: Especially my own name. Simon was the man from before. Peter was the name I could not bear after.

LUKE: Because you did not feel like a rock.

PETER: I was not a rock. I was water thrown against one.

LUKE: Yet years later, men called you Peter.

PETER: Years later I learned a name can be a promise before it is a description.

LUKE: What happened after you woke?

PETER: Noise. Torches. Men arriving with purpose. We had none. Steel came free of its sheath. Someone cried out. There was blood. Then there was no fight to fight.

LUKE: And he was taken.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You followed.

PETER: At a distance.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because I loved him.

LUKE: And because you were afraid?

PETER: Because I loved him, and because I was afraid. Those things lived in me together. I followed because I could not leave him. I kept away because I did not want to be taken with him.

LUKE: You still believed you might do something.

PETER: I always believed there must be something I could do. Find a door. Watch where they led him. Learn which men guarded him. Wait for the moment courage returned.

LUKE: Did you think it had only left for a moment?

PETER: I thought courage was a thing I owned. Misplaced, perhaps. Never gone.

LUKE: And it was cold.

PETER: It was.

LUKE: You went to the fire.

PETER: I did.

LUKE: Do you remember deciding?

PETER: No. That is one cruelty of it. The great shame of my life began with cold hands.

LUKE: You wanted warmth.

PETER: Only warmth.

LUKE: Who was there?

PETER: Servants. Guards. People belonging to that house. People who knew how to stand there because it was their place. I tried to borrow their ease.

LUKE: By the fire.

PETER: By the fire.

LUKE: Did you see him from where you stood?

PETER: At times.

LUKE: Did he see you?

PETER: At the end.

LUKE: Before we reach the end—when the maid first looked at you, did you know she would speak?

PETER: Her face changed.

LUKE: Recognition?

PETER: Perhaps not even that. Suspicion becoming pleasure. You have seen it: a little mystery appears in a dull night, and everyone is grateful for it.

LUKE: She was not a soldier.

PETER: No.

LUKE: She did not bind you.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did she threaten you?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then what frightened you?

PETER: Being seen.

LUKE: As his disciple.

PETER: As his.

LUKE: You had wanted that before.

PETER: More than anything.

LUKE: What changed?

PETER: The cost stood close enough to touch.

LUKE: When she said you were with him, what did the first denial feel like?

PETER: Small.

LUKE: Small?

PETER: A step sideways. Not a flight. Not yet. I told myself I was avoiding foolishness. That I could serve him better if no one knew me. That a living disciple outside the bonds might still be useful.

LUKE: You gave it a purpose.

PETER: Fear is a gifted scribe. It can write a noble reason for almost anything.

LUKE: And the second denial?

PETER: Larger. I had to protect the first.

LUKE: From what?

PETER: From becoming what it was. If I admitted myself then, the first denial would stand exposed. So I buried it under another.

LUKE: Then the third.

PETER: The third was not hiding.

LUKE: No?

PETER: It was building a wall and daring heaven to pull it down.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: Read it. Do not soften your voice.

LUKE: I would not.

PETER: Do not read the last line as if the tears repaired anything.

LUKE: I understand.

PETER: They did not.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:66-72), read by Luke: And as Peter was beneath in the palace, there cometh one of the maids of the high priest:  
And when she saw Peter warming himself, she looked upon him, and said, And thou also wast with Jesus of Nazareth.  
But he denied, saying, I know not, neither understand I what thou sayest. And he went out into the porch; and the cock crew.  
And a maid saw him again, and began to say to them that stood by, This is one of them.  
And he denied it again. And a little after, they that stood by said again to Peter, Surely thou art one of them: for thou art a Galilæan, and thy speech agreeth thereto.  
But he began to curse and to swear, saying, I know not this man of whom ye speak.  
And the second time the cock crew. And Peter called to mind the word that Jesus said unto him, Before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice. And when he thought thereon, he wept.

[long silence]

PETER: My speech.

LUKE: Your speech betrayed you.

PETER: Galilee would not leave my tongue even when I tried to make him leave it.

LUKE: “Thy speech agreeth thereto.”

PETER: I had opened my mouth beside him for years. Asked questions. Made promises. Objected. Laughed. Sang the psalms. My voice had lived near his voice until strangers could hear where I belonged.

LUKE: And you used that same voice to deny it.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: “I know not this man of whom ye speak.”

PETER: Do you hear the distance in it?

LUKE: “This man.”

PETER: Not his name. I would not give them even his name.

LUKE: Were you trying not to say it?

PETER: I do not know. Perhaps my mouth refused one final betrayal my fear had asked of it. Or perhaps I was only a coward in every possible way.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: You asked me to help you understand.

LUKE: I did. I am trying to understand the truth, not assist your cruelty toward yourself.

PETER: For many years I thought they were the same labor.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: Now I know cruelty can wear sackcloth and call itself repentance.

LUKE: What is the difference?

PETER: Repentance turns toward him. Cruelty keeps a man bent over his own ruin, admiring how completely he has destroyed himself.

LUKE: You learned that quickly?

PETER: No.

LUKE: How long?

PETER: Longer than the night. Longer than the shore. In some ways, I am still learning it.

LUKE: Tell me about the sound.

PETER: The cock?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: The first cry should have awakened me.

LUKE: It did not?

PETER: I heard it. I did not awaken.

LUKE: What do you mean?

PETER: A warning can enter the ear without reaching the will. I heard a bird in the dark. Perhaps I remembered. Perhaps I pushed the memory down before it formed. I wanted one more moment inside the lie.

LUKE: And the second cry?

PETER: There was nowhere left to push it.

LUKE: Mark says you called the words to mind.

PETER: They arrived whole.

LUKE: The warning.

PETER: Yes. Not as an accusation. That would have been easier.

LUKE: Easier?

PETER: An accusation I could have fought. I had fought correction before. No—it came as truth. He had known me. I had not.

LUKE: My account keeps one other thing.

PETER: The look.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: You asked those who were there.

LUKE: As many as I could. I weighed their memories against one another. I kept only what I believed was sure.

PETER: Then read what you kept.

SCRIPTURE (Luke 22:61), read by Luke: And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.

PETER: There are looks a man escapes by closing his eyes. This was not one.

LUKE: What was in it?

PETER: Do not ask me to name all that was in his face.

LUKE: I will not press you.

PETER: I have tried. Anger would have been simple. Disappointment—I expected that. Weariness. Judgment. Even disgust. I deserved to find any of them.

LUKE: Did you?

PETER: He was suffering.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Men were lying about him. Striking him. Carrying him toward death. And still, for that moment, I was not lost in the room to him.

LUKE: He found you.

PETER: His face found mine.

LUKE: Was there love in the look?

PETER: [after a pause] That was why I could not bear it.

LUKE: Not because love excused what you had done.

PETER: No. Because it did not. He saw it. All of it. The first little lie. The second. The curse. The oath. He saw the boast that had grown them. He saw me choosing my own skin while his blood was being drawn. Love did not make him blind.

LUKE: Then what did love do?

PETER: It left me nowhere to hide.

LUKE: You went out.

PETER: I do not remember passing through the gate. I remember stone under my hand. I remember trying not to make a sound and then no longer caring who heard me.

LUKE: You wept.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Mark ends the night there for you.

PETER: That is where memory ends it too. I know other things occurred. Dawn came. Men moved through streets. But inside me, I remained beside that wall.

LUKE: Through the day that followed?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did you see the others?

PETER: Some.

LUKE: What did you say?

PETER: What is there to say?

LUKE: Did they know?

PETER: John knew enough by my face. He did not ask me for the whole of it.

LUKE: Were you grateful?

PETER: I hated him for being kind.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Kindness leaves the wound open. Accusation gives you something to push against.

LUKE: Did you blame yourself for not being there later?

PETER: I blamed myself for everything. There is an arrogance even in that.

LUKE: Explain.

PETER: I took the whole darkness and made myself its center. As if my courage would have stopped the rulers. As if one fisherman’s faithfulness would have driven nails back out of wood. Shame told me I was worthless and indispensable at the same time.

LUKE: That is a cruel knot.

PETER: It tightens when pulled.

LUKE: What could loosen it?

PETER: I did not know.

LUKE: Did you pray?

PETER: Not at first.

LUKE: Why not?

PETER: To whom would I speak?

LUKE: To God.

PETER: Every prayer I knew opened into his face.

LUKE: And you could not bear the face.

PETER: No.

LUKE: What did you do instead?

PETER: I repeated the night.

LUKE: In memory.

PETER: I corrected it.

LUKE: Corrected it?

PETER: Again and again. In one telling, I answered the maid with courage. In another, I drew the blade and did not miss what mattered. In another, I stayed awake. In another, I held my tongue at supper and never boasted.

LUKE: Did any telling comfort you?

PETER: For the length of a breath. Then the true night returned.

LUKE: You could imagine a faithful Simon.

PETER: A thousand of them.

LUKE: But you could not become one by imagining him.

PETER: No. A man can spend years trying to repent backward.

LUKE: To change what happened.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: But repentance can only move in the other direction.

PETER: I know that now.

LUKE: When did you first begin to know it?

PETER: When someone came running from the tomb.

LUKE: Mary.

PETER: Mary first among them, yes. Breathless. Terrible with hope.

LUKE: You believed her?

PETER: I ran.

LUKE: That was not my question.

PETER: It is the only answer I had then.

LUKE: John ran with you.

PETER: John ran ahead of me.

LUKE: He was younger.

PETER: John has made certain the whole world will remember that.

LUKE: He wrote it plainly.

PETER: Plainly. Twice.

LUKE: Does it trouble you?

PETER: It troubles me that he tells the truth with such a light foot.

LUKE: But you entered first.

PETER: I have never been good at standing outside a door.

LUKE: Even when you do not understand what is beyond it.

PETER: Especially then.

LUKE: You saw the linen.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And the place where the body had been.

PETER: The emptiness was not empty. That is the nearest I can come.

LUKE: Did you know?

PETER: I saw.

LUKE: Did you know?

PETER: Not yet.

LUKE: What was the distance between them?

PETER: A lifetime. Three steps. I cannot say.

LUKE: Did hope come at once?

PETER: Hope came like pain returning to a numb hand. I wanted it and feared it.

LUKE: Why fear it?

PETER: If he lived, then I must stand before him.

LUKE: Was that worse than believing he was dead?

PETER: Nothing was worse than that. But shame is not reasonable. It can dread the very mercy it needs.

LUKE: I have carried something from Mary.

PETER: From Mary Magdalene?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: You have spoken with her.

LUKE: In the garden where she found the stone moved. She entrusted me with what she remembered—the terror, the running, the message that would not leave her.

PETER: What message?

LUKE: Mark kept it in one sentence.

PETER: Luke—

LUKE: I did not tell you earlier because I wanted to understand whether you had heard your name in it.

PETER: Read it.

LUKE: Mark kept it like this.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[long silence]

PETER: And Peter.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: She remembered that?

LUKE: Exactly that.

PETER: After all these years.

LUKE: Especially that.

PETER: “His disciples and Peter.”

LUKE: How did it sound when you first heard it?

PETER: Like being separated from them.

LUKE: That was your first thought?

PETER: Of course. His disciples—and then the man outside the number.

LUKE: And your second thought?

PETER: My name had been carried out of the tomb.

LUKE: Not forgotten.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Not hidden inside the others.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Named.

PETER: [voice unsteady] He knew which man would believe mercy was meant for everyone but him.

LUKE: So the message crossed the distance.

PETER: It began to.

LUKE: “And Peter.”

PETER: Two words can take years to enter a man.

LUKE: Yet you went to Galilee.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Because you were summoned?

PETER: Because he was there. Because there was nowhere else left in the world.

LUKE: Did you know forgiveness had been offered?

PETER: I knew I had not been cast away.

LUKE: Is that the same thing?

PETER: Not to a man ashamed.

LUKE: What is the difference?

PETER: A condemned man may be called into the room to hear his sentence. I knew the door was open. I did not yet know what waited beyond it.

LUKE: You had seen him alive before the morning by the sea.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: John calls the shore the third showing to the gathered disciples.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Then why did the wound remain?

PETER: Because joy is not the same as mending. We saw him alive. Do you understand? Alive. There are no words broad enough for those days. Grief broke open and light came through everything.

LUKE: But the denial remained.

PETER: I loved him. I rejoiced. I worshiped. And still there was a courtyard between us.

LUKE: Did he place it there?

PETER: No.

LUKE: You did.

PETER: I had built it carefully.

LUKE: Did you speak privately before the shore?

PETER: There are meetings I will not open here.

LUKE: I will honor that.

PETER: But this much I can say: I knew he had not rejected me. Still, I did not know what remained of me.

LUKE: What remained of Peter.

PETER: Or whether Peter had ever been more than his kindness toward Simon.

LUKE: So you went fishing.

PETER: So I went fishing.

LUKE: John made it sound sudden.

PETER: It was sudden.

LUKE: He kept your words.

PETER: Of course he did.

LUKE: Shall I?

PETER: Read them. They are some of my wiser words. Brief. Practical. Entirely unsuccessful.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:3), read by Luke: Simon Peter saith unto them, I go a fishing. They say unto him, We also go with thee. They went forth, and entered into a ship immediately; and that night they caught nothing.

PETER: John takes a fisherman’s whole night and closes it with three words.

LUKE: “They caught nothing.”

PETER: Merciless.

LUKE: Was it your boat?

PETER: One I knew well enough.

LUKE: Your old work.

PETER: My old hands, at least. Rope does not ask what you did in a courtyard.

LUKE: Is that why you went?

PETER: Partly.

LUKE: What was the other part?

PETER: Hunger. Restlessness. The need to be useful.

LUKE: Did you mean to return to fishing for good?

PETER: That is what people ask.

LUKE: And?

PETER: I do not know.

LUKE: You knew that night better than anyone.

PETER: I knew what I told myself. That men must eat. That boats rot when neglected. That nets do not mend themselves. All true.

LUKE: And beneath that?

PETER: Beneath that I wanted one thing I could still do.

LUKE: But you caught nothing.

PETER: Not a scale.

LUKE: How did the others receive that?

PETER: Badly, but with restraint. At first.

LUKE: At first?

PETER: Fishermen grow theological near dawn. Every empty net becomes evidence of someone’s hidden sin.

LUKE: Whose sin did they suspect?

PETER: Mine was too easy, so we blamed the moon.

LUKE: You can laugh at it now.

PETER: I laughed then. A poor laugh, but it served.

LUKE: Did they follow you onto the boat because they trusted you?

PETER: Or because none of them knew what else to do.

LUKE: You said, “I go.”

PETER: They said, “We also.”

LUKE: You were still leading.

PETER: That frightened me more than the empty net.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Men had begun moving when I moved. What if I led them back into everything we had left? What if my failure became their direction?

LUKE: Did you tell them to remain ashore?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Why not?

PETER: I was glad not to be alone.

LUKE: What did you speak of through the night?

PETER: The net. The wind. Where the fish were not. Ordinary things spoken too carefully.

LUKE: No one mentioned the courtyard.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Or the tomb.

PETER: Not for a while.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: When wonder is too large, men sometimes discuss rope.

LUKE: And at dawn?

PETER: A figure on shore. A voice over water. Then the net came alive.

LUKE: When did you know?

PETER: John knew first.

LUKE: Again.

PETER: You enjoy that.

LUKE: A little.

PETER: He saw the sign of him before he saw him clearly. John’s heart often arrived while the rest of us were still counting.

LUKE: And you?

PETER: I heard John. Then I was in the water.

LUKE: You did not wait for the boat.

PETER: The boat was too slow.

LUKE: You were not young enough to swim in your clothes.

PETER: I was not wise enough to remember that.

LUKE: What drew you so urgently?

PETER: It was him.

LUKE: Only that?

PETER: What else is there?

LUKE: Fear might have kept you in the boat.

PETER: Fear had kept me by one fire. It would not keep me from another.

LUKE: Did you see the coals before you reached land?

PETER: I smelled them.

LUKE: The smoke.

PETER: Charcoal has a close smell. Wood smoke wanders. Charcoal stays low. It clings to cloth. Hair. Hands.

LUKE: The same kind of fire.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: As the courtyard.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did you stop in the water?

PETER: My feet found the bottom. I saw him. Then I saw the fire.

LUKE: And the years between vanished.

PETER: There were no years between. That is what memory can do. Cold water around my legs. Dawn before me. Night inside my mouth.

LUKE: Could it have been chance?

PETER: You may call it chance if you wish.

LUKE: I do not.

PETER: Nor do I.

LUKE: What did you expect beside that fire?

PETER: The question.

LUKE: Immediately?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: But it did not come immediately.

PETER: No.

LUKE: What came first?

PETER: Breakfast.

LUKE: [softly] Breakfast.

PETER: That nearly undid me.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Rebuke I had prepared for. I had composed answers through sleepless nights. Confessions. Promises I would not make again. I was ready to be examined, if there can be readiness for such a thing.

LUKE: But there was bread.

PETER: Bread. Fish. Warmth.

LUKE: He had prepared it before you came ashore.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You were wet.

PETER: Dripping like a fool.

LUKE: Hungry.

PETER: Empty from the night.

LUKE: Ashamed.

PETER: More than I can say.

LUKE: And he fed you.

PETER: Before I repaired anything. Before I proved anything. Before I could force out one of my careful speeches.

LUKE: John set down the morning in detail.

PETER: John understood that the fire mattered.

LUKE: Did you tell him?

PETER: We spoke of it later.

LUKE: More than once?

PETER: Many times. He remembered the fire in the courtyard too.

LUKE: And kept the same kind of fire in both places.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: May I read the shore?

PETER: Read slowly.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:9-14), read by Luke: As soon then as they were come to land, they saw a fire of coals there, and fish laid thereon, and bread.  
Jesus saith unto them, Bring of the fish which ye have now caught.  
Simon Peter went up, and drew the net to land full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and three: and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken.  
Jesus saith unto them, Come and dine. And none of the disciples durst ask him, Who art thou? knowing that it was the Lord.  
Jesus then cometh, and taketh bread, and giveth them, and fish likewise.  
This is now the third time that Jesus shewed himself to his disciples, after that he was risen from the dead.

PETER: “Come and dine.”

LUKE: Those are his words.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You did not have to earn the meal.

PETER: We had caught the fish.

LUKE: After he showed you where.

PETER: You are becoming troublesome.

LUKE: I have traveled with Paul.

PETER: Then you cannot help it.

LUKE: There were already fish on the coals.

PETER: There were.

LUKE: He did not need your catch.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Yet he asked you to bring it.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: I have wondered.

LUKE: What do you think?

PETER: That what he gives and what we bring need not be enemies. He had made the meal. Still, there was a place in it for the work of our hands.

LUKE: Even your hands.

PETER: Even mine.

LUKE: The hands that had warmed themselves while you denied him.

PETER: The same hands dragged the net toward his fire.

LUKE: A hundred and fifty and three fish.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Who counted?

PETER: Fishermen count.

LUKE: Every fish?

PETER: A physician counts bones. A shepherd counts sheep. A fisherman counts fish.

LUKE: Even in the presence of a man risen from the dead?

PETER: Especially then. The mind needs somewhere small to stand.

LUKE: Did the number mean something?

PETER: It meant we had a great many fish.

LUKE: Nothing more?

PETER: Luke, if John wished to tell you what every fish meant, he would have done so. He had ink.

LUKE: Fair enough.

PETER: Though he likely watched me count and saved the number because he knew it would irritate careful men forever.

LUKE: You were laughing there?

PETER: A little. Not yet freely.

LUKE: Why did none of you ask who he was?

PETER: Because we knew.

LUKE: Yet John says you did not dare ask.

PETER: We knew, and the knowing was too great for ordinary speech. His body bore truth we could see. His presence carried what sight alone could not settle. We were learning to recognize him without holding him inside the old shape of our expectations.

LUKE: He was familiar.

PETER: Completely.

LUKE: And changed.

PETER: Completely.

LUKE: You ate in silence?

PETER: Mostly. Bread breaks loudly when no one speaks.

LUKE: Could you taste it?

PETER: Not at first.

LUKE: You watched the fire.

PETER: I watched his hands.

LUKE: What did you feel?

PETER: Grief. Joy. Terror. Hunger. Peace. Then grief again. Feelings do not wait politely for one another.

LUKE: Were you waiting for him to look at you as he had in the courtyard?

PETER: He had looked at me already.

LUKE: Was it the same?

PETER: He was the same.

LUKE: And you?

PETER: I no longer trusted the man who looked back.

LUKE: When the meal ended, did the others move away?

PETER: Far enough. Near enough.

LUKE: They knew?

PETER: Every man at that fire knew there was unfinished business.

LUKE: Did you wish them gone?

PETER: I had denied him before strangers. Perhaps it was mercy that I answered him before brothers.

LUKE: He asked whether you loved him.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Not whether you were sorry.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Not why you had failed.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Not what punishment you deserved.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Peter, may I read all three together?

PETER: You must.

LUKE: John set it down.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.  
He saith to him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my sheep.  
He saith unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

[long silence]

LUKE: When did you understand there would be three?

PETER: At the second.

LUKE: Not the first?

PETER: The first cut elsewhere.

LUKE: “More than these.”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You had once claimed more than the others.

PETER: Though all failed, I would not. I had measured my love against theirs and declared myself tallest.

LUKE: How did you hear the question on the shore?

PETER: As the last day of boasting.

LUKE: Your answer does not compare you with them.

PETER: I had finished with comparisons.

LUKE: “Thou knowest that I love thee.”

PETER: That was all I had.

LUKE: Was it difficult to say?

PETER: Love was the one thing the denial had not made false.

LUKE: Some might say the denial proved otherwise.

PETER: I said otherwise to myself for years. But love can be real and still be weaker than fear. That is not a defense of the failure. It is the sorrow of it.

LUKE: You loved him in the courtyard.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And denied him.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Both are true.

PETER: Both.

LUKE: Then how could you trust your answer beside the sea?

PETER: I did not trust my courage. I did not trust my record. I did not say, “Look what I have done.” I placed the truth where it had always belonged.

LUKE: In his knowledge.

PETER: “Thou knowest.”

LUKE: The look in the courtyard had told you he knew your failure.

PETER: The questions by the sea let me trust that he also knew my love.

LUKE: Without pretending the failure had not happened.

PETER: Without pretending.

LUKE: The second question left out the comparison.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Only you and him.

PETER: And the fire.

LUKE: Did the second answer come more easily?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Because you knew a third was coming.

PETER: I began to know.

LUKE: You could have wished him to stop at two.

PETER: I did.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because mercy was touching the wound, and I mistook the pain for harm.

LUKE: Then came the third.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: John says you were grieved.

PETER: John was gentle.

LUKE: What word would you use?

PETER: Broken open.

LUKE: Because the number was complete.

PETER: Three denials. Three questions.

LUKE: One for each.

PETER: Not payment.

LUKE: No?

PETER: I did not purchase forgiveness with three answers.

LUKE: Then what happened?

PETER: He brought me back to the place where I had fallen, and this time he did not leave me alone there.

LUKE: At the same kind of fire.

PETER: At the same smell. The same low smoke. The same face before me. But no servants waiting to expose me. No darkness to hide in. Morning.

LUKE: Why not ask once?

PETER: Once would have comforted me.

LUKE: And three?

PETER: Three reached me.

LUKE: It hurt.

PETER: Deeply.

LUKE: Was the hurt necessary?

PETER: Love does not fear the necessary wound.

LUKE: What did the third question uncover?

PETER: That I still believed my worst moment had more authority over me than he did.

LUKE: And when you said, “Thou knowest all things”?

PETER: I gave up my last hiding place.

LUKE: Which was?

PETER: The thought that if he truly knew all things, he could not want me.

LUKE: But he did know.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The boast.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The sleep.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The blade.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The distance.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The warmth you chose.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: The oaths.

PETER: All of it.

LUKE: And your love.

PETER: And my love.

LUKE: “Thou knowest all things.”

PETER: There is terror in those words until there is rest.

LUKE: When did the rest come?

PETER: When he gave me work.

LUKE: “Feed my sheep.”

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You expected a rebuke.

PETER: I received a flock.

LUKE: You expected to be dismissed.

PETER: I was entrusted.

LUKE: Why did that matter more than being told to go in peace?

PETER: Because forgiveness can seem like a door quietly closed behind your back. You are released, but no longer wanted in the house.

LUKE: And this was not release from him.

PETER: It was return to him.

LUKE: With responsibility.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: For lives he loved.

PETER: That is the part that still humbles me. He did not give me stones to carry. Stones would have been easier. He gave me lambs.

LUKE: Living things can be lost.

PETER: Hurt. Neglected. Frightened away. Yes.

LUKE: Did you feel worthy?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Do you now?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then how do you carry the work?

PETER: Worthiness was never the strength of my hands. His knowledge was.

LUKE: “Thou knowest.”

PETER: He knew what I was. He knew what grace could make of it. I was done arguing both points.

LUKE: Did the others receive you differently after that morning?

PETER: Some did.

LUKE: Did anyone distrust you?

PETER: I distrusted myself enough for all of them.

LUKE: That is not what I asked.

PETER: Yes. Some watched me. Why should they not? A restored man is not owed the immediate confidence of everyone he has wounded.

LUKE: Did that anger you?

PETER: At times.

LUKE: What did you do?

PETER: Fed them.

LUKE: Even those who doubted you.

PETER: Especially them.

LUKE: Is that how the wound changed?

PETER: Slowly.

LUKE: Not all at once at the fire?

PETER: Something was finished there. Something began there.

LUKE: What was finished?

PETER: The lie that my failure was stronger than his call.

LUKE: And what began?

PETER: Learning to live as a forgiven man.

LUKE: Is that difficult?

PETER: Very.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Shame is accustomed to being consulted. You make a decision, and it clears its throat. You speak boldly, and it reminds you of the courtyard. Someone trusts you, and it asks whether they would if they knew everything.

LUKE: What do you answer?

PETER: He knows everything.

LUKE: And still gave you the sheep.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did the memory ever leave?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then what is forgiveness, if not forgetting?

PETER: The memory no longer gets to name the future.

LUKE: You remember without being imprisoned.

PETER: Some days.

LUKE: Only some?

PETER: Luke, I am telling you the truth, not carving a statue.

LUKE: Forgive me.

PETER: There. You see how quickly the word comes.

LUKE: And how slowly it enters.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: When the cock cried on later mornings, what happened?

PETER: At first, I woke with my heart in the courtyard.

LUKE: For how long?

PETER: Years.

LUKE: Every morning?

PETER: Not every morning. Enough.

LUKE: And later?

PETER: Later I began to remember the shore too.

LUKE: The fire answered the fire.

PETER: The smell held both nights. That was strange mercy. The thing that once carried me into shame began carrying me through it.

LUKE: Did you avoid charcoal before Galilee?

PETER: I tried.

LUKE: Afterward?

PETER: I learned to sit beside it.

LUKE: Deliberately?

PETER: Sometimes. A man should not worship his healing, but neither should he flee the place where it found him.

LUKE: Did you tell the story there?

PETER: Often.

LUKE: To whom?

PETER: Frightened men. Proud men. Men certain they would never fail. Men certain failure had ended them.

LUKE: Which were easier to speak with?

PETER: The failures.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Their hands were already empty.

LUKE: And the proud?

PETER: I knew how tightly they held the net.

LUKE: What did you tell them?

PETER: The record.

LUKE: All of it?

PETER: The boast. The warning. The sleep. The denial. The tears. The empty tomb. My name in the message. The boat. The useless night. The fire. The meal. The questions. The sheep.

LUKE: Did you tell them what to feel?

PETER: No. A man who hears that account will find his own courtyard soon enough.

LUKE: And his own fire?

PETER: I pray so.

LUKE: You said something was finished at the shore, and something began. John did not end with the sheep.

PETER: No.

LUKE: There was another word for you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: About age.

PETER: And hands.

LUKE: Your hands again.

PETER: My hands had always gone where I commanded. Oar. Net. Blade. Fire. I thought freedom meant fastening my own garment and choosing my own road.

LUKE: But your road would narrow.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did the words frighten you?

PETER: I had once promised death before I understood faithfulness. On the shore, I was not permitted the comfort of pretending faithfulness would be painless.

LUKE: Shall I read it?

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.  
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

[brief silence]

LUKE: “Follow me.”

PETER: The road began again.

LUKE: With the same words as before?

PETER: Not the same man hearing them.

LUKE: What had changed?

PETER: The first time, I thought following proved what kind of man I was.

LUKE: And the second?

PETER: Following would show what kind of Lord he is.

LUKE: You knew the road would cost your life.

PETER: Every road costs a life in the end.

LUKE: But this was particular.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You were forgiven, and still asked to suffer.

PETER: Forgiveness is not exemption.

LUKE: Nor retirement.

PETER: No.

LUKE: The work remained.

PETER: The work became possible.

LUKE: Because you were no longer trying to prove you would never fail?

PETER: A shepherd watching his own dignity will lose the sheep.

LUKE: Then what does he watch?

PETER: The flock. The weather. The dark beyond the fire. He listens for what is small and afraid.

LUKE: Because he has been small and afraid.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Would you undo the courtyard if you could?

PETER: [long pause] Yes.

LUKE: Even knowing what came by the sea?

PETER: Sin is not made good because mercy is greater. I would not choose the wound.

LUKE: But you would not surrender what you learned there.

PETER: I would surrender every lesson if surrendering it could spare him my denial. That is not offered to me.

LUKE: No.

PETER: What is offered is this: the wound need not be wasted.

LUKE: It became part of how you fed them.

PETER: When a man told me he had gone too far, I did not have to imagine the country.

LUKE: You had lived there.

PETER: I knew the cold. I knew the little fire men gather around when they are afraid. I knew how a tongue can become a locked gate. I knew the sound of morning arriving too late.

LUKE: And you knew another fire.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Bread waiting.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: A question asked until it reached the truth.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Work waiting beyond forgiveness.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Peter, when you say now, “I know him,” do you trust yourself?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then whom do you trust?

PETER: The one who knew me first.

LUKE: Even in the courtyard.

PETER: Especially there.

LUKE: You began this conversation with the denial.

PETER: It still belongs at the beginning.

LUKE: But not at the end.

PETER: No.

LUKE: What belongs at the end?

PETER: There is no end yet.

LUKE: You are an old man.

PETER: Older than the fool who jumped from the boat. Not much wiser near water.

LUKE: Your hands shake.

PETER: They do.

LUKE: Other men help fasten your garment.

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: Does that frighten you?

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: What do you do with the fear?

PETER: I do not boast that it is gone.

LUKE: No.

PETER: I bring it with me.

LUKE: Where?

PETER: Along the road.

LUKE: Following.

PETER: Following.

[long silence]

LUKE: I think I understand why Mark kept the worst of it.

PETER: Tell me.

LUKE: Because the man who said, “I know not this man,” did not disappear from the record.

PETER: No.

LUKE: He became the man entrusted to say who Jesus is.

PETER: Be careful, Luke.

LUKE: With what?

PETER: Do not make me the answer.

LUKE: I did not mean to.

PETER: I am not what heals the story.

LUKE: The one who returned to you is.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: At the fire.

PETER: At the fire.

LUKE: He did not erase Simon.

PETER: No.

LUKE: He called him by name.

PETER: Again and again.

LUKE: And Peter?

PETER: Peter had work to do.

LUKE: Forgiven.

PETER: Forgiven.

LUKE: Not finished.

PETER: [softly] Not finished.

[brief silence]

LUKE: I should leave before the light is gone.

PETER: You have traveled in worse light.

LUKE: I have traveled with worse company.

PETER: Paul again?

LUKE: I said nothing.

PETER: Your face has become a written account.

LUKE: I am going to find Thomas next.

PETER: Thomas.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Do not hurry him.

LUKE: I will try not to.

PETER: Sit long enough for the silence to become honest.

LUKE: As I have here?

PETER: Longer. Thomas built careful doors.

LUKE: And if he will not open them?

PETER: Remain outside awhile.

LUKE: What should I ask him first?

PETER: Ask what he could not believe.

LUKE: And then?

PETER: Ask what made him stay with the others anyway.

LUKE: I will.

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: Yes?

PETER: When you write the fire—

LUKE: Yes?

PETER: Do not forget the bread.
