Director’s note: I built this around what the body remembers: a bitter mouth, cold hands, a rooster in the dark, and charcoal smoke returning where mercy could reach the wound.  
The restoration comes slowly, because Peter has spent years believing that sorrow is the same thing as repentance—and Christ gives him a life to live, not merely a past to regret.

LUKE: This is Luke. I am gathering the stories of the Messiah from those who walked with Him, ate with Him, feared for Him, failed Him, and found Him faithful. These conversations are dramatized. The scripture is not. My hope is that, through their witness, we come closer to Him. This is The Record Speaks.

PETER: The worst of it is still in my mouth.

LUKE: The denial?

PETER: No. The taste of it.

LUKE: I do not understand.

PETER: You asked what I remember first.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Not the maid. Not the soldiers. Not even the rooster. My mouth.

LUKE: What was in it?

PETER: A lie. Then another. Then an oath made filthy enough that they would believe me.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: Simon.

LUKE: You prefer Simon?

PETER: For this, yes.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Peter was the name He gave me. Simon was the man warming his hands.

LUKE: Were you cold?

PETER: Bitterly.

LUKE: I had not thought of the cold.

PETER: You were not there.

LUKE: No.

PETER: Forgive me.

LUKE: There is nothing to forgive.

PETER: There is always something to forgive. That is not despair, Luke. I know the difference now.

LUKE: Tell me the difference.

PETER: Despair says the wound is all you are. Repentance says the wound is real, and you must bring it into the light.

LUKE: Is that why you agreed to speak with me?

PETER: I agreed because you came a long way and would not stop asking.

LUKE: That also.

PETER: And because men make saints out of those whose knees they have never seen shaking.

LUKE: You do not want them to forget yours did.

PETER: I do not want them to mistake mercy for merit.

LUKE: Then begin with the cold.

PETER: We had been awake, then asleep, then awake again in terror. The night had gone wrong so quickly that my thoughts could not keep their footing. There had been the garden. Torches between the trees. Steel drawn. Men running. I ran too.

LUKE: Yet you followed afterward.

PETER: At a distance.

LUKE: You followed.

PETER: At a distance is how a coward follows. Near enough to see what becomes of the man he loves. Far enough to say he does not know Him.

LUKE: You had tried to defend Him in the garden.

PETER: I had tried to defend the man I thought He ought to be.

LUKE: And when He would not be that man?

PETER: I did not know how to stand beside Him.

LUKE: Because He did not resist?

PETER: Because He did not need what I knew how to give.

LUKE: A sword.

PETER: Noise. Strength. Promises. My whole life was full of things I could give quickly.

LUKE: And He asked you for what?

PETER: To stay awake.

LUKE: Mark kept both things—the promise and the sleeping.

PETER: He kept them because I told him to.

LUKE: You told him to keep the worst of it?

PETER: If he had softened it, I would have made him put it back.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because it happened.

LUKE: There are many things that happened which no one wrote.

PETER: This one explains too much to leave out.

LUKE: What does it explain?

PETER: Why I do not trust the loudest thing in me merely because it is sincere.

LUKE: Your promise was sincere.

PETER: Every word of it.

LUKE: Then I will read it as Mark received it.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:29-31), read by Luke: But Peter said unto him, Although all shall be offended, yet will not I.  
And Jesus saith unto him, Verily I say unto thee, That this day, even in this night, before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice.  
But he spake the more vehemently, If I should die with thee, I will not deny thee in any wise. Likewise also said they all.

[brief silence]

LUKE: You still hear how vehemently you said it.

PETER: My whole chest was behind it. I thought force was proof.

LUKE: Proof of love?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Was it not love?

PETER: It was love without self-knowledge. A fire with no hearth around it. It leapt high and lit nothing. I meant to die with Him. I could see myself doing it.

LUKE: You had imagined the moment.

PETER: A clean moment. That was the deceit. One blow. One brave answer. Blood, perhaps. Men would know where I stood. I thought faithfulness would arrive like a trumpet.

LUKE: Instead?

PETER: It came as an hour in a garden when my eyes were heavy.

LUKE: Mark kept that too.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:37-38), read by Luke: And he cometh, and findeth them sleeping, and saith unto Peter, Simon, sleepest thou? couldest not thou watch one hour?  
Watch ye and pray, lest ye enter into temptation. The spirit truly is ready, but the flesh is weak.

PETER: There. That is the whole man.

LUKE: Ready spirit. Weak flesh.

PETER: I used to hear the second part as an insult.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: As a diagnosis.

LUKE: A physician’s answer.

PETER: You would say that.

LUKE: I would.

PETER: He knew before I knew. That is one of the difficult mercies.

LUKE: To be known?

PETER: To be known without being abandoned.

LUKE: You did not understand that yet.

PETER: I thought being known meant being measured. I thought being measured meant being found wanting. And I thought being found wanting meant being put away.

LUKE: So when He foretold your denial—

PETER: I heard an accusation and answered with a defense.

LUKE: He had not accused you.

PETER: No.

LUKE: He warned you.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And when He found you sleeping—

PETER: I was ashamed.

LUKE: Before the courtyard.

PETER: Shame had already begun its work. People speak of the three denials as though I walked into the courtyard clean and came out ruined. I carried small fractures in with me. I had argued when I should have listened. Slept when I should have prayed. Struck when I should have trusted. Fled when I should have stayed. By the time the maid looked at me, I was holding myself together with both hands.

LUKE: And warming them over the fire.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: What kind of fire?

PETER: Charcoal.

LUKE: You are certain?

PETER: You do not forget that smell.

LUKE: Describe it.

PETER: It does not leap like brushwood. It glows. The heat stays low and close. You bend toward it. The smoke clings to wool, to hair, to the skin of your hands. Fish cooked over it tastes of the whole fire, not only the flame.

LUKE: There was fish in the courtyard?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then why fish?

PETER: Because there was fish the next time.

LUKE: We will come to that.

PETER: I have been coming to it for years.

LUKE: Stay in the courtyard a little longer.

PETER: You say that as though it is a place.

LUKE: Is it not?

PETER: Some nights.

LUKE: I am sorry.

PETER: Do not be sorry yet. Read what Mark wrote.

LUKE: All of it?

PETER: Do not spare me in my own telling.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 14:66-72), read by Luke: And as Peter was beneath in the palace, there cometh one of the maids of the high priest:  
And when she saw Peter warming himself, she looked upon him, and said, And thou also wast with Jesus of Nazareth.  
But he denied, saying, I know not, neither understand I what thou sayest. And he went out into the porch; and the cock crew.  
And a maid saw him again, and began to say to them that stood by, This is one of them.  
And he denied it again. And a little after, they that stood by said again to Peter, Surely thou art one of them: for thou art a Galilæan, and thy speech agreeth thereto.  
But he began to curse and to swear, saying, I know not this man of whom ye speak.  
And the second time the cock crew. And Peter called to mind the word that Jesus said unto him, Before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice. And when he thought thereon, he wept.

[long silence]

PETER: Mark did not write the look.

LUKE: No.

PETER: You did.

LUKE: I gathered it from those who remembered.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (Luke 22:61), read by Luke: And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.

PETER: That look.

LUKE: Could He see you clearly from where He stood?

PETER: Clearly enough.

LUKE: Were there men between you?

PETER: There were always men between us that night.

LUKE: What did you see in His face?

PETER: I have spent years refusing to answer that badly.

LUKE: Then answer it slowly.

PETER: I saw pain.

LUKE: Because you had denied Him?

PETER: Yes. But not surprise.

LUKE: He already knew.

PETER: He had known at supper. He had known in the garden. Perhaps He had known when He first found me among the nets. I cannot tell you how far back that knowledge reached. Only that there was no discovery in His face.

LUKE: What else?

PETER: He was being held. Struck. Questioned. Men had made a spectacle of Him, and still, when He looked at me, I did not feel that He was occupied with Himself.

LUKE: You felt seen.

PETER: Entirely.

LUKE: Did you see anger?

PETER: I wanted to.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Anger would have been easier. Anger gives a man something to push against. I could have defended myself inwardly. I could have said I was afraid, that the courtyard was full of danger, that any one of them might have dragged me in beside Him. I could have told myself I had remained nearer than the others.

LUKE: But His face gave you no defense.

PETER: His face gave me the truth.

LUKE: Which was?

PETER: That He knew me, and I had said I did not know Him.

LUKE: You went out.

PETER: I do not remember deciding to move. The walls would not hold still. The night air struck my mouth. I could hear the rooster again—or perhaps I only hear it again now. There are memories that happen once, and memories that continue happening because you give them somewhere to live.

LUKE: Where did this one live?

PETER: In every dawn for a long while.

LUKE: Every rooster?

PETER: At first.

LUKE: You lived near many.

PETER: Too many.

LUKE: Did you begin to hate the sound?

PETER: I hated that they went on with their small ordinary lives. Dawn came. Men stirred. Nets were mended. Bread was broken. Fires were lit. The world had not stopped to accuse me.

LUKE: But you had.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You said you wept.

PETER: I shook until my teeth struck together. I tried to be silent and could not. It was not noble grief. There was no prayer in it that I remember. Only the terrible collapse of the man I had believed myself to be.

LUKE: Where did you go?

PETER: Away.

LUKE: Alone?

PETER: Luke.

LUKE: Forgive me.

PETER: Some things belong to the telling. Some belong to those hours alone.

LUKE: I understand.

PETER: Do you?

LUKE: No. But I can leave them where they are.

PETER: Thank you.

[brief silence]

LUKE: You said the lie remained in your mouth.

PETER: It did.

LUKE: Even after you wept?

PETER: Tears wash the face. They do not undo speech.

LUKE: What did you want to do?

PETER: Return to the courtyard and answer again.

LUKE: You could not.

PETER: That is the torment of repentance when it first begins. A man imagines that, if he could only return to the precise place, he would choose differently and close the wound himself.

LUKE: Did you imagine it often?

PETER: I would see the maid turn toward me. This time I would not shrink. This time my voice would be steady. This time I would say who He was and who I was with Him. Then the vision would end, because there was no returning.

LUKE: Did you believe you would see Him again?

PETER: Not as He had been.

LUKE: Before the crucifixion, you mean.

PETER: We heard what was happening. We learned where they had taken Him. There are failures you think are still waiting for repair, and then a door closes that no hand can open.

LUKE: You thought His death sealed your last words to Him.

PETER: Not words to Him. That was worse. They were words about Him, spoken where He could hear.

LUKE: Your last public confession before His death was a denial.

PETER: Three denials.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Do not make it one thing too quickly. The first was surprise. The second was choice. By the third I had learned what the lie could do for me, and I made it stronger.

LUKE: The oath.

PETER: I reached for language that would separate me from Him. I fouled my own mouth to make the distance convincing.

LUKE: Three times.

PETER: Three times.

LUKE: You count them separately.

PETER: Would you not?

LUKE: I do not know.

PETER: I counted everything then. Three denials. Two rooster cries in Mark’s telling. One look. One dead Lord. Numbers gave edges to what had no edge.

LUKE: And the years afterward?

PETER: At first I counted those too.

LUKE: Since that night?

PETER: Since the morning after. Then the next. I thought sorrow was a debt, and if I paid it faithfully enough I might become honest again.

LUKE: How does a man pay sorrow?

PETER: He keeps himself from comfort. He distrusts laughter. He takes every good thing with lowered eyes. If peace enters the room, he asks whether it has mistaken the house.

LUKE: Did you do that?

PETER: For longer than I should have.

LUKE: Even after you saw Him risen?

PETER: Seeing Him alive changed the world. It did not immediately change every room inside me.

LUKE: That is difficult to understand.

PETER: Is it?

LUKE: I would think joy swallowed everything.

PETER: Joy is not always a great mouth swallowing grief. Sometimes it is a lamp placed beside it. You see both more clearly.

LUKE: When you first learned the tomb was open—

PETER: I ran.

LUKE: John was faster.

PETER: John enjoys keeping that detail.

LUKE: You went in first.

PETER: I could not remain at the door.

LUKE: What were you seeking?

PETER: Him. An answer. Punishment. Mercy. I did not know. I only knew that, if there was any place He might be, I had to go there.

LUKE: And He was not there.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Yet you saw the linen.

PETER: We saw enough to destroy certainty, not enough to build understanding.

LUKE: Later you saw Him.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And still the courtyard remained.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did He condemn you?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Did He turn you away?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Then why did the wound remain?

PETER: Because silence can be mercy, but a guilty man may hear it as delay.

LUKE: You thought the rebuke was coming.

PETER: I thought it should.

LUKE: Did you avoid being alone with Him?

PETER: I stayed near the others.

LUKE: That is not quite an answer.

PETER: It is the answer I can give.

LUKE: You had once insisted that, even if all the others failed, you would not.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Afterward, were the others a shelter?

PETER: They had seen me run. They knew enough. Shame prefers strangers; among friends it must breathe ordinary air. James would hand me bread. John would ask me to help with something. No one made me perform my sorrow.

LUKE: Did they ask about the courtyard?

PETER: Eventually.

LUKE: And you told them?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: All of it?

PETER: More than once. A confession is rarely finished the first time a man speaks it. At first he tells the shape that allows him to survive the telling. Later, if grace holds him, he tells the truth without arranging the light.

LUKE: Is that what Mark received?

PETER: The truth without arranged light.

LUKE: He kept you beneath the palace. He kept the maid looking closely. He kept your Galilæan speech betraying you.

PETER: My own tongue gave me away before I used it to hide.

LUKE: And he kept the cursing.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You could have let that disappear.

PETER: It would not have disappeared from me.

LUKE: But no one else needed to know.

PETER: I used to believe confession meant forcing others to look at my shame. Now I think it means refusing to conceal the place where Christ was merciful.

LUKE: We have not reached His mercy yet.

PETER: We have. You simply do not recognize the shore from this side.

LUKE: Then take me there.

PETER: Not yet.

LUKE: What remains?

PETER: The night before the shore.

LUKE: John wrote one sentence about it.

PETER: John can place a whole darkness in one sentence.

LUKE: He wrote that you announced you were going fishing.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Because boats do not ask questions.

LUKE: Is that the whole answer?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Because you knew how to fish?

PETER: I had once thought I did.

LUKE: You had left the nets years before.

PETER: Hands remember what a man leaves.

LUKE: Did you mean to leave everything else?

PETER: I did not say so.

LUKE: Did you think it?

PETER: I thought many things I would not let become words.

LUKE: Such as?

PETER: That perhaps love had survived in me, but usefulness had not. That perhaps a man could be received back into company and still be unfit for trust. That perhaps I could remain near the story without being given any part in its future.

LUKE: You expected to be forgiven but set aside.

PETER: I did not dare use the word forgiven.

LUKE: What word did you use?

PETER: Tolerated.

LUKE: By Him?

PETER: By everyone.

LUKE: That is a cold word.

PETER: It was a cold night.

LUKE: So you returned to the boat.

PETER: I knew the weight of a net. The pull of wet rope. The sound of water against wood. I knew what failure meant there. An empty boat was only an empty boat.

LUKE: John wrote that the others went with you.

PETER: They did.

LUKE: Did that comfort you?

PETER: I was grateful they did not make me go alone.

LUKE: John set it down this way.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:3), read by Luke: Simon Peter saith unto them, I go a fishing. They say unto him, We also go with thee. They went forth, and entered into a ship immediately; and that night they caught nothing.

PETER: Nothing.

LUKE: You sound almost fond of it.

PETER: Now I am.

LUKE: Not then?

PETER: Then it felt like mockery. I could not be the man I had promised to be, and apparently I could no longer catch fish.

LUKE: Did anyone speak of Him?

PETER: Not much.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: We were learning that His absence could fill a boat more completely than another man’s presence.

LUKE: And near morning?

PETER: The shore appeared in pieces. A dark line. Stones. A figure.

LUKE: You did not know Him at once.

PETER: No.

LUKE: What changed?

PETER: The net filled.

LUKE: And John knew.

PETER: John often arrived at knowledge before I did.

LUKE: Yet you reached the shore first.

PETER: When John said who it was, there was no world between the boat and land.

LUKE: You went into the water.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Were you afraid?

PETER: Entirely.

LUKE: Yet you went toward Him.

PETER: Entirely.

LUKE: What did you expect when your feet found the bottom?

PETER: I did not plan beyond reaching Him.

LUKE: You have done that more than once.

PETER: Yes. The trouble comes when I remember the wind, or the men, or myself. But for a few steps—

LUKE: There was only Him.

PETER: Only Him.

LUKE: And then you smelled the fire.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Charcoal.

PETER: Charcoal.

LUKE: The same kind.

PETER: There are only two charcoal fires John mentions. One beneath the palace. One on the shore.

LUKE: Did you notice at once?

PETER: Before I saw what was cooking.

LUKE: What did it do to you?

PETER: The body is quicker than thought. My mouth dried. My hands went cold though I had just pulled myself through the water. For one breath I was back beneath the palace, watching sparks settle on stone.

LUKE: Did you stop?

PETER: I may have. I do not know. Memory can halt a man inside while his feet continue.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Waiting?

PETER: He had made breakfast.

LUKE: That detail moves you.

PETER: You have not spent years believing that the first thing waiting for you at the fire will be judgment, only to find bread.

LUKE: Who lit it?

PETER: John does not say.

LUKE: But the fire was ready before you reached land.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And the fish?

PETER: Already laid there.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: You tell me. You are the one who asks questions as if every answer has another room behind it.

PETER: Hm.

PETER: No.

PETER: Not quite so.

PETER: Yet made room for it.

LUKE: You pulled it.

PETER: The others helped.

LUKE: John names you.

PETER: John saw my need to have both hands on something.

PETER: No.

PETER: We counted.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: Fishermen count fish.

LUKE: Scholars have made many meanings of that number.

PETER: Scholars have cleaner hands than fishermen.

LUKE: You think it means only the number of fish?

PETER: I think there were one hundred and fifty and three fish. If the Lord has put further meaning in it, He will not be troubled by my poor arithmetic.

LUKE: John’s account is careful. Let me read the breakfast.

PETER: Read it slowly.

LUKE: What did the bread taste like?

PETER: Bread.

LUKE: Only bread?

PETER: What answer do you expect?

LUKE: I do not know.

PETER: It tasted warm. A little smoke. My hands trembled when I took it.

LUKE: Did He look at you?

PETER: He looked at all of us.

LUKE: That is not quite an answer either.

PETER: Yes. He looked at me.

LUKE: Like the courtyard?

PETER: It was the same face.

LUKE: But different?

PETER: I was different.

LUKE: Because you had denied Him?

PETER: Because He had died and was standing beside a fire giving me bread.

LUKE: Did you speak during the meal?

PETER: I do not remember words.

LUKE: You ate in silence?

PETER: There are silences that conceal, and silences that uncover. This one uncovered everything.

LUKE: Did the others know what the charcoal meant to you?

PETER: John did. Perhaps all of them. No one moved away.

LUKE: You must have wondered why He chose that fire.

PETER: At first I did not dare believe He had chosen it for me.

LUKE: You thought it accidental?

PETER: I wanted it to be.

LUKE: Because if it was chosen—

PETER: Then we were going somewhere.

LUKE: Back to the courtyard.

PETER: I feared that.

LUKE: Did you think He meant to expose you before the others?

PETER: He did not need to. They knew.

LUKE: Then what did you fear?

PETER: That He would ask whether my promise had been true.

LUKE: It had been true when you made it.

PETER: A promise is not proved by how a man feels while speaking it.

LUKE: You expected Him to speak of the denial.

PETER: Or the boasting. Or the sleeping. Or the sword. There was no shortage of material.

LUKE: Instead He asked about love.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Why was that harder?

PETER: Because I loved Him.

LUKE: You say that plainly.

PETER: I loved Him in the courtyard.

LUKE: While denying Him?

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Some would say the denial proved otherwise.

PETER: Then they have not yet discovered what fear can do inside a man without killing love.

LUKE: You do not excuse it.

PETER: No. Love did not make the denial less evil. The denial did not make the love unreal. Both were in me. That was what I could not bear.

LUKE: You wanted to be one thing.

PETER: A faithful man or a faithless man. Condemned or clean. Give me a name and I will carry it. But He knew I was a man who loved Him and denied Him. He did not allow either truth to swallow the other.

LUKE: The first question included the others.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Once you had claimed that, though all others failed, you would not.

PETER: I remembered.

LUKE: Did He say more than He needed?

PETER: Never.

LUKE: John set the exchange down with very little around it.

PETER: There was sea behind Him. The coals had gone red beneath white ash. One of the fish split where the skin had charred. I remember that because I did not want to look up.

LUKE: But you did.

PETER: He was speaking to me.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: Simon.

LUKE: Yes. Simon. Will you read it?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Why not?

PETER: My voice still tries to change when it reaches my answers. You read what John wrote. I will not improve myself in the hearing.

LUKE: All three?

PETER: All three.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:15-17), read by Luke: So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.

PETER: I was grieved.

LUKE: John says so.

PETER: He was kind to say only that.

LUKE: What happened inside you?

PETER: The first question opened the door.

LUKE: To the courtyard?

PETER: I smelled it. The smoke. The damp stone. I heard voices gathering around what I had said.

LUKE: And your answer?

PETER: Smaller than the boast had been.

LUKE: You did not compare yourself with the others.

PETER: I could not.

LUKE: You placed the truth in His knowledge.

PETER: That was all I had left. Not the strength of my resolve. Not the greatness of my courage. He knew whether I loved Him.

LUKE: Was that a relief?

PETER: It was terror and relief together. If He knew all things, then I could hide nothing. If He knew all things, then the courtyard was not the only thing He knew.

LUKE: He knew the love as well as the denial.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Then He gave you work.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: After the first answer.

PETER: I scarcely heard it.

LUKE: Why?

PETER: I was waiting for judgment to finish.

LUKE: And He asked again.

PETER: The second question took away the comparison. There was only Him and me.

LUKE: And again you placed the answer in His knowledge.

PETER: My own knowledge of myself had failed. His had not.

LUKE: Then again, the sheep.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did you understand what He was doing?

PETER: Not until the third.

LUKE: Why did the third grieve you?

PETER: You know why.

LUKE: I want to hear you say it.

PETER: Because I had denied Him three times.

LUKE: Did you know at once that the questions answered the denials?

PETER: At the third I knew.

LUKE: What did it feel like?

PETER: Like a blade entering the precise place that needed opening.

LUKE: Painful.

PETER: Merciful.

LUKE: Both?

PETER: Luke, a wound closed over filth will keep its poison. He did not soothe the skin. He opened it.

LUKE: At the same kind of fire.

PETER: At the same kind of fire.

LUKE: I have wondered whether the fire was part of the question.

PETER: Explain.

LUKE: You had wanted to return to the courtyard and answer again. You said repentance tormented you because you could not go back.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: But He brought the courtyard to you.

PETER: No soldiers. No maid. No danger.

LUKE: The danger was past.

PETER: Not all of it. Pride was still dangerous. Shame was dangerous.

LUKE: Yet He made the place again—charcoal, morning cold, your hands near the heat—and allowed you to answer three times.

PETER: He did more than allow it.

LUKE: He asked for it.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: You could not return to the night, so He returned its shape to you.

PETER: I have thought of the questions. The number. The work He gave me. I have thought of the fire every morning I smelled one. But I had not—

LUKE: Not what?

PETER: I had not thought of Him bringing the courtyard to me.

LUKE: Perhaps I am wrong.

PETER: No. No, I do not think you are.

[long pause]

PETER: The first fire was made by men who wanted warmth while they condemned Him.

LUKE: And the second?

PETER: He made it a place of bread.

LUKE: The first gathered accusers.

PETER: The second gathered brothers.

LUKE: At the first, you warmed yourself.

PETER: At the second, He fed us.

LUKE: At the first, you said you did not know Him.

PETER: At the second, I could only appeal to how completely He knew me.

LUKE: He did not erase the place.

PETER: He entered it.

LUKE: Is that forgiveness?

PETER: That is how He forgave me.

LUKE: You had been waiting for Him to say what punishment the failure deserved.

PETER: He gave me sheep.

LUKE: People.

PETER: His people.

LUKE: Trust.

PETER: Terrible trust.

LUKE: Terrible?

PETER: What would you call it? I had failed to stay awake with one suffering friend, and He placed His lambs in my care.

LUKE: Did you feel worthy?

PETER: Worthiness was no longer the question.

LUKE: What was?

PETER: Whether I loved Him.

LUKE: And if you did?

PETER: Feed them.

LUKE: Not punish yourself.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Not spend the rest of your life proving you would have done differently.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Feed them.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Is that what ended your long repentance?

PETER: It did not end.

LUKE: But the wound was closed.

PETER: Closed is not erased. He did not give me forgetfulness.

LUKE: What did He give?

PETER: A memory I could live from instead of die inside.

LUKE: The charcoal no longer belongs only to the courtyard.

PETER: No.

LUKE: Nor the rooster only to the denial?

PETER: Roosters are still difficult.

LUKE: Even now?

PETER: Some mornings more than others.

LUKE: What do you do when you hear one?

PETER: I rise.

LUKE: That is all?

PETER: There is usually someone hungry.

LUKE: You mean the work.

PETER: I mean a person. Repentance becomes dangerous when it remains a man alone with his own soul. He can polish his grief until it looks holy.

LUKE: And feeding another interrupts that.

PETER: A hungry lamb does not need the beauty of my remorse. It needs food.

LUKE: You speak as if sorrow can become vanity.

PETER: Mine did.

LUKE: How?

PETER: I began to value the pain because it seemed like the only gift I could still offer Him. I could not undo the denial, but I could make certain I never enjoyed forgetting it.

LUKE: You thought continued misery showed continued love.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: And the work corrected you?

PETER: His mercy corrected me. The work kept the correction alive.

LUKE: How?

PETER: When another man fails, I know the sound his mind makes afterward. It sounds like a door being barred from the inside. If I had forgotten the courtyard, I might stand outside and command him to open. Because I remember, I sit near the door.

LUKE: Until he opens it?

PETER: Until he can hear that someone has remained.

LUKE: Is that feeding?

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: What else?

PETER: Telling the truth without making despair sound righteous. Guarding the weak without despising weakness. Refusing to use another man’s failure to make my faithfulness appear larger.

LUKE: You learned that at the first question.

PETER: More than these.

LUKE: You no longer answer for anyone else.

PETER: I have enough trouble answering for Simon.

LUKE: Yet He called you Simon there, not Peter.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did that wound you?

PETER: At first.

LUKE: You thought He had withdrawn the name?

PETER: For a breath.

LUKE: Had He?

PETER: No. He called the whole man forward. Not the rock I wished to be. The son his father had named. The man before all his claims. Simon.

LUKE: Why did you ask me to call you Simon when we began?

PETER: Because I wanted the wound to speak before the office did.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: Now you may call me Peter.

LUKE: Peter.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: When did you first believe you were not merely tolerated?

PETER: At the third question.

LUKE: Not the first?

PETER: The first could have been an examination.

LUKE: The second?

PETER: A confirmation.

LUKE: And the third?

PETER: The third told me He had counted with me.

LUKE: The denials.

PETER: He knew there were three rooms in the wound. He entered each one.

LUKE: And left work in each?

PETER: Lambs. Sheep. Sheep.

LUKE: Why not simply tell you that you were forgiven?

PETER: Would I have believed it?

LUKE: If He had said it?

PETER: I would have believed He meant it. I might not have understood what it meant.

LUKE: The work showed you.

PETER: Forgiveness was not permission to remain on the shore rehearsing my failure. He returned me to love’s labor.

LUKE: Forgiven was not finished.

PETER: No.

LUKE: It was a beginning.

PETER: A beginning with scars.

LUKE: Yours?

PETER: His first.

LUKE: You saw them.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: At breakfast?

PETER: I saw His hands.

LUKE: What did that do to your shame?

PETER: It made shame feel too small for the world He had opened.

LUKE: Too small?

PETER: Not false. Small. I had been staring at the wound my sin had made in me. His hands showed me the wound love had accepted for us all.

LUKE: Did you touch them?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Why not?

PETER: I did not need to.

LUKE: Thomas did.

PETER: Thomas needed what Thomas needed.

LUKE: You do not fault him?

PETER: No.

LUKE: Some do.

PETER: Some have never tried to carry faith through grief.

LUKE: You and Thomas spoke of that morning?

PETER: We have spoken of many things.

LUKE: I want to find him.

PETER: You should.

LUKE: Not yet. There is something I have held back.

PETER: From me?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: Why?

LUKE: I was asked to wait until you had spoken of the shore.

PETER: By whom?

LUKE: Mary Magdalene.

[long silence]

PETER: You saw Mary?

LUKE: Before I came here.

PETER: How is she?

LUKE: Steady. She still carries the garden morning as though dawn once entered the world through a place in her chest.

PETER: That sounds like Mary.

LUKE: She knew I would speak with you. She wanted me to remember something when you reached the fire.

PETER: What?

LUKE: Not her own words. The message given that morning—the one Mark kept.

PETER: I know it.

LUKE: Let me read it.

PETER: Luke—

LUKE: You told me not to spare you in your own telling. Do not spare yourself in this.

PETER: Read it.

SCRIPTURE (Mark 16:7), read by Luke: But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

[long silence]

PETER: And Peter.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I have heard it many times.

LUKE: Mary knew that.

PETER: Then why send it again?

LUKE: She thought you might hear it differently after speaking of the fire.

PETER: And Peter.

LUKE: You were still named.

PETER: Apart from the others.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: I used to hear that as proof I had fallen outside them.

LUKE: And now?

PETER: He knew I would hear the message and think it belonged to everyone but me.

LUKE: So your name was placed where you could not escape it.

PETER: Before the shore.

LUKE: Before the questions.

PETER: Before the bread.

LUKE: You were summoned before you were restored.

PETER: No.

LUKE: No?

PETER: Perhaps the summons was already restoration beginning.

LUKE: Mary hoped you would see that.

PETER: She carried this all the way to you?

LUKE: She carried it out of the garden. I only carried it here.

PETER: And Peter.

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: A man can live years inside two words.

LUKE: Which two?

PETER: And Peter.

LUKE: Not the words from the courtyard?

PETER: Those too. But they do not stand alone now.

LUKE: What stands against them?

PETER: My name in a message from an empty tomb. Bread beside a charcoal fire. Three questions. Sheep I did not deserve and was commanded to love.

LUKE: Does the rooster still accuse you?

PETER: Sometimes.

LUKE: What answers it?

PETER: Morning.

LUKE: Only morning?

PETER: Morning came to both fires. At the first, I thought dawn exposed me. At the second, dawn found Him waiting.

LUKE: With bread.

PETER: With bread.

LUKE: Did you ever tell Mary what her message meant to you?

PETER: Not well enough.

LUKE: I can carry something back.

PETER: No words.

LUKE: What, then?

PETER: Sit with her. Let her speak of the garden. Do not hurry her to the joy. There was grief there first.

LUKE: I will remember.

PETER: She knows what it is to seek Him while unable to imagine the form in which He will come.

LUKE: You knew Him on the shore before the questions.

PETER: I knew it was the Lord. I did not yet know what the Lord would do with me.

LUKE: That distance matters.

PETER: Between sight and knowing?

LUKE: Yes.

PETER: A man may see an empty tomb and carry death home in his thoughts. He may see the risen Lord and still wait for rejection. The eyes arrive before the heart sometimes.

LUKE: What helps the heart arrive?

PETER: He does.

LUKE: Through what?

PETER: Whatever He chooses. Linen laid aside. A name sent through a woman. A net suddenly heavy. Bread. A wound reopened carefully enough to heal.

LUKE: Sheep.

PETER: Especially sheep.

LUKE: Why especially?

PETER: Because they do not let a shepherd make a shrine of yesterday. They wander today. Hunger today. Bleed today.

LUKE: And he must follow.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: John says the conversation did not end with the sheep.

PETER: No.

LUKE: After restoring the work, He spoke of its cost.

PETER: He did.

LUKE: Were you afraid?

PETER: I listened.

LUKE: You had once declared yourself ready to die.

PETER: This time I made no declaration.

LUKE: Why not?

PETER: I had learned that tomorrow’s courage is not stored in today’s mouth.

LUKE: Yet He told you what would come.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Did it feel like a judgment?

PETER: No.

LUKE: A warning?

PETER: More like a road shown from its beginning.

LUKE: A hard road.

PETER: Harder than the clean death I had imagined for myself that night.

LUKE: Because it required years before it?

PETER: Years of being led. Years of tending what belonged to Him. Years in which old strength would not be enough.

LUKE: You once thought faithfulness would be proved in one brave moment.

PETER: He gave me a long obedience instead.

LUKE: And at its end?

PETER: I would not choose the road.

LUKE: Yet you would be carried upon it.

PETER: The young man girds himself. He decides where to go. He trusts his legs. An older man learns that following is consent before it is control.

LUKE: You are speaking differently than the man at supper.

PETER: He was certain of himself.

LUKE: And you?

PETER: I am certain of Him.

LUKE: That is enough?

PETER: It must be.

LUKE: Shall I read the end?

PETER: Yes.

SCRIPTURE (John 21:18-19), read by Luke: Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.  
This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

[long silence]

LUKE: The first call and the last word there are the same road.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Follow.

PETER: Yes.

LUKE: Yet the man who hears it on the shore is not the man who first left the nets.

PETER: No.

LUKE: What changed?

PETER: The young Simon thought following meant never failing.

LUKE: And Peter?

PETER: Peter knows following means returning when Christ calls his name.

LUKE: Even from the courtyard.

PETER: Especially from the courtyard.

LUKE: Do you wish the denial had never happened?

PETER: Every day.

LUKE: Even knowing what came at the second fire?

PETER: Mercy does not make evil necessary. I will not call the wound good because the healer was good.

LUKE: Then what do you call it?

PETER: Redeemed.

LUKE: There is a difference.

PETER: All the difference.

LUKE: If you could speak to the Simon warming his hands—

PETER: I cannot.

LUKE: I know. But if—

PETER: No. That is the old temptation. To return and repair it myself.

LUKE: You would say nothing?

PETER: The Simon at that fire would not believe the man I am now. He would think I had come to rebuke him.

LUKE: What would convince him?

PETER: Bread.

LUKE: Bread?

PETER: Sit beside him. Make no defense of what he is about to do. Do not pretend fear is small. Wait until morning. Then bring him to the other fire.

LUKE: You would not tell him he is forgiven?

PETER: I would tell him Christ is not finished with him.

LUKE: Is that different?

PETER: It is forgiveness with a road beneath it.

LUKE: And if he says he is unworthy?

PETER: He is.

LUKE: Peter—

PETER: So am I. That is why worthiness cannot be the road.

LUKE: Love, then.

PETER: His first.

LUKE: And yours?

PETER: Answered badly. Answered fearfully. Answered again.

LUKE: Three times.

PETER: As many as He asks.

LUKE: Does He still ask?

PETER: Every hungry face asks. Every frightened brother. Every woman who thinks grief has made her foolish. Every man certain his failure has removed his name from the message.

LUKE: And what do you give them?

PETER: What I was given.

LUKE: Bread?

PETER: When I have it.

LUKE: Truth?

PETER: Without arranged light.

LUKE: And hope?

PETER: I show them the two fires.

LUKE: Both?

PETER: If I show only the courtyard, they will think sin is the strongest thing in the story. If I show only the shore, they may think mercy has no knowledge of what happened in the dark.

LUKE: So you keep Mark’s worst detail.

PETER: And John’s breakfast.

LUKE: The curse and the bread.

PETER: My denial and His question.

LUKE: Your grief and His work.

PETER: Both fires. One Lord.

[brief silence]

LUKE: It is growing late.

PETER: There will be light enough for the road.

LUKE: I should let you rest.

PETER: I will not sleep yet.

LUKE: The old habit?

PETER: Perhaps I am still learning to watch one hour.

LUKE: What will you do?

PETER: There are nets to mend before morning.

LUKE: I thought you left them.

PETER: These belong to a neighbor.

LUKE: Of course.

PETER: Where will you go?

LUKE: To find Thomas.

PETER: Be patient with him.

LUKE: I will try.

PETER: Do more than try.

LUKE: Then tell me how.

PETER: Let him put his hand where his faith broke. Do not be afraid of the wound.

LUKE: Because Christ was not?

PETER: Because Christ came back for us there.

LUKE: At the wound.

PETER: At the fire.

LUKE: Good night, Peter.

PETER: Good night, Luke.

[softly]

PETER: And Peter.
