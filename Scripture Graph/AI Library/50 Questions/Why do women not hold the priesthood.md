# Why do women not hold the priesthood?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
In the present Church, only worthy male members are ordained to offices in the [[Aaronic Priesthood]] and [[Melchizedek Priesthood]]; Official Declaration 2 itself preserves this boundary even as it revokes the earlier racial restriction, extending ordination to 'all worthy male members... without regard for race or color.' No book of scripture — Old Testament, Book of Mormon, or Doctrine and Covenants — states an explicit theological rationale for the sex-based boundary itself. The ancient pattern was explicitly patrilineal (Aaron and his sons in [[Exodus 29]]; Levites organized under Aaron's household in [[Numbers 3]]), and [[D&C 84]]'s priesthood-transmission narrative names only men while claiming the priesthood 'continues... in all generations' (D&C 84:6-17); neither passage argues that women are categorically excluded from religious authority as such, and women appear materially engaged at the tabernacle ([[Exodus 38]]:8) and among the righteous in vision ('glorious Mother Eve' and her faithful daughters, [[D&C 138]]:39). Current official teaching distinguishes priesthood *office* from priesthood *power and authority*: D&C 84:20-21 ties access to 'the power of godliness' to priesthood ordinances, and modern apostles teach that women exercise delegated priesthood authority in callings and officiate in some [[Temples|temple]] ordinances under priesthood keys, without holding priesthood office. The New Testament data on women's teaching and authority is itself contested — restrictive instructions (1 Timothy 2:11-15; 1 Corinthians 14:34-35) sit alongside women who pray, prophesy, teach, and labor as Paul's coworkers (1 Corinthians 11:5; Romans 16; Titus 2:3-5) — and the Pastoral Epistles' authorship is itself disputed among scholars. The honest summary: present practice is settled, and its function (delegated authority, ordinances, service) is taught with real textual anchoring; the deeper reason ordination itself is reserved to men rests on trust that living prophets currently convey the Lord's design — a claim of continuing revelation, not something the canonical text argues for on its own.
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- **Official Declaration 2's own wording** restricts the 1978 revelation to 'all worthy male members,' showing the sex-based ordination boundary was left untouched even while the racial one was removed by revelation (Official Declaration 2).
- **Patrilineal Aaronic ordination.** Exodus 29 ordains Aaron and his sons specifically; Numbers 3 organizes Levite service by male household lineage — the ancient pattern is explicitly, not merely assumed, hereditary and male.
- **D&C 84's all-male priesthood chain** (Moses, Jethro, Caleb, Elihu, Jeremy, Gad, Esaias, Abraham, Melchizedek, Noah, Enoch, Abel, Adam) is presented as continuing 'in all generations,' giving the modern priesthood an explicitly male genealogical self-description (D&C 84:6-17).
- **The office/power distinction is textually grounded, not invented for the modern debate.** D&C 84:20-21 ties access to 'the power of godliness' to priesthood ordinances rather than to holding priesthood office as such — a hook multiple modern apostles (Oaks, Andersen, Stevenson) build on to explain women's relationship to priesthood power without ordination.
- **Women are not scripturally invisible in priesthood-adjacent space.** Exodus 38:8 has women assembling at the tabernacle door and materially contributing to its furnishings; D&C 138 places Eve and 'many faithful daughters' among the righteous. Absence of ordination is not absence from sacred service.
- **The New Testament restriction texts are internally contested, which is itself evidence.** The same letter that commands women's silence (1 Corinthians 14:34-35) presupposes women praying and prophesying (1 Corinthians 11:5) — a real textual tension, not a resolved doctrine, and it shows the New Testament basis for restricting women's religious authority is more fragile than a surface reading suggests.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- **No disclosed reason:** Saying that the Lord designated the practice identifies the claimed source of the rule, not the reason for it. None of the cited canonical passages records a revelation saying why women cannot be ordained to these offices.

- **Delegation does not remove the asymmetry:** Women may exercise authority under keys, but they generally do not hold the offices or presiding keys from which institutional authorization is delegated. A critic can therefore reasonably argue that expanded terminology does not erase unequal access to formal governance.

- **The scriptural record is mixed:** Male priestly lineages and restrictive Pauline passages coexist with women praying, prophesying, teaching, leading house-church activity, and laboring as prominent gospel coworkers. The record does not yield a simple universal-silence model.

- **Equality language creates pressure:** [[Galatians 3]] 28 directly concerns equal covenant inclusion rather than office, but critics argue that a permanent sex-based allocation of governing office requires a stronger justification than the text supplies.

- **Historical inheritance is a live explanation:** Biblical and Christian institutions developed in strongly patriarchal societies. The possibility that the modern restriction partly inherited those structures cannot be excluded merely by asserting revelation; divine origin and cultural continuity make overlapping predictions about a male-only institution.

- **The 1978 precedent warrants caution:** Earlier defenders supplied theological explanations for the racial restriction that the Church later disavowed or ceased teaching. Official Declaration 2 does not prove that the sex restriction will change, but [[Why were Black members restricted from priesthood and temple until 1978]] shows the danger of inventing explanations where no revealed reason has been given.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
- A thoughtful believer may accept prophetic authorization without claiming access to God's unrevealed rationale. That is a faith position, not a historical demonstration, and it is more defensible when stated with that limitation.

- Equal worth need not logically entail identical assignments. [[Galatians 3]] concerns belonging to Christ and inheritance; using it alone to abolish every differentiated role goes beyond its immediate argument. Conversely, differentiated roles do not by themselves prove divine appointment.

- Current teaching offers a substantive, though incomplete, correction to the premise: women are not ordained to priesthood offices, but they are not outside priesthood power, authority, ordinances, or service. See [[Priesthood Authority in the Family and the Church (Elder Dallin H. Oaks, October 2005)]] and [[Women’s Service and Leadership in the Church (Gospel Topics)]].

- Women's temple officiation and the history surveyed in [[Joseph Smith’s Teachings about Priesthood, Temples, and Women (Essay)]] show that Latter-day Saint practice cannot accurately be reduced to the proposition that only men ever act with priesthood authority.

- Male patterns in scripture may be taken by believers as precedents consistent with the present order. Honesty requires acknowledging that precedent is not the same as an explicit, transhistorical prohibition.

- Believers may also affirm continuing [[Revelation]]: current leaders administer the order they understand God to have authorized, while leaving future direction to God. This stance neither predicts change nor declares change impossible.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Frameworks — how this can fit, and how it cannot

<!-- SG:BEGIN frameworks -->
**What the text requires.** The cited texts require recognition of authorized ministry and ordinances (Articles of Faith 1:5; [[D&C 84]] 19–30), a male Israelite priestly pattern (Exodus 28:1; 29:9), real New Testament restrictions ([[1 Timothy 2]] 11–15; [[1 Corinthians 14]] 34–35), and real female prayer, prophecy, teaching, and gospel labor ([[1 Corinthians 11]] 5; Acts 18:26; Romans 16; Philippians 4:2–3). They do not expressly institute the modern sex rule, give its reason, or declare whether it can ever change. Both the claim that scripture plainly requires permanent male-only ordination and the claim that Galatians plainly abolishes every sex-differentiated office attach conclusions to the text that it does not itself state.

- **Present revelatory designation — plausible within Latter-day Saint premises; not independently supported as revelation.** [[Power in the Priesthood (Elder Neil L. Andersen, October 2013)]] presents the official answer that the Lord designated men for ordination. Acceptance depends on trust in prophetic authority. The sources establish that leaders teach the claim, not that an outside method has verified its divine origin or disclosed its rationale.

- **Office/authority distinction — independently supported as current doctrine; incomplete as a causal explanation.** [[The Powers of the Priesthood (President Dallin H. Oaks, April 2018)]] rejects treating ordained men as synonymous with priesthood. [[The Keys and Authority of the Priesthood (Elder Dallin H. Oaks, April 2014)]] and [[Women’s Service and Leadership in the Church (Gospel Topics)]] say women exercise priesthood authority under keys. This substantially corrects the question's premise but does not explain why ordination and most keys remain male-only.

- **Permanent complementary offices — possible and partly text-supported; detailed rationales often ad hoc.** Male priestly precedent, Pauline headship, and 1 Timothy's creation appeal can support enduring differentiation. Claims that motherhood is the exact female counterpart to priesthood office, or that particular innate traits necessitate the arrangement, are not established by the cited texts and become ad hoc when presented as revealed explanations.

- **Context-limited New Testament restrictions — plausible and textually supported; application to Restoration offices remains indirect.** Women pray and prophesy in [[1 Corinthians 11]], Priscilla helps instruct Apollos in Acts 18:26, and women are prominent coworkers in Romans 16 and Philippians 4. These facts support readings that limit silence and teaching prohibitions to particular settings or activities. They do not themselves prove female ordination to modern priesthood office.

- **Development through continuing revelation — possible, not predicted.** The texts do not expressly call the sex rule eternal, and Official Declaration 2 shows that priesthood eligibility policy can change through claimed revelation. The racial and sex restrictions have distinct histories and wording, so treating 1978 as proof of a future change would be a false equivalence.

- **Patriarchal institutional inheritance — plausible critical model; underdetermined by the supplied evidence.** Male-dominated biblical and historical settings offer a natural account of institutional continuity. The present pattern is consistent with cultural inheritance, but consistency is not proof: a genuinely revealed differentiated order could appear in the same historical form.

- **Full functional equality inferred from baptismal equality — possible normative model; not compelled by Galatians alone.** [[Galatians 3]] securely establishes equal covenant inclusion and inheritance. Moving from that premise to identical eligibility for every office requires an additional theological argument.

**Considered and set aside.** The claim that women simply already hold priesthood was set aside because official sources distinguish holding an ordained office from exercising delegated authority. An absolute-silence interpretation was set aside because it inadequately accounts for [[1 Corinthians 11]] 5. Female spiritual inferiority or unworthiness was set aside because it lacks support in the cited sources and conflicts with equal covenant inclusion and women's reception of saving ordinances. Motherhood as a complete explanatory counterpart was set aside because not every woman is a mother, fatherhood is not excluded from ordained men, and no cited revelation makes that equivalence.
<!-- SG:END frameworks -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
**Established:** Current Church practice reserves ordination to Aaronic and Melchizedek Priesthood offices to men. Official teaching distinguishes office, keys, delegated authority, divine power, receipt of ordinances, and officiation; women participate in several of these categories. Scripture contains both male institutional patterns and substantial female ministry.

**Open:** The cited canon does not disclose why the modern ordination boundary exists, whether every Pauline restriction was local or universal, whether Junia was called an apostle, how closely early Christian ministries map onto Restoration offices, or whether the present rule is immutable. Historical and revelatory explanations remain underdetermined by the public evidence summarized here.

**Matter of faith:** That God, rather than inherited institutional convention, presently requires male-only ordination rests on accepting prophetic claims such as those summarized in [[Power in the Priesthood (Elder Neil L. Andersen, October 2013)]]. Believers can affirm that claim honestly, but should not convert unrevealed speculation about God's motives into doctrine. Critics likewise cannot turn the absence of a public rationale into proof that no revelation occurred.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->
