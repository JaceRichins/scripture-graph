# How old is the earth according to the Bible?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
The Bible does not give a numerical age for the earth. [[Genesis 1]] presents six numbered creation days and a seventh day of rest, while [[Genesis 5]] and [[Genesis 11]] provide genealogical ages; treating the days as ordinary days and the genealogies as a complete chronology produces a young-earth estimate of roughly six thousand years, but that estimate is an interpretation and calculation, not a biblical statement. Other serious readings understand the days as analogical, literary, functional, or extended intervals and therefore leave the planet's age open. In Restoration scripture, [[Abraham 4]] calls the intervals successive times, and current Church teaching says their length has not been revealed while acknowledging the scientific estimate of about 4.5 billion years. See [[Creation]] and [[How do faith and science fit together]].
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- **What the text clearly supplies:** [[Genesis 1]] gives six numbered days, repeated evening-and-morning formulas, and a seventh-day climax (Genesis 1:5, 8, 13, 19, 23, 31; Genesis 2:1-3). Exodus 20:8-11 and 31:15-17 use that divine pattern to ground the Sabbath. These are the strongest textual points for ordinary consecutive days.

- **What it does not supply:** no verse totals the age of the earth, names a BC creation date, or carries a creation chronology to the present. [[Genesis 5]] and [[Genesis 11]] contain numerical genealogies, but a final date requires choices about the days, genealogical completeness, textual tradition, and external historical anchors.

- **Evidence for semantic and literary latitude:** Genesis 2:4 summarizes the whole making as occurring ‘in the day,’ and [[Genesis 1]] exhibits a structured movement through ordered realms, their inhabitants or governors, and Sabbath. These observations permit broader readings without proving any one of them.

- **Restoration clarification:** [[Abraham 4]] repeatedly calls the intervals times (Abraham 4:8, 13, 19, 23, 31). Abraham 3:24 and D&C 93:33 also distinguish organizing a world from bringing all matter into existence. This makes ‘age’ potentially ambiguous: age of matter, age of the organized planet, and duration of mortal history need not be identical.

- **Independent physical evidence:** the [U.S. Geological Survey](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/geotime/age.html) reports an age of about 4.54 billion years from radiometric evidence. That figure is scientifically strong but is not a hidden numerical teaching of Genesis. The Church's current [Religion and Science](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/religion-and-science?lang=eng) treatment acknowledges approximately 4.5 billion years while emphasizing that scripture reveals chiefly who created and why.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- **The straightforward-reading objection:** numbered days joined to evening and morning normally sound like ordinary days, and Exodus 20:11 treats God's week as the pattern for Israel's week. Long-age readings can therefore look like revisions introduced only after modern geology.

- **The chronology objection:** [[Genesis 5]] and [[Genesis 11]] unusually provide ages at fatherhood, not merely names. Calling every difficulty a genealogical gap can evade data that plainly invite chronological reckoning. Even large gaps would not produce billions of years if Adam appeared on the sixth ordinary day.

- **The scientific objection:** a planet only thousands of years old conflicts sharply with mutually reinforcing radiometric, astronomical, and geological evidence. This is not a small disagreement at the margins of current science. Appeals to radically accelerated decay or wholesale changes in physical law create additional heat, consistency, and testability problems.

- **The concordism objection:** simply redefining each day as a geological era does not make Genesis a close match to modern reconstruction. The narrative places vegetation before the day-four luminaries and organizes creatures by ancient observational categories rather than evolutionary descent. One-to-one day-age mappings are often selective.

- **The historical-critical objection:** Genesis reflects ancient cosmological and liturgical interests. On this reading, it neither teaches modern young-earth science nor secretly encodes modern geology; asking it for planetary age mistakes its genre and historical setting.

- **The Restoration-text objection:** D&C 77:6-7 speaks of seven thousand years of the earth's temporal existence, and verse 12 places sanctification at the beginning of the seventh thousand years. A reader can reasonably hear this as support for a young temporal chronology; an old-earth interpretation must explain rather than ignore that language.

- **The theological objection to easy harmonization:** labeling every conflict figurative can make interpretation unfalsifiable. A responsible old-earth believer must identify textual reasons for a nonliteral or analogical reading, not merely invoke divine mystery whenever evidence is inconvenient.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
- A thoughtful young-earth believer can acknowledge that the Bible never prints an absolute age while still arguing that its most natural internal sequence implies one. That is a defensible interpretation claim, but it should not be restated as an explicit verse or insulated from contrary physical evidence.

- A thoughtful old-earth believer can point to Genesis 2:4, the highly patterned form of [[Genesis 1]], and the difference between theological narration and scientific measurement. These considerations make non-calendar readings possible or plausible; they do not prove that the author intended geological ages.

- [[Abraham 4]] offers specifically Restoration-based latitude by calling the stages times without assigning terrestrial duration. The Church's [Creation study guide](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/creation-study-guide?lang=eng) consequently says that the length of each day has not been revealed. This is stronger than using 2 Peter 3:8 as an exact conversion formula, which its context does not authorize.

- D&C 77 can be read as a schematic history of the earth's mortal or temporal phase represented by Revelation's seals, not as a measurement of the age of its matter or pre-Adam formation. That distinction is plausible in an organization-of-existing-materials theology, but the passage does not define all its boundaries, so it should not be claimed as a decisive proof of either total age.

- [[How do faith and science fit together]] provides a disciplined response: D&C 88:77-80 encourages study of the earth and heavens, and D&C 93:24 does not permit contradictory factual claims to become true merely by assigning them to separate domains. Believers may revise interpretations while retaining the explicit doctrines of divine creation, purpose, human divine image, and accountability.

- Scientific dating cannot determine whether creation has divine agency or purpose. Conversely, belief in a Creator does not choose a decay constant, geological chronology, or creation date. Keeping those claims distinct protects both faith and intellectual honesty.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Frameworks — how this can fit, and how it cannot

<!-- SG:BEGIN frameworks -->
**First distinguish what the text requires.** The Bible requires, at minimum, that Genesis portray God as the source of the ordered world, creation as purposeful and good, humanity as bearing God's image, and the sequence as culminating in sacred rest (Genesis 1:1, 26-31; Genesis 2:1-3). It requires readers to take the six-plus-one pattern seriously because Exodus 20:11 uses it theologically. It does **not** explicitly require the date 4004 BC, a present age of roughly six thousand years, a particular ancient textual tradition, or a modern mechanism of formation. Those belong to interpretive models. Likewise, 4.54 billion years is independently measured rather than revealed by the chapter.

- **Calendar-day creation plus closed genealogy — possible; textually plausible; not independently supported.** Numbered days, evening and morning, Exodus's Sabbath analogy, and the numerical genealogies support it. Its full young-earth conclusion requires all stages to be ordinary days, Adam to follow immediately, the genealogies to be chronologically closed, and external calendar anchors to be correct. Standard geochronology strongly challenges the resulting planetary age.

- **Analogical workdays — plausible.** God's work is presented in a pattern genuinely analogous to Israel's workweek, while the duration of divine and human days need not be identical. This preserves sequence and Sabbath theology without yielding an age. Its weakness is that Genesis never pauses to explain such a duration distinction.

- **Day-age or progressive creation — possible, but detailed concordance is often ad hoc.** Genesis 2:4 shows broader day-language, and [[Abraham 4]] explicitly speaks of successive times. Long intervals are therefore permitted. Exact attempts to map each day onto a geological era are weakened by the narrative order and usually depend on selective correspondences.

- **Literary-framework reading — plausible and literarily supported.** Days 1-3 establish ordered domains or conditions; days 4-6 install corresponding inhabitants or governors; Sabbath is the climax. This explains real features of the final form. It leaves age undetermined and does not, merely by identifying structure, prove that no historical claims are present.

- **Functional or cosmic-temple inauguration — possible to plausible; independently supported by comparative ancient-context work, but disputed in extent.** On this model, the week chiefly assigns functions and establishes the ordered world as God's sacred domain. John H. Walton's *The Lost World of Genesis One* is a prominent formulation. Ancient context makes the approach serious, but claims that Genesis contains no material-origin interest at all go beyond what all scholars accept.

- **Ancient cosmology with theological accommodation — plausible and historically supported.** A critical form treats Genesis as an ancient account whose cosmological pictures belong to its world and whose purpose was never modern science. A believing form holds that revelation communicated true theology through the conceptual world of its first audience. The critical model does not by itself disprove divine inspiration; the accommodating model does not by itself explain every textual-historical difficulty.

- **Gap or ruin-reconstruction model — possible at the level of logical harmonization, generally ad hoc as a solution to age.** It places a long interval or destroyed world between Genesis 1:1 and 1:2. The text does not state such a catastrophe or interval, so the model has less direct support than literary or analogical readings.

- **Old earth with a later Adam and mortal-history phase — possible in Restoration theology; details open.** Abraham 3:24 and D&C 93:33 allow preexisting materials, while D&C 77 may describe a seven-thousand-year temporal phase rather than total planetary age. This can preserve an old planet and a meaningful historical [[Adam]], but scripture does not reveal the biological, demographic, or chronological bridge. See [[Were Adam and Eve real people]] and [[Fall of Adam and Eve]].

- **Old-earth evolutionary creation — independently supported regarding physical antiquity, theologically possible, officially unmandated.** Geochronology strongly supports an ancient planet. The Church states that creation accounts are not scientific descriptions and takes no position on evolution. Scripture does not provide the evolutionary mechanism, so accepting it comes from empirical inquiry rather than extracting it from Genesis.

- **Purely symbolic Adam and creation — possible as a critical literary reconstruction, but incomplete for the full Restoration canon.** Symbolic dimensions are unmistakable, yet Restoration scripture repeatedly presents Adam and Eve as identifiable participants in a wider historical and salvific story. A purely symbolic reading therefore does not exhaust that canon's presentation.

**Considered and set aside:** an exact rule that one creation day equals one thousand years, because Psalm 90:4 and 2 Peter 3:8 are comparisons rather than conversion keys; Ussher's 4004 BC as something ‘the Bible says,’ because it is a later calculation; genealogical gaps as a sufficient billion-year solution, because gaps alone cannot reconcile an ordinary six-day creation with deep time; and the claim that literary artistry automatically eliminates historical reference, because structured narratives can still make historical claims.
<!-- SG:END frameworks -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
**Established:** the Bible attributes the world to God, presents creation in a six-plus-one pattern, and supplies genealogical figures from Adam onward. It does not state the earth's total age. A roughly six-thousand-year answer is a recognizable traditional inference, not the wording of scripture. Modern geochronology's approximately 4.54-billion-year estimate is independently very strong.

**Open to interpretation:** whether the Genesis days are duration-identical to ordinary days, analogical workdays, long stages, or a chiefly literary or functional arrangement; how completely the genealogies function as chronology; how D&C 77's temporal existence relates to pre-Adam formation; and how the identifiable Adam of Restoration scripture relates to biological history.

**Institutional Latter-day Saint position:** current teaching says the duration of the creative days has not been revealed, acknowledges the scientific estimate, and emphasizes who created the earth and why. Neither young-earth creationism nor a detailed evolutionary scenario should be presented as revealed Church doctrine. See [[Creation]] and [[How do faith and science fit together]].

**Matter of faith:** that God, acting through [[Jesus Christ]], is ultimately responsible for creation and that creation serves the [[Plan of Salvation]]. The planet's measured physical age is an empirical question; the theological meaning of creation is not established or refuted merely by calculating that age.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->

## Further study

<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[Genesis 1]] and [[Genesis 2]] slowly, distinguishing sequence, literary structure, and explicit theological claims. Then examine [[Genesis 5]], [[Genesis 11]], Exodus 20:8-11, Exodus 31:15-17, Psalm 90:4, and 2 Peter 3:8-9 to test the major chronological arguments in context.

For Restoration comparison, read Moses 1-3, [[Abraham 4]], Abraham 3:22-25, D&C 77:6-12, D&C 88:42-45, 77-80, D&C 93:24, 29-35, and D&C 101:32-34. Compare rather than silently collapse Genesis, Moses, and Abraham. Continue through [[Creation]], [[How do faith and science fit together]], [[Were Adam and Eve real people]], and [[Fall of Adam and Eve]].

Primary and institutional sources: James Ussher's *Annals of the World* for the famous calculated chronology; the Church's [Creation study guide](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/creation-study-guide?lang=eng); its [Religion and Science](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/religion-and-science?lang=eng) treatment; and the [U.S. Geological Survey explanation of earth's age](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/geotime/age.html).

Representative interpretive scholarship: Meredith G. Kline, ‘Because It Had Not Rained,’ for the framework approach; C. John Collins, *Genesis 1–4*, for an analogical-day proposal; John H. Walton, *The Lost World of Genesis One*, for functional and temple-oriented interpretation; and Ronald L. Numbers, *The Creationists*, for the history of modern creationist movements. These sources argue different positions and should be compared rather than treated as a consensus package.
