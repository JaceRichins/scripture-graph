# How does the Church regard LGBTQ members and same-sex marriage?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
The Church teaches that people who experience same-sex attraction or identify as gay, lesbian, or bisexual should be treated with love and genuine belonging; the attraction or identity is not itself considered sinful and does not by itself prevent worthy Church participation. It also teaches that marriage ordained by God is between a man and a woman, that sexual relations belong only within that marriage, and that it therefore does not sanction same-sex sexual relationships or same-sex marriage. These are current institutional teachings stated most directly in [[Same-Sex Attraction (Gospel Topics)]], [[Chastity (Gospel Topics)]], [[Same-Sex Marriage (Gospel Topics)]], and [[Two Great Commandments (President Dallin H. Oaks, October 2019)]]. The canon supplies a repeated male-female marriage pattern and Restoration sealing theology, but it does not use modern categories of orientation or directly analyze civil same-sex marriage; applying it to those questions requires interpretation and acceptance of Church authority. The tension is real: a formally general chastity rule leaves many gay and lesbian members without a presently approved marital path, and neither the canon nor current teaching gives a detailed account of every faithful LGBTQ member's eternal relational future.
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- **Direct official statements:** [[Same-Sex Attraction (Gospel Topics)]] distinguishes attraction and gay, lesbian, or bisexual identity from sinful conduct and says these do not by themselves exclude a person from participation, callings, or temple attendance. [[Chastity (Gospel Topics)]] and [[Same-Sex Marriage (Gospel Topics)]] state that same-sex sexual relations and same-sex marriage are outside Church standards.

- **A repeated canonical pattern:** [[Genesis 2]] presents a man and wife becoming one flesh. [[Matthew 19]] invokes creation as male and female and the one-flesh union in answering a divorce question. [[D&C 49]] calls marriage ordained of God and describes one man, one wife, and one flesh. This strongly supports continuity between current teaching and the canon, but none of these passages directly discusses modern orientation or civil same-sex marriage.

- **Restoration sealing theology:** [[D&C 131]] connects covenant marriage with the highest degree of celestial glory, while [[D&C 132]] makes enduring marriage dependent on divine law, authority, sealing, and covenant faithfulness. D&C 132 must not be presented as a simple monogamy text: its stated setting includes the justification and regulation of plural marriage. Even with that historical complexity, these sections show why the Church treats marriage as part of [[Eternal Marriage]] and the [[Plan of Salvation]], not merely as a cultural preference.

- **Formal rule and practical effect:** The attraction-conduct distinction is substantive, but formal generality does not make its impact equal. Heterosexual members may pursue a Church-approved marriage; members whose enduring attraction is exclusively same-sex presently have no equivalent marital path.

- **Love and belonging are also doctrinal obligations:** [[1 John 4]] tests professed love of God through treatment of one's brother. The close parallel between 1 Corinthians 13:4-7 and Moroni 7:45, together with Moroni 7:47's identification of charity as the pure love of Christ, makes contempt, humiliation, cruelty, and exclusion incompatible with the canon's measure of discipleship. [[The Doctrine of Belonging (Elder D. Todd Christofferson, October 2022)]] expressly includes members who experience same-sex attraction.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- **The texts underdetermine the modern conclusion.** [[Genesis 2]], [[Matthew 19]], and [[D&C 49]] positively describe male-female marriage, but a paradigmatic description is not automatically an exhaustive prohibition. Matthew 19 directly addresses divorce, and none of these chapters analyzes orientation or modern civil same-sex marriage.

- **The Pauline vocabulary is disputed.** The King James expressions in 1 Corinthians 6:9 are not modern identity categories. Traditional interpreters reasonably see a prohibition of male same-sex conduct, while other interpreters dispute the scope and historical referents of the underlying Greek terms. This verse alone cannot supply a complete account of gay or lesbian identity and marriage.

- **A formally equal rule creates unequal possibilities.** Heterosexual members may seek the kind of marriage celebrated throughout Church life; members with exclusively same-sex attraction may not. For them the teaching can require lifelong abstinence from sexual intimacy and companionate marriage.

- **Belonging language may exceed lived reality.** Official condemnation of cruelty does not establish that local culture, family reactions, discipline, or repeated emphasis on heterosexual ideals never produces isolation. Claims about mental-health outcomes and causation require carefully designed research, but the lived burden cannot be assessed from institutional statements alone.

- **Concrete institutional history raises questions.** The Church's Proposition 8 involvement, the 2015 restrictions affecting members in same-sex marriages and their children, and the 2019 revision remain important subjects for primary-source study. Critics argue that these episodes caused harm and show that disciplinary and pastoral boundaries can be contingent even when leaders distinguish them from doctrine.

- **Historical analogies complicate claims of immutability.** The beginning and ending of authorized plural marriage and the 1978 removal of priesthood and temple restrictions are disputed analogies, not proofs that same-sex sealing will be recognized. They nevertheless make it unsafe to infer that strong present teaching alone demonstrates that every formulation or application is permanently fixed.

- **Eternal prospects remain unrevealed.** [[D&C 131]] and [[D&C 132]] do not explain the future relationships of a faithful member whose enduring mortal orientation is same-sex. Trust that God will act justly may be a legitimate faith response, but detailed claims about changed orientation, compulsory heterosexual marriage, or undisclosed exceptions exceed the text.

- **LGBTQ is not one evidentiary category.** The supplied sources address same-sex attraction and marriage more directly than transgender, nonbinary, bisexual, or queer experience. A dossier about the umbrella term must not pretend that evidence about one group settles every other question.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
- A thoughtful traditional response concedes that the principal texts do not use modern categories but argues that revelation may establish a normative pattern without cataloguing every later alternative. The cumulative male-female pattern in [[Genesis 2]], [[Matthew 19]], [[D&C 49]], [[D&C 131]], and [[D&C 132]] is stronger than any single proof-text, especially within a faith that accepts modern prophetic authority.

- Believers distinguish logical entailment from covenant authority. The canon may not furnish a freestanding philosophical proof against every same-sex marriage, yet the Church can still regard its authorized marriage form as binding because of its understanding of priesthood keys, ordinances, and continuing revelation. That answer depends on accepting the Church's authority claims; it cannot simply be imposed as a neutral conclusion on those who do not share them.

- The attraction-conduct distinction rejects the damaging inference that an involuntary attraction is itself guilt. [[Same-Sex Attraction (Gospel Topics)]] gives members a basis for identifying their experience honestly while retaining Church membership. The distinction is meaningful, though it does not answer the objection about unequal relational opportunity.

- First Corinthians 7:7-9, 25-35 shows that the canon can honor unmarried discipleship rather than treating marriage as the measure of mortal worth. Believers may therefore understand celibate discipleship as a real Christian vocation. The responsible response must still acknowledge that chosen singleness and a required lifetime without an approved partner are not psychologically or relationally identical.

- Policy revision need not entail doctrinal reversal. Institutions can alter disciplinary classifications, procedures, or pastoral practice while maintaining a theological definition of marriage. Conversely, history means believers should identify carefully what is canon, what is authoritative interpretation, and what is revisable administration.

- Charity is not a public-relations supplement to orthodoxy. Under 1 John 4:20-21, 1 Corinthians 13:1-7, and Moroni 7:44-48, a believer who defends marriage doctrine through contempt, mockery, rejection, or indifference contradicts central scriptural commands. [[Following Jesus Being a Peacemaker (Elder Neil L. Andersen, April 2022)]] supplies an institutional example of cooperation across disagreement.

- The most honest believing response to unrevealed eternal questions is restraint: God is just and merciful, but the Church has not disclosed enough to map every LGBTQ member's postmortal relationships. Hope may be warranted within faith; confident speculation is not.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
**Established:** The Church currently distinguishes same-sex attraction or gay, lesbian, or bisexual identity from sin; permits worthy participation by members who experience or use those descriptions; defines divine marriage as male-female; restricts sexual relations to that marriage; and does not sanction same-sex marriage. Its position has substantial internal coherence with [[Genesis 2]], [[Matthew 19]], [[D&C 49]], [[D&C 131]], [[D&C 132]], and its doctrines of [[Eternal Marriage]] and the [[Plan of Salvation]]. The same canon makes charity and humane treatment obligatory, so contempt for LGBTQ people cannot be defended as fidelity to scripture.

**Open:** The texts do not settle every lexical question in 1 Corinthians 6, directly address modern orientation or gender identity, explain the eternal relationships of every faithful LGBTQ member, or show which administrative practices are permanently required by doctrine. The supplied sources also do not justify treating transgender questions as identical to questions about same-sex attraction or marriage.

**A matter of interpretation:** Whether the positive male-female pattern logically excludes every same-sex union, and how much weight to assign continuing prophetic interpretation beyond the words of the texts, depend on hermeneutical and ecclesiological commitments. The Church's official answer is clear even where the argument from scripture alone is not exhaustive.

**A matter of faith:** Acceptance of sealing authority, prophetic stewardship, and promised divine resolution of unrevealed eternal circumstances rests on Latter-day Saint faith claims. It is intellectually legitimate for a believer to accept those claims while acknowledging the severe cost borne by some members. It is also coherent for a critic to recognize the theology's internal structure yet judge its exclusion of same-sex marriage morally unjust. Honesty requires neither disguising the doctrine nor minimizing the burden.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->

## Further study

<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
- Read the principal texts in context: [[Genesis 2]]; [[Matthew 19]], including its teaching about nonmarriage; [[D&C 49]]; [[D&C 131]]; [[D&C 132]]; [[1 Corinthians 6]]; [[1 Corinthians 7]]; [[1 John 4]]; and [[Moroni 7]].

- Compare the official primary sources [[Same-Sex Attraction (Gospel Topics)]], [[Same-Sex Marriage (Gospel Topics)]], [[Chastity (Gospel Topics)]], and [[Marriage (Gospel Topics)]]. Consult the current General Handbook alongside them because administrative provisions can change more readily than canonical texts.

- Read the Church's theological and pastoral framing in “The Family: A Proclamation to the World,” [[Two Great Commandments (President Dallin H. Oaks, October 2019)]], [[The Plan and the Proclamation (Elder Dallin H. Oaks, October 2017)]], [[Why Marriage, Why Family (Elder D. Todd Christofferson, April 2015)]], [[The Doctrine of Belonging (Elder D. Todd Christofferson, October 2022)]], and [[Following Jesus Being a Peacemaker (Elder Neil L. Andersen, April 2022)]].

- For faithful first-person engagement, read Tom Christofferson, *That We May Be One*. For critical historical analysis, read Taylor G. Petrey, *Tabernacles of Clay: Sexuality and Gender in Modern Mormonism*, and Gregory A. Prince, *Gay Rights and the Mormon Church: Intended Actions, Unintended Consequences*. These works approach the subject differently and should not be mistaken for official doctrine.

- Study original Church announcements and handbook language surrounding major policy changes rather than relying solely on summaries. Ask separately what changed in doctrine, discipline, eligibility, terminology, and pastoral practice.

- Pair institutional texts with accounts from active, former, celibate, partnered, and mixed-orientation LGBTQ Latter-day Saints and their families. No single life story establishes the experience of the whole population, but excluding these voices would leave the lived question inadequately studied.

## Frameworks — how this can fit, and how it cannot
<!-- SG:BEGIN frameworks -->
**What the text requires.** The canon's explicit marriage examples and ordinances are male-female: Genesis 2:18-24, Matthew 19:4-9, D&C 49:15-17, D&C 131:1-4, and D&C 132:15-20. It treats sexual conduct as embodied and covenantal (1 Corinthians 6:12-20), recognizes unmarried discipleship (Matthew 19:10-12; 1 Corinthians 7:7-9, 25-35), and commands charity (1 John 4:7-12, 20-21; 1 Corinthians 13:1-7; Moroni 7:44-48). It does not employ modern concepts of orientation or gender identity, expressly analyze a loving same-sex marriage, dictate civil marriage law, or reveal each LGBTQ person's eternal relational future. D&C 132 also cannot honestly be reduced to a simple monogamous pattern: the section expressly addresses plural wives and therefore shows both continuity in gendered marriage and historical variation in authorized marital form.

- **Canonical-continuity model — plausible and independently supported within current Latter-day Saint interpretation.** The male-female pattern recurs in creation, Jesus's teaching, and Restoration revelation. Current teaching reads this pattern normatively through [[Marriage]], [[Eternal Marriage]], [[Family]], and [[Chastity]]. It strongly explains the Church's position, though positive recurrence is not by formal logic alone an explicit adjudication of every later form.

- **Normative-but-not-exhaustive model — possible and textually plausible, but institutionally rejected for same-sex marriage.** Genesis and Matthew may affirm a foundational male-female vocation without defining every morally possible union. Their silence about orientation and Matthew 19's divorce context support considering this reading. It must still explain the gendered Restoration texts and conflicts with current official teaching.

- **Traditional Pauline-prohibition model — plausible; disputed in scope.** This reads 1 Corinthians 6:9 as prohibiting male same-sex conduct within Paul's broader embodied ethic. Lexical and historical disputes make it stronger than silence but weaker than an uncontested statement about every modern LGBTQ identity or marriage.

- **Exploitation-limited Pauline model — possible; disputed and insufficient alone.** This restricts Paul's contested terms to exploitative, status-marked, or otherwise abusive ancient conduct. Cultural distance and lexical uncertainty support considering it, but the text does not expressly state all proposed limitations. Even if accepted, it would not by itself overturn gendered Restoration sealing theology.

- **Restoration-sealing model — independently supported as current LDS doctrine.** [[D&C 131]] and [[D&C 132]] make authorized covenant marriage part of exaltation and divine sealing. Present interpretation applies that order to male-female couples. D&C 132's plural-marriage setting shows that the historical form of authorized marriage has not been uniformly monogamous, while its authorization structure explains why civil recognition alone does not produce ecclesiastical recognition.

- **Celibate-discipleship model — possible and institutionally supported; pastorally costly.** Matthew 19 and 1 Corinthians 7 prevent singleness from being treated as spiritual failure. The model becomes misleading if it equates chosen singleness with a required lifetime without an approved partner or minimizes loneliness and loss.

- **Belonging without doctrinal change — plausible and independently supported by current teaching.** [[The Doctrine of Belonging (Elder D. Todd Christofferson, October 2022)]], [[Same-Sex Attraction (Gospel Topics)]], and [[Following Jesus Being a Peacemaker (Elder Neil L. Andersen, April 2022)]] support participation, friendship, civility, and cooperation while retaining the marriage standard. This can matter greatly without resolving exclusion from same-sex partnership or sealing.

- **Development through future revelation — possible but presently speculative.** Continuing revelation and past doctrinal development mean future reinterpretation cannot be ruled out logically. No cited revelation or official source authorizes same-sex sealing. Prediction of its occurrence, timing, or form would therefore be ad hoc.

- **Policy development without doctrinal change — plausible and historically supported at the administrative level.** Discipline, eligibility, terminology, and pastoral practice can change while leaders maintain a male-female marriage doctrine. This does not by itself determine whether a disputed policy was required by doctrine or whether deeper development is impossible.

- **Institutional-contingency model — plausible as a partial critical account; reductive if total.** Culture, leadership judgment, and institutional pressures can shape formulation and enforcement. Yet reducing the entire position to prejudice or politics fails to explain its real integration with [[Eternal Marriage]], ordinances, and longstanding scriptural interpretation.

- **Eschatological-resolution model — possible as faith and hope; ad hoc when detailed.** A believer may trust divine justice and mercy where revelation is incomplete. Claims that orientation will disappear, that every faithful LGBTQ person will enter a heterosexual union, or that a specific undisclosed exception exists are not revealed facts.

**Considered and set aside:** The claim that scripture says nothing relevant ignores its gendered marriage pattern and sexual ethic. The claim that scripture explicitly answers every modern LGBTQ question ignores its historical vocabulary and interpretive gaps. Sodom was not used as controlling proof because it was not part of the supplied direct findings and carries substantial contextual disputes. Comparisons with plural marriage and the former racial restriction remain legitimate challenges to simplistic immutability arguments but cannot predict the same outcome here.
<!-- SG:END frameworks -->
