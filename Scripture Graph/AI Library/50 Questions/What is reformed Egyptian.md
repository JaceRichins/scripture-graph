# What is reformed Egyptian?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
“Reformed Egyptian” is the Book of Mormon’s name for the characters in which Moroni says the Nephite record was written. Mormon 9:32-34 says those characters were called that “among us,” had been handed down and altered to fit Nephite speech, differed from their also-altered Hebrew, apparently conserved plate space, and were unknown to outsiders. The passage leaves unclear whether the term names chiefly a script, a written register, or a script-language combination. Adapted scripts and changing languages are historically ordinary enough to make the general idea possible, but no independently available Nephite source corpus permits identification or decipherment. What is settled is what the book claims; whether that description reports an ancient American writing tradition remains open and is not established by the English text alone.
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- **The primary definition:** Mormon 9:32 calls the record’s characters “reformed Egyptian,” says the name was used “among us,” and describes the characters as inherited and altered according to Nephite speech. [[Moroni (son of Mormon)]] is the speaker.

- **The contrast with Hebrew:** Mormon 9:33 says Nephite Hebrew had also changed and links the choice of characters to limited plate space. This supports an altered, economical scribal system more directly than the common assumption of unchanged Egyptian.

- **Earlier narrative preparation:** 1 Nephi 1:2 associates Lehi’s household with Jewish learning and Egyptian language; Mosiah 1:4 associates Lehi’s Egyptian learning with reading engravings on the brass plates. These passages make the later claim narratively coherent, though they are internal coordination rather than independent historical witnesses.

- **Material constraints:** Jacob 4:1-3 says engraving on plates was difficult and forced brevity, fitting Moroni’s plate-space explanation. See [[Records and Sacred Writings]] and [[Were metal plates used for records in antiquity]].

- **Comparative plausibility, not identification:** Egyptian changed historically, and examples exist of Semitic material represented in Egyptian characters. This rebuts the claim that adapted Egyptian-derived writing is intrinsically impossible, but no known example has been demonstrated to be Moroni’s system.

- **The decisive evidentiary limit:** There is no independently available source-language Book of Mormon manuscript against which the English can be checked. [[Do chiasmus and Hebraisms show the Book of Mormon is ancient]] therefore judges alleged source-language features indirect, while [[Book of Mormon Assessment]] finds strong KJV-shaped mediation of the received English.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- **No independently identified script:** “Reformed Egyptian” is not an established Egyptological name for a known Nephite corpus. Mormon 9 supplies the label, but the alleged source is unavailable for ordinary examination, comparison, dating, or decipherment.

- **Ambiguous terminology:** The passage moves between characters, speech, Hebrew, and language without explaining their technical relationship. Calling reformed Egyptian a known language, a particular Egyptian script, or a cipher goes beyond the text.

- **Internal coherence is not external corroboration:** The links among 1 Nephi 1:2, Mosiah 1:4, Jacob 4:1-3, and Mormon 9:32-34 can reflect ancient transmission, but a single modern author or final narrative controller could create the same coordination.

- **The copied-characters episode is not a decipherment:** Joseph Smith—History 1:62-64 and [[The Contributions of Martin Harris]] report copied characters and consultations with scholars. Those reports do not provide an agreed bilingual text, reproducible translation, or independent identification of the language. Later retellings of the Anthon encounter must not be treated as stronger than their conflicting and interested historical sources permit.

- **English Hebraisms cannot recover the missing source:** The English is heavily biblical and often KJV-shaped. Chiasmus and apparent Semitic syntax may be relevant to provenance, but they cannot demonstrate that particular English constructions translate particular reformed Egyptian forms. See [[Do chiasmus and Hebraisms show the Book of Mormon is ancient]].

- **Economical modern explanation:** A nineteenth-century KJV-informed composition can explain the unfamiliar language, divine translation device, plate constraints, and internal literary preparation as narrative world-building without positing an unattested Nephite script. On the evidence presently assessed in [[Book of Mormon Assessment]], this is a serious and comparatively economical historical model.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
- A thoughtful believer need not claim that “reformed Egyptian” was the standard ancient name of a familiar script. “Called among us” and “altered by us” positively predict a local designation and a changing system, so failure to find that exact technical label in Egyptian sources is not by itself a textual contradiction.

- Known language change, script adaptation, and the representation of Semitic material with Egyptian characters show that the broad scribal idea is possible. This response establishes general plausibility only; it does not identify Moroni’s characters.

- The book does not require the received English to preserve transparent source-language syntax. Its canonical translation model invokes divine power and prepared means of interpretation; see [[How was the Book of Mormon translated]] and [[Book of Mormon Translation (Essay)]]. A believer may therefore accept an ancient source rendered through familiar biblical English.

- That model still carries an evidentiary cost: unless it supplies prior criteria for when translation is close and when it is expansive or KJV-harmonized, it can accommodate almost any English result after the fact. Compatibility is not independent confirmation.

- Believers may regard Moroni’s testimony, Restoration revelation, witnesses, and spiritual experience as mutually meaningful within faith. Historical analysis must nevertheless keep that theological warrant distinct from reproducible linguistic authentication.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Frameworks — how this can fit, and how it cannot

<!-- SG:BEGIN frameworks -->
**What the text itself requires.** Mormon 9:32-34 requires only that the final narrator present the record as using inherited, altered characters called reformed Egyptian by his own people; that Nephite Hebrew had also changed; that Hebrew would have required more plate capacity; and that outsiders did not know the language. The text does not identify a standard Egyptian script, give an alphabet, explain the underlying spoken language, say that every Nephite used it, or require word-for-word source-transparent English. Objections based merely on the absence of an Egyptological system bearing that exact name attach partly to an assumption the text does not make. Conversely, defenses that confidently identify it as hieratic, demotic, a shorthand, or a specific cipher also add assumptions.

- **Altered Egyptian-derived scribal system for an ancient Nephite language — plausible within the narrative; weakly independently supported.** This most straightforward historical reading combines 1 Nephi 1:2, Mosiah 1:4, Jacob 4:1-3, and Mormon 9:32-34. General examples of script adaptation and language change support feasibility. No independently dated Nephite inscription, bilingual, or source manuscript identifies the proposed system, so feasibility remains much stronger than authentication.

- **Egyptian characters encoding a substantially Semitic or mixed spoken language — possible.** The distinction between characters and “manner of speech,” together with Nephi’s Jewish-Egyptian cultural description, permits this model. Ancient cross-script writing supplies an analogy, not a match. The precise linguistic mixture is unrecoverable from the passage.

- **Specialized record-keeper register, shorthand, or cipher — possible but presently ad hoc.** Plate scarcity and outsider incomprehension could fit compression or restricted scribal conventions. The text never calls the system shorthand or cipher, and no specimen establishes how it worked; specificity beyond that is conjecture.

- **Ancient record rendered through loose, revelatory, KJV-shaped translation — plausible but only weakly independently supported.** This accepts the narrated ancient record while allowing extensive English biblical mediation. It fits the canonical divine-translation claims in [[How was the Book of Mormon translated]] and preserves the limited ancient-direction findings weighed by [[Book of Mormon Assessment]]. It needs principled criteria for translation and expansion; otherwise KJV dependence is merely absorbed after the fact.

- **Ancient core with substantial revelatory or nineteenth-century expansion — possible.** This separates an ancient inscribed substrate from much of the received English wording. It accommodates both reformed Egyptian and pervasive KJV language, but no surviving source independently marks the proposed layers. Invoking expansion only where difficulty arises becomes ad hoc.

- **Nineteenth-century KJV-informed historical composition — plausible.** On this model, reformed Egyptian is part of a coherent narrative explanation for an inaccessible record and miraculous translation. It economically fits the unavailable source, final-form coordination, and strong KJV-shaped English mediation identified by [[Book of Mormon Assessment]]. The present corpus has not established a particular composition process, source book, collaborator, or claim of conscious fraud.

- **Inspired nineteenth-century scripture without an ancient linguistic substrate — possible as a theological model.** Divine inspiration and ancient provenance are distinct. A reader could accept religious inspiration while interpreting reformed Egyptian as part of the revelatory narrative rather than an independently recoverable historical language. Historical methods cannot verify the divine component.

- **Ordinary, source-transparent scholarly translation from a known Egyptian language — strongly lowered and set aside.** The book itself says the language was altered and unknown to outsiders, Joseph is not presented as an ordinarily trained Egyptologist, no source corpus is available, and the English shows pervasive KJV shaping.

- **Deciphering reformed Egyptian from English Hebraisms or chiasmus — set aside.** Without aligned source text, multiple English phenomena remain compatible with ancient source structure, biblical imitation, natural narrative symmetry, dictation, or modern composition. [[Do chiasmus and Hebraisms show the Book of Mormon is ancient]] accordingly rates the ancient-provenance force as weak rather than decisive.
<!-- SG:END frameworks -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
**Established:** The Book of Mormon uses “reformed Egyptian” as its own community-specific name for inherited and altered record characters; connects them with altered Nephite speech, plate limitations, and divinely prepared interpretation; and embeds that account in a broader narrative of Egyptian literacy and metal records. It does not furnish a technical grammar, decipherment key, or unambiguous distinction between script and language.

**Open:** Whether any extant copied-character document accurately represents the plates; what historical script family, if any, the characters resembled; what language they encoded; how the alleged source relates to the English wording; and whether an ancient Nephite source existed. Comparative scribal evidence makes adaptation conceivable but does not authenticate this case.

**Matter of faith:** Accepting Moroni’s report as ancient testimony and Joseph Smith’s work as divine translation depends materially on confidence in the Book of Mormon and Restoration revelation. That confidence may be rationally integrated with historical evidence, but it is not produced by an independently deciphered reformed Egyptian corpus.

The required [[Book of Mormon Assessment]] finds strong evidence for pervasive biblical and KJV-shaped mediation of the English, weak independent support from several ancient-direction lines, and no present verdict on overall historicity. Reformed Egyptian should not be inflated into a fourth independently verified ancient line, nor should lack of a presently available source be misstated as proof that no source ever existed.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->

Begin with Mormon 8-9, especially Mormon 9:32-34, then compare 1 Nephi 1:1-3, Mosiah 1:2-5, Jacob 4:1-3, and Ether 12:23-27. For the claimed translation process, read Joseph Smith—History 1:34-35 and 1:59-64, D&C 1:29, and [[How was the Book of Mormon translated]].

Use [[Book of Mormon Translation (Essay)]] for the Church’s official historical framing and [[The Contributions of Martin Harris]] for the character-copy episode. Finally, read [[Book of Mormon Assessment]], which weighs the KJV-mediation and ancient-provenance lines together and explains why neither internal literary coordination nor isolated parallels settle provenance.

For comparative background, consult James P. Allen, *The Ancient Egyptian Language: An Historical Study* (2013), and Stephen D. Ricks and John A. Tvedtnes, “Jewish and Other Semitic Texts Written in Egyptian Characters” (1996), while keeping general scribal possibility distinct from a demonstrated Nephite identification.
