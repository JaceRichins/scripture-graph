# Did Joseph Smith marry other men's wives?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
Yes—if “marry” includes the confidential religious marriages or sealings practiced in Nauvoo. Joseph Smith participated in ceremonies with women who were then civilly married to living men; the official [[Plural Marriage in Kirtland and Nauvoo (Essay)]] acknowledges this category, and [[Zina D. H. Young]] and [[Mary Elizabeth Rollins Lightner]] provide documented examples. These were not additional civil marriages recognized by law. Some were described as eternity-only, and most or all of the women apparently continued living with their civil husbands. The ceremony-level answer is therefore established, while the purpose, terms, consent, and possible sexual character of particular unions remain disputed. D&C 132 supplies Joseph's claimed sealing authority but does not name these women or plainly reconcile their cases with verse 61's description of additional wives as virgins vowed to no other man.
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- [[Plural Marriage in Kirtland and Nauvoo (Essay)]] expressly acknowledges that Joseph was sealed to women who were already married and explains that the surviving record does not establish identical terms for every union.

- [[Zina D. H. Young]] reported a sealing to Joseph after her civil marriage to Henry Jacobs. Her later affidavit identifies the ceremony, while not establishing sexual relations or a second household.

- [[Mary Elizabeth Rollins Lightner]] reported a sealing to Joseph while married to Adam Lightner and described it as applying to the next life; she continued living with Adam. This strongly supports the existence of an overlapping sealing while also showing why “marriage” requires definition.

- D&C 132 directly joins Joseph's sealing authority with plural marriage and instructs Emma to receive women given to him. It therefore supplies contemporary canonical evidence that the system concerned Joseph personally, although it does not list the married women.

- The convergence of participant testimony, witnesses, sealing-related records, institutional histories, and scholars who disagree sharply about interpretation makes complete denial of the ceremonies untenable. That convergence is much stronger for the ceremonies than for sexual polyandry, uniform motives, or uniform marital terms.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- **“Sealing” may not mean a conventional second earthly marriage.** Some women described eternity-only arrangements, and most or all continued living with their civil husbands. Calling every ceremony ordinary polyandry can imply a shared household or simultaneous conjugal relationships that the evidence does not establish.

- **The sexual question remains unresolved.** Marital terminology and secrecy permit the possibility that some unions were sexual, but the surviving evidence does not securely demonstrate that Joseph had sexual relations with a woman who simultaneously maintained conjugal relations with her civil husband. Absence of documentation is not proof of abstinence, yet possibility is not proof of occurrence.

- **D&C 132 does not fit the cases neatly.** Verse 61 describes additional wives as virgins vowed to no other man. Verses 41-44 discuss adultery, broken vows, and reassignment, but do not clearly authorize simultaneous marriage to two faithful living husbands. Verse 39's treatment of Bathsheba and Uriah also prevents a simple claim that sealing authority makes prior marriage irrelevant.

- **Other canonical texts establish contrary norms.** D&C 42:22 commands exclusive fidelity, and Jacob 2:27 states a one-wife rule while centering the sorrow caused to women. Jacob 2:30 may be read as a divine-command exception, but it does not explain the already-married cases.

- **Consent and power remain ethically serious.** Joseph was simultaneously prophet, claimant to exclusive sealing authority, proposed husband, and reporter of divine commands carrying eternal consequences. Emma apparently did not know about every union. Later participant defense is relevant evidence of sincere belief, but it does not eliminate the possibility of religious pressure or inadequate informed consent.

- **The record is late and interested.** Many details come from affidavits and reminiscences recorded decades later amid institutional, legal, and theological disputes. Such evidence must be weighed rather than discarded, but it cannot supply the certainty of contemporary documentation.

- **The strongest critical model** holds that concentrated religious authority, confidentiality, and promised or threatened eternal consequences enabled relationships unavailable under civil and ordinary Christian marriage. D&C 132's Joseph-specific authorization and treatment of Emma make this model historically and ethically plausible, though they do not prove conscious fraud or a predominantly sexual motive.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
- A careful believer can concede the historical answer without equating every sealing with an ordinary second household. The documented distinction between time-and-eternity and eternity-only unions, along with the women's continued residence with their civil husbands, supports a real category difference in at least some cases.

- Early Nauvoo sealing practice was developing. Believing historians propose that some married-woman sealings linked families eternally before parent-child sealing patterns were standardized. That model has contextual support in early adoptive sealing practices, but it remains a reconstruction because Joseph and the women left few contemporary explanations.

- Participant faith deserves evidentiary weight without being made conclusive. [[Zina D. H. Young]], [[Mary Elizabeth Rollins Lightner]], and most women who later discussed the practice remained believers and defended Joseph. Their testimony makes a purely cynical explanation less automatic, but sincere religious conviction cannot independently prove that the command came from God or remove the power imbalance.

- Believers may read Jacob 2:30 and [[Doctrine and Covenants 132]] as establishing that God can authorize an exception to ordinary monogamy. That is a coherent theological reading for someone who independently accepts Joseph's prophetic authority. History alone, however, cannot verify the divine speaker, and the married-woman cases are not expressly explained by the revelation's clearest plural-wife rule.

- Joseph's reported reluctance, the social danger, and the practice's cost to participants complicate a reduction to sexual appetite. They do not answer every ethical objection, establish divine authorization, or excuse secrecy from Emma.

The strongest believing response is therefore not denial: it is that the ceremonies occurred within a sincere but still-developing theology of eternal kinship, varied in character, and cannot all be reduced to conventional or sexual polyandry.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Frameworks — how this can fit, and how it cannot

<!-- SG:BEGIN frameworks -->
**What the sources require.** D&C 42 supplies an earlier norm of exclusive fidelity. Jacob 2 makes monogamy the stated rule and contains a disputed divine-command exception. D&C 132 later claims divine authority for plural marriage, identifies Joseph as sealing-key holder, addresses Emma and women given to Joseph, and describes additional wives in verse 61 as virgins vowed to no other man. The canon neither lists Joseph's married wives nor plainly explains simultaneous sealings to women with faithful living husbands. Historical sources establish the ceremonies. Neither “all were sexual marriages” nor “all were merely symbolic” is required by the evidence.

- **Mixed-practice model — independently supported and plausible.** Joseph entered unions of differing types: time and eternity, eternity only, and others whose terms are unrecoverable. Participant accounts and [[Plural Marriage in Kirtland and Nauvoo (Essay)]] support the categories. No category should be assigned to a disputed case without case-specific evidence.

- **Eternity-only kinship model — independently supported for some cases; plausible but unproved for others.** A woman remained in her civil household while a sealing connected her or her family to Joseph in the next life. Mary's testimony, Zina's eternity language, continued civil households, and early adoptive sealing patterns support this model. Extending it to every married woman would be ad hoc.

- **Marital-reassignment model — possible and case-dependent.** A civil marriage continued legally while Joseph's sealing displaced it eternally, perhaps because the first husband was considered unfaithful or outside the covenant. D&C 132:41-44 provides limited conceptual space. The model becomes ad hoc where no evidence indicates broken vows or reassignment.

- **Simultaneous conjugal-polyandry model — possible but disputed.** Marital terminology, confidentiality, and gaps in the record keep open the possibility that at least one overlapping union was sexual. The absence of clear contemporary direct testimony and the continued civil households prevent treating this as established.

- **Developing-ritual or dynastic-linking model — independently supported and plausible.** Nauvoo sealing practices were not yet standardized, and some horizontal or adoptive bonds were later replaced by familiar parent-child and spousal patterns. This explains diversity but does not itself resolve consent or reconcile each case with D&C 132:61.

- **Sincere divine-command model — possible as theology, not independently established historically.** Joseph and participants reported revelation and spiritual confirmation. A reader who independently accepts Joseph's prophetic authority can understand exceptions as divinely commanded. Historical inquiry can establish the reports and sincerity, not their supernatural source.

- **Sincere but fallible implementation model — plausible within a believing framework.** Joseph may have received genuine sealing principles while applying them imperfectly amid secrecy, marital conflict, and ritual development. The model accommodates faith and documented harm, but needs meaningful criteria for distinguishing revelation from error or it becomes unfalsifiable.

- **Prophetic-abuse model — plausible critical interpretation.** Joseph's concentration of revelatory, marital, and sealing authority, confidential proposals, Emma's incomplete knowledge, and D&C 132's promises and threats support concern about coercion or abuse of power. They do not directly establish conscious fraud, primary sexual motive, or each participant's inner voluntariness.

- **Levirate precedent — set aside as a poor fit.** Deuteronomy 25 presupposes that the first husband is dead and therefore does not authorize concurrent claims involving two living men.

- **Complete-denial model — set aside as ad hoc.** Denying all overlapping unions requires dismissing convergent participant testimony, witnesses, records, institutional acknowledgment, and agreement among historians who otherwise disagree sharply.
<!-- SG:END frameworks -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
**Established:** Joseph Smith entered confidential religious marriages or sealings with women who were civilly married to living men; at least some women and later institutional histories explicitly described the ceremonies as marriages or sealings to him. These unions lacked civil recognition. The canon's principal marriage revelation authorizes Joseph's plural-marriage system but does not identify or directly explain the married-woman cases.

**Strongly supported but qualified:** Some unions were understood as eternity-only, and most or all of the women continued living with their civil husbands. The practice varied enough that one explanation should not be imposed on every case. Emma's knowledge and consent were incomplete.

**Open:** The precise count and classification of disputed wives; which husbands knew or consented; whether any married-woman relationship included sex; and how Joseph himself reconciled such sealings with the virgin and prior-vow conditions of D&C 132:61.

**Ethical assessment:** The secrecy, marital overlap, asymmetric authority, and eternal stakes create legitimate concerns about fidelity, informed consent, and prophetic conflict of interest even if no sexual polyandry occurred. Eternity-only language narrows the accusation but does not make those concerns disappear.

**Matter of faith:** That God commanded or ratified the practice, that reported spiritual witnesses were divine, and that the sealings possess eternal efficacy. Historical evidence can document these claims and the participants' sincerity; it cannot independently establish their supernatural truth.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->

## Further study

<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
- Read D&C 132 completely, especially verses 7, 15-20, 37-52, and 61-65. Compare it with D&C 42:22-24 and Jacob 2:23-35 so neither the claimed exception nor the standing norms are omitted.

- Read [[Plural Marriage in Kirtland and Nauvoo (Essay)]] together with its footnotes. Attend to its qualifications about identification, household arrangements, sexuality, Emma's knowledge, and possible purposes.

- For named cases, begin with the documentary sources underlying [[Zina D. H. Young]] and [[Mary Elizabeth Rollins Lightner]]. Distinguish the date of each claimed ceremony from the later date on which a participant described it.

- Compare historians who share much of the evidence but disagree about classification and sexuality: Todd Compton's *In Sacred Loneliness* and Brian C. Hales's *Joseph Smith's Polygamy*. Use their citations to reach affidavits, correspondence, sealing records, and contemporaneous journals rather than relying only on their conclusions.

- Study the July 1843 manuscript of D&C 132 and its historical setting, then follow [[Eternal Marriage]], [[Why did Joseph Smith practice plural marriage]], and [[Why did Emma Smith deny plural marriage]].

- Use [[Mercy Thompson and the Revelation on Marriage]] as evidence that time-versus-eternity categories existed, while remembering that Mercy's first husband was deceased and her arrangement does not duplicate a living-husband case.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
