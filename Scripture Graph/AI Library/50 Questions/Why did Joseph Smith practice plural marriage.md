# Why did Joseph Smith practice plural marriage?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
The best-supported historical answer is that [[Joseph Smith Jr]] said he practiced plural marriage because God commanded him to restore an ancient, priesthood-authorized form of marriage and to extend eternal covenant relationships; [[D&C 132]] frames it as restoration, Abrahamic proving, fulfillment of promises, and multiplication of posterity. Contemporary documentation and later participant testimony establish that the practice was real, but they cannot establish that the command came from God or disclose one motive behind every union. Serious possibilities include sincere revelatory conviction, restorationist and sealing aims, personal or sexual interests, institutional power, self-deception, and mixed motives. Any adequate answer must also reckon with secrecy, misleading public denials, Emma's pain, young wives, women already civilly married, and the coercive language of D&C 132. Thus the sources establish Joseph's stated rationale much more firmly than they settle its divine origin or his complete inner motivation. See [[Plural Marriage (Gospel Topics)]] and [[Plural Marriage in Kirtland and Nauvoo (Essay)]].
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest supporting points

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- **A contemporary rationale exists.** The manuscript Revelation, 12 July 1843, now [[D&C 132]], is direct evidence that Joseph presented the practice as commanded restoration, covenant sealing, Abrahamic testing, and fulfillment of divine promises. Because plural marriage was already underway, it is evidence for his articulated rationale by 1843, not a neutral record of how every earlier relationship began.

- **The practice itself is multiply documented.** Contemporary records, family correspondence, sealing information, and later testimony from participating women and men converge on Joseph's plural marriages and his authorization of the practice. [[Plural Marriage (Gospel Topics)]] and [[Plural Marriage in Kirtland and Nauvoo (Essay)]] expressly acknowledge this; denial that Joseph practiced it is not a strong historical model.

- **The canonical framework is command-dependent.** [[Jacob 2]] makes monogamy the rule and permits an exception when God commands; [[D&C 132]] claims that such authorization was given through sealing keys. This explains the logic Joseph taught, although it cannot authenticate his claim to revelation.

- **Several later witnesses recalled reluctance and compulsion.** Accounts collected in connection with [[Mercy Thompson and the Revelation on Marriage]] report that Joseph resisted until warned by an angel. Multiple recollections support the conclusion that informed participants knew this explanation, but their retrospective character makes them only moderate evidence for the event itself.

- **The evidence resists a single practical explanation.** Some unions appear conjugal; others may have been eternity-only or family-linking sealings. Some women were single and others already married. This variation makes simple explanations such as reproduction, widow care, or sexuality alone inadequate as universal accounts.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Strongest objections

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- **Secrecy and public denial:** Joseph privately practiced plural marriage while publicly responding in ways that concealed or denied what ordinary hearers would have understood as plurality. Distinguishing authorized sealing from adultery or [[John C. Bennett]]'s spiritual wifery supplies context, but it does not remove the ethical problem of the misleading public-private divide. The issue belongs alongside [[Honesty]] and [[Why was the Nauvoo Expositor destroyed]].

- **Consent under unequal power:** Joseph was prophet, mayor, militia leader, and religious gatekeeper to people he approached. Some proposals reportedly carried spiritual promises or warnings. Helen Mar Kimball was fourteen, below even the era's usual marrying age. Later loyalty or defense does not retroactively dissolve the original age and authority imbalance; see [[Helen Mar Kimball]].

- **Emma's position:** Some marriages occurred without [[Emma Smith]]'s knowledge, and her acceptance was limited and fluctuating. D&C 132 invokes consent but threatens Emma and ultimately permits an exemption when a wife refuses. Critics therefore argue that the revelation sanctifies male authority more than meaningful female consent. See [[Why did Emma Smith deny plural marriage]].

- **Women with living husbands:** Joseph was sealed to women already civilly married. Eternity-only and dynastic explanations plausibly fit some cases, but the evidence does not permit every case to be assigned confidently to that category. See [[Did Joseph Smith marry other men's wives]] and [[Zina D. H. Young]].

- **Canonical tension:** [[Jacob 2]] condemns the Nephites' appeal to David and Solomon, whereas [[D&C 132]] treats authorized wives differently and limits David's condemned conduct to the Uriah-Bathsheba episode. Jacob 2:30 permits an exception, but the tension remains real.

- **Interested revelation:** The revelation granted Joseph exceptional marital authority and threatened dissenters. A critic can therefore read it as religious language legitimating his desires and protecting his position. No surviving source can exclude rationalization or conscious exploitation merely because Joseph used scriptural categories.

- **Weak fit for common practical defenses:** A surplus of women, general widow care, and production of known offspring do not adequately explain the Nauvoo practice. These popular explanations are not the principal reasons stated in D&C 132 and fit several marriages poorly.

- **Institutional retreat:** Legal and political pressure played a major role in the practice's eventual end. Critics see this as evidence that claimed revelation followed institutional necessity; the chronology in [[The Manifesto and the End of Plural Marriage (Essay)]] deserves examination rather than a merely devotional summary.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Responses

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
- A sincere-revelation model has genuine explanatory power: Joseph's restorationist theology, the costly risks of the practice, the 1843 revelation, and recollections of reluctance fit a person who believed himself commanded. They do not independently prove that God or an angel spoke.

- Confidentiality is understandable in a setting of intense social hostility, legal exposure, threatened violence, and unauthorized sexual systems falsely claiming Joseph's approval. That context can explain secrecy without proving predation, but it cannot make every public denial candid.

- Latter-day Saint interpreters distinguish ordinary civil marriage from several nineteenth-century sealing functions. Continued cohabitation between already-married women and their civil husbands supports an eternity-only or kinship interpretation in some cases. Responsible defense treats this as case-specific, because evidence of conjugal relations exists for at least some other unions.

- [[Helen Mar Kimball]] later defended plural marriage and described family-sealing purposes. Her testimony should be heard rather than dismissed as false consciousness; equally, it does not cancel the ethical concern created by her age or settle whether her youthful consent was adequately free.

- [[Jacob 2]] can be reconciled with [[D&C 132]] by reading Jacob's prohibition as the standing rule and Jacob 2:30 as a command-based exception, while reading its condemnation of David and Solomon as directed at unauthorized excess. This is a possible and coherent reconciliation, but D&C 132's narrower account of David still requires interpretation.

- Believers can distinguish divine authorization from flawless implementation: a prophet could sincerely receive a command yet administer it with fear, secrecy, cultural limitation, or moral error. That model preserves faith without calling every action exemplary, although deciding which failures are compatible with prophetic calling remains a theological judgment.

- A practice can be temporarily commanded and later withdrawn; therefore discontinuance does not logically refute its claimed origin. Conversely, appeal to changing revelation cannot by itself prove the origin divine. [[The Manifesto and the End of Plural Marriage (Essay)]] documents a history compatible with both prophetic-adaptation and institutional-survival models.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
**Established:** [[Joseph Smith Jr]], not [[Brigham Young]], introduced plural marriage among selected Latter-day Saints; he entered numerous plural sealings, authorized others, practiced confidentially, and dictated the revelation now [[D&C 132]] in July 1843. His stated rationale centered on divine command, restoration, sealing authority, Abrahamic testing, covenant promises, and posterity. The record also establishes severe marital conflict with [[Emma Smith]], a fourteen-year-old wife, sealings to women with living husbands, and evidence of conjugal relations in at least some unions. The central historical fact is settled even though the exact list and nature of every union are not.

**Open:** The origin and date of every early relationship, the temporal or sexual character of many sealings, the evidentiary weight of retrospective angel accounts, and the relative importance of theology, affection, sexuality, authority, fear, and self-interest remain disputed. The documents do not give direct access to Joseph's complete mental state.

**Matter of faith and moral judgment:** Historical evidence cannot demonstrate that God commanded plural marriage, that an angel enforced it, or that the sealings possess eternal efficacy. Nor can it mechanically decide how much flawed implementation a prophetic calling can bear. A thoughtful believer may conclude that Joseph obeyed a real command imperfectly while acknowledging deception, coercive structures, and suffering; a thoughtful critic may conclude that sincere or calculated human motives produced the revelation. Neither conclusion should erase the evidence favoring the other side. The trustworthy answer is therefore narrower than either pole: we know with high confidence what Joseph claimed and substantially what he did, but not by historical evidence alone whether God required it.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->

## Further study

<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Begin with [[D&C 132]], reading especially verses 1, 7, 18–19, 34–40, 45–48, 51–54, and 61–65, beside Jacob 2:23–35 and [[D&C 131]]. Then compare the official summaries [[Plural Marriage (Gospel Topics)]], [[Plural Marriage in Kirtland and Nauvoo (Essay)]], and [[Plural Marriage in The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Essay)]]. For the later history, read [[Plural Marriage and Families in Early Utah (Essay)]], [[The Manifesto and the End of Plural Marriage (Essay)]], and [[Did plural marriage continue after the Manifesto]].

Primary-source study should include the Joseph Smith Papers manuscript Revelation, 12 July 1843; relevant William Clayton journal entries; family correspondence involving [[Sarah Ann Whitney]] and [[Newel K. Whitney]]; the 26 May 1844 discourse reported by Thomas Bullock; the Nauvoo Expositor; and testimony from women in the Temple Lot litigation. Read these with attention to date, proximity, audience, and retrospective interest.

For contrasting modern reconstructions, compare Todd Compton, *In Sacred Loneliness*; Brian C. Hales, *Joseph Smith's Polygamy*; Richard Lyman Bushman, *Joseph Smith: Rough Stone Rolling*; and Kathryn M. Daynes, *More Wives Than One*. Follow individual evidence through [[Who was Fanny Alger]], [[Helen Mar Kimball]], [[Emily Dow Partridge]], [[Lucy Walker]], [[Mary Elizabeth Rollins Lightner]], [[Zina D. H. Young]], [[Did Joseph Smith marry other men's wives]], and [[Why did Emma Smith deny plural marriage]].

## Frameworks — how this can fit, and how it cannot
<!-- SG:BEGIN frameworks -->
**What the texts require:** [[Jacob 2]] explicitly makes one wife and no concubines the rule for its Nephite audience. Its conventional reading preserves an exception when God commands plurality to raise up seed, although a minority grammatical reading understands verse 30 differently; in either case, the chapter does not identify Joseph's marriages as authorized. [[D&C 132]] claims authorization through revelation and sealing keys and invokes restoration, covenant, Abrahamic testing, promises, and posterity. It does not say every Saint must practice plurality, that every sealing had the same purpose, or that widow care or a shortage of men required the practice. [[D&C 131]] connects exaltation with eternal marriage but does not expressly require multiple spouses. Jacob 2 and D&C 132 also describe David differently enough to create a genuine canonical tension. Whether D&C 132 is divine cannot be established by the document's own claim.

- **Sincere obedience to a perceived divine command — plausible; strongly supported as Joseph's professed rationale.** The 1843 revelation, his broader restorationist program, and later participant recollections predict command language, Abrahamic framing, reluctance, and risk. They do not independently establish supernatural authorship.

- **Restoration of biblical and priesthood order — strongly supported as Joseph's theological program.** D&C 132 expressly reinterprets patriarchal plurality and places valid unions under sealing authority. This explains Joseph's teaching; it remains compatible with either genuine revelation or human rationalization.

- **Eternal or dynastic covenant-building — plausible for some unions.** Sealing theology and relationships with women who continued living with civil husbands support family-linking or eternity-only interpretations in some cases. Extending that explanation to every union is unsupported because evidence indicates a temporal or conjugal dimension in some others. See [[Eternal Marriage]], [[Covenants]], and [[Did Joseph Smith marry other men's wives]].

- **Raising posterity — textually stated but weak as a comprehensive historical explanation.** Jacob 2:30, under its conventional reading, and D&C 132:63 mention seed or multiplication. No biological child through a plural wife has been securely established, and several unions were poorly structured for reproduction. The rationale may have been prospective or programmatic rather than descriptive of every marriage.

- **Literal angelic compulsion — possible within a believing worldview, but not independently established.** Multiple retrospective accounts support the conclusion that informed participants transmitted this explanation. Their dates, possible dependencies, and apologetic setting prevent them from verifying an objective angelic appearance. See [[Mercy Thompson and the Revelation on Marriage]].

- **Sincere religious misperception or self-deception — plausible.** This predicts genuine conviction, costly conduct, scriptural elaboration, and moral error without either divine command or calculated fraud. Joseph's private experience remains inaccessible, so the model cannot be confirmed directly.

- **Sexual or romantic desire rationalized through revelation — plausible as a contributing model, underdetermined as the sole cause.** Conjugal evidence in some unions, secrecy, repeated proposals, and personal benefits fit this explanation. Diverse sealing forms and the absence of a direct admission make a sexuality-only account too reductive.

- **Charismatic authority and family-network consolidation — possible and partly supported.** Several proposals joined families to Joseph and occurred within pronounced religious authority relationships. This model illuminates institutional and power dynamics but cannot alone explain every participant's agency, affection, or theological commitment.

- **Conscious fabrication for exploitation — possible but not established.** Misleading denials, asymmetric benefits, coercive language, and vulnerable recipients fit deliberate manipulation. Costs, diverse union types, Joseph's larger religious program, and the absence of a private admission prevent treating calculated fraud as demonstrated.

- **Mixed motives — plausible and probably less reductive, but elastic.** Sincerity, sexuality, affection, self-interest, fear, covenant-building, and institutional power may coexist and vary by relationship. Without case-specific evidence, this model can explain almost anything and therefore must not substitute for concrete judgments.

**Considered and set aside:** A numerical surplus of women and generalized widow care are rejected as principal explanations because they are not D&C 132's governing rationale and fit young or already-married women poorly. The claim that all sealings were nonsexual is rejected because evidence supports conjugal relations in some; the claim that all were ordinary cohabiting marriages is rejected because several women remained with civil husbands. Universal necessity of plurality for exaltation is rejected because D&C 131 and D&C 132:19 do not expressly require multiple spouses. The claim that Joseph never practiced plural marriage is rejected by convergent documentary and participant evidence. Later discontinuance under legal pressure is compatible with both continuing-revelation and institutional-adaptation models and therefore has little power to decide the practice's original source.
<!-- SG:END frameworks -->
