# How reliable is the biblical text?

## Concise answer

<!-- SG:BEGIN concise-answer -->
Textually, remarkably well-attested by ancient standards — and honestly
imperfect. The New Testament has ~5,800 Greek manuscripts with very early
fragments (P52, c. AD 125–175); the Old Testament's Masoretic transmission
is corroborated across a millennium by Qumran
([[Dead Sea Scrolls and Old Testament reliability]]). The overwhelming
majority of variants are trivial; a handful are substantial (Mark's long
ending, John 7:53–8:11, 1 John 5:7), and honest study flags them. This
matches the Restoration's nuanced position: the Bible is the word of God
"as far as it is translated correctly" ([[Articles of Faith 1#^aoff-1-8|A of F 1:8]]),
with 1 Nephi 13 teaching that "plain and precious things" were lost in
transmission — a claim about content selection and interpretation more than
about scribal copying accuracy.
<!-- SG:END concise-answer -->

## Strongest evidence

<!-- SG:BEGIN strongest-evidence -->
- Manuscript wealth and earliness (NT unmatched in classical literature).
- Qumran's confirmation of stable OT consonantal transmission.
- Independent versional evidence (Septuagint, Vulgate, Peshitta) allowing
  triangulation of early readings.
<!-- SG:END strongest-evidence -->

## Real textual issues to know

<!-- SG:BEGIN objections -->
- Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11 are absent from the earliest manuscripts.
- The OT shows genuine textual plurality in places (Jeremiah's two editions;
  Samuel's Qumran readings).
- Autographs do not survive; "reliability" means reliable transmission of an
  early text-form, not photographic access to originals.
<!-- SG:END objections -->

## Restoration perspective

<!-- SG:BEGIN responses -->
Latter-day Saint scripture affirms the Bible while denying transmission
inerrancy (1 Nephi 13:24–29; A of F 1:8) — a position that sits comfortably
with mainstream textual criticism's actual findings, and one reason this
vault treats textual criticism as friend, not threat. The JST is understood
as inspired commentary/restoration, mostly not as textual restoration in the
critical sense.
<!-- SG:END responses -->

## Honest assessment

<!-- SG:BEGIN assessment -->
For study purposes: trust the text broadly, know the famous variant passages,
and read margin notes in modern editions. The interesting Restoration
questions are about canon and interpretation, not copying fidelity.
<!-- SG:END assessment -->
