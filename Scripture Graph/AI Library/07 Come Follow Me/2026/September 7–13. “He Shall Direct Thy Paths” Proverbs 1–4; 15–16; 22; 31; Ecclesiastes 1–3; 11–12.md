# September 7–13. “He Shall Direct Thy Paths”: Proverbs 1–4; 15–16; 22; 31; Ecclesiastes 1–3; 11–12

*Come, Follow Me 2026* · [source](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/37?lang=eng)

You might think of the book of Proverbs as a collection of wise counsel from loving parents (see [[Proverbs 1#^prov-1-8|Proverbs 1:8]]). Its main message is that if you seek wisdom—especially God’s wisdom—life will go well. But Proverbs is followed by the book of Ecclesiastes, which seems to say, “It’s not that simple.” The Preacher quoted in Ecclesiastes observed that he “gave [his] heart to know wisdom” but still found “vexation of spirit” and “much grief” ([[Ecclesiastes 1#^eccl-1-17|Ecclesiastes 1:17–18]]). In various ways, the book asks, “Can there be real meaning in a world where everything seems vain, temporary, and uncertain?”

And yet, while the two books look at life from different perspectives, they teach similar truths. Ecclesiastes declares: “Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man” ([[Ecclesiastes 12#^eccl-12-13|Ecclesiastes 12:13]]). This is the same principle found throughout Proverbs: “Trust in the Lord with all thine heart” ([[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-5|Proverbs 3:5]]; see also [[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-7|verse 7]]). Life is always better—if not always perfect—when we trust and follow the Lord Jesus Christ.

For an overview of these books, see “[Proverbs, book of](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/proverbs-book-of?lang=eng)” and “[[AI Library/02 Gospel Topics/Bible Dictionary/Ecclesiastes (Bible Dictionary)|Ecclesiastes]]” in the Bible Dictionary.

## Ideas for Learning at Home and at Church

*[[Proverbs 1|Proverbs 1–4]]; [[Proverbs 15|15–16]]; [[Ecclesiastes 1|Ecclesiastes 1–3]]; [[Ecclesiastes 11|11–12]]*

### “Incline thine ear unto wisdom.”

The books of Proverbs and Ecclesiastes are filled with insights about wisdom. Consider marking the word “wisdom” and related words, like “knowledge” and “understanding,” as you find them in [[Proverbs 1|Proverbs 1–4]]; [[Proverbs 15|15–16]]; [[Ecclesiastes 1|Ecclesiastes 1–3]]; [[Ecclesiastes 11|11–12]]. How do these chapters affect the way you think about wisdom? Based on what you find, how would you describe the wisdom that “the Lord giveth”? ([[Proverbs 2#^prov-2-6|Proverbs 2:6]]). What blessings come from God’s wisdom?

See also [[Matthew 7#^matt-7-24|Matthew 7:24–27]]; [[Matthew 25#^matt-25-1|25:1–13]].

*[[Proverbs 1#^prov-1-7|Proverbs 1:7]]; [[Proverbs 2#^prov-2-5|2:5]]; [[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-7|3:7]]; [[Proverbs 15#^prov-15-33|15:33]]; [[Proverbs 16#^prov-16-6|16:6]]; [[Proverbs 31#^prov-31-30|31:30]]; [[Ecclesiastes 12#^eccl-12-13|Ecclesiastes 12:13]]*

### “Be not wise in thine own eyes: fear the Lord.”

Another theme found throughout Proverbs and Ecclesiastes is “the fear of the Lord.” Watch for this phrase as you read. What do you feel it means to fear the Lord? Look for insights in Elder David A. Bednar’s message “[[AI Library/10 General Conference/2015/April/Therefore They Hushed Their Fears (Elder David A. Bednar, April 2015)|Therefore They Hushed Their Fears]]” (*Ensign* or *Liahona*, May 2015, 46–49). How is fear of the Lord different from other kinds of fear?

See also [[Proverbs 8#^prov-8-13|Proverbs 8:13]].

*[[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-5|Proverbs 3:5–7]]; [[Proverbs 4|4]]*

### “Trust in the Lord with all thine heart.”

How would you try to persuade someone that it’s better to “trust in the Lord” than to “lean … unto [our] own understanding”? ([[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-5|Proverbs 3:5]]). What comparisons or object lessons would you use? As you ponder [[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-5|Proverbs 3:5–7]], think of ways you might complete sentences like these: *Trusting the Lord is like … Leaning on our own understanding is like …*

Why is it unwise to lean on our own understanding? How have you discovered that the Lord is trustworthy?

Even so, we might find it difficult to trust the Lord at times. Why is that? Elder Gerrit W. Gong suggests some reasons, along with helpful counsel, in “[[AI Library/10 General Conference/2021/October/Trust Again (Elder Gerrit W. Gong, October 2021)|Trust Again]]” (*Liahona*, Nov. 2021, 97–99). What stories or teachings do you find in this message that might help someone restore their trust in the Lord?

Both [[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-6|Proverbs 3:6]] and [[Proverbs 4|Proverbs 4]] compare life to a “path” or a “way.” What do you think it means to let the Lord “direct [your] paths”? ([[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-6|Proverbs 3:6]]). What do you find in [[Proverbs 4|chapter 4]] that helps you “ponder the path of [your] feet”? ([[Proverbs 4#^prov-4-26|verse 26]]). For example, what do [[Proverbs 4#^prov-4-11|verses 11–12]] and [[Proverbs 4#^prov-4-18|18–19]] teach about the blessings of following the right path? What do [[Proverbs 4#^prov-4-26|verses 26–27]] mean to you?

See also [[2 Nephi 31#^2ne-31-18|2 Nephi 31:18–21]]; “[Lord, I Would Follow Thee](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/lord-i-would-follow-thee?lang=eng),” *Hymns*, no. 220.

*[[Proverbs 15|Proverbs 15]]; [[Proverbs 16#^prov-16-24|16:24–32]]*

### “A soft answer turneth away wrath.”

Some of the ideas in [[Proverbs 15|Proverbs 15–16]] might inspire you to improve the way you communicate with others, especially loved ones. For example, think about specific times when “a soft answer” helped to “[turn] away wrath” ([[Proverbs 15#^prov-15-1|Proverbs 15:1]]).

You might also think of times when the Savior exemplified the teachings in [[Proverbs 15#^prov-15-1|Proverbs 15:1–4, 18]] (see [[Mark 12#^mark-12-13|Mark 12:13–17]]; [[John 8#^john-8-1|John 8:1–11]]). How can you follow His example as you interact with others?

How might the counsel in [[Proverbs 15|Proverbs 15]]; [[Proverbs 16#^prov-16-24|16:24–32]] apply to today’s digital communication? See if you can find a verse in these chapters that you could rephrase as advice about interacting over social media or through text.

See also Neil L. Andersen, “[[AI Library/10 General Conference/2022/April/Following Jesus Being a Peacemaker (Elder Neil L. Andersen, April 2022)|Following Jesus: Being a Peacemaker]],” *Liahona*, May 2022, 17–20; Ronald A. Rasband, “[[AI Library/10 General Conference/2024/April/Words Matter (Elder Ronald A. Rasband, April 2024)|Words Matter]],” *Liahona*, May 2024, 70–77; *For the Strength of Youth: A Guide for Making Choices* (2026), [30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/for-the-strength-of-youth/06-love-god-love-your-neighbor?lang=eng).

*[[Proverbs 31#^prov-31-10|Proverbs 31:10–31]]*

### “A woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised.”

[[Proverbs 31#^prov-31-10|Proverbs 31:10–31]] describes “a virtuous woman,” or a woman of great spiritual strength, capability, and influence. You might try summarizing in your own words what each of these verses says about virtuous women. Which of these traits can you emulate?

## Ideas for Teaching Children

*[[Proverbs 1#^prov-1-7|Proverbs 1:7]]; [[Proverbs 2#^prov-2-5|2:5]]; [[Proverbs 15#^prov-15-33|15:33]]; [[Proverbs 16#^prov-16-6|16:6]]; [[Ecclesiastes 12#^eccl-12-13|Ecclesiastes 12:13]]*

### To “fear God” means to love and obey Him.

To help your children understand passages about fearing God, you could substitute the word “fear” with words like *reverence*, *love*, or *obedience* (see also [[Hebrews 12#^heb-12-28|Hebrews 12:28]]). How does this affect the way we think about these verses? How do we show that we fear the Lord?

*[[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-5|Proverbs 3:5–7]]*

### I can trust in the Lord with all my heart.

You and your children could make up actions to go with [[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-5|Proverbs 3:5–7]], like making a heart with your hands, leaning to one side, walking in place, and pointing to your eyes. How can we show that we trust Jesus Christ with all our hearts?

To show what it means to “lean not unto thine own understanding” ([[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-5|Proverbs 3:5]]), consider inviting your children to lean against something sturdy and stable, like a wall. Then they could try leaning against something that is not sturdy, like a broom. Or they could try leaning a stick or a pencil against objects of varying sturdiness, such as a book or a piece of paper. Why is it important to “trust in the Lord” ([[Proverbs 3#^prov-3-5|verse 5]]) and not our “own understanding”?

*[[Proverbs 15#^prov-15-1|Proverbs 15:1, 18]]*

### I can use kind words.

Perhaps you and your children can think of situations when you or they might feel angry. Read [[Proverbs 15#^prov-15-1|Proverbs 15:1]] together, and help your children apply this verse to the situations they thought of. Maybe they could practice some “soft answers.” A song about kindness, such as “[Love Is Spoken Here](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/love-is-spoken-here?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 190–91), could reinforce this principle.

To learn what it means to be “slow to anger” ([[Proverbs 15#^prov-15-18|Proverbs 15:18]]), you and your children could share times when you (or someone you know) felt angry but chose to be kind. Help your children think of things we can be *quick to* if we are “slow to anger.” For example, we can be quick to think about Jesus, to ask Heavenly Father for help, to think of a Primary song, or, if possible, to walk away.
