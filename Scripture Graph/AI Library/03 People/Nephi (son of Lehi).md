# Nephi (son of Lehi)

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Nephi son of Lehi is the first-person narrator and principal figure of [[1 Nephi 1]] through [[2 Nephi 33]]. A son of [[Lehi (father of Nephi)]] and [[Sariah]], he presents himself as a seeker of revelation, custodian of sacred records, interpreter of prophecy, shipbuilder, teacher of Christ, and founder of the community later called the people of Nephi. His story also includes profound family rupture, armed preparation, political leadership, and the morally difficult killing of [[Laban (keeper of the plates)]]. Because most biographical information comes through Nephi's own purposeful and selective record (1 Nephi 9:2–6), his account is best read as theological autobiography rather than independent biography.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
Nephi's arc begins with a deliberate response to Lehi's preaching. He says he sought the Lord, had his heart softened, and believed his father (1 Nephi 2:16), after which the Lord addressed him concerning faithfulness and future leadership (1 Nephi 2:19–24). During [[Obtaining the brass plates]], his declaration that God prepares a way for commanded tasks (1 Nephi 3:7) is followed by two failed attempts and then by the killing of Laban (1 Nephi 4:10–18). The text records Nephi's own reluctance before giving the theological and political reasons by which he understood the command; its claimed divine authorization does not remove the episode's moral difficulty for readers.

Nephi does not remain dependent on Lehi's visions. He seeks to see and know for himself (1 Nephi 10:17; 11:1), receives an expansive vision centered on [[Jesus Christ]], and interprets the iron rod as the word of God (1 Nephi 15:23–24). His wilderness leadership joins practical initiative to prayer: when his bow breaks, he makes another and asks Lehi where to hunt (1 Nephi 16:18–31). At [[Bountiful (Old World)]] he says God commands him to build a ship and teaches him how (1 Nephi 17:7–11; 18:1–4); the completed [[Voyage to the promised land]] includes his brothers binding him before the party reaches land (1 Nephi 18:10–23).

Following Lehi's death, conflict becomes permanent. Nephi reports that divine warning led him and those who trusted the warnings to flee (2 Nephi 5:1–6). The resulting settlement is called the [[Land of Nephi]]; Nephi describes defensive preparations, a temple, skilled labor, and the consecration of [[Jacob (son of Lehi)]] and [[Joseph (son of Lehi)]] as priests and teachers (2 Nephi 5:8–16, 26). His people desired him as king, though Nephi says he was himself 'desirous that they should have no king' and acted only 'according to that which was in my power' (2 Nephi 5:18); Jacob later identifies him as their first ruler and reports a successor was anointed before his death, with 'Nephi' becoming a royal throne name for later rulers rather than a claim about unbroken hereditary succession (Jacob 1:9–12).

Nephi is also a self-conscious editor. [[1 Nephi 9]] distinguishes large plates devoted principally to political history from small plates devoted principally to ministry, while acknowledging that the present account is selective and was commanded 'for a wise purpose' he says he did not know. Jacob's receipt of those plates (Jacob 1:1–4) and Mormon's later account of finding and including that smaller record (Words of Mormon 1:3–7) continue that internal recordkeeping framework — strong evidence of a sustained, self-conscious compositional structure, though this alone does not establish the record's ancient origin. Nephi's doctrinal center is Christ: he reports visions of the Messiah's birth, ministry, rejection, and death (1 Nephi 11:18–33) and says his people write of Christ so their children may know the source of forgiveness (2 Nephi 25:19–26).

Name matching requires care. The Nephi active in Helaman 7–11 and 16 is [[Nephi (son of Helaman)]], a later prophet and chief judge, although Helaman 7:7's 'my father Nephi' is a retrospective, ancestral reference back to the founder rather than a claim about a living contemporary. The Nephi introduced at the opening of 3 Nephi, and the record-keeping Nephi of 4 Nephi 1:19, are still later figures — [[Nephi (the disciple)]] and his descendants. Many other occurrences of 'Nephi' in the canon (e.g., Alma 45, 50, 62; Mosiah 25) name only the land, city, or people founded in his memory, not the man himself.

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
General conference speakers most often use Nephi as a model of personally sought revelation. [[Continuing Revelation (President Henry B. Eyring, October 2014)]] contrasts Nephi's receptive response to Lehi's dream and commandments with the resistance of other family members. [[An Easiness and Willingness to Believe (Elder Michael T. Ringwood, October 2009)]] emphasizes Nephi seeking the Lord until his heart was softened (1 Nephi 2:16, 19), while [[Prepared to Obtain Every Needful Thing (Elder David A. Bednar, April 2019)]] highlights his desire to see, hear, and know Lehi's vision for himself.

Other talks draw lessons from particular episodes. [[Be Your Best Self (President Thomas S. Monson, April 2009)]] uses Nephi's identification of the iron rod with God's word (1 Nephi 15:23–24). [[Pray, He Is There (President Susan H. Porter, April 2024)]] presents his response to the broken bow — making a new bow and asking Lehi where to hunt — as a pattern of acting and then seeking guidance. [[The Things of My Soul (Elder Ronald A. Rasband, October 2021)]] draws on Nephi's later declaration, 'upon these I write the things of my soul' (2 Nephi 4:15), part of his closing psalm of praise and self-examination. These are devotional applications of the narrative; they do not independently verify its historical claims.

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
Read [[Lehi (father of Nephi)]], [[Sariah]], [[Laman]], [[Lemuel]], and [[Sam (brother of Nephi)]] for the family relationships that shape Nephi's story. [[Jacob (son of Lehi)]], [[Joseph (son of Lehi)]], and [[Zoram (servant of Laban)]] show how his community and record continued beyond the original household.

For the principal turning points, study [[Obtaining the brass plates]], [[Lehi's dream of the tree of life]], [[Bountiful (Old World)]], [[Voyage to the promised land]], and [[Land of Nephi]]. [[Revelation]], [[Records and Sacred Writings]], and [[Exodus and Deliverance]] illuminate the major theological and literary patterns in his account. [[1 Nephi 9]], [[2 Nephi 5]], and [[Jacob 1]] are especially important for understanding his editorial program, political legacy, and death.

Keep [[Nephi (son of Helaman)]] and [[Nephi (the disciple)]] nearby when following index results: they are distinct later figures who share his name.
