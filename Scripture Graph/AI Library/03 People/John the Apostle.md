# John the Apostle

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
John the Apostle is the son of Zebedee, brother of [[James (son of Zebedee)]], and one of the [[Apostles]] whom [[Jesus Christ]] called from a fishing livelihood (Matthew 4:21–22; Mark 1:19–20). The Synoptic Gospels repeatedly place him with [[Peter (apostle)]] and James at pivotal events, including the raising of Jairus's daughter, the [[Transfiguration]], and [[Gethsemane]] (Mark 5:37; 9:2; 14:33). Acts later presents John beside Peter in temple ministry, public witness, and ministry among Samaritans (Acts 3:1–10; 4:13–20; 8:14–17), while Galatians 2:9 names a John—ordinarily understood as this apostle—among the Jerusalem pillars.

Christian and Latter-day Saint tradition also identifies John son of Zebedee with the Fourth Gospel's unnamed disciple whom Jesus loved and with John of Patmos. Those identifications form a coherent received portrait but are not equally explicit in the New Testament: the beloved disciple is never personally named, Revelation calls its seer only John, 1 John is anonymous, and 2–3 John name their sender only as “the elder.” Restoration scripture makes additional canon-internal claims: 1 Nephi 14:19–27 identifies an apostle named John as an apocalyptic writer; [[Doctrine and Covenants 7]] presents John as granted a continuing ministry until Christ's coming; and [[3 Nephi 28]] explicitly compares the three disciples' desire with that of “John, my beloved.” John the Apostle should not be confused with [[John the Baptist]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
John begins as a son of Zebedee and fisherman who leaves his work to follow Jesus (Matthew 4:21–22; [[Mark 1]]). Jesus appoints him among the Twelve and gives James and John the name “Boanerges,” interpreted as “sons of thunder” (Mark 3:13–19). With Peter and James, John witnesses the raising of Jairus's daughter, the [[Transfiguration]], and Jesus's anguish in [[Gethsemane]] (Mark 5:37; 9:2; 14:33). The Gospels also preserve less flattering episodes: John tries to stop an unaffiliated exorcist, James and John propose calling fire on a Samaritan village, and the brothers seek honored places in Jesus's glory. Jesus answers with correction, suffering, and service rather than status (Luke 9:49–55; Mark 10:35–45). Luke also names Peter and John as those sent to prepare the Passover (Luke 22:7–13).

Acts presents John as a durable public witness. He joins Peter in the healing at the temple ([[Acts 3]]), faces rulers without abandoning their testimony ([[Acts 4]]), and prays and lays hands on baptized Samaritans, who receive the Holy Ghost ([[Acts 8]]). Galatians 2:9 lists a John with James and Cephas among the reputed pillars; this John is ordinarily identified as the apostle, although Paul supplies no patronymic there.

The Fourth Gospel requires a separate evidentiary category. Its unnamed disciple whom Jesus loved reclines near Jesus at the supper, receives responsibility for Jesus's mother at the cross, reaches the empty tomb with Peter, and is connected with the Gospel's testimony and writing (John 13:23–26; [[John 19]]; [[John 20]]; [[John 21]]). The Gospel never names this disciple John. John 21 also corrects a rumor arising from Jesus's conditional saying: Jesus had not directly declared that the disciple would never die (John 21:20–24). Identifying this figure with John son of Zebedee is ancient Christian tradition and established Latter-day Saint usage, but it remains an interpretation rather than an explicit naming in the Gospel.

Restoration scripture develops the canonical portrait. 1 Nephi 14:19–27 identifies an apostle named John as the writer of a reserved apocalyptic record. [[Doctrine and Covenants 7]] presents John asking to remain so that he might bring souls to Christ and receiving a commission extending until Christ's coming. [[3 Nephi 28]] says the three disciples desired the same thing that “John, my beloved” had desired; verses 7–9 supply the shared tarrying and ministerial context even though the chapter does not narrate John's original request. Doctrine and Covenants 27:12 and 128:20 further place John with Peter and James in the [[Priesthood Restoration]]. These are firm claims within the Latter-day Saint canon; their force as externally established history depends on accepting the revelatory authority of those texts.

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
Modern conference teaching uses John chiefly as a witness of Christ while generally adopting traditional Johannine authorship and identity:

- In [[The Power of Spiritual Momentum (President Russell M. Nelson, April 2022)]], President Russell M. Nelson cites John 20:31 to emphasize that the Savior's miracles were recorded to foster belief that Jesus is the Christ.
- [[But We Heeded Them Not (Elder David A. Bednar, April 2022)]] invokes the Apostle John's description of Jesus Christ as the Word.
- [[The Character of Christ (President D. Todd Christofferson, April 2026)]] recalls Jesus caring for his mother from the cross and placing her in the care of “his beloved Apostle John,” applying the traditional identification of the beloved disciple.
- [[Mrs. Patton—the Story Continues (President Thomas S. Monson, October 2007)]] appeals to the testimony of John the Revelator alongside that of Paul.

Earlier reports likewise use writings traditionally attributed to John. [[Conference Report, April 1905 (General Conference (full report), April 1905)]] invokes 1 John 3:2 concerning divine sonship and future transformation, while [[Conference Report, October 1920 (General Conference (full report), October 1920)]] uses 1 John 4:11 to teach reciprocal love. These devotional uses document longstanding Latter-day Saint reception of the Johannine writings; they do not independently resolve historical questions about whether John son of Zebedee personally composed the Gospel, epistles, or Revelation.

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
Begin with [[Mark 1]], [[Mark 9]], and [[Acts 3]] for John's call, formation, and ministry beside [[Peter (apostle)]]. [[James (son of Zebedee)]] is essential as John's brother and repeated companion. Read [[John 19]], [[John 20]], and [[John 21]] together for the beloved disciple at the cross, empty tomb, and final lakeside scene, remembering that those chapters do not name him John.

Then compare [[John 21]] with [[Doctrine and Covenants 7]] and [[3 Nephi 28]]. That sequence distinguishes the New Testament's conditional saying and corrected rumor from Restoration scripture's affirmative account of John's request and the parallel commission examined in [[Who were the Three Nephites]]. [[1 Nephi 14]] supplies the Latter-day Saint canonical identification of an apostle named John as an apocalyptic writer. Follow [[Priesthood Restoration]] for John's latter-day role with Peter and James, and consult [[Revelation]] and [[Patmos]] with the caution that Revelation itself does not call its John a son of Zebedee or one of the Twelve. See [[John the Baptist]] separately to avoid confusing two distinct New Testament figures.
