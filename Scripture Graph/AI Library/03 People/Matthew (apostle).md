# Matthew (apostle)

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Matthew is securely identified in the New Testament as one of Jesus's Twelve Apostles and, in Matthew 10:3, as “the publican,” or tax collector. His individual canonical profile is brief: Jesus calls him from the receipt of custom (Matthew 9:9), he receives authority and a missionary charge with the Twelve (Matthew 10:1–8), and Acts still lists him among the apostles after the Ascension (Acts 1:13). Mark 2:14 and Luke 5:27 instead name the tax collector in their parallel call accounts “Levi.” Matthew and Levi are traditionally identified, but the text never explicitly equates the names. Likewise, traditional attribution of the Gospel to the apostle is ancient but is not the same thing as an explicit statement of authorship within the narrative.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
In [[Matthew 9]], Jesus sees Matthew at the receipt of custom, says “Follow me,” and Matthew rises and follows (Matthew 9:9). The narrative supplies no prior preparation, inner monologue, or later individual speech. Matthew 10:3 then identifies him as both a member of the Twelve and “the publican.” As part of that body he shares the authority and mission described in Matthew 10:1, 5–8.

The Synoptic comparison requires care. [[Mark 2]] and [[Luke 5]] place a tax collector named Levi in substantially the same narrative position (Mark 2:14; Luke 5:27–29). Luke says Levi “left all” and made a feast, while Matthew 9:10 does not identify whose house hosted the meal. Treating those details as Matthew's depends on the traditional Matthew-Levi identification; it is a reasonable harmonization, not an explicit equation in any cited verse.

Matthew is otherwise presented mainly through group references. He appears in the apostolic rosters (Matthew 10:3; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:15) and among the apostles gathered after the Ascension (Acts 1:13). He is also reasonably included among the eleven commissioned by the risen Jesus in [[Matthew 28]], although Matthew 28:16 does not repeat their names. The canon does not narrate Matthew's later travels or death.
<!-- SG:END scripture-profile -->

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
In [[The Twelve (President Boyd K. Packer, April 2008)]], President Boyd K. Packer names “Matthew, a publican, or tax collector” while reviewing the original Twelve. The treatment follows Matthew 10:3 and emphasizes Matthew's place in an apostolic quorum rather than developing a separate biography. [[Teachings of Jesus (Elder Dallin H. Oaks, October 2011)]] cites Matthew 10:1 to teach that Jesus gave priesthood power to His Apostles; because Matthew is named in the roster immediately following, that corporate teaching includes him. Talks in the supplied context about the Pharisee and the publican concern the unnamed publican of Jesus's parable, not Matthew, and should not be used as biographical evidence about him.
<!-- SG:END conference -->

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
Read [[Matthew 9]] beside [[Mark 2]] and [[Luke 5]] to examine the Matthew-Levi question without concealing the difference in names. [[Matthew 10]] locates Matthew within the [[Apostles]] and records the authority and mission given by [[Jesus Christ]] to the Twelve. Acts 1:13 provides the last explicit canonical mention of Matthew. [[Missionary Work]] and [[Conversion]] illuminate the principal themes of his sparse narrative. For the external authorship tradition, consult [[Papias]] while keeping separate the apostle, the ancient attribution, and the authorship of the surviving Greek Gospel. [[Why do the Gospels differ from one another]] provides the broader interpretive setting for the divergent names in the call accounts.
