# Martin Harris

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Martin Harris was an early participant in the Restoration whose roles joined witness, material sacrifice, correction, and institutional responsibility. [[D&C 5]] begins with his request for a witness that Joseph Smith possessed the plates and makes the promised manifestation conditional. Restoration historical sources identify Harris as one of the Three Witnesses and as the financial backer whose property helped fund publication of the Book of Mormon; [[D&C 19]] commands that financing but does not name him in its body text. Other revelations directly name him as an example in laying money before the bishop, a steward over revelations and property, a participant in a binding covenant, and a high councilor. The same record is not uniformly flattering: D&C 58:39 calls him to repent for seeking worldly praise. Later sources report a prolonged period outside the Church followed by rebaptism and renewed public affirmation of his witness. His sincerity, sacrifice, and continued profession are historically significant, but they do not by themselves establish the plates' antiquity, physical character, or divine origin.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
Martin first appears by name in [[D&C 5]], which is occasioned by his desire for a witness that Joseph Smith possessed the plates (D&C 5:1). The promised view is conditional upon humility, mighty prayer, faith, sincerity, acknowledgment of wrongdoing, covenant keeping, and obedience (D&C 5:24-29). His permitted declaration is narrowly defined: he may say, “I have seen them, and they have been shown unto me by the power of God” (D&C 5:26). The text therefore presents witnessing as accountable service, while D&C 5:32 warns that failure to humble himself would lead to transgression. The passage addresses Joseph and speaks of Harris in the third person; that is a feature of its literary form, not independent evidence about every detail of how Harris received it.

[[Joseph Smith—History 1]] records that Harris took copied characters to New York (Joseph Smith—History 1:63). It then names Professor [[Charles Anthon]] within an account explicitly introduced as Harris's report to Joseph (Joseph Smith—History 1:63-65). The canon thus establishes what Joseph says Harris reported, not independently what Anthon said or concluded. Applying Isaiah 29:11-12 to this encounter is a Latter-day Saint interpretive claim: neither Isaiah 29 nor 2 Nephi 27 names Harris, Anthon, or a nineteenth-century setting. A believing fulfillment reading and critical literary-shaping explanations both require evidence beyond the absence or presence of those names.

The later revelations broaden Harris's responsibilities. [[D&C 58]] makes him an example in laying money before the bishop (D&C 58:35), anticipates further direction about his inheritance (D&C 58:38), and tells him to repent because he seeks worldly praise (D&C 58:39). [[D&C 52]] assigns him to travel with [[Edward Partridge]], [[Sidney Rigdon]], and [[Joseph Smith Jr]] (D&C 52:24). [[D&C 70]] names him among six stewards over the revelations (D&C 70:1-4), and [[D&C 82]] includes him among nine men bound by covenant (D&C 82:11). [[D&C 102]] records his high-council service (D&C 102:3, 34). [[D&C 104]] assigns him a land stewardship and directs him to devote money to proclaiming God's words as Joseph directs (D&C 104:24, 26).

Historical sources strongly connect Harris with the lost manuscript discussed in [[D&C 3]] and [[D&C 10]], with the unnamed recipient financing publication in [[D&C 19]], and with the witness event associated with [[D&C 17]]. Those identifications should not be rewritten as names appearing in the revelations' body text. Read together, the sources support a portrait of a consequential but corrigible participant whose conviction involved substantial material risk and public responsibility. They support study of his credibility and commitments; they do not alone prove the Book of Mormon's origin.

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
The supplied conference sources treat Martin Harris retrospectively rather than adding canonical doctrine about him. [[Conference Report, October 1918 (General Conference (full report), October 1918)]] preserves a speaker's recollection that he heard Harris reaffirm his Book of Mormon witness publicly in 1870 after many years outside the Church. This is relevant evidence for what Harris continued to profess, but it is a recollection published decades later, not a complete contemporaneous transcript or independent verification of the plates.

[[Conference Report, October 1924 (General Conference (full report), October 1924)]] reports plans for a monument at Harris's grave in Clarkston, Utah, documenting his later institutional commemoration. [[Conference Report, October 1925 (General Conference (full report), October 1925)]] describes him as a substantial farmer near [[Palmyra]] who was considered honest by his neighbors. That favorable reputation is useful attributed context, but its late, faith-promoting setting limits its independent evidentiary weight, and general honesty cannot determine the objective nature or divine origin of Harris's reported witness experience.

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
Begin with [[D&C 5]] and [[Witnesses of the Book of Mormon]] for the conditional promise and Harris's historical witness role, then read [[How reliable are the Book of Mormon witnesses]] for the competing physical, visionary, and testimonial explanations. [[The Contributions of Martin Harris]] surveys his wider service, while [[05 All Is Lost]] and [[What happened to the lost 116 pages]] examine the manuscript crisis and distinguish later identification from what [[D&C 3]] and [[D&C 10]] actually name.

For the people central to these events, continue with [[Joseph Smith Jr]], [[Oliver Cowdery]], [[David Whitmer]], and [[Charles Anthon]]. The less familiar canonical profile appears in [[D&C 58]], [[D&C 70]], [[D&C 82]], [[D&C 102]], and [[D&C 104]], which present Harris as a corrected disciple, financial and revelatory steward, covenant participant, and councilor. [[Consecration]], [[Repentance]], and [[Testimony]] provide the most direct thematic pathways.
