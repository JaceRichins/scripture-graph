# Jesus Christ

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Jesus Christ is the central saving figure across the Old and New Testaments, the Book of Mormon, and Doctrine and Covenants. John's prologue presents him as the incarnate Word, present in the beginning and active in creation ([[John 1]]); the Gospels show him as a genuinely mortal figure — wearied by travel, loving particular friends, crucified and buried — who is also proclaimed the risen Son of God ([[John 11]]; [[John 19]]; [[John 20]]). Paul builds a mature theology from his death and resurrection: reconciliation initiated by God while people were still 'without strength' and 'enemies' (Romans 5:6-11), and a deliberate Adam-Christ typology in which Adam is explicitly called 'the figure of him that was to come' (Romans 5:14). The Book of Mormon anticipates him six centuries before his birth ([[2 Nephi 25]]) and narrates his post-resurrection ministry among the Nephites ([[3 Nephi 19]]) and the church his disciples formed in his name ([[4 Nephi 1]]). Latter-day Saint teaching further identifies the premortal Christ with Jehovah of the Old Testament — a consistent doctrinal claim across a century of conference addresses, but an interpretation of Old Testament theophanies rather than something those texts name explicitly, and it should not be read as assigning every biblical occurrence of 'God' or 'the Lord' to Jesus without attention to context.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
John's prologue places Christ's identity before his mortal birth: the Word exists “in the beginning,” participates in creation, becomes flesh, and is identified in the prologue with Jesus Christ (John 1:1–3, 14, 17). The Gospel narratives simultaneously portray a genuinely embodied life—Jesus grows weary and asks for water (John 4:6–7), loves Martha, Mary, and [[Lazarus]] (John 11:5), teaches, confronts opposition, and submits to arrest and crucifixion (John 18:1–5; John 19:1, 16–18). [[John 11]] makes both his character and claim visible: he loves and grieves, yet declares himself the resurrection and the life and calls Lazarus from the tomb (John 11:5, 25, 35, 43–44).

The passion is not treated as an isolated tragedy. [[John 19]] narrates Jesus's death, while [[John 20]] narrates the empty tomb and appearances of the risen Jesus (John 19:30; John 20:1, 16, 19–20). Paul interprets that death as God's initiative toward sinners and enemies, resulting in reconciliation through Christ (Romans 5:6–11). He explicitly contrasts Adam and Christ: Adam is associated with sin, condemnation, and death; Christ with grace, justification, obedience, and life (Romans 5:14–19; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22). First Corinthians 5:7 also calls Christ “our passover,” linking his sacrifice with [[Passover]] while using the removal of leaven to demand communal holiness.

The Book of Mormon portrays prophets centuries before Jesus as anticipating the Messiah's birth, rejection, resurrection, and infinite atonement ([[1 Nephi 10]]; [[2 Nephi 25]]). Those dates and fulfillments are claims internal to the record and should not be mistaken for independent proof of its antiquity. Within its narrative, the objection that Christ would appear only near Jerusalem (Helaman 16:18–19) receives an answer when the risen Jesus appears at Bountiful ([[3 Nephi 19]]; see [[Christ's appearance at the temple in Bountiful]]). [[4 Nephi 1]] then associates his disciples' church, baptisms, miracles, and initial absence of contention with ministry in Jesus's name (4 Nephi 1:1–5, 13).

Restoration scripture extends the portrait into premortality, heavenly glory, and the spirit world. [[D&C 76]] reports a vision of Christ at God's right hand and calls him the Father's Only Begotten (D&C 76:13–14, 22–24); this is a visionary witness, not an independently repeatable observation. [[D&C 138]] interprets his death as the great atoning sacrifice and depicts his redemptive work among the dead (D&C 138:2, 7, 18, 29–30). Across these collections, Christ's saving work calls for more than verbal profession: [[Moroni 7]] joins belief in him with discernment, hope, charity, and becoming like him (Moroni 7:16–17, 33, 41, 47–48).

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
Latter-day Saint general conference teaching consistently identifies the premortal Jesus Christ as Jehovah, the God of Israel, distinct from the Father. Joseph F. Smith's *Gospel Doctrine* (1919) states that the 'first-born was and is Jehovah, or Jesus Christ, to whom all others are' subject. *Conference Report, April 1916* lists the 'Preexistence of Jesus Christ as Jehovah' among its doctrinal summaries, and *Conference Report, April 1920* headlines that 'Jehovah of the Old Testament is Jesus Christ of the New.' The 1908 report recounts the Kirtland Temple vision in which Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery 'beheld the Lord Jesus Christ, as Jehovah, the God of Israel.' Elder Donald L. Hallstrom (2012) teaches that 'the great Jehovah was also named Jesus,' whose 'crowning achievement was the Atonement'; Elder Christoffel Golden Jr. (2013) affirms 'Jesus Christ is the great Jehovah, the God of Israel, the promised Messiah'; Elder Jonathan S. Schmitt (2022) lists 'Great Jehovah' among Christ's names; and President Dallin H. Oaks (2023) teaches that scriptural words attributed to 'God' or 'the Lord' after the Fall are 'almost always the words of Jehovah, our risen Lord, Jesus Christ' — a hedge worth preserving rather than flattening into a universal rule.
<!-- SG:END conference -->

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
Begin with [[John 1]] for preexistence and incarnation, [[John 11]] for love and life-giving authority, [[John 19]] for the crucifixion, and [[John 20]] for resurrection appearances. Read [[Romans 5]] with [[Adam]] and [[Grace]] to follow Paul's argument from condemnation to reconciliation, then [[1 Corinthians 15]] and [[Resurrection of Jesus]] for the Adam–Christ resurrection correspondence. [[Passover]] supplies essential background for Christ's identification as the sacrificed Passover.

For the Book of Mormon portrait, read [[1 Nephi 10]] and [[2 Nephi 25]] for messianic expectation, [[Christ's appearance at the temple in Bountiful]] and [[3 Nephi 19]] for the risen Christ's ministry, and [[Moroni 7]] for Christ-centered moral discernment, hope, and [[Charity]]. [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]], [[Premortal Life]], [[The Godhead]], and [[Vision of the redemption of the dead]] organize the major Latter-day Saint doctrinal connections. Historical questions should be kept distinct from confessional conclusions; [[Did Jesus exist as a historical person]], [[Did Jesus rise from the dead]], [[Why do the Gospels differ from one another]], and [[Is the God of the Old Testament the same as the God of the New]] address those separate lines of inquiry.
