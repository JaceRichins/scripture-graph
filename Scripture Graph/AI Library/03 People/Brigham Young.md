# Brigham Young

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Brigham Young was an early Latter-day Saint apostle and institutional leader whose ministry became central to the Church's transition after Joseph Smith's death and to the pioneer movement west; [[Chapter 1 The Ministry of Brigham Young]] provides the supplied Church overview, while [[How was Brigham Young chosen to succeed Joseph Smith]] is the necessary focused study of the contested succession process. The canon itself is much narrower. It names him only in D&C 124:127, D&C 126:1, and D&C 138:53: as a president over the Twelve's traveling council, as a servant whose offering was accepted and whose family separation was no longer required, and as one of the choice spirits reserved for latter-day work. These texts express a favorable theological appraisal, but they are not a full biography and do not adjudicate every later policy, teaching, or controversy associated with him.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
In [[D&C 124]], Brigham Young is appointed "a president over the Twelve traveling council" (D&C 124:127). That wording establishes leadership within the Twelve; it should not be silently expanded into an explicit revelation naming him Joseph Smith's future successor.

[[D&C 126]] turns from office to costly service and family obligation. Brigham is called a "well-beloved brother," told that his offering is acceptable, and released from the former requirement to leave his family (D&C 126:1). The verse supports an inference of previously accepted sacrifice, but it does not specify everything included in that offering. [[‘Take Special Care of Your Family’]], which cites an 1836 letter to Mary Ann Angell Young, is useful historical context rather than an expansion of the verse's revealed wording.

In [[D&C 138]], Brigham appears with [[Joseph Smith Jr]], [[Hyrum Smith]], [[John Taylor]], and [[Wilford Woodruff]] among choice spirits reserved to help lay the foundations of the latter-day work (D&C 138:53). This is the vision's retrospective, premortal interpretation of his mission. Taken together, the passages portray appointment, accepted sacrifice, family concern, and divine preparation. They do not narrate the later succession crisis, pioneer leadership, territorial government, plural marriage, racial restriction, or disputed teachings; those questions require historical evidence beyond the naming verses.
<!-- SG:END scripture-profile -->

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
The supplied conference corpus invokes Brigham Young in several different ways, and those uses should not be treated as equivalent. [[Quench Not the Spirit Which Quickens the Inner Man (Elder Keith K. Hilbig, October 2007)]] and [[The Light in Their Eyes (President James E. Faust, October 2005)]] cite editions of *Discourses of Brigham Young*, using his collected teachings within discussions of spiritual life. Because the supplied excerpts give only the citations, they do not support reconstructing or quoting the underlying statements here.

[[You Are Not Alone in the Work (President Henry B. Eyring, October 2015)]] cites an October 7, 1858 letter from Henry Eyring to Brigham Young, using documentary history connected with shared service. [[Tithing Opening the Windows of Heaven (Elder Neil L. Andersen, October 2023)]] mentions educational institutions bearing Brigham Young's name; that is institutional commemoration, not substantive teaching about his character or doctrine.
<!-- SG:END conference -->
