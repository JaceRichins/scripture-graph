# Lehi (father of Nephi)

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Lehi is the Jerusalem prophet and family patriarch whose departure opens the Book of Mormon’s main Nephite narrative. The text places his call in the first year of Zedekiah, depicts him warning Jerusalem, and says that a divine command led him to take his household into the wilderness (1 Nephi 1:4–20; 2:1–5). He is husband to [[Sariah]] and father of [[Laman]], [[Lemuel]], [[Sam (brother of Nephi)]], [[Nephi (son of Lehi)]], [[Jacob (son of Lehi)]], and [[Joseph (son of Lehi)]]. Almost everything preserved about him is mediated by Nephi, who says he abridged his father’s record and omitted many of his visions and teachings (1 Nephi 1:16–17; 9:1). This page concerns [[Lehi (father of Nephi)]], not the commander named Lehi, the city or lands named Lehi, the biblical place in Judges 15, or [[Lehi (son of Helaman)]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
**Call and rejection at Jerusalem.** Nephi's account introduces his father as a lifelong resident of Jerusalem in the first year of Zedekiah's reign (1 Nephi 1:4). Lehi prays "with all his heart, in behalf of his people" (1 Nephi 1:5), receives visions concerning the city's coming destruction, and begins to prophesy publicly, provoking mockery and threats on his life (1 Nephi 1:18-20). Warned in a dream, he departs into the wilderness with his household, leaving behind his house, inheritance, gold, and silver (1 Nephi 2:1-4).

**Family.** Lehi initially travels with Sariah and four sons — Laman, Lemuel, Sam, and Nephi; Jacob and Joseph are born later in the wilderness (1 Nephi 2:5; 18:7).

**Records and genealogy.** Lehi's command that his sons retrieve Laban's brass-plate record (1 Nephi 3:2-4) becomes central to [[Records and Sacred Writings]]. After its recovery he searches the plates "from the beginning" and finds a genealogy by which "he knew that he was a descendant of Joseph" (1 Nephi 5:10, 14) — a claim the narrative makes, not an independently proven fact. Decades later, Amulek's own genealogy traces back through Nephi to "Lehi, who came out of the land of Jerusalem, who was a descendant of Manasseh, who was the son of Joseph" (Alma 10:3). Benjamin credits Lehi's own training in "the language of the Egyptians" with his ability to read the plates and teach his children (Mosiah 1:4).

**Visionary and father.** In [[Lehi's dream of the tree of life]], Lehi tastes fruit that fills him with joy and wants his family to share it; Sariah, Sam, and Nephi partake, but Laman and Lemuel refuse, and Lehi tenderly exhorts them afterward (1 Nephi 8:10-18, 37-38). He also teaches of a coming Messiah and forerunner and of Israel's scattering and gathering (1 Nephi 10; see [[Scattering of Israel]]).

The record does not present Lehi as flawless: when a hunting failure leaves the family without food, "my father began to murmur against the Lord his God" along with his elder sons; after Nephi seeks direction, Lehi prays, is chastened, humbles himself, and the [[Liahona (compass)|Liahona]] provides new direction (1 Nephi 16:20-30). During the ocean voyage, when Laman and Lemuel bind Nephi and a storm threatens the ship, Lehi and Sariah are brought near death with grief and illness before the storm subsides and the company reaches the promised land (1 Nephi 18:9-23; see [[Voyage to the promised land]]).

**Final testament.** In the promised land, Lehi teaches that inhabiting the land depends on being "brought by the hand of the Lord" (2 Nephi 1:6) and prospering there on keeping the commandments (2 Nephi 1:9), then delivers to Jacob a discourse on agency, opposition, and the Fall — "there is an opposition in all things" (2 Nephi 2:11) and "Adam fell that men might be; and men are, that they might have joy" (2 Nephi 2:25) — before blessing his family and dying (2 Nephi 4:3-12). Jacob later traces the Nephite chastity standard to teaching "given to our father, Lehi" (Jacob 2:33-34), and Nephi anchors the record's chronology to "fifty and five years" since Lehi left Jerusalem (Jacob 1:1).

**Remembered ancestor.** Lehi's departure remains the record's fixed reference point long after his death: Mosiah's reign is dated 509 years from it (Mosiah 29:46), record custody is traced back to it when Mosiah charges Alma the Younger (Mosiah 28), and Nephi son of Helaman recites a witness-chain naming Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Lehi among the Nephite record's own prophetic forebears (Helaman 8). The Nephite land south is named "Lehi" because "the Lord did bring... Lehi into the land south" (Helaman 6:10). Generations later, Mormon pauses his abridgment to identify himself as "a pure descendant of Lehi," attributing his people's prosperity to keeping "his commandments" (3 Nephi 5:20, 22), while elsewhere judging his own generation's wickedness as exceeding all prior generations "among the children of Lehi" (Mormon 4:12) — though the exact wording of that verse could not be mechanically verified here and should be rechecked before quoting.

**Disambiguation.** Other indexed occurrences of "Lehi" are not this man: a missionary son of Helaman shares the name a few centuries later (Helaman 5; 11; see [[Lehi (son of Helaman)]]); a Nephite chief captain named Lehi serves under [[Captain Moroni]] in the war chapters (Alma 43; 49-53; 61-62); the collective name [[Anti-Nephi-Lehi]] is unrelated to Lehi acting as a person; and "Lehi" in Judges 15 is a biblical place tied to Samson.

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
Lehi recurs often in modern teaching as a model of revelation, obedience, and honest family struggle:
- President Henry B. Eyring ([[Continuing Revelation (President Henry B. Eyring, October 2014)]]) uses the family's differing responses to Lehi's revelation to teach that personal revelation cannot be borrowed from another's experience (1 Nephi 2:12-16).
- Elder Ronald A. Rasband cites Lehi's unquestioning obedience in sending his sons for the brass plates ([[Build a Fortress of Spirituality and Protection (Elder Ronald A. Rasband, April 2019)]]) and Nephi's continued record-keeping after "Father Lehi's death" ([[The Things of My Soul (Elder Ronald A. Rasband, October 2021)]]).
- Elder Quentin L. Cook ([[Safely Gathered Home (Elder Quentin L. Cook, April 2023)]]) frames Lehi as leader of the exodus from Jerusalem and cites his olive-tree teaching on the scattering of Israel.
- President Susan H. Porter ([[Pray, He Is There (President Susan H. Porter, April 2024)]]) cites Lehi's prayer after Nephi's bow broke as a father seeking guidance for a child in need.
- President Mark L. Pace ([[It Is Wisdom in the Lord That We Should Have the Book of Mormon (President Mark L. Pace, April 2024)]]) highlights Lehi's tree-of-life vision as a model of personal experience with God's love.
- Elder W. Craig Zwick ([[What Are You Thinking (Elder W. Craig Zwick, April 2014)]]) and Elder L. Todd Budge ([[Consistent and Resilient Trust (Elder L. Todd Budge, October 2019)]]) both draw on Lehi comforting Sariah and rejoicing in a promised blessing while still in the wilderness (1 Nephi 5:5-6).
- Elder Claudio R. M. Costa ([[Don't Leave for Tomorrow What You Can Do Today (Elder Claudio R. M. Costa, October 2007)]]) points to Lehi as a father who understood his duty to teach his children.

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
Begin with [[Lehi's departure from Jerusalem]] and [[Lehi's dream of the tree of life]], then read [[Obtaining the brass plates]], [[Liahona (compass)]], and [[Voyage to the promised land]] for the principal stages of Lehi’s journey. [[Sariah]] supplies an indispensable second parental perspective, while [[Nephi (son of Lehi)]] is both son and principal surviving narrator. Lehi’s final teachings are especially important for [[Jacob (son of Lehi)]], [[Joseph (son of Lehi)]], [[Agency]], [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]], and [[Scattering of Israel]]. Consult [[Records and Sacred Writings]] for the role of the brass plates and Nephi’s selective recordmaking. For name control, distinguish the subject from [[Lehi (son of Helaman)]], the military Lehi associated with [[Captain Moroni]], and the collective name [[Anti-Nephi-Lehi]].
