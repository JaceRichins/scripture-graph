# James (brother of the Lord)

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
James is securely identified by Paul as "the Lord's brother" (Galatians 1:19), and the people of Jesus's hometown name a James among his brothers (Matthew 13:55–56; Mark 6:3). The precise biological meaning of that relationship is interpreted differently across Christian traditions; Latter-day Saint study materials commonly call James a half-brother of [[Jesus Christ]], but the New Testament itself simply uses brother-language. Paul later places James among Jerusalem's leading "pillars" (Galatians 2:9), and the prominent James in Acts 12, 15, and 21 is very likely the same man. He must be distinguished from [[James (son of Zebedee)]] and, unless one adopts a disputed traditional identification, from James the son of Alphaeus, one of the Twelve.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
The Gospels first place James in Jesus's family rather than among the named disciples: Matthew 13:55–56 and Mark 6:3 list him with Jesus's other brothers and sisters. John 7:3–5 says collectively that Jesus's brothers did not yet believe, but it does not name James, so an individual claim about his unbelief remains inferential. Acts 1:14 later places Jesus's brothers with [[Mary (mother of Jesus)]] and the disciples in prayer.

Paul reports that the risen Christ appeared to “James” (1 Corinthians 15:7). This is probably the Lord's brother, though the verse itself does not distinguish among men of that name. The appearance provides a plausible turning point between the brothers' earlier unbelief and James's later prominence, but no canonical narrative explicitly describes his conversion.

The clearest evidence for James's standing comes from Paul. Galatians 1:18–19 directly identifies him during a Jerusalem visit, while Galatians 2:7–10 names James, Cephas, and John as pillars who recognize the missions of [[Paul (apostle)]] and [[Barnabas]]. Galatians 2:11–14 also says that visitors “came from James” before [[Peter (apostle)]] withdrew from Gentile table fellowship. That association raises a real question about James's circle, but the passage does not say James ordered or approved Peter's conduct.

Acts probably depicts the same James as a central Jerusalem leader. Peter asks that news of his deliverance be reported to James and the brethren (Acts 12:17). At the [[Jerusalem conference]], James listens to the preceding testimony, invokes prophetic scripture, and judges that Gentile converts should not be unnecessarily troubled, while recommending limited abstentions (Acts 15:13–21); the final decision is presented as the action of the apostles, elders, whole church, and Holy Ghost (Acts 15:22–29). Later, James and all the elders receive Paul, rejoice over the Gentile mission, and collectively advise a conciliatory response to Jewish believers concerned about the law (Acts 21:17–26).

The Epistle of James opens only with “James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ” (James 1:1). Attribution to the Lord's brother is traditional and described as probable in Latter-day Saint study aids, but it is not established by the letter's self-identification. Jude 1:1 likewise calls [[Jude (brother of James)]] James's brother; connecting both men with the Gospel family list is plausible, not explicit.

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
For the family evidence, read [[Matthew 13]], [[Mark 6]], and [[John 7]] alongside [[Jesus Christ]] and [[Mary (mother of Jesus)]]. For James's post-Resurrection role, the essential sequence is [[Acts 12]], [[Acts 15]], [[Acts 21]], [[1 Corinthians 15]], [[Galatians 1]], and [[Galatians 2]]; these connect him especially with [[Jerusalem conference]], [[Peter (apostle)]], [[Paul (apostle)]], and [[Barnabas]]. Compare [[Jude (brother of James)]] for the probable sibling connection.

Name control is indispensable. [[James (son of Zebedee)]] is explicitly killed in Acts 12:2, while the relationship between this subject and James the son of Alphaeus is disputed rather than canonically stated. [[The Twelve (President Boyd K. Packer, April 2008)]] and [[Hosanna to the Most High God (Elder Ronald A. Rasband, April 2023)]] enumerate the original Twelve and should not be cited as direct teaching about the Lord's brother. [[24 Truth Shall Prevail]] concerns the nineteenth-century James Fielding and is an unrelated index match.
