# Oliver Cowdery

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Oliver Cowdery was a principal figure in the early Restoration. Joseph Smith—History presents him as a schoolteacher who met Joseph Smith in April 1829 and soon became a scribe during the Book of Mormon translation (Joseph Smith—History 1:66–67). The canon identifies him as an apostle and the Church's second elder (D&C 20:3; 21:10), places him among the Three Witnesses through the historical framing of D&C 17, and reports his participation with Joseph in priesthood-restoration and Kirtland Temple experiences. It also presents a qualified portrait: Oliver receives revelatory gifts and substantial institutional responsibilities, yet is warned against pride, corrected concerning translation, subject to church-wide revelatory boundaries, and required to share custody of commandments and money with a companion. These texts establish the Restoration movement's authoritative claims about Oliver; they do not by themselves independently prove the reported supernatural events. The supplied canonical material does not provide a complete account of his later separation from or return to the Church.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
**Arrival, translation, and priesthood.** In [[Joseph Smith—History 1]], Oliver Cowdery arrives in April 1829 and begins writing for Joseph Smith as translation continues (1:66–67). The account reports that [[John the Baptist]] conferred the [[Aaronic Priesthood]] on both men, directed them to baptize one another, and explained that this authority did not include laying on hands for the gift of the Holy Ghost (1:68–73; compare [[D&C 13]]). [[D&C 27]] connects the two men, through its recipient context and plural language, with ordination involving Peter, James, and John. These are canonical reports of heavenly messengers, not independent empirical verification of their appearance.

**Personal revelation and disciplined gifts.** [[D&C 6]]:18–20 requires Oliver to stand by [[Joseph Smith Jr]] in difficult circumstances while both admonishing Joseph and receiving admonition. Verses 22–24 present inward peace and knowledge of a private matter as a personalized witness; for later investigators, the episode has limited independent force because the revelation is the principal account of both the experience and its interpretation. [[D&C 6]]:25–28 conditionally offers Oliver a translation gift. [[D&C 8]] describes revelation in mind and heart and distinguishes it from an additional gift of Aaron. Early textual witnesses use sprout or rod terminology, making a relationship with period divining culture plausible, although neither the precise operation nor the reasons for each revision are settled. Through its historical recipient framing, [[D&C 9]] applies to Oliver the instruction to study a matter, ask, and attend to the described confirmation or stupor.

**Calling and authority.** [[D&C 18]] compares Oliver and [[David Whitmer]] with Paul and commissions them to search out the Twelve by considering candidates' desires and works (18:9, 37–39). [[D&C 20]]:3 calls Oliver an apostle and the second elder, while [[D&C 21]]:10 calls him “mine apostle” and directs him to ordain Joseph. This usage predates the 1835 organization of the Quorum of the Twelve and should not be treated as membership in that later body. [[D&C 23]]:1 blesses Oliver while warning him against [[Pride]], and [[D&C 24]]:10 commissions continued ministry. In the [[Hiram Page]] controversy, [[D&C 28]] assigns Oliver a private corrective role while reserving commandments for the whole Church to Joseph.

**Administrative responsibilities.** [[D&C 69]]:1–2 entrusts Oliver with commandments and money but requires [[John Whitmer]] to accompany him; the arrangement provides shared custody or accountability without establishing mistrust. [[D&C 102]] records Oliver among the high-priest councilors, and [[D&C 104]] assigns him property and joint stewardship of the printing office.

**Kirtland and a name correction.** Through its historical framing, [[D&C 110]] presents Joseph and Oliver as the participants in a first-person plural report of appearances by the Lord, Moses, Elias, and [[Elijah]]. The chapter connects the manifestations with temple acceptance, gathering, Abrahamic blessing, restored keys, and Malachi's heart-turning promise. Its application to [[Eternal Marriage]] and work for the dead belongs to later interpretive and institutional teaching rather than the terminology of the 1836 text alone. Finally, [[D&C 117]]:12,15 names Oliver Granger, not Oliver Cowdery, and must not be used as evidence about this subject.
