# David Whitmer

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
David Whitmer was an early participant in the Restoration and one of the Three Witnesses to the Book of Mormon, with [[Oliver Cowdery]] and [[Martin Harris]]. The Doctrine and Covenants presents him chiefly through assigned responsibilities: [[D&C 14]] calls “David” to assist and promises a witness role; [[D&C 18]] names Whitmer with Cowdery, compares their calling with Paul's, and commissions them to search out the Twelve; [[D&C 52]] sends Whitmer and Harvey Whitlock to travel and preach. Later Restoration histories connect him with the selection of the original Twelve in 1835, although his precise acts should not be conflated with their ordination or blessing. He was excommunicated in 1838 and did not rejoin the Church. Later Church sources report that he nevertheless continued to affirm his Book of Mormon testimony. That persistence is relevant to his consistency and possible sincerity, but it does not by itself establish the objective or supernatural character of the experience he described. His continuing testimony and continuing institutional estrangement both belong in an intellectually honest account.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
The canon portrays David Whitmer through responsibilities rather than through a full biography or portrait of temperament. In [[D&C 14]], the historically identified recipient is told, “thou art David, and thou art called to assist” (D&C 14:11). He is also promised utterance through the Holy Ghost so that he may stand as a witness of things he will hear and see (D&C 14:8). In light of later history, that language plausibly foreshadows his place among the Three Witnesses, but the verse itself names neither the plates nor that specific event; a more general missionary or revelatory witness remains possible.

[[D&C 18]] addresses Whitmer with [[Oliver Cowdery]] “by the way of commandment” and says they have the same calling as [[Paul (apostle)]] (D&C 18:9). The immediate command to preach repentance supports a missionary reading. The phrase “same calling” can also sustain a broader apostolic reading, and the verse does not determine whether it denotes office, function, or both. D&C 18:37–39 commissions Cowdery and Whitmer to search out the Twelve, recognize the candidates by their desires and works, and show the revelation to those selected. Those criteria do not expressly exclude revelatory guidance in the selection process.

[[D&C 52]] adds a concrete missionary assignment: David Whitmer and Harvey Whitlock are to journey and “preach by the way” (D&C 52:25). Together, these passages show an assisting servant, promised witness, preacher, and participant in finding the Twelve. They do not narrate Whitmer's later separation from the Church or settle how his reported witness experience should be interpreted.

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
The named conference reports treat David Whitmer through retrospective historical accounts, not through canon or unmediated interview transcripts.

- [[Conference Report, October 1918 (General Conference (full report), October 1918)]] recounts the 1878 visit of [[Orson Pratt]] and [[Joseph F. Smith]] to Whitmer in Richmond, Missouri. It says Whitmer had been cut off from the Church in 1838, never rejoined, and was found “in the possession of his faculties, clear and keen.” The report is denominational and retrospective, and its elapsed-year figures appear rounded; the visitor's impression is not an independent clinical assessment or proof of the events Whitmer remembered.

- [[Conference Report, April 1924 (General Conference (full report), April 1924)]] describes James H. Hart interviewing Whitmer about his Book of Mormon testimony in the early 1880s. The account was put into verse and reportedly read to and approved by Whitmer. That supports his assent to the resulting representation, not the recovery of his exact original words.

- The indexed fragment from [[Conference Report, October 1928 (General Conference (full report), October 1928)]] introduces something said “to David Whitmer and Oliver Cowdery conjointly.” Because the continuation is not supplied, the exact verses quoted and their use should not be asserted here.

These reports show that Church speakers regarded Whitmer's later testimony and scriptural commissions as significant. Their historical and apologetic use should be compared with Whitmer's own writings rather than treated as independent proof of his reported experience.
<!-- SG:END conference -->

- Read [[Oliver Cowdery]], [[Martin Harris]], and [[Witnesses of the Book of Mormon]] for the Three Witnesses. [[John Whitmer]], [[Christian Whitmer]], and [[Peter Whitmer Jr]] were David's brothers and members of the separate Eight Witnesses group; David was not one of the Eight.

- Use [[How reliable are the Book of Mormon witnesses]] to weigh persistence, sincerity, memory, social commitments, and competing explanations without treating any one of them as decisive. [[How did the Three Witnesses live out their lives]] places Whitmer's continued testimony beside his permanent separation from the Church.

- Consult [[Fayette]], [[Chapter 9 Gifts of the Spirit]], and [[Book of Mormon Translation (Gospel Topics)]] for the Whitmer household's place in translation history.

- Read [[Apostles]], [[Paul (apostle)]], and [[Kirtland]] alongside D&C 18:9, 37–39 for the unresolved meaning of Whitmer's Pauline calling and the later organization of the Twelve.
