# Joseph Smith Jr

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Joseph Smith Jr. (1805–1844) is the founding prophet of the Latter-day Saint Restoration, credited in the canon with receiving the First Vision, translating the Book of Mormon (D&C 1:29), and receiving priesthood and temple manifestations later understood as restoring apostolic and sealing authority. He must not be confused with his father, [[Joseph Smith Sr]] — distinguished from him by name in the same passages (D&C 102:3; D&C 104:43-46) that also provide for his father's household — nor with the later Church leaders Joseph F. Smith or Joseph Fielding Smith, whose similarly spelled names appear in unrelated conference and library citations. D&C 21:1 gives him five titles at once — seer, translator, prophet, apostle, elder — years before the Quorum of the Twelve existed. The revelations do not present him as infallible: D&C 93:47 rebukes him directly, and D&C 67:5 acknowledges his known imperfections. D&C 135:1 records his killing, with his brother [[Hyrum Smith]], at [[Carthage]] on 27 June 1844; that same section's assessment of his significance (D&C 135:3) is the Church's own memorial judgment rather than an externally verifiable measurement.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
Joseph's canonical story begins with a prayer prompted by scripture and the vision now called the [[First Vision]] (Joseph Smith—History 1:17); because more than one account of that vision survives, [[First Vision Accounts (Essay)]] is essential for placing the canonized 1838 narration within the larger documentary record. Joseph Smith—History next connects him with [[Moroni (angel)]] and records Moroni's Restoration-specific wording of the Elijah prophecy (Joseph Smith—History 1:38-39; D&C 2:1-3) — wording that adds priesthood-restoration language absent from the canonical Malachi text.

The earliest revelations define Joseph through translation and testimony: D&C 1:29 attributes his translation of the Book of Mormon to the power of God; D&C 5:1-2 frames [[Martin Harris]]'s request for assurance around Joseph's claim to have received plates, and D&C 5:25 and D&C 17:4-5 add designated witnesses whose testimony supplements, rather than replaces, his own. These passages support [[Translation of the Book of Mormon]] and [[Witnesses of the Book of Mormon]], but establish only what the canon claims about the plates and the translation, not independent historical verification of either.

Joseph is repeatedly paired with [[Oliver Cowdery]] in priesthood narratives: Joseph Smith—History 1:68-72 reports that [[John the Baptist]] conferred the [[Aaronic Priesthood]] on both men (paralleled closely by D&C 13:1's address to 'my fellow servants'), and D&C 27 identifies Peter, James, and John with a further apostolic ordination and ministerial keys. D&C 76:22-24 presents Joseph and [[Sidney Rigdon]] as joint witnesses of a vision of Jesus Christ, and D&C 110 reports appearances to Joseph and Oliver in the Kirtland Temple, including Elijah — read by later Latter-day Saint tradition as fulfilling the promise Moroni had quoted in 1823, though the section's own language speaks of 'the keys of this dispensation' rather than an explicit sealing-key formula. These are direct records of what Joseph and his companions reported; visionary testimony of this kind is significant evidence of the claim but not a publicly repeatable demonstration of it.

The canon also shows Joseph as a working church leader, not only a visionary: he was acknowledged a president of the newly organized high council at his own house (D&C 102:1,3), assigned to preside over the [[Zion's Camp]] expedition (D&C 103:21-22,35), and, by the Nauvoo period, granted a building lot with provision for his father's support within his household (D&C 104:43-46). Yet the revelations resist portraying him as infallible: D&C 67:5 acknowledges his known imperfections and D&C 93:47 rebukes him directly for not keeping the commandments. The arc closes with [[Martyrdom of Joseph and Hyrum Smith]]: D&C 135:1,6 records that he and Hyrum were shot at [[Carthage]] on 27 June 1844 at age thirty-eight, while D&C 135:3's estimate of his significance is a memorial judgment by contemporaries at his death, not a neutral historical measurement.

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
In [[“A Choice Seer Will I Raise Up” (Elder Craig C. Christensen, October 2016)]], Elder Craig C. Christensen presents Joseph Smith as the Prophet of the Restoration and bears personal testimony of his sacred mission — an authoritative example of modern apostolic teaching, though its testimonial purpose should be distinguished from independent historical adjudication.

Other library matches supplied for this dossier do not securely concern this Joseph Smith: citations naming 'Joseph Fielding Smith Jr.' (as compiler, in the Cook and Eyring addresses) and 'Joseph F. Smith, Jr.' (an assistant historian, in early twentieth-century conference reports) refer to a later Church leader, not to the 1805–1844 subject of this page, and should not be cited as evidence about him.
