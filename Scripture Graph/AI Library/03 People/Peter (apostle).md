# Peter (apostle)

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Simon Peter—also called Simon, Peter, and Cephas—was a Galilean fisherman, the brother of [[Andrew (apostle)]], a married man, and one of Jesus's Twelve Apostles (Matthew 4:18-20; 8:14; 10:2). The Gospels give him unusual prominence without presenting him as flawless. He confesses [[Jesus Christ]] as the Christ through revelation and receives the promise of the keys, yet immediately misunderstands the Passion and is sharply corrected (Matthew 16:13-23). He witnesses the [[Transfiguration]], promises exceptional loyalty, denies Jesus three times, and is later commissioned by the risen Lord to feed the flock (Matthew 17:1-5; 26:69-75; John 21:15-19). Acts portrays him as a principal witness at the [[Day of Pentecost]], a healer and missionary, and a pivotal participant in the admission of Gentiles (Acts 2-12). Galatians recognizes his apostleship while preserving Paul's public correction of his conduct at Antioch (Galatians 2:7-14). Restoration scripture also associates Peter with priesthood keys and receives 1 Peter as the textual starting point for the vision recorded in D&C 138. Across these texts, authority, courage, misunderstanding, failure, correction, and renewed service remain in view together.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
**Call and prominence.** Peter and [[Andrew (apostle)]] leave their nets when Jesus calls them (Matthew 4:18-20), and Matthew lists Peter first among the Twelve (Matthew 10:2). Peter is often the disciple whose response is recorded: he ventures onto the water and falters (Matthew 14:28-31), confesses Jesus as the Christ (Matthew 16:13-17), objects to the predicted Passion (Matthew 16:21-23), and asks how often forgiveness is required (Matthew 18:21-22). He also belongs with [[James (son of Zebedee)]] and [[John the Apostle]] to the smaller group present at the [[Transfiguration]] and in [[Gethsemane]] (Matthew 17:1-5; Mark 14:33-37).

**Confession, keys, and correction.** Jesus attributes Peter's confession to revelation from the Father and addresses him with the rock, church, keys, and binding-and-loosing promises (Matthew 16:16-19). Christians disagree over the rock's referent and the institutional scope of the promise. The personal address and name-wordplay give a Petrine reading real contextual force, while Matthew 18:18 extends binding and loosing to a plural audience without repeating the word "keys." Latter-day Saint scripture adds a Restoration framework: D&C 128 associates Peter, James, and John with a declaration that they possess the keys of the kingdom. None of these observations alone settles every later claim about exclusivity, succession, or jurisdiction. Matthew itself immediately balances Peter's prominence with his grave misunderstanding of Jesus's suffering and death (Matthew 16:21-23).

**Denial and renewed commission.** During the Passion, Peter professes exceptional loyalty, fails to remain awake, uses a sword against Malchus, follows Jesus into the high priest's courtyard, and then denies him three times (Matthew 26:33-40, 69-75; John 18:10-27). Matthew records increasing intensity in the denials and Peter's bitter weeping. John 21 portrays the risen Jesus asking Peter three times about his love and repeatedly commissioning him to feed or tend the flock (John 21:15-17). The parallel with the three denials is compelling but interpretive rather than explicitly identified by John. John says Jesus's next words signify the kind of death by which Peter would glorify God but does not name its place or exact manner (John 21:18-19). D&C 7 presents Peter as desiring to come speedily into Christ's kingdom; later traditions about Roman or inverted crucifixion remain outside the canonical account.

**Leadership in Acts.** Peter preaches Christ's resurrection at Pentecost and calls the crowd to repentance and baptism (Acts 2:14-41). With John he continues prayer at the [[Jerusalem]] temple and heals a man in Jesus's name (Acts 3:1-10). He ministers in [[Samaria]], heals Aeneas, prays before Tabitha is restored, and is delivered from Herod's prison (Acts 8:14-17; 9:32-43; 12:3-11). In Acts 10-11, revelation moves him beyond his initial resistance: he learns not to call any person common or unclean, witnesses the Holy Ghost fall upon Gentiles before baptism, and defends their admission to the church. Acts 15 later recalls this event in the dispute over Gentile obligations.

**Tension with Paul and later canon.** [[Paul (apostle)]] calls Cephas a pillar and recognizes his mission to the circumcision, yet reports rebuking him at [[Antioch]] after Peter withdrew from Gentile table fellowship out of fear (Galatians 2:7-14). This is strong evidence that recognized apostolic authority did not make Peter behaviorally inerrant, though Galatians preserves Paul's account rather than Peter's explanation. First and Second Peter present themselves as writings of the apostle (1 Peter 1:1; 2 Peter 1:1), but modern historical scholarship debates direct Petrine authorship, especially of 2 Peter. D&C 138 specifically receives 1 Peter as the starting point for the [[Vision of the redemption of the dead]], expanding its compressed language into an organized ministry among the dead.

## Teachings & Conference
<!-- SG:BEGIN conference -->
Latter-day Saint preaching repeatedly returns to Peter's confession and the risen Christ's commission to care for the flock. President Boyd K. Packer quotes Peter's declaration that Jesus is "the Christ, the Son of the living God" in [[The Twelve (President Boyd K. Packer, April 2008)]]. Elder Steven C. Barlow quotes the risen Jesus's question to Peter, "Lovest thou me?", in [[“Lovest Thou Me” (Elder Steven C. Barlow, October 2025)]]. [[God Will Do Something Unimaginable (Elder Dieter F. Uchtdorf, October 2020)]] mentions Peter and the other disciples fishing on the Sea of Tiberias, while [[The Imperfect Harvest (Elder Vern P. Stanfill, April 2023)]] identifies Andrew as Simon Peter's brother.

Earlier reports also preserve recurring use of Petrine passages. [[Conference Report, October 1900 (General Conference (full report), October 1900)]], [[Conference Report, October 1901 (General Conference (full report), October 1901)]], [[Conference Report, April 1928 (General Conference (full report), April 1928)]], and [[Conference Report, October 1929 (General Conference (full report), October 1929)]] quote Peter's confession. [[Conference Report, April 1927 (General Conference (full report), April 1927)]] instead quotes his question, "Lord, to whom shall we go?" from John 6, and [[Conference Report, October 1930 (General Conference (full report), October 1930)]] quotes his entry into the empty sepulchre. These citations demonstrate sustained use of Peter's words and experiences; the supplied excerpts alone do not establish a single uniform interpretation across all the addresses.
<!-- SG:END conference -->

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
Begin with [[Matthew 16]] for Peter's confession, promises, misunderstanding, and rebuke; compare [[Matthew 18]] for the plural use of binding and loosing. Read [[Matthew 26]], [[John 18]], and [[John 21]] together for his confidence, denial, remorse, renewed commission, and predicted death. [[Acts 2]], [[Acts 3]], and [[Acts 10]] trace his movement into public witness, temple ministry, and Gentile inclusion, while [[Galatians 2]] supplies Paul's indispensable and less flattering account of the Antioch conflict.

Among people, follow [[Andrew (apostle)]], [[James (son of Zebedee)]], [[John the Apostle]], [[Paul (apostle)]], and [[Barnabas]]. The most useful topical paths are [[Apostles]], [[Revelation]], [[Repentance and Forgiveness]], [[Jews and Gentiles]], [[Missionary Work]], and [[Temples]]. For Restoration treatments of Peter, priesthood keys, and ministry among the dead, read [[D&C 7]], [[D&C 128]], [[D&C 138]], [[Vision of the redemption of the dead]], and [[Death and the Spirit World]].
