# 600-501 BC — what was happening

_All the world's scripture history in this century, side by side. Dating honesty: every entry says how its date is known._

## In the Old World
- **597 BC** — **Jehoiachin deported and Zedekiah installed** — The political background corresponds to Nebuchadnezzar's 597 BCE removal of Jehoiachin and installation of Mattaniah, renamed Zedekiah, as vassal king. — [[Nebuchadnezzar II]] · [[Jehoiachin]] · [[Zedekiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Ezekiel 17]] *(historical)*
- **597 BC** — **Jehoiachin (Coniah) surrenders, deported to Babylon** — 2 Kings 24:8-15; first major Babylonian deportation of Judah's royal house and elite — [[Coniah (Jehoiachin)]] · [[Nebuchadrezzar]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 22]] *(historical)*
- **597 BC–586 BC** — **Jeremiah urges submission after Jeconiah's deportation** — Verse 3 places the message in Zedekiah's reign, while verse 20 treats the deportation of Jeconiah and the nobles as past. Verse 1's reference to Jehoiakim creates a textual or chronological difficulty. — [[Jeremiah]] · [[King Zedekiah]] · [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Jeconiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 27]] *(approximate)*
- **597 BC–593 BC** — **Jeremiah's yoke sign during Zedekiah's early reign** — Internal evidence (vv.3, 12, 20) situates the oracle after Nebuchadnezzar's 597 BC deportation of Jeconiah and during Zedekiah's reign, though v.1's heading names Jehoiakim. — [[Jeremiah]] · [[Zedekiah]] · [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 27]] *(approximate)*
- **594 BC–593 BC** — **Seraiah carries Jeremiah's Babylon oracle** — Jeremiah 51:59 places Seraiah's journey with King Zedekiah in the fourth year of Zedekiah's reign. — [[Jeremiah]] · [[Seraiah]] · [[King Zedekiah]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 51]] *(approximate)*
- **593 BC–570 BC** — **Ezekiel among the exiles** — dry bones; two sticks that become one — [[Ezekiel]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Ezekiel 1]] · [[Ezekiel 37]] *(historical)*
- **593 BC–571 BC** — **Ezekiel's exilic ministry and oracles against false prophets** — Ezekiel's oracles are internally dated by the regnal years of King Jehoiachin's exile (e.g., Ezekiel 1:2), placing his prophetic activity roughly 593-571 BC among the Babylonian exiles, spanning the years before and after Jerusalem's fall in 586 BC. — [[Ezekiel]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Ezekiel 13]] *(approximate)*
- **593 BC–586 BC** — **Ezekiel's oracles against Jerusalem before its fall** — Ezekiel 16 has no internal date formula, but belongs within the body of oracles the book elsewhere dates (e.g., Ezekiel 1:2; 8:1) to the years between Ezekiel's call (c. 593 BC) and Jerusalem's fall (586 BC). — [[Ezekiel]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Ezekiel 16]] *(approximate)*
- **593 BC–571 BC** — **Ezekiel's exilic ministry and vision of the dry bones** — Ezekiel's book is internally dated by the year of King Jehoiachin's captivity (cf. 1:2), spanning roughly the 5th to 27th year (~593-571 BCE); chapter 37 itself lacks its own date formula but falls within this broader exilic ministry. — [[Ezekiel]] · [[Ezekiel 37]] *(approximate)*
- **589 BC–586 BC** — **Nebuchadrezzar's Siege of Jerusalem under Zedekiah** — Correlates with the narrative of Zedekiah's reign and Jerusalem's fall recorded in 2 Kings 24-25, conventionally dated via regnal chronology and external Babylonian records to c. 589-586 BCE. — [[King Zedekiah]] · [[Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 21]] *(historical)*
- **588 BC–587 BC** — **Jeremiah in the dungeon** — the prophet Lehi's contemporaries tried to silence — [[Jeremiah]] · [[Zedekiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Jeremiah 37]] · [[Jeremiah 38]] *(historical)*
- **588 BC–586 BC** — **Zedekiah rebels and Jerusalem is defeated** — Zedekiah's appeal to Egypt and rebellion led to Babylon's final siege and Jerusalem's fall; proposed chronologies sometimes date the fall one year earlier. — [[Zedekiah]] · [[Nebuchadnezzar II]] · [[Pharaoh Hophra]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Ezekiel 17]] *(approximate)*
- **588 BC** — **Babylonian siege of Jerusalem begins (Ezekiel's dated oracle)** — Ezekiel 24:1-2 dates the oracle to the ninth year, tenth month, tenth day of Ezekiel's exile; the same date for the siege's start is echoed in 2 Kings 25:1 and Jeremiah 52:4, allowing correlation with standard Babylonian regnal chronology. — [[Ezekiel]] · [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Ezekiel 24]] *(historical)*
- **588 BC–587 BC** — **Jeremiah's imprisonment and purchase of the field of Anathoth** — Internal synchronism in Jeremiah 32:1 (tenth year of Zedekiah = eighteenth year of Nebuchadrezzar) as conventionally correlated to the final siege of Jerusalem. — [[Jeremiah]] · [[Zedekiah]] · [[Nebuchadrezzar]] · [[Hanameel]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Anathoth]] · [[Jeremiah 32]] *(traditional dating)*
- **588 BC–586 BC** — **Nebuchadnezzar's final siege of Jerusalem and Zedekiah's servant covenant** — Dated by regnal synchronisms in Jeremiah and 2 Kings to the final years of Zedekiah's reign, ending with the fall of Jerusalem; corroborated externally by the Lachish ostraca describing the same siege. — [[Zedekiah]] · [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Jeremiah (prophet)]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 34]] *(historical)*
- **588 BC–586 BC** — **Jeremiah imprisoned during Jerusalem's final siege** — The narrative occurs during Babylon's final siege and ends when Jerusalem is taken; the fall is commonly dated to 587 or 586 BCE. — [[Jeremiah]] · [[Zedekiah]] · [[Ebed-melech]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 38]] *(approximate)*
- **588 BC–586 BC** — **Babylonian siege and fall of Jerusalem under Zedekiah** — Conventional biblical chronology for the final siege under Nebuchadnezzar II, the setting of Jeremiah 38's events; the wider timeframe is broadly (though not universally) corroborated by external Babylonian records. — [[Zedekiah]] · [[Jeremiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 38]] *(historical)*
- **588 BC–586 BC** — **Siege and Fall of Jerusalem to Babylon** — Regnal-year/month/day formula in Jeremiah 39:1-2, matched by the parallel notices in 2 Kings 25:1-3 and Jeremiah 52:4-6. — [[King Zedekiah]] · [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Jeremiah (prophet)]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 39]] *(traditional dating)*
- **588 BC–586 BC** — **Babylonian siege and destruction of Jerusalem** — Conventional dating of Nebuchadnezzar's siege and destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple, the historical backdrop for Lamentations 4's famine and destruction imagery. — [[Zedekiah]] · [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Lamentations 4]] *(historical)*
- **587 BC–538 BC** — **Fall of Jerusalem and the Babylonian exile** — Conventional dating of the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem (586/587 BC) through the first year of Cyrus and the end of exile (539/538 BC) referenced in 2 Chronicles 36:17-23. — [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Zedekiah]] · [[Cyrus]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[2 Chronicles 36]] *(historical)*
- **587 BC–570 BC** — **Ezekiel's oracles against Ammon, Moab, Edom, Philistia** — Ezekiel 25 itself carries no date formula, but the surrounding oracles-against-nations material (e.g., Ezekiel 26:1; 29:1; 32:1) is dated across roughly 587-571 BCE, suggesting a comparable window for this chapter's undated oracles, delivered in the context of Jerusalem's fall. — [[Ammon]] · [[Moab]] · [[Ezekiel 25]] *(approximate)*
- **587 BC–586 BC** — **Oracle against Tyre near Jerusalem's fall** — Ezekiel 26:1 assigns the oracle to the eleventh year, while verse 2 presupposes news or expectation of Jerusalem's collapse; the missing month prevents a more exact date. — [[Ezekiel]] · [[Nebuchadrezzar]] · [[Tyre]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Ezekiel 26]] *(approximate)*
- **587 BC–573 BC** — **Ezekiel's Oracles Against Tyre and Nebuchadnezzar's Siege** — Ezekiel 26:1 dates the Tyre oracle cycle (chs. 26-28) to the 11th year of the captivity, c. 587/586 BC; Josephus (citing Phoenician/Tyrian records via Menander) reports Nebuchadnezzar's siege of Tyre lasted about 13 years, ending c. 573 BC. — [[Ezekiel]] · [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Tyre]] · [[Ezekiel 27]] *(historical)*
- **587 BC–586 BC** — **Fall of Jerusalem and onset of Babylonian exile** — Lamentations 1 describes the aftermath of Jerusalem's fall as an accomplished, recent catastrophe (captivity, desolate feasts, violated sanctuary), consistent with the Babylonian destruction of the city and temple recorded in 2 Kings 25. — [[Jeremiah (traditional author)]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Lamentations 1]] *(historical)*
- **587 BC–586 BC** — **Conquest, desolation, and captivity of Jerusalem** — The chapter's captivity, hunger, invaded sanctuary, and ruined city fit the Babylonian conquest traditionally dated to 587/586 BC, but the poem itself names neither Babylon nor a year. — [[People of Jerusalem]] · [[People of Judah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Lamentations 1]] *(approximate)*
- **587 BC–586 BC** — **Fall of Jerusalem and devastation of its sanctuary** — The chapter is conventionally read against Jerusalem's Babylonian siege and destruction, historically narrated in 2 Kings 25 and Jeremiah 52; Lamentations 2 itself does not name Babylon or supply a date. — [[Jerusalem]] · [[Zion]] · [[Lamentations 2]] *(approximate)*
- **586 BC** — **Babylon destroys Jerusalem and the temple** — exactly as Lehi and Jeremiah warned — [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Zedekiah]] · [[Jeremiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[2 Kings 25]] · [[Lamentations 1]] *(historical)*
- **586 BC–538 BC** — **Babylonian exile and postexilic return to Jerusalem** — Verse 1 names the Babylonian captivity 'for their transgression'; verses 2-34 describe the subsequent resettlement of Jerusalem by returning Israelites, priests, Levites, and Nethinims. — [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[1 Chronicles 9]] *(historical)*
- **586 BC–582 BC** — **Gedaliah appointed governor of Judah after Jerusalem's fall** — Follows the fall of Jerusalem and deportation to Babylon (586 BC) described in Jeremiah 39-40 and 2 Kings 25. — [[Gedaliah]] · [[Jeremiah]] · [[Nebuzar-adan]] · [[Mizpah]] · [[Jeremiah 40]] *(historical)*
- **586 BC–582 BC** — **Remnant of Judah forcibly relocates to Egypt** — Following the assassination of Gedaliah (cf. Jeremiah 41), Johanan's faction, fearing Babylonian reprisal, takes the surviving population -- including Jeremiah and Baruch -- to Tahpanhes in Egypt against the oracle just delivered in Jeremiah 42. — [[Jeremiah (prophet)]] · [[Baruch]] · [[Johanan]] · [[Egypt]] · [[Jeremiah 43]] *(approximate)*
- **586 BC–538 BC** — **Likely exilic setting behind Psalm 106's closing plea** — Verse 47's plea to 'gather us from among the heathen' reflects an audience experiencing dispersion; many scholars place the psalm's final form in the exilic or early post-exilic period. — [[Psalm 106]] *(approximate)*
- **585 BC–572 BC** — **Nebuchadnezzar's costly siege of Tyre** — Later oracle in Ezekiel 29:17-20 describes an unrewarded siege of Tyre; a roughly thirteen-year siege is remembered via Josephus's citation of the Phoenician historian Menander of Ephesus. — [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Tyre]] · [[Ezekiel 29]] *(historical)*
- **570 BC–567 BC** — **Nebuchadnezzar's campaign against Egypt (Amasis)** — A fragmentary Babylonian chronicle (BM 33041) records a campaign by Nebuchadnezzar II against Amasis of Egypt in his 37th regnal year, often cited as the closest external correlate to Jeremiah's prediction, though the tablet breaks off before the outcome is described. — [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Egypt]] · [[Jeremiah 43]] *(historical)*
- **570 BC** — **Overthrow of Pharaoh Hophra (Apries) by Amasis** — Attested in Herodotus, Histories 2.161-169, and consistent with Egyptian sources documenting Amasis's accession after a civil conflict with Apries. — [[Pharaoh-hophra (Apries)]] · [[Amasis]] · [[Egypt]] · [[Jeremiah 44]] *(historical)*
- **551 BC** — **Daniel's vision at Shushan, third year of Belshazzar** — Text sets the vision in Belshazzar's third year; conventional chronology places this around 551 BCE, though Belshazzar's co-regency dating is itself debated among historians. — [[Daniel]] · [[Gabriel]] · [[Shushan]] · [[Elam]] · [[Daniel 8]] *(traditional dating)*
- **550 BC–539 BC** — **Proposed exilic setting for Isaiah 40-55** — Many historical-critical scholars attribute chapters 40-55 to an anonymous exilic-era prophetic voice addressing Judean captives shortly before Cyrus's decree ending the Babylonian exile. — [[Isaiah 42]] *(historical)*
- **539 BC** — **Daniel in the lions' den** — faith that shuts mouths — [[Daniel]] · [[Darius]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Daniel 6]] *(traditional dating)*
- **539 BC** — **Fall of Babylon to Cyrus and the Medes/Persians** — Conventional ancient Near Eastern chronology (Babylonian and Persian records) places Cyrus the Great's conquest of Babylon in 539 BC, the event most commonly identified as the historical referent behind Isaiah 13:17's naming of 'the Medes.' — [[Cyrus the Great]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Isaiah 13]] *(historical)*
- **539 BC** — **Fall of Babylon to Cyrus the Persian** — Attested by the Nabonidus Chronicle and later classical historians (Herodotus, Xenophon); conventionally treated as the historical referent behind oracles of Babylon's judgment in Jeremiah 50-51, though the chapter itself gives no internal date. — [[Nebuchadrezzar (referenced, prior era)]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 50]] *(historical)*
- **539 BC** — **Fall of Babylon to Cyrus's Medo-Persian forces** — External records (Cyrus Cylinder, Babylonian/Nabonidus Chronicle) date Babylon's capture to 539 BC; often read as connected to this chapter's 'kings of the Medes' motif, though the manner of conquest differs from the chapter's violent imagery (see counterarguments). — [[Babylon]] · [[Jeremiah 51]] *(historical)*
- **538 BC** — **Cyrus lets the exiles return** — seventy years, as Jeremiah said — [[Cyrus]] · [[Zerubbabel]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Ezra 1]] *(historical)*
- **520 BC** — **Encouragement to rebuild the temple** — The oracle is dated to the twenty-first day of the seventh month within Haggai's sequence in the second regnal year of Darius, conventionally corresponding to 520 BCE. — [[Haggai]] · [[Zerubbabel]] · [[Joshua son of Josedech]] · [[Judah]] · [[Haggai 2]] *(approximate)*
- **520 BC** — **Haggai's temple oracles under Darius I** — Internally dated to the 2nd year of Darius I (Hystaspes), whose reign is externally fixed by Persian records to 522-486 BCE. — [[Zerubbabel]] · [[Joshua (high priest)]] · [[Haggai]] · [[Darius I]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Haggai 2]] *(historical)*
- **520 BC–480 BC** — **Zechariah's postexilic prophetic ministry** — Zechariah is traditionally dated to the early Persian period, contemporary with Haggai and the rebuilding of the second temple under Zerubbabel and Joshua the high priest. — [[Zechariah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Zechariah 10]] *(traditional dating)*
- **520 BC–519 BC** — **Zechariah's night-vision cycle (measuring-line vision)** — Zechariah 1:7 dates the surrounding night-vision cycle to the 24th day of the 11th month in the 2nd year of Darius I, conventionally placed c. 520-519 BC, during the post-exilic return and temple-rebuilding period. — [[Zechariah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Zechariah 2]] *(traditional dating)*
- **516 BC** — **The second temple finished** — smaller, and still the house of the LORD — [[Zerubbabel]] · [[Haggai]] · [[Zechariah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Ezra 6]] · [[Haggai 2]] *(historical)*
