# AD 1-100 — what was happening

_All the world's scripture history in this century, side by side. Dating honesty: every entry says how its date is known._

## In the Old World
- **AD 27** — **Jesus baptized in the Jordan** — this is my beloved Son — [[Jesus Christ]] · [[John the Baptist]] · [[Jordan River]] · [[Matthew 3]] · [[Mark 1]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 27–AD 30** — **The mortal ministry in Galilee and Judea** — three years that split history in two — [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Peter]] · [[John (apostle)]] · [[Mary Magdalene]] · [[Galilee]] · [[Capernaum]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[John 6]] · [[Luke 15]] · [[John 11]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 28** — **The Sermon on the Mount** — the law of the kingdom — repeated at Bountiful — [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Galilee]] · [[Matthew 5]] · [[Matthew 6]] · [[Matthew 7]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 30** — **Gethsemane, the cross, the tomb** — the Atonement wrought — dating traditionally AD 33, scholarly ~30/33 — [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Peter]] · [[Pilate]] · [[Judas Iscariot]] · [[Gethsemane]] · [[Golgotha]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Luke 22]] · [[Matthew 27]] · [[John 19]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 30** — **Pentecost — the Spirit poured out** — three thousand in a day — [[Peter]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Acts 2]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 30–AD 33** — **Day of Pentecost: outpouring of the Holy Ghost at Jerusalem** — Traditionally dated to the Jewish Feast of Weeks (Pentecost), fifty days after Jesus's death and resurrection, within the range of years (AD 30-33) commonly proposed for the crucifixion. — [[Peter (apostle)]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Acts 2]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 30** — **The Resurrection** — the first fruits of them that slept — [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Mary Magdalene]] · [[Peter]] · [[Thomas]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Emmaus]] · [[John 20]] · [[Luke 24]] · [[Matthew 28]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 33–AD 36** — **Paul's Damascus road conversion (recounted in this speech)** — Traditional chronology places Paul's vision and conversion within a few years of Jesus' crucifixion, based on comparison with Galatians 1:15-18 and the Acts 9 narrative; this speech (delivered c. AD 57) recounts that earlier event decades after it occurred. — [[Paul (apostle)]] · [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Ananias]] · [[Damascus]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Acts 22]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 33–AD 36** — **Stephen's Speech and Martyrdom in Jerusalem** — Placed by scholarly consensus shortly after the founding of the Jerusalem church and before Saul's conversion (Acts 9), commonly dated c. AD 33-36. — [[Stephen (martyr)]] · [[Saul (king)]] · [[Egypt]] · [[Acts 7]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 33** — **Olivet Discourse delivered near the Jerusalem temple** — Traditionally placed during the week before Jesus' crucifixion, per the synoptic narrative sequence surrounding Matthew 24/Mark 13/Luke 21. — [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Mount of Olives]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Joseph Smith—Matthew 1]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 34–AD 39** — **Paul's escape from Damascus under Aretas IV** — Nabatean king Aretas IV Philopator died c. AD 39/40, giving a rough terminus ante quem for this episode; the exact date and the nature of Aretas's authority over Damascus remain disputed among historians. — [[Paul (Apostle)]] · [[Aretas IV (Nabatean king)]] · [[Damascus]] · [[2 Corinthians 11]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 34** — **Stephen the first martyr** — he saw the Son of Man standing — [[Stephen]] · [[Saul of Tarsus]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Acts 7]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 35** — **Saul on the Damascus road** — the persecutor becomes the apostle — [[Paul]] · [[Ananias]] · [[Damascus]] · [[Acts 9]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 40–AD 42** — **Cornelius's conversion; Gentiles first receive the gospel** — Placed within the general, disputed chronology of early Acts (after Paul's conversion, before the Jerusalem council of Acts 15); no internal date is given in the text itself, so this range is only a rough scholarly estimate. — [[Peter (apostle)]] · [[Cornelius]] · [[Caesarea]] · [[Joppa]] · [[Acts 10]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 41–AD 43** — **Peter's Defense and the Antioch Church's Growth** — Placed after the Cornelius episode (Acts 10) and before Herod Agrippa I's persecution and death in AD 44 (Acts 12); exact Acts chronology here is debated among scholars. — [[Peter (apostle)]] · [[Barnabas]] · [[Saul of Tarsus]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Antioch]] · [[Acts 11]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 44–AD 48** — **Agabus's Famine Prophecy and Relief to Judea** — Josephus (Antiquities 20) records a severe famine in Judea during the reign of Claudius, generally dated to the tenure of procurator Tiberius Julius Alexander, c. AD 46-48. — [[Agabus]] · [[Barnabas]] · [[Saul of Tarsus]] · [[Antioch]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Acts 11]] *(historical)*
- **AD 46–AD 57** — **Paul's missionary journeys** — the gospel crosses into Europe — [[Paul]] · [[Barnabas]] · [[Silas]] · [[Timothy]] · [[Antioch]] · [[Athens]] · [[Corinth]] · [[Acts 13]] · [[Acts 16]] · [[Acts 17]] · [[Acts 19]] *(historical)*
- **AD 46–AD 48** — **Paul and Barnabas Begin the First Missionary Journey** — Acts itself gives no dates; conventional harmonization of Acts with external data (e.g., the Gallio inscription for Paul's later Corinth ministry) places the first missionary journey roughly here, though the figure is approximate and debated. — [[Paul]] · [[Barnabas]] · [[Sergius Paulus]] · [[Cyprus]] · [[Antioch in Pisidia]] · [[Acts 13]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 46–AD 48** — **Paul and Barnabas's Lycaonian mission (first journey)** — Scholarly reconstruction of Pauline chronology from Acts and Galatians; commonly placed within Paul's first missionary journey, though exact dates are debated. — [[Paul]] · [[Barnabas]] · [[Lystra]] · [[Iconium]] · [[Acts 14]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 48–AD 50** — **Jerusalem deliberation concerning Gentile believers** — Common historical reconstructions place the Jerusalem meeting around AD 49, but Acts itself supplies no absolute year. — [[Peter]] · [[James]] · [[Paul]] · [[Barnabas]] · [[Antioch]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Acts 15]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 49** — **The Jerusalem council** — the door opened to the Gentiles — [[Peter]] · [[Paul]] · [[James (brother of Jesus)]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Acts 15]] *(historical)*
- **AD 49–AD 51** — **Paul ministers in Thessalonica, Berea, and Athens** — Common reconstructions place these events during Paul's second missionary journey shortly before the Gallio episode narrated in Acts 18; Acts 17 itself supplies no absolute date. — [[Paul]] · [[Silas]] · [[Timothy]] · [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Thessalonica]] · [[Athens]] · [[Acts 17]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 50–AD 51** — **Paul writes 1 Thessalonians, likely from Corinth** — Widely accepted scholarly dating during Paul's second missionary journey, shortly after founding the Thessalonian church (cf. Acts 17-18), making it likely the earliest surviving New Testament writing. — [[Paul (apostle)]] · [[Timothy (companion of Paul)]] · [[Thessalonica]] · [[1 Thessalonians 1]] *(historical)*
- **AD 50–AD 51** — **Paul writes 1 Thessalonians, likely from Corinth** — Commonly dated via Paul's stay in Corinth during the proconsulship of Gallio (cf. Acts 18:11-12), making it one of Paul's earliest surviving letters; the dating rests on evidence external to this chapter. — [[Paul]] · [[Timothy]] · [[Corinth]] · [[Thessalonica]] · [[1 Thessalonians 4]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 50–AD 51** — **Paul's First Epistle to the Thessalonians written from Corinth** — Traditionally dated to Paul's second missionary journey, commonly placed at Corinth (cf. Acts 18:1-11); widely regarded as one of the earliest surviving Pauline letters. — [[Paul the Apostle]] · [[Silvanus (Silas)]] · [[Timothy]] · [[Corinth]] · [[1 Thessalonians 5]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 51–AD 52** — **Paul's Corinth ministry anchored by the Gallio inscription** — An inscription at Delphi places Gallio as proconsul of Achaia around AD 51-52 (cf. Acts 18:12), anchoring the Corinth phase of Paul's second journey shortly after which Timothy's report described in this chapter would have reached him. — [[Paul]] · [[Timothy (companion of Paul)]] · [[Gallio]] · [[Athens]] · [[Corinth]] · [[1 Thessalonians 3]] *(historical)*
- **AD 51–AD 52** — **Gallio's proconsulship of Achaia anchors Paul in Corinth** — The Delphi Inscription records a Claudian rescript naming Gallio as proconsul of Achaia, conventionally dated to AD 51-52, giving Acts 18:12 a fixed external anchor point. — [[Paul]] · [[Gallio]] · [[Corinth]] · [[Acts 18]] *(historical)*
- **AD 53–AD 55** — **Paul writes 1 Corinthians from Ephesus** — Scholarly dating places the letter during Paul's extended Ephesian ministry (cf. Acts 19; 1 Corinthians 16:8), a few years after founding the Corinthian church described in Acts 18. — [[Paul (apostle)]] · [[Corinth]] · [[1 Corinthians 1]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 53–AD 55** — **Paul writes First Corinthians from Ephesus** — Standard Pauline chronology places composition during Paul's extended stay in Ephesus, roughly two decades after the crucifixion. — [[Paul]] · [[Corinth]] · [[Ephesus]] · [[1 Corinthians 11]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 53–AD 55** — **Paul writes First Corinthians from Ephesus** — Widely accepted scholarly dating places composition during Paul's roughly three-year stay in Ephesus, mid-first century AD, addressing reports of division and disorder in the Corinthian church. — [[Paul]] · [[Corinth]] · [[Ephesus]] · [[1 Corinthians 12]] *(historical)*
- **AD 53–AD 57** — **Paul writes 1 Corinthians from Ephesus** — Conventional New Testament chronology places the letter during Paul's roughly three-year residence in Ephesus (cf. Acts 19), commonly dated to the mid-50s AD, before his final journey toward Jerusalem. — [[Paul (Apostle)]] · [[Ephesus]] · [[Corinth]] · [[1 Corinthians 13]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 55–AD 56** — **Paul writes 2 Corinthians during his third missionary journey** — Widely accepted scholarly dating places 2 Corinthians during Paul's third missionary journey, written from Macedonia after leaving Ephesus. — [[Paul (apostle)]] · [[2 Corinthians 3]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 56–AD 58** — **Paul's farewell to the Ephesian elders at Miletus** — Placed near the end of Paul's third missionary journey, shortly before his final trip to Jerusalem before Pentecost (v.16); reconstructed from Acts' internal itinerary combined with broader Pauline chronology. — [[Paul (apostle)]] · [[Timothy (companion of Paul)]] · [[Ephesus]] · [[Miletus]] · [[Acts 20]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 57–AD 59** — **Paul's Sanhedrin trial and transfer to Caesarea** — Not dated within the chapter itself; placed via standard reconstructions of Pauline chronology (e.g. the Felix-to-Festus transition and related external synchronisms) that scholars use to date Paul's Caesarean custody. — [[Paul]] · [[Ananias]] · [[Claudius Lysias]] · [[Felix]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Caesarea]] · [[Acts 23]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 59–AD 62** — **Paul's voyage and Roman imprisonment** — shipwreck, a viper, and letters from chains — [[Paul]] · [[Luke]] · [[Malta]] · [[Rome]] · [[Acts 27]] · [[Acts 28]] *(historical)*
- **AD 60–AD 62** — **Colossians written during a Pauline imprisonment** — Traditional dating places Colossians among Paul's 'Prison Epistles,' composed during a Roman imprisonment; some scholars propose an earlier Ephesian imprisonment instead, which is not resolvable from this chapter alone. — [[Paul (apostle)]] · [[Rome (traditional)]] · [[Colossians 4]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 64–AD 67** — **Peter and Paul martyred under Nero** — I have finished my course — [[Peter]] · [[Paul]] · [[Nero]] · [[Rome]] · [[2 Timothy 4]] · [[2 Peter 1]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 64–AD 68** — **Traditional dating of 2 Peter near Peter's death** — Tradition places composition shortly before Peter's martyrdom under Nero, consistent with 2 Peter 1:14's statement that Christ had shown Peter his death was near; critical scholarship disputes this and often dates the letter into the early-to-mid 2nd century. — [[Peter (apostle)]] · [[2 Peter 1]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 64–AD 68** — **2 Peter composed / traditional apostolic dating** — Tradition places 2 Peter shortly before Peter's martyrdom under Nero; this is disputed by scholars who read 3:16's reference to a collected body of Pauline 'scripture' as evidence of later, possibly pseudonymous composition. — [[Peter (apostle)]] · [[Paul (apostle)]] · [[2 Peter 3]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 70** — **Rome destroys Jerusalem and the temple** — not one stone upon another, as He said — [[Jerusalem]] · [[Matthew 24]] · [[Luke 21]] *(historical)*
- **AD 85–AD 95** — **Traditional composition of the Johannine epistles at Ephesus** — Early Christian tradition attributes 1 John to the apostle John writing from Ephesus late in the first century, addressing a community facing secession by teachers who denied Jesus' full messianic identity (cf. 1 John 2:19, 22). — [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Ephesus]] · [[1 John 2]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 90–AD 95** — **Traditional composition of 1 John at Ephesus** — Christian tradition attributes 1 John to the Apostle John late in his life, commonly dated c. AD 90-95, likely written from or for the church at Ephesus. — [[John the Apostle]] · [[Ephesus]] · [[1 John 5]] *(traditional dating)*
- **AD 95** — **John's Revelation on Patmos** — the last apostle sees the end from the beginning — [[John (apostle)]] · [[Patmos]] · [[Revelation 1]] · [[Revelation 21]] *(approximate)*

## In the Book of Mormon lands
- **AD 1** — **Birth sign and threatened believers** — The chapter's internal ninety-second-year notation and official chapter heading place the reported birth sign at approximately A.D. 1. — [[Nephi]] · [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Samuel the Lamanite]] · [[Zarahemla]] · [[3 Nephi 1]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 2–AD 4** — **Gadianton robbers resurge in the mountains** — 3 Nephi 1:27-30 places renewed Gadianton violence in the Nephite record's 93rd and 94th years, following the sign of Christ's birth. — [[Lachoneus]] · [[Zarahemla]] · [[3 Nephi 1]] *(Book of Mormon internal dating)*
- **AD 5–AD 16** — **Forgetting the sign and escalation of Gadianton war** — The official chapter heading dates the chapter approximately A.D. 5–16, while verses 4–19 synchronize older reckonings with years nine through fifteen after the sign. — [[Nephi]] · [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Gadianton robbers]] · [[converted Lamanites]] · [[Zarahemla]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[3 Nephi 2]] *(approximate)*
- **AD 16** — **Giddianhi's ultimatum to Lachoneus** — The chapter dates Giddianhi's epistle to 'the sixteenth year from the coming of Christ' (3 Nephi 3:1), per the Nephite post-advent calendar established in 3 Nephi 1-2. — [[Giddianhi]] · [[Lachoneus]] · [[Zarahemla]] · [[3 Nephi 3]] *(Book of Mormon internal dating)*
- **AD 17** — **Nephite gathering to the Zarahemla-Bountiful line** — The full mobilization and gathering of the Nephites to their defensive position is dated to 'the seventeenth year, in the latter end of the year' (3 Nephi 3:22). — [[Gidgiddoni]] · [[Lachoneus]] · [[Zarahemla]] · [[Land of Bountiful]] · [[3 Nephi 3]] *(Book of Mormon internal dating)*
- **AD 18–AD 22** — **Nephite defeat of Giddianhi and Zemnarihah's robber armies** — Years are reckoned internally by Nephite record-keepers from the sign of Christ's birth established in 3 Nephi 1; the chapter heading dates the episode to approximately A.D. 19-22. — [[Giddianhi]] · [[Zemnarihah]] · [[Gidgiddoni]] · [[land northward]] · [[wilderness]] · [[3 Nephi 4]] *(Book of Mormon internal dating)*
- **AD 22–AD 26** — **Nephite repentance and end of the Gadianton robbers** — Based on the chapter's own year-count (22nd through 25th year since the sign of Christ's birth, 3 Nephi 5:7); the 'A.D.' correlation in the traditional chapter heading is an editorial addition, not part of the ancient text. — [[Mormon]] · [[Nephi (the disciple, son of Nephi)]] · [[Land of Mormon]] · [[3 Nephi 5]] *(Book of Mormon internal dating)*
- **AD 22–AD 25** — **Postwar repentance and suppression of robber combinations** — 3 Nephi 5:7 explicitly marks the passing of the twenty-second through twenty-fifth years; the official heading correlates the chapter with approximately A.D. 22–26. — [[Nephites]] · [[captured robbers]] · [[3 Nephi 5]] *(Book of Mormon internal dating)*
- **AD 26–AD 30** — **Nephite prosperity, pride, and apostasy (3 Nephi 6)** — Years are counted internally in the text from the sign of Christ's birth ('the twenty and sixth year' through 'the thirtieth year'). — [[Lachoneus (son)]] · [[Gidgiddoni]] · [[Zarahemla]] · [[3 Nephi 6]] *(Book of Mormon internal dating)*
- **AD 26–AD 30** — **Postwar restoration and descent into conspiracy** — The chapter's own year markers place resettlement in year 26, disputation in year 29, and extreme wickedness and conspiracy at the beginning of year 30. — [[Gidgiddoni]] · [[Lachoneus]] · [[Jesus Christ]] · [[Satan]] · [[Zarahemla]] · [[3 Nephi 6]] *(Book of Mormon internal dating)*
- **AD 30–AD 33** — **Nephite government collapses; Nephi's three-year ministry of repentance** — Explicit internal year markers tied to the Nephite calendar reckoned from the sign of Christ's birth (3 Nephi 7:1, 13, 14, 21, 23, 26). — [[Nephi (son of Nephi)]] · [[Jacob (self-proclaimed king of the secret combination)]] · [[Northernmost part of the Nephite lands]] · [[3 Nephi 7]] *(Book of Mormon internal dating)*
