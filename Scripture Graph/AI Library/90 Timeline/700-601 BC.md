# 700-601 BC — what was happening

_All the world's scripture history in this century, side by side. Dating honesty: every entry says how its date is known._

## In the Old World
- **700 BC** — **Isaiah's oracle of Zion's restoration and renaming** — Traditionally attributed to Isaiah of Jerusalem's 8th-century BC ministry; critical scholarship instead commonly dates Isaiah 56-66 to the post-exilic period, a point of ongoing dispute. — [[Jerusalem]] · [[Isaiah 62]] *(traditional dating)*
- **697 BC–642 BC** — **Manasseh's 55-year reign over Judah** — 2 Kings 21:1 records a 55-year reign; absolute dates follow standard reconstructions synchronized with Assyrian annals naming Manasseh as a tributary vassal of Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal. — [[Manasseh]] · [[Hezekiah]] · [[Amon]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Kings 21]] *(approximate)*
- **697 BC–642 BC** — **Reign of Manasseh, king of Judah, in Jerusalem** — Conventional regnal chronology (following the biblical account of a 55-year reign) places Manasseh's reign c. 697-642 BC, generations before Jeremiah's ministry began, which is why Jeremiah 15:4 treats him as a past cause of present judgment. — [[Manasseh]] · [[Hezekiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Jeremiah 15]] *(traditional dating)*
- **681 BC** — **Assassination of Sennacherib at Nineveh** — The Babylonian Chronicle records Sennacherib's murder by a son during a succession struggle in 681 BCE, with Esarhaddon emerging as successor, broadly corroborating Isaiah 37:37-38. — [[Sennacherib]] · [[Assyria]] · [[Isaiah 37]] *(historical)*
- **671 BC–663 BC** — **Assyrian conquest and sack of Egypt under Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal** — Assyrian royal records document Esarhaddon's 671 BC invasion of Egypt and Ashurbanipal's 663 BC sack of Thebes; some scholars propose these events as a partial, later fulfillment of the 'cruel lord'/'fierce king' judgment of Isaiah 19:4, though this identification is not certain. — [[Egypt]] · [[Assyria]] · [[Isaiah 19]] *(historical)*
- **663 BC–612 BC** — **Likely composition window of the book of Nahum** — Elsewhere in the book, Nahum 3:8 refers to the fall of Thebes (663 BC) as already past, while the fall of Nineveh (612 BC) is anticipated as still future; this brackets the book's likely composition, though chapter 1 itself contains no explicit date. — [[Nahum]] · [[Nineveh]] · [[Nahum 1]] *(historical)*
- **642 BC–640 BC** — **Amon's two-year reign and assassination** — 2 Kings 21:19 records a two-year reign, ending in assassination by his own servants and the enthronement of Josiah. — [[Amon]] · [[Manasseh]] · [[Josiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Kings 21]] *(approximate)*
- **640 BC–609 BC** — **Reign of Josiah of Judah** — Conventional regnal chronology derived from the Deuteronomistic accession/reign-length formula in 2 Kings 22:1 (age eight, thirty-one years in Jerusalem). — [[Josiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Kings 22]] *(traditional dating)*
- **640 BC–609 BC** — **Zephaniah's prophetic ministry under King Josiah** — Zephaniah 1:1 dates the prophet's activity to the reign of Josiah, king of Judah (traditionally ca. 640-609 BC). — [[Zephaniah]] · [[Josiah]] · [[Judah]] · [[Zephaniah 2]] *(traditional dating)*
- **627 BC–609 BC** — **Jeremiah's covenant sermon in Judah's cities** — Many commentators associate this covenant-renewal oracle with the reign of Josiah, possibly connected to the covenant renewal following the discovery of the law book (2 Kings 23:1-3), though the exact occasion and date within Jeremiah's ministry are debated among scholars. — [[Jeremiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Anathoth]] · [[Jeremiah 11]] *(traditional dating)*
- **623 BC–621 BC** — **Discovery of the book of the law (18th year)** — Correlated with Josiah's eighteenth regnal year (2 Chronicles 34:8), conventionally dated to about 622 BCE within his c. 640-609 reign. — [[Josiah]] · [[Hilkiah]] · [[Shaphan]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Chronicles 34]] *(traditional dating)*
- **622 BC** — **Josiah's reform; the book found** — a lost book of the law shakes a kingdom — [[Josiah]] · [[Huldah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Kings 22]] *(historical)*
- **612 BC** — **Fall of Nineveh to Babylonian-Median coalition** — Nineveh fell in 612 BC to a coalition of Babylonian, Median, and allied forces, ending Assyrian imperial dominance; attested in the Babylonian Chronicles and archaeological destruction layers. — [[Nineveh]] · [[Zephaniah 2]] *(historical)*
- **609 BC** — **Battle of Megiddo and death of Josiah** — Necho II's march toward Carchemish to aid the remnant Assyrian state against Babylon is generally dated to 609 BC based on Babylonian chronicle records; Josiah's death at Megiddo is placed in the same campaign. — [[Josiah]] · [[Megiddo]] · [[2 Chronicles 35]] *(historical)*
- **609 BC–587 BC** — **Successive reigns of Judah's last four kings** — Regnal notices for Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin, and Zedekiah given in 2 Chronicles 36:1-11. — [[Jehoahaz]] · [[Jehoiakim]] · [[Jehoiachin]] · [[Zedekiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Chronicles 36]] *(traditional dating)*
- **609 BC** — **Death of Josiah at Megiddo** — Josiah's death confronting Pharaoh-nechoh at Megiddo (2 Kings 23:29) is widely dated to 609 BCE based on synchronism with Egyptian and Neo-Babylonian records of Necho's campaign toward the Euphrates. — [[Josiah]] · [[Pharaoh-nechoh]] · [[Megiddo]] · [[2 Kings 23]] *(historical)*
- **609 BC** — **Jehoahaz (Shallum) deposed, exiled to Egypt** — 2 Kings 23:31-34; conventional date shortly after Josiah's death at Megiddo, when Pharaoh Necho installed Jehoiakim in his place — [[Shallum (Jehoahaz)]] · [[Pharaoh Necho]] · [[Egypt]] · [[Jeremiah 22]] *(historical)*
- **609 BC–598 BC** — **Execution of the prophet Urijah by Jehoiakim** — Narrated as occurring during Jehoiakim's reign (609-598 BC) without a more precise internal date; placed alongside Jeremiah's own trial. — [[Urijah]] · [[Jehoiakim]] · [[Elnathan]] · [[Egypt]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Jeremiah 26]] *(approximate)*
- **609 BC–598 BC** — **Jeremiah tests the Rechabites during Jehoiakim's reign** — Jeremiah 35:1 places the word in the days of Jehoiakim; the conventional bounds of his reign do not provide a precise date for the scene. — [[Jeremiah]] · [[Jehoiakim]] · [[Nebuchadrezzar]] · [[Rechabites]] · [[Judah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Jeremiah 35]] *(approximate)*
- **609 BC–608 BC** — **Jeremiah's temple-gate sermon (Jeremiah 7 / parallel to Jer. 26)** — Jeremiah 26:1 dates the closely related temple-sermon narrative to 'the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim,' commonly placed around 609-608 BCE; Jeremiah 7 itself supplies no internal date. — [[Jeremiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Jeremiah 7]] *(traditional dating)*
- **609 BC–587 BC** — **Jeremiah's pre-exilic judgment oracles against Judah** — The chapter is undated internally; it is traditionally located within Jeremiah's ministry, which began in 627 BC (Jeremiah 1:2) and continued until Jerusalem's destruction in 587/586 BC. — [[Jeremiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Judah]] · [[Jeremiah 9]] *(traditional dating)*
- **605 BC** — **Daniel taken to Babylon** — first deportation — courtiers in exile — [[Daniel]] · [[Nebuchadnezzar]] · [[Babylon]] · [[Daniel 1]] *(historical)*
- **605 BC** — **Babylon defeats Egypt at Carchemish** — Jeremiah 46:2 dates the defeat to Jehoiakim's fourth year; the Babylonian Chronicle independently places Nebuchadnezzar's victory over Egypt at Carchemish in 605 BCE. — [[Pharaoh-necho]] · [[Nebuchadrezzar]] · [[Jehoiakim]] · [[Carchemish]] · [[Euphrates River]] · [[Jeremiah 46]] *(historical)*
