# 800-701 BC — what was happening

_All the world's scripture history in this century, side by side. Dating honesty: every entry says how its date is known._

## In the Old World
- **796 BC–767 BC** — **Reign of Amaziah of Judah** — Conventional biblical chronology places Amaziah's 29-year reign in the early-to-mid 8th century BC, following his father Joash of Judah; exact dates vary by chronological scheme. — [[Amaziah]] · [[Joash (king of Israel)]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Chronicles 25]] *(traditional dating)*
- **792 BC–740 BC** — **Uzziah's reign and Jotham's administrative role** — A common reconstruction places Uzziah's long reign around 792–740 BC and uses co-regency to reconcile the fifty-two-year regnal notice with the wider royal chronology; exact dates remain disputed. — [[Uzziah]] · [[Jotham]] · [[Isaiah]] · [[Azariah]] · [[Judah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Chronicles 26]] *(approximate)*
- **792 BC–740 BC** — **Reign of Uzziah (Azariah) of Judah** — Conventional biblical chronology synchronizing Judah's regnal lengths with Israel and Assyrian records; exact dates are disputed due to a probable co-regency with his father Amaziah and later with his son Jotham. — [[Uzziah]] · [[Amaziah]] · [[Jotham]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Chronicles 26]] *(traditional dating)*
- **760 BC–722 BC** — **Amos and Hosea warn the north** — justice like waters; love that will not let go — [[Amos]] · [[Hosea]] · [[Samaria]] · [[Amos 5]] · [[Hosea 11]] *(approximate)*
- **760 BC–750 BC** — **Amos's ministry under Jeroboam II, before the Uzziah earthquake** — Amos 1:1 dates the words to the overlapping reigns of Uzziah (Judah) and Jeroboam II (Israel), 'two years before the earthquake'; conventional scholarship places this in the decade before ca. 750 BC, though the exact year of the earthquake is not independently fixed by the text. — [[Amos]] · [[Uzziah]] · [[Jeroboam II]] · [[Tekoa]] · [[Amos 1]] *(approximate)*
- **760 BC–750 BC** — **Amos's prophetic ministry to the Northern Kingdom** — Conventionally dated to the reigns of Jeroboam II of Israel and Uzziah of Judah, per the book's superscription, roughly a generation before Israel's fall to Assyria in 722 BCE. — [[Amos]] · [[Israel (Northern Kingdom)]] · [[Amos 5]] *(traditional dating)*
- **755 BC–722 BC** — **Hosea prophesies through Israel's final kings** — Hosea's ministry is conventionally dated from the later reign of Jeroboam II through Samaria's fall, encompassing the political instability described in Hosea 10:3,7,15. — [[Hosea]] · [[Samaria]] · [[Hosea 10]] *(approximate)*
- **753 BC–732 BC** — **Israelite throne instability, multiple usurper kings** — 2 Kings 15 records roughly six kings of Israel within about two decades, several installed by assassination, offered here as historical background (not proof of authorial intent) for Hosea 8:4's charge that kings were 'set up... not by me.' — [[Zechariah (king of Israel)]] · [[Shallum]] · [[Menahem]] · [[Pekah]] · [[Samaria]] · [[Hosea 8]] *(historical)*
- **750 BC–734 BC** — **Reign of Jotham of Judah** — Sixteen-year reign length given in 2 Chronicles 27:1, 8; absolute dates depend on disputed co-regency reconstructions with Uzziah and are not fixed by the text itself. — [[Jotham]] · [[Uzziah]] · [[Ahaz]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Chronicles 27]] *(approximate)*
- **740 BC–690 BC** — **Isaiah's ministry in Jerusalem** — the prophet Nephi quotes most — [[Isaiah]] · [[Hezekiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Isaiah 6]] · [[Isaiah 53]] · [[2 Kings 19]] *(approximate)*
- **740 BC–700 BC** — **Assyrian expansion and threat toward Jerusalem** — The oracle presupposes Neo-Assyrian victories over multiple Syrian and Israelite cities and portrays an Assyrian advance toward Jerusalem. — [[king of Assyria]] · [[Assyria]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Isaiah 10]] *(approximate)*
- **735 BC–715 BC** — **Reign of Ahaz king of Judah** — Reconstructed from regnal synchronisms with Pekah of Israel (2 Kings 16:1) and cross-checked against Assyrian tribute records; dates vary by roughly a decade across scholarly reconstructions due to possible co-regencies. — [[Ahaz]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Kings 16]] *(approximate)*
- **734 BC–701 BC** — **Assyrian crisis oracles in Isaiah 7-10** — Scholars date Isa 7-10 to either the Syro-Ephraimite crisis (734-732 BCE) or Sennacherib's 701 BCE invasion of Judah; the march route in 10:28-32 does not clearly match either historical campaign. — [[King of Assyria]] · [[Assyria]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Isaiah 10]] *(approximate)*
- **734 BC–722 BC** — **Maher-shalal-hash-baz sign and Assyrian campaigns against Damascus/Samaria** — Isaiah 8:1-4's child-naming sign is generally correlated with Tiglath-Pileser III's 734-732 BC campaigns against Damascus and Israelite territory, with Samaria's final fall following in 722 BC under Sargon II. — [[Isaiah]] · [[Rezin]] · [[Remaliah's son (Pekah)]] · [[Damascus]] · [[Samaria]] · [[Isaiah 8]] *(historical)*
- **732 BC** — **Assyrian conquest of Damascus and death of Rezin** — Dated from Tiglath-pileser III's Assyrian royal annals recording the fall of Damascus, a date widely treated as secure and used to corroborate 2 Kings 16:9. — [[Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria]] · [[Rezin]] · [[Damascus]] · [[2 Kings 16]] *(historical)*
- **726 BC–725 BC** — **Hezekiah's national Passover reform at Jerusalem** — Traditionally placed early in Hezekiah's reign, shortly after his temple purification (2 Chronicles 29) and near the time of the Assyrian conquest of the northern kingdom; exact regnal dating for Hezekiah is disputed among chronologists. — [[Hezekiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Chronicles 30]] *(traditional dating)*
- **722 BC** — **Assyria destroys northern Israel** — the ten tribes scattered — the 'lost tribes' — [[Samaria]] · [[Assyria]] · [[2 Kings 17]] *(historical)*
- **722 BC** — **Assyrian conquest of Samaria (background referent of Ezek. 23:9-10)** — Widely dated Assyrian conquest of the northern kingdom's capital, treated in 2 Kings 17; Ezekiel 23:9-10 describes Aholah/Samaria being delivered to Assyrian lovers who slay her sons and daughters, matching this event's memory. — [[Assyrian conquerors]] · [[Samaria]] · [[Ezekiel 23]] *(historical)*
- **722 BC–721 BC** — **Assyrian conquest ends the Northern Kingdom's monarchy** — 2 Kings 17 records Shalmaneser's siege of Samaria and the deportation of Israel, matching Hosea 10:6's prediction that the Bethel calf would be carried to Assyria. — [[Samaria]] · [[Assyria]] · [[Hosea 10]] *(historical)*
- **722 BC** — **Fall of Samaria and collapse of the northern kingdom** — Conventional date for Assyria's conquest of Samaria, the event Hosea 13:16 appears to anticipate; corroborated externally by Assyrian records and 2 Kings 17. — [[Samaria]] · [[Hosea 13]] *(historical)*
- **715 BC–686 BC** — **Hezekiah's reign and first-month temple reform** — Approximate range commonly cited from synchronistic reconstructions (e.g., Thiele-type chronologies) using Assyrian-period synchronisms; Hezekiah's exact accession date and any co-regency with Ahaz remain disputed among chronologists. — [[Hezekiah]] · [[David (king)]] · [[Aaron (brother of Moses)]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Chronicles 29]] *(historical)*
- **715 BC–686 BC** — **Micah's Zion oracle cited as precedent from Hezekiah's reign** — Attributed to 'the days of Hezekiah king of Judah' (Jeremiah 26:18), whose reign is conventionally dated c. 715-686 BC. — [[Micah]] · [[Hezekiah]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Jeremiah 26]] *(historical)*
- **715 BC–686 BC** — **Hezekiah's Scribes Compile Solomon's Proverbs** — Proverbs 25:1 states that Hezekiah's men copied out this collection; years reflect the traditional dating of Hezekiah's reign over Judah. — [[Hezekiah]] · [[Solomon]] · [[Judah]] · [[Proverbs 25]] *(traditional dating)*
- **703 BC–700 BC** — **Hezekiah's illness, sign, and the Babylonian embassy** — Placed within Hezekiah's reign (commonly reconstructed c. 715-687 BC), correlated with the historically attested reigns of Merodach-baladan II over Babylon; exact placement of the illness within Hezekiah's 29-year reign relative to Sennacherib's 701 BC campaign is debated. — [[Hezekiah]] · [[Isaiah]] · [[Berodach-baladan]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[Babylon]] · [[2 Kings 20]] *(approximate)*
- **701 BC** — **Jerusalem spared from Sennacherib** — the angel and the Assyrian camp — [[Hezekiah]] · [[Isaiah]] · [[Sennacherib]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Kings 19]] · [[Isaiah 37]] *(historical)*

## In the Book of Mormon lands
- **734 BC–701 BC** — **Isaiah's oracles against Assyria (8th century B.C. crisis)** — Isaiah's prophecies concerning Assyria are traditionally situated within the Syro-Ephraimite crisis through the era of Sennacherib's invasion of Judah, though this chapter itself gives no internal date. — [[Isaiah]] · [[Assyria]] · [[Jerusalem]] · [[2 Nephi 20]] *(approximate)*
