# Abraham 2 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Abraham 2]] · My notes: [[Abraham 2 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Abraham 2 follows Abraham from Ur through Haran and Canaan toward Egypt. Divine commands govern the principal departures, while famine repeatedly shapes the setting and explicitly prompts the move toward Egypt. Verses 6 and 9-11 give the chapter's fullest statement of the [[Abrahamic Covenant]], joining land and posterity with ministry, [[Priesthood]], Gospel reception, salvation, and blessing for all families of the earth. The narrative also preserves hardship and unresolved tension: Terah remains after returning to idolatry, famine continues after the land promise, and the chapter ends with Sarai facing an unreported outcome in Egypt.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious narrative outline is:

1. **Ur and Haran (vv. 1-5):** famine, family relationships, the call to leave, and Terah's decision to remain.
2. **Call and covenant (vv. 6-11):** divine appearance and promises concerning land, seed, name, ministry, Priesthood, and universal blessing.
3. **Response and departure (vv. 12-16):** Abraham recalls deliverance and leaves Haran.
4. **Journey and worship in Canaan (vv. 17-20):** prayer, sacrifice, altars, and confirmation of the land promise.
5. **Famine and Egypt (vv. 21-25):** famine prompts further travel, and the chapter closes with the unresolved instruction concerning Sarai.

This is a practical outline, not proof of a formal literary design.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verses 9-11 present the [[Abrahamic Covenant]] as both blessing and ministry: Abraham's seed is to bear ministry and [[Priesthood]] to all nations. Covenant identity is not described only in biological terms, because those who receive the Gospel may be accounted Abraham's seed; verse 11 also separately affirms a promise concerning literal bodily seed. The blessing offered to all families is identified with the Gospel, [[Salvation]], and [[Eternal Life]]. Verse 6 attaches the phrase "when they hearken to my voice" to the promised possession, although the chapter does not fully explain how that condition relates to the word "everlasting."
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The journey is repeatedly punctuated by [[Prayer]], altars, offerings, and sacrifice. This supports a thematic reading of travel accompanied by worship, but it does not establish a formal pattern or prove that the altars were missionary activity.

Abraham 2 has a strong literary relationship with [[Genesis 12]]. The chapters share the departure call, movement toward Canaan, the land promise, altar building near Bethel and Ai, southward travel, famine-driven movement toward Egypt, and the sister strategy. Abraham 2 also contains material absent from Genesis 12, including extended Priesthood and Gospel language. Direct dependence on or revision of the King James Genesis narrative is a leading critical explanation; restoration, inspired expansion, or inheritance from a shared tradition are alternative interpretations. The parallel alone cannot establish direction, date, independent antiquity, historical accuracy, or a particular theory of revelation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary Parallel
> Abraham 2 and Genesis 12 share a closely ordered narrative sequence: a command to leave, migration toward Canaan, a land promise, altar building near Bethel, famine-driven travel to Egypt, and the sister strategy concerning Sarai. This is strong evidence of literary relationship or shared narrative tradition, but it does not by itself determine the direction, date, or mechanism of dependence.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · Strong literary relationship; provenance disputed · [[Literary Parallel in Abraham 2|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Cross-Text Chronological Tension
> Abraham 2 reports that Abraham was sixty-two when he departed Haran, whereas Genesis 12 reports that Abram was seventy-five when he departed Haran. On their most straightforward readings, the chronological notices differ.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · Accepted observation; harmonizations disputed · [[Cross-Text Chronological Tension in Abraham 2|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Extensive Textual And Narrative Parallel
> Abraham 2 has a strong literary relationship with Genesis 12: both narrate the call from kindred, departure from Haran with Sarai and Lot, arrival at Moreh, a land promise, altar building near Bethel and Ai, southward travel, famine-driven movement to Egypt, and the sister strategy concerning Sarai.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · Strong literary relationship; provenance disputed · [[Extensive Textual And Narrative Parallel in Abraham 2|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal Literary Pattern *(tentative)*
> A recurring pattern organizes much of the chapter: divine speech or providential pressure is followed by Abraham's response in departure, prayer, altar building, or further travel. This pattern supports reading the chapter as a theological migration narrative centered on revelation and response.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.7 · Useful thematic pattern; formal design unproven

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How does "when they hearken to my voice" qualify the promise of an everlasting possession?
- How do the Gospel-based and literal senses of Abraham's seed relate in verses 10-11?
- What might "the souls that we had won in Haran" mean: converts, servants, dependents, or people otherwise incorporated into the household?
- Why does grievous famine follow so closely after confirmation of the land promise?
- Do the ages sixty-two in Abraham 2:14 and seventy-five in [[Genesis 12]] describe separate departures, different chronological traditions, or an unresolved discrepancy?
- How should readers evaluate the risks borne by Sarai when her response and the Egyptian outcome are not recorded in this chapter?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[Abraham 1]] for the deliverance recalled in Abraham 2:13. Then compare Abraham 2:3-25 with [[Genesis 11]], [[Genesis 12]], and [[Acts 7]]. Track exact wording as well as narrative differences: who initiates the migration, when the divine call occurs, Abraham's stated age, the covenant language, and who originates the sister strategy. Distinguish the observable textual relationships from competing explanations of their direction, date, and origin.
