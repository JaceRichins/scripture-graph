## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious outline is:

- Verses 1–3: counsel concerning completion, rest, and sanctification of the seventh time.
- Verses 4–6: transition to formation and the watering of the ground.
- Verses 7–13: Adam's embodiment, the garden, his work, and the command concerning the tree.
- Verses 14–19: the formation of the woman and the one-flesh statement.
- Verses 20–21: formation and naming of animals, followed by the statement that no help meet was found for Adam.

The final unit does not fit a straightforward chronology because the woman has already been formed. The text may be arranging related material topically, but it does not explain the ordering.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter contributes directly to several doctrinal themes. Rest and sanctification of the seventh time connect [[Creation]] with the [[Sabbath Day]] (verses 1–3). The distinction between Adam's spirit and formed body is relevant to [[Premortal Life]], although verse 7 does not explain the spirit's origin. Adam's duty to dress and keep the garden places meaningful work before the Fall (verse 11). Verses 16–18 ground [[Marriage]] in companionship, kinship language, cleaving, and becoming one flesh; they do not by themselves articulate every later doctrine associated with [[Eternal Marriage]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter's most conspicuous linguistic feature is the plural subject and pronouns "the Gods... we... among themselves," replacing Genesis's singular "God"/"the LORD God" (English past-tense verbs like "said" and "formed" do not themselves mark number, so the contrast lies in subject and pronoun, not verb form). A second distinctive substitution is "the seventh time" (occurring four times across vv.2-3) in place of Genesis's "the seventh day"; verse 13's aside about the Lord's time, Kolob's time, and Adam's not-yet-appointed reckoning cautions against assuming this "time" language maps onto ordinary days, though it supplies no numerical duration. Verse 13 is also the chapter's only first-person intrusion — "Now I, Abraham, saw that..." Notably, the woman is never called "Eve" in this chapter; she is named "Woman" (v.17) and referred to as "the woman" and "his wife" throughout.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Abraham 5 is closely related to [[Genesis 2]] and [[Moses 3]], yet close comparison exposes a meaningful ordering difference: in Genesis and Moses, the animals are brought to Adam and named before the woman is formed, while in Abraham 5 the woman's formation and the one-flesh statement (vv.14-19) appear first, with the animal-naming episode and the summary statement that "there was found an help meet for him" (v.21) following afterward — most plausibly a recapitulating summary rather than a new chronological claim. The chapter's closing image, "they were both naked...and were not ashamed" (v.19), also functions as a deliberate-feeling setup for the shame that follows the Fall in [[Genesis 3]].
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Comparative Ancient Near Eastern Religion *(tentative)*

> [!evidence] Biblical Source Criticism *(tentative)*

Historical-critical biblical scholarship generally explains this two-account structure in Genesis as the combination of two originally distinct source traditions (commonly labeled P and J); Latter-day Saint and other harmonizing readings instead treat the two accounts as complementary cosmic and focused tellings of a single Creation.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Who does "the Gods" refer to — the full premortal council described in [[Abraham 3]], a smaller subset, or another arrangement? The chapter does not say.
- What is implied — and what is not implied — by the Gods taking Adam's spirit and putting it into his formed body (v.7)?
- Does "the seventh time" describe a measurable period, a divine mode of reckoning, or simply a narrative stage, given verse 13's caution about Kolob's time versus Adam's not-yet-appointed reckoning?
- Why does verse 21 state that a help meet was found for Adam after the woman's formation has already been narrated (vv.15-16) — recap, or reordering?
- Why does the text's narrator interject only once, at verse 13, and only to mention Kolob?
