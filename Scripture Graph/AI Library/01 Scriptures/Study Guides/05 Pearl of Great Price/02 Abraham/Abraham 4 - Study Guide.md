## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **First time (vv. 1–5):** heaven and earth are formed; light is divided from darkness.

2. **Second time (vv. 6–8):** the expanse divides waters and is called Heaven.

3. **Third time (vv. 9–13):** dry land appears and the earth brings forth seed-bearing vegetation.

4. **Fourth time (vv. 14–19):** lights are organized for illumination, rule, signs, seasons, days, and years.

5. **Fifth time (vv. 20–23):** waters and the open expanse are populated; living creatures are blessed to multiply.

6. **Sixth time (vv. 24–31):** land animals and humanity are formed; humanity receives blessing, food, dominion, and the charge to multiply.

A broad literary correspondence links periods one and four (light and lights), two and five (sky/waters and their inhabitants), and three and six (productive land and land-dwelling life).

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents [[Creation]] as purposeful and ordered. The Gods speak, counsel, prepare, organize, supervise, and anticipate obedience. Humanity is formed male and female in the image of the Gods and receives blessing, food, dominion, and the charge to multiply.

Several conclusions require restraint. The plural grammar and explicit counsel are textual observations, but Abraham 4 does not identify every participant or fully explain their relationships. Its organization-and-formation vocabulary is consistent with LDS teaching about pre-existing matter, but this chapter alone does not establish matter's origin or eternality. Likewise, reading dominion as stewardship is a plausible ethical application, not a duty explicitly defined in the chapter.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Abraham 4 repeatedly uses process-oriented verbs: the Gods organize, form, prepare, order, set, and watch. The words “create” and “created” do not occur. This vocabulary depicts directed arrangement and implementation but does not independently explain the ultimate origin of matter.

The numbered conclusions use “second time” through “sixth time,” while continuing to describe evening, morning, day, and night. The repeated forms “were obeyed,” “would be obeyed,” “would obey,” and “shall be very obedient” establish a motif of ordered responsiveness without demonstrating moral agency in nonhuman creation. Verse 2's description of the Spirit of the Gods as “brooding” is noteworthy, but its referent and significance remain undefined.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter is organized into six numbered periods, each closed by a night-and-day refrain. Divine speech, ordering or preparation, execution, naming, blessing, and reports of obedience recur, but not every element appears in every period. A broad two-triad correspondence links light with the heavenly lights, sky and waters with their inhabitants, and productive land with land-dwelling life. This is a useful literary outline rather than an exact symmetry or proof of authorial intent.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Repeated Textual Motif
> The recurring statements that created domains “were obeyed,” creatures “would be obeyed,” and the completed plan would be “very obedient” provide strong textual evidence that responsiveness to divine ordering is a deliberate motif in Abraham 4.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · Strong repeated textual motif · [[Repeated Textual Motif in Abraham 4|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Philological/Vocabulary Argument *(tentative)*
> The chapter's consistent use of 'organize/form/prepare/order' rather than 'create' is often cited in LDS teaching as textual support for the doctrine that the Gods organized pre-existing elements rather than creating the earth from nothing (creatio ex nihilo).
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · Believing interpretation; external attribution unsupported

> [!evidence] Speculative Interpretive Reading *(tentative)*
> The chapter's preference for 'time' over 'day' has sometimes been read as leaving open whether creation periods were literal 24-hour days, possibly gesturing toward the non-terrestrial time-reckoning discussed elsewhere in the Book of Abraham. Nothing in Abraham 4 itself specifies a duration for these 'times.'
> — strength 0.2 · confidence 0.4 · Speculative and externally unsourced
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does the shift from “were obeyed” to “would be obeyed” and “shall be very obedient” portray a plan being previewed, an implementation in progress, or a stylistic mixture of perspectives?

- What does the text establish about “the Spirit of the Gods,” and which conclusions would require evidence from other passages?

- How should dominion be understood when read beside the Gods' preparation, blessing, and provision for nonhuman life?

- Which claims about the nature of matter can actually be derived from “organized and formed,” and which depend on teachings outside this chapter?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
