# Moses 8 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Moses 8]] · My notes: [[Moses 8 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Moses 8 bridges the world of Enoch and the flood. Its genealogy establishes Noah within Enoch's covenant line (Moses 8:1-12), after which the chapter contrasts Noah's obedient household with a society characterized by refusal to hearken, self-exaltation, corruption, and violence (Moses 8:13-30). The flood announcement is therefore not abrupt: it follows prophetic commissioning, repeated warning, a conditional invitation to repent, and persistent rejection.

A distinctive feature is the chapter's explicit gospel framework. Noah teaches as in the beginning, receives the Gospel given to Enoch, and calls the people to belief, [[Repentance]], [[Baptism]] in the name of Jesus Christ, and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] (Moses 8:16, 19, 24).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Covenant genealogy and Noah's birth** — Enoch, Methuselah, Lamech, and Noah (Moses 8:1-12)
2. **Competing identities and failure to hearken** — Noah's household is called the sons of God; others claim the title while rejecting God's voice (Moses 8:13-16, 20-21)
3. **Commission, warning, and gospel invitation** — Noah is ordained, protected, and sent to preach repentance and ordinances (Moses 8:17-24)
4. **Prophetic grief and divine judgment** — Noah grieves, finds grace, and walks with God while the earth becomes corrupt and violent (Moses 8:25-30)
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents the [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]] as continuous with Enoch's ministry rather than as a response invented only after the flood. Its compact pattern is belief, [[Repentance]], [[Baptism]] in Christ's name, and reception of the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] (Moses 8:24).

Judgment is paired with warning and moral agency. The people are told what will happen if they do not repent, but they repeatedly refuse to hearken (Moses 8:17, 20-24). [[Grace]] appears in relation to Noah's just conduct and walk with God (Moses 8:27), although the verse alone does not settle broader debates about the relationship between grace and works.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The phrase "sons of God" is used in contrasting settings. The narrator applies it to Noah and his sons after stating that they hearkened and gave heed (Moses 8:13). Verses 14-15 then describe daughters of Noah's sons marrying the "sons of men," while resistant speakers later claim, "we are the sons of God" even though the narrative says they do not hearken (Moses 8:20-21). This juxtaposition supports a contrast between claimed religious identity and actual obedience, but the groups are not defined fully enough to settle the wider, disputed interpretation of [[Genesis 6]].

The 120 years in Moses 8:17 may describe a period of warning before the flood because the number immediately precedes a conditional call to repent. It may instead limit human lifespan, so the question should remain open.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Moses 8 first follows the genealogical movement of [[Genesis 5]] and then retells much of the moral setting of [[Genesis 6]]. Repetition supplies its principal emphasis: the people do not hearken (Moses 8:15, 20-21, 24), while "violence" appears in both the assessment and the final judgment announcement (Moses 8:28, 30).

One especially important difference from [[Genesis 6]] concerns grief. Genesis 6:6 attributes repentance and grief over humanity to the Lord. Moses 8:25-26 instead attributes them twice to Noah. The repetition makes this an integrated feature of Moses 8's present wording, but it does not determine which form is earlier or explain how the difference arose.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What distinguishes the narrator's use of "sons of God" in Moses 8:13 from the people's self-description in Moses 8:21?
- Does the 120-year statement describe a countdown to the flood, a lifespan limitation, or both?
- How does the attribution of grief to Noah in Moses 8:25-26 affect the portrayal of both Noah and God when compared with [[Genesis 6]]?
- Why does the narrative repeat refusal to hearken before repeating that the earth is filled with violence?
