# Jacob 3 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Jacob 3]] · My notes: [[Jacob 3 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The pure in heart receive the pleasing word of God—Lamanite righteousness exceeds that of the Nephites—Jacob warns against fornication, lasciviousness, and every sin. About 544–421 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Comfort for the pure in heart (vv. 1–2):** firmness of mind, [[Prayer]], [[Faith]], consolation, justice, and divine love.

2. **Warning to the unrepentant (vv. 3–4):** threatened cursing, dispossession, destruction, and removal of the righteous.

3. **Comparison with the Lamanites (vv. 5–9):** observance of the marriage commandment, family love, comparative righteousness, judgment reversal, and the prohibition on reviling because of dark skin.

4. **Children and awakening (vv. 10–11):** consequences of parental example and a summons to escape spiritual death and hell.

5. **Closing record notice (vv. 12–14):** omitted teachings, the larger plates, and the naming of the plates of Jacob.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter places [[Justice and Mercy]] alongside [[Repentance]]. God promises consolation and justice to faithful sufferers, while the Lamanites' observance of the marriage commandment is followed by preservation and mercy. Nephite identity does not exempt the unrepentant from judgment, and inherited stigma does not prevent recognition of present righteousness.

Family conduct is spiritually consequential. Love between spouses and toward children is praised, while corrupt parental example can grieve children and bring grave accountability at the [[Final Judgment]]. Verse 10 emphasizes parental influence and culpability without stating that children lack agency.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Jacob's purity language should not be flattened into a single category. He distinguishes current Nephite wrongdoing, Lamanite unbelief and hostility attributed to ancestral iniquity, comparative family righteousness, inherited "filthiness," and the darkness of Lamanite skins.

Verses 8–9 reverse the audience's presumption of superiority: Jacob warns that the Lamanites' skins may be whiter at God's throne, then forbids reviling them because of dark skin and commands the Nephites to remember their own filthiness. "Whiter" may involve moral-purity symbolism, visible appearance, or a deliberate interaction between both. The passage clearly condemns contempt, but it does not fully explain or retract its inherited curse framework.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The rhetoric moves from comfort to confrontation. Jacob addresses the pure in heart, turns to the double pronouncement "wo, wo," and then uses the despised Lamanites' conduct to challenge Nephite moral superiority. The question "how much better are you than they" functions rhetorically rather than requesting information.

Verse 11 accumulates imperatives—hearken, arouse, shake, awake, and loose—to portray repentance as awakening and release. Its devil and punishment imagery closely parallels [[2 Nephi 9]].

Verses 12–14 shift from sermon to record commentary. The "hundredth part" statement emphasizes selective preservation and has a close textual counterpart in [[Helaman 3]]. These parallels establish recurring literary language, not by themselves the direction of dependence or historical provenance.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary/Theological Analysis *(tentative)*
> Jacob 3's portrayal of Lamanite righteousness alongside a stated skin curse (vv.3-9) can be read as the text internally complicating a simple 'skin curse equals wickedness' equation, since it separates Lamanite conduct from Lamanite skin color; but this reading sits in tension with other Book of Mormon passages (e.g., 2 Nephi 5, Alma 3) that more directly link skin color to divine curse, so the book's stance is not uniform and this chapter does not resolve the tension.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · Important but disputed interpretation

> [!evidence] Close Textual And Rhetorical Evidence *(tentative)*
> Jacob 3 supplies strong textual evidence that Jacob is attacking Nephite contempt grounded in ethnicized appearance and inherited stereotypes: he condemns their hatred, questions their presumed superiority before the Creator, anticipates a reversal at judgment, and forbids reviling based on skin darkness.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.8 · Textual core accepted; modern framing disputed
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. Is "more righteous" a general assessment, or is it limited to the marriage and family conduct Jacob specifies?

2. How does the command against reviling because of skin darkness interact with the chapter's continued inherited-curse language?

3. Does "our father" in verse 5 refer specifically to Lehi, or does the phrase invoke ancestral authority without identifying one person?

4. Why does Jacob address the pure in heart before returning to the offenders? Might this publicly console people harmed by the conduct condemned in [[Jacob 2]]?

5. How should the strong parental accountability of verse 10 be understood alongside individual agency?

6. What governed the selection of material for these plates, given Jacob's statement that he spoke many more things and his emphasis on limited record capacity?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
