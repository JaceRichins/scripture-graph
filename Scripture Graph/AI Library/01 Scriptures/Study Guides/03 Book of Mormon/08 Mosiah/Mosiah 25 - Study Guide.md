# Mosiah 25 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Mosiah 25]] · My notes: [[Mosiah 25 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
After the separated communities return to Zarahemla, Mosiah gathers the population and reads the accounts of Zeniff's people and Alma's people. The histories evoke joy, grief, gratitude, and concern before the chapter turns to collective identity, baptism, and institutional organization. Alma baptizes Limhi and his people, and Mosiah authorizes churches throughout Zarahemla. Although organized into seven congregations, they are described as one church because they share teaching centered on repentance and faith in God. The official heading dates the episode to about 120 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Gathering and population (vv. 1-4):** Nephites and the people of Zarahemla assemble in two bodies; both together remain fewer than the Lamanites.

2. **Records, response, and identity (vv. 5-13):** Mosiah reads the histories of Zeniff and Alma. The audience's responses range from joy to anguish, after which several populations are formally numbered as Nephites.

3. **Teaching and baptism (vv. 14-18):** Alma preaches repentance, faith, and remembrance of deliverance; Limhi and his people request and receive baptism after the manner associated with [[Mosiah 18]].

4. **Many congregations, one church (vv. 19-24):** Mosiah authorizes Alma to establish churches with priests and teachers. Seven congregations share common teaching and identity.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter repeatedly joins [[Faith]] and [[Repentance]] as the content of Alma's preaching (vv. 15, 22). [[Baptism]] follows expressed desire and belief and brings those baptized into the church of God (vv. 17-18). Deliverance is not treated merely as political escape: Alma urges the rescued people to remember that the Lord delivered them (v. 16), connecting [[Exodus and Deliverance]] with [[Gratitude]].

The chapter also distinguishes organizational plurality from ecclesial division. Separate congregations, teachers, and assemblies remain 'one church' because they receive the same teaching (vv. 20-23). This is a narrative description of that community, not by itself a complete doctrine of church organization for every setting.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The verbs **called**, **numbered**, and the repeated noun **name** make identity a central concern. The descendants associated with Amulon reject their fathers' names and take the name of Nephi (v. 12); the people of Zarahemla are numbered with the Nephites for a dynastic reason (v. 13); and church participants desire to take the name of Christ or God and are called the people of God (vv. 23-24). These uses suggest overlapping familial, political, and religious identities, though the passage does not define their precise legal or ritual boundaries.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 9-11 use anaphora ('And again, when they thought...') to stage three sequential emotional responses to the same historical record, letting sorrow, gratitude, and anguish coexist without resolving into a single tone. The chapter opens with a numerical framing (vv.2-3) that reads almost like a census, and this quantifying impulse recurs at the end with the count of 'seven churches' (v.23), bracketing the chapter with two acts of counting a newly defined community.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Narrative Demographic Claim *(tentative)*
> The specific claims that Nephites were fewer than Zarahemla's people, and that Lamanites were more than double the combined Nephite-Zarahemla population, are unverifiable narrative statements with no independent corroboration; they may be approximate or rhetorically framed to set up the political and demographic tensions that surface later in Alma.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.8 · speculative

> [!evidence] Internal Textual/Political Claim *(tentative)*
> Verse 13 states directly that the numerically larger people of Zarahemla were absorbed into the Nephite polity specifically because 'the kingdom had been conferred upon none but those who were descendants of Nephi,' which is internal textual evidence that political legitimacy in this society was tied to descent rather than population size.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.8 · accepted observation, disputed larger claim
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does 'numbered with the Nephites' in verses 12-13 chiefly describe ethnicity, citizenship, dynastic allegiance, or some combination?

- What kind of power does Mosiah give Alma in verse 19: priestly authority itself, royal recognition of existing authority, or legal jurisdiction to organize throughout Zarahemla?

- Are the two bodies in verse 4 directly related to the 'large bodies' Alma visits in verse 15 or to the seven churches in verse 23? The chapter does not explicitly equate them.

- What distinction, if any, is intended by 'the name of Christ, or of God' in verse 23?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the baptismal continuity claimed in verse 18 with [[Mosiah 18]], and compare the community's remembrance of Alma's deliverance with [[Mosiah 24]]. These comparisons can clarify what Mosiah 25 summarizes, but they should not erase its distinctive emphasis on public memory, reconfigured identity, and organization after reunification.
