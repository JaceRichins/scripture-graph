# Mosiah 22 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Mosiah 22]] · My notes: [[Mosiah 22 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Plans are made for the people to escape from Lamanite bondage—The Lamanites are made drunk—The people escape, return to Zarahemla, and become subject to King Mosiah. About 121–120 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Deliberation and constraint** (22:1–2) — the people are gathered for their voice; armed resistance is judged impossible.
2. **Gideon's proposal** (22:3–8) — Gideon asks to be heard on the strength of past counsel, then reveals the back pass and the wine-tribute plan.
3. **Authorization and execution** (22:9–10) — Limhi accepts the plan; the wine is sent and the guards drink freely.
4. **Departure and journey** (22:11–13) — the people flee by night around Shilom and, after many days, reach Zarahemla.
5. **Arrival and incorporation** (22:13–14) — they join Mosiah's people, become his subjects, and their records (and the records they had found) pass to Mosiah.
6. **Failed pursuit** (22:15–16) — a Lamanite army loses their trail after two days.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter contains no sermon and states no miracle, revelation, or angelic mechanism for the escape (contrast [[Mosiah 24]], where the Lord is credited with causing the guards' deep sleep). Its place under [[Exodus and Deliverance]] comes from the narrative shape — bondage, night departure, wilderness, new home — not from any explicit theological claim in the text.

[[Freedom and Liberty]] here is qualified: the people escape Lamanite bondage only to become Mosiah's subjects (22:13), not to gain independence. The chapter also touches [[Government and Laws]]: Limhi seeks the people's voice (22:1) but still personally authorizes Gideon's plan (22:9), and [[Records and Sacred Writings]], since Mosiah receives both Limhi's people's own records and the records they had found (22:14).
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
'Wilderness' recurs about nine times in sixteen verses (22:2, 6, 8, 11–13, 15–16), the chapter's dominant spatial motif — the space of both danger and deliverance.

Unlike [[Mosiah 24]]'s account of Alma's people, which explicitly credits the Lord with putting the Lamanite guards into a deep sleep, Mosiah 22 attributes the guards' incapacity to ordinary wine and credits the plan to Gideon's practical judgment. Whether the author intends a contrast between human and divine deliverance, or is simply narrating what each source recorded, the text itself does not say — the comparison is suggestive but should not be overstated.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- The chapter states no miracle or revelation behind the escape, in contrast to [[Mosiah 24]]. Is that a meaningful narrative choice, or just the style of a terse record?
- Does the text present Gideon's wine stratagem as morally preferable to armed conflict, or simply as the only workable option once combat was judged impossible?
- What does seeking 'the voice of the people' (22:1) show about how this Nephite monarchy operated under pressure — and what would go too far to conclude from a single emergency meeting?
- How does Mosiah's reception of two sets of records (22:14) fit into the larger history of Nephite records reaching Zarahemla?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
