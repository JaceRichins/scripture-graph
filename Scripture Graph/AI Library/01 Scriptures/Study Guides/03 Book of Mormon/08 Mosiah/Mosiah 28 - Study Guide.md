# Mosiah 28 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Mosiah 28]] · My notes: [[Mosiah 28 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The sons of Mosiah go to preach to the Lamanites—Using the two seer stones, Mosiah translates the Jaredite plates. About 92 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Missionary desire and authorization (vv. 1–9):** The sons plead to preach among the Lamanites. Mosiah seeks [[Revelation]], receives permission and promises, and lets them depart.
2. **Royal succession problem (v. 10):** No son will accept the kingdom.
3. **Translation and its reception (vv. 11–19):** Mosiah translates the record brought by Limhi's people; its account produces both sorrow and knowledge.
4. **Transfer of custody (v. 20):** Records and interpreters pass to Alma with a command to preserve and continue them.

Verses 11–20 contain recapitulation rather than a completely linear sequence: verse 11 already says Mosiah had translated the record, verses 13–19 explain that translation, and verse 20 resumes with “as I said unto you.”
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verses 3–4 connect the sons' concern for others' [[Salvation]] with the Spirit's work, their former sins, divine mercy, and continuing anguish. Their experience can reasonably be read as illustrating how [[Conversion]] may deepen compassion, though the text does not fully explain their psychology.

The chapter also portrays discernment through persistent human desire and divine authorization: the sons plead, Mosiah inquires, and the Lord answers (vv. 5–8; see [[Revelation]]). The promise of deliverance should not be generalized into immunity from hardship.

Verses 14–16 present the interpreters as providentially preserved for interpreting languages and disclosing earlier wrongdoing. Verse 16 associates possession of these instruments with being called a seer, but it is not a complete definition of every scriptural use of that title.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter describes the translation instrument as “two stones which were fastened into the two rims of a bow” (v. 13) and later calls the objects “the interpreters” (v. 20). The phrase “Urim and Thummim” does not occur in this chapter; that chapter-level observation should not be expanded into a conclusion about terminology elsewhere.

“Every creature” occurs in both halves of the chapter (vv. 3, 15), once in connection with declaring salvation and once with disclosing former iniquities. The repetition may invite comparison, but it is insufficient by itself to prove deliberate literary patterning rather than reuse of a common scriptural idiom.

Verse 18 holds sorrow and rejoicing together: the account causes the people to mourn while giving them knowledge in which they rejoice.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Two forward notices organize later reading. Mosiah 28:9 postpones the sons' proceedings, resumed in [[Alma 17]], while Mosiah 28:19 postpones the destroyed people's account, presented in [[Ether 1]]. These notices make the chapter a narrative junction rather than a closed episode.

The allusion to the great tower, confounded language, scattering, and Adam places the translated record within the scriptural world of [[Genesis 11]]. This is a clear literary connection. By itself, however, it cannot decide whether the relationship reflects ancient shared tradition, direct scriptural dependence, or later literary construction.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the narrator place the succession problem immediately after the missionaries' departure?
- How does remembering one's own need for mercy change the way one approaches an enemy?
- Why can the same historical record appropriately produce both mourning and rejoicing?
- What responsibilities accompany control of records and instruments of interpretation?
- How do the two promises in verse 7—belief and deliverance—unfold in [[Alma 17]] and the chapters that follow?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
