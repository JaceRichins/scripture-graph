# Mosiah 26 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Mosiah 26]] · My notes: [[Mosiah 26 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Many members of the Church are led into sin by unbelievers—Alma is promised eternal life—Those who repent and are baptized gain forgiveness—Church members in sin who repent and confess to Alma and to the Lord will be forgiven; otherwise, they will not be numbered among the people of the Church. About 120–100 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
- **vv.1-6** — A generation too young to have understood king Benjamin's address (they were children when he spoke, v.1) disbelieves the resurrection and Christ's coming, refuses baptism and church membership, and remains 'a separate people as to their faith' (v.4); amid internal dissensions, some of this group draw church members into sin with 'flattering words' (vv.5-6).
- **vv.7-14** — An unprecedented case: the accused are brought from teachers to priests to Alma the high priest (v.7); facing many witnesses but no precedent, Alma is 'troubled in his spirit' (v.10) and brings the matter to king Mosiah, who declines to judge and returns it to Alma (vv.11-12); Alma prays and receives revelation (vv.13-14).
- **vv.15-32** — The revelation: blessings on Alma and the baptized congregation for their faith in 'words alone,' with no mention of sign or miracle (vv.15-19); a covenant of eternal life to Alma and a description of church membership as hearing and responding to Christ's voice (vv.20-22); Christ's self-identification as both Creator and the one who bears the sins of the world, followed by a judgment scene including the 'second trump' (vv.23-28); and a repeatable confess-repent-forgive procedure, opened and closed by the refrain 'I say unto you, Go' (vv.29, 32).
- **vv.33-39** — Implementation: Alma writes the revelation down, judges the accused according to it, blots out the names of the unrepentant, and the church returns to peace and growth despite outside persecution.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- [[Baptism]] is defined as baptism 'unto repentance' (v.22), tying entry into the church directly to repentance rather than treating it as an isolated ritual.
- Church discipline is presented as a repeatable covenant: confession and sincere repentance are met with forgiveness 'as often as my people repent' (v.30), while one who refuses to forgive a repentant neighbor 'hath brought himself under condemnation' (v.31). See [[Repentance and Forgiveness]].
- Verses 23-27 draw together the [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]], [[Creation]], resurrection, and [[Final Judgment]] around Christ's declaration that he both 'taketh upon me the sins of the world' and 'hath created them' (v.23). The passage is a devotional and judicial discourse, not a systematic doctrinal exposition, and shouldn't be read as covering any one of these topics completely.
- Alma's revelation becomes written policy: he records it specifically so he 'might judge the people of that church according to the commandments of God' (v.33), an example of revelation becoming a durable written standard for governance. See [[Records and Sacred Writings]].

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter repeatedly uses the language of being **numbered** among a people (vv. 32, 35–36), suggesting membership that can be recognized administratively as well as spiritually. It also develops a cluster around **name**, **voice**, and **knowing** (vv. 18, 20–28): Christ's people bear his name, hear his voice, and know him. The final judgment reverses the claim of nonrecognition—the unredeemed then know who he is, but he declares that he never knew them.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The revelation to Alma opens with a doubled 'blessed' formula (vv.15-19) that credits both Alma's and the congregation's faith to hearing prophetic testimony — 'words alone' — with no mention of any accompanying sign. The forgiveness instructions of vv.29-32 are framed by a repeated command, 'Therefore I say unto you, Go' (v.29) and 'Now I say unto you, Go' (v.32); whether this reflects deliberate literary framing or is simply how the discourse marks successive instructions, the text does not say.

Several phrases in this discourse are recognizable from the Gospel of Matthew: 'a place... at my right hand' (v.24) recalls the sheep set on the right hand who 'inherit the kingdom' (Matthew 25:33-34), and 'I never knew them' (v.27) closely echoes the warning to false disciples, 'I never knew you: depart from me' (Matthew 7:23). Most strikingly, the closing phrase 'everlasting fire prepared for the devil and his angels' (v.27) is a near word-for-word match of Matthew 25:41, and v.39's 'pray without ceasing, and...give thanks in all things' closely parallels 1 Thessalonians 5:17-18 — both New Testament texts composed generations after this discourse's internal date of roughly 120-100 B.C. These overlaps are compatible with several explanations: the English translation drawing on familiar KJV idiom for a genuinely distinct original, a consistent revealed teaching repeated to different peoples, or direct literary dependence on the King James Bible during the Book of Mormon's composition. None of these is established by the parallel alone, and readers should weigh them as live, competing possibilities.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Verse 1 attributes the rising generation's failure to understand to their youth when Benjamin spoke, while verse 3 attributes it to unbelief and hardened hearts. How do opportunity, age, and choice relate here?
- The text does not explain why Mosiah declines to judge the accused (v.12), only a few verses after establishing that he had already given Alma 'authority over the church' (v.8). Is this a general distinction between civil and ecclesiastical authority, or a one-time deference specific to this case?
- What does confession add to repentance in vv.29 and 35-36, and what, if anything, does the chapter say about its scope or limits?
- Does 'remained so ever after' (v.4) describe the dissenting group's status for the rest of the historical record, or only up to this point in the narrative — and how does that relate to the widespread conversions described after Christ's visit in 3-4 Nephi?
- How does 'as often as my people repent will I forgive them' (v.30) relate to the exclusion described in v.32, that whoever 'will not repent...shall not be numbered among my people'?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
