# Ether 3 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Ether 3]] · My notes: [[Ether 3 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The brother of Jared sees the finger of the Lord as He touches sixteen stones—Christ shows His spirit body to the brother of Jared—Those who have a perfect knowledge cannot be kept from within the veil—Interpreters are provided to bring the Jaredite record to light.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Preparation and petition (vv1-5)** — stones are molten; the brother of Jared prays, confessing fallen weakness while asking the Lord to make the stones shine.
2. **Progressive theophany (vv6-13)** — the veil is removed and the finger is seen (v6); fear and divine reassurance follow (vv7-9); the brother of Jared asks the Lord to "show thyself unto me" (v10), affirms his belief (vv11-12), and the Lord shows Himself fully (v13).
3. **Self-identification and editorial reflection (vv14-20)** — Christ identifies Himself and explains the nature of the spirit body already shown (vv14-16); Moroni steps into first person to explain how perfect knowledge kept the brother of Jared within the veil (vv17-20).
4. **Sealing and panoramic vision (vv21-28)** — secrecy is commanded until the Lord "glorifies my name in the flesh" (v21); the record is to be sealed in a confounded language along with two stones that will later "magnify" it (vv22-24); the brother of Jared is shown all inhabitants of the earth, past and future, and told to seal this too (vv25-28).
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **[[Faith]] as the enabling condition for revelation.** The Lord ties the vision to faith rather than to ritual: "never has man come before me with such exceeding faith as thou hast" (Ether 3:9). Moroni returns to this very episode as an illustration in his faith discourse in [[Ether 12]] (12:19-21).
- **[[The Godhead]].** Verse 14's "I am Jesus Christ. I am the Father and the Son" is one of the Book of Mormon's most direct statements on the relationship between Father and Son. Ether 3 alone does not explain the formula — whether it concerns identity, role, covenantal relationship, or representation — and it remains a genuinely debated point; it should not be treated as a settled proof-text in any direction.
- **[[Premortal Life]] and [[Creation]].** The body Christ shows is "the body of my spirit," distinct from the mortal body he says he will later take (vv9, 16), and this spirit form is explicitly given as the pattern after which humanity was created (v16).
- **Faith and knowledge.** Moroni describes the brother of Jared's experience as a shift from faith to "perfect knowledge" — "he had faith no longer, for he knew, nothing doubting" (v19) — best read as knowledge replacing uncertainty about this specific revealed matter, not as any loss of trust or fidelity.
- **[[Records and Sacred Writings]].** The command to seal the account in a confounded language, paired with stones that will later "magnify" it "in mine own due time," frames scripture transmission as requiring both human faithfulness and future divine intervention.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The central vision follows a recognizable pattern found across scriptural theophanies — manifestation, fear and falling down, divine question and reassurance, and an escalating request followed by fuller revelation (vv6-13). Whether this reflects a shared ancient convention or an author's fluency with the King James Bible's theophany language is a fair question either way; the text alone cannot settle it, and any claim of a specific literary "type-scene" would need a feature-by-feature comparison this chapter alone doesn't provide. The narrative also repeatedly widens its scope — from stones, to a finger, to Christ's full appearance and premortal identity, to all inhabitants of the earth — and the movement from darkness toward light works on both a literal and a suggestive level, though the text never explicitly makes the stones a symbol of spiritual enlightenment. Note too Moroni's visible authorial hand: he steps out of the narrative in first person ("I, Moroni," v17) to admit the limits of his own account, a self-aware editorial habit that recurs through his abridgment of the Jaredite record.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does it mean that faith, specifically, is what the Lord identifies as unprecedented here (v9) — general faith, or faith in a particular promise (compare v26, "if he would believe... it should be shown unto him")?
- How should Ether 3:14's "I am the Father and the Son" be read alongside other Book of Mormon Christophanies and later revelation on the Godhead — as the same doctrine differently expressed, or as doctrine still being clarified?
- Why does the brother of Jared fear being smitten after seeing the finger, and what does that reveal about what he expected God to be like?
- Does "ye are redeemed from the fall" (v13) describe an immediate change of standing, an assurance of future salvation, or something specific to entering God's presence in that moment?
- Why does Moroni interrupt the narrative with his own commentary (vv17-20) rather than simply continuing the account — what does this suggest about his purpose as editor?
- What is the relationship between the two stones given here (vv23-24) and the instrument later associated with translating the Nephite record — is that connection made explicitly, or is it inferred from later chapters?
