# Ether 15 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Ether 15]] · My notes: [[Ether 15 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Millions of the Jaredites are slain in battle—Shiz and Coriantumr assemble all the people to mortal combat—The Spirit of the Lord ceases to strive with them—The Jaredite nation is utterly destroyed—Only Coriantumr remains.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Remembrance and failed negotiation (vv. 1–6):** Coriantumr remembers the prophetic warnings, begins to repent, and offers up his kingdom; Shiz demands Coriantumr's life.

2. **Retreat and total mobilization (vv. 7–15):** The armies move through Ripliancum, Ogath, and Ramah, then spend four years gathering nearly everyone into two armed camps.

3. **Lamentation and renewed violence (vv. 16–22):** Both sides grieve their losses but return to battle. The narrator explains their persistence through hardness, blindness, and satanic power.

4. **Terminal attrition (vv. 23–32):** Survivor counts fall rapidly until only Coriantumr and Shiz remain; Coriantumr kills Shiz and collapses.

5. **Record and final testimony (vv. 33–34):** Ether witnesses fulfillment, completes and hides his record, and closes with his hope of salvation.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
**Individual repentance amid collective hardness.** Coriantumr begins to repent (15:3) and repeatedly seeks peace (15:4, 15:18), while the wider population explicitly refuses to repent (15:6). The text does not say his repentance became complete, nor does it state a general rule about collective judgment — only that in this instance, one leader's contrition did not stop the destruction.

**The Spirit ceasing to strive.** Ether 15:19 explains the final battles' persistence: "the Spirit of the Lord had ceased striving with them, and Satan had full power over the hearts of the people... that they might be destroyed." The language is strongly deterministic — hardening is described as done to the people, for a purpose — and readers differ on whether this describes a near-total loss of restraint or is compatible with continued individual agency (compare [[Agency]] and [[Satan and Temptation]]). The text itself does not resolve this tension.

**Faith regardless of outcome.** Ether's closing words (15:34) treat translation and continued mortal life as equally acceptable, conditioned only on being saved in the [[Kingdom of God]] — a brief but pointed statement about ultimate priorities.

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter uses several devices to convey total annihilation:

- **A refrain of lamentation** — "howling and lamentation" that "did rend the air exceedingly" recurs after each inconclusive day (15:16-17), marking time and escalating dread.
- **A numerical countdown** — explicit survivor counts (52/69, then 32/27) narrow the scale from "millions" (15:2) to two men (15:29), a stark literary contraction.
- **Simile of drunkenness** — "drunken with anger, even as a man who is drunken with wine" (15:22) renders irrational continued violence in visceral, physical terms.
- **Grotesque physical detail** — Shiz's body rising "upon his hands" after decapitation (15:31) is an unusually graphic image even within the Book of Mormon's war narratives, underscoring the scene's horror.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the text emphasize that the people, not just their leaders, refused to repent (15:6) — what does this suggest about individual versus collective moral agency?
- Why do repeated nights of lamentation (15:16-17) fail to change anyone's course?
- What does it mean for "the Spirit of the Lord" to "cease striving" and for Satan to have "full power" over hardened hearts (15:19) — near-total loss of restraint, or language compatible with continued agency?
- Why does the narrator pause to identify the battlefield hill Ramah with the place Mormon later hid the Nephite records (15:11) — what is the effect of linking the Jaredite and Nephite ends to one location?
- Ether's fate (translated or mortal death) is left unresolved (15:34) — why might the record end without resolving it?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
