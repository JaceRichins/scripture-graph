# Ether 5 — Study Guide

## Overview

<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter has three compact movements:

1. **Controlled access to the record (vv.1-2)** — Moroni prohibits translating the sealed material except when it becomes "wisdom in God," while allowing that the plates may be shown to those who assist in bringing forth the work.
2. **A layered testimony (vv.3-5)** — three are shown the plates "by the power of God"; their testimony, the work itself, and the joint record of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost together stand as testimony "against the world at the last day"; repentance remains open.
3. **Moroni's authority and judgment (v.6)** — Moroni invites the reader to judge whether he has authority, but ties its final verification to a future meeting before God.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Sealed records and the timing of revelation** (v.1): the sealed portion is withheld conditionally, pending "wisdom in God" — not necessarily promised for future translation, but reserved to divine discretion. See [[Records and Sacred Writings]] and [[Revelation]].
- **The law of witnesses** (vv.3-4): truth is to be established by multiple witnesses, echoing the biblical formula of Deuteronomy 19:15, quoted almost verbatim in 2 Corinthians 13:1 ("In the mouth of two or three witnesses shall every word be established"). The echo is a genuine verbal parallel; it does not by itself settle whether the wording reflects ancient legal culture or nineteenth-century King James phrasing.
- **The Godhead as joint witness** (v.4): the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are said to "bear record" of God's power and word shown forth in the work — see [[The Godhead]]. The verse offers no systematic account of how the three relate; it simply names all three as witnesses.
- **Testimony paired with mercy** (vv.4-5): a testimony that could "stand against the world" is followed by a conditional path to the [[Kingdom of God]] through [[Repentance]] — see [[Justice and Mercy]]. The grammatical antecedent of "they" in v.5 is not fully certain (see Questions).
- **Personal accountability** (v.6): Moroni ties his own authority to a future, personal accounting before God — an eschatological claim, not an immediately testable one.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Several expressions require restraint. “These things” in verse 3 may refer to the plates, their contents, the work, or some combination; the immediate wording does not precisely define its scope. The antecedent of “they” in verse 5 is also broad, probably referring to people in the world addressed in verse 4 rather than only the three witnesses. Finally, “authority” in verse 6 clearly concerns Moroni's right to make these declarations, but the verse does not explicitly define it as a particular priesthood office.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The phrase "in the mouth of three witnesses shall these things be established" (v.4) closely echoes the biblical legal-testimonial formula of Deuteronomy 19:15, quoted almost verbatim in 2 Corinthians 13:1. This is a genuine, checkable verbal parallel; what it proves is more limited — it fits equally well with an ancient author working in Mosaic legal idiom or a nineteenth-century dictation steeped in King James phrasing, and cannot by itself decide between those explanations.

More broadly, the chapter's testimony progressively widens: from hidden, sealed words, to physical plates, to human witnesses, to the work itself, to the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, to "the world," and finally to a last-day appearance before God — a courtroom-like movement from evidence to testimony to judgment.

Readers commonly connect the two groups named in vv.2-3 — those who "assist to bring forth this work" and the "three" shown the plates "by the power of God" — to the later historical distinction between the Eight Witnesses and the Three Witnesses. The text itself, however, does not clearly separate two groups; "three" in v.3 may simply specify the number and manner of the group already introduced in v.2, so this correspondence should be read as retrospective, not a claim the chapter makes about itself.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What exactly does "these things" (v.3) and "this work" (v.4) encompass — the plates' existence, their contents, or the truth of the record as a whole?
- Does vv.2-3 describe a single group of assistants later specified as "three," or two distinct groups (as commonly read against the later Eight and Three Witnesses)? The grammar doesn't settle this.
- What does it mean for the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost to "bear record" of a physical, historical record (v.4), compared to bearing record of a person or doctrine as in [[3 Nephi 11]]?
- Is the "they" who may repent in v.5 "the world" named at the end of v.4, or a broader or different group?
- What kind of "authority" does Moroni ask readers to judge in v.6 — prophetic commission, authority to write and seal, or something broader?
- What might "except by and by it shall be wisdom in God" (v.1) imply about the conditions for someday revealing the sealed portion, without assuming that it will be?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
