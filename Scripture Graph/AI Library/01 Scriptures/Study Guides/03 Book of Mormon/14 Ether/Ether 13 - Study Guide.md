# Ether 13 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Ether 13]] · My notes: [[Ether 13 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Ether speaks of a New Jerusalem to be built in America by the seed of Joseph—He prophesies, is cast out, writes the Jaredite history, and foretells the destruction of the Jaredites—War rages over all the land.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
Ether 13 divides at verse 13:

1. **Ether's prophecy, summarized by Moroni (vv1-12).** vv2-6 introduce the New Jerusalem and the future rebuilding of Jerusalem of old — the text itself supplies the reasoning that the latter 'could not be a new Jerusalem for it had been in a time of old' (v5). vv6-8 name the Joseph-in-Egypt narrative a 'type' for the remnant of Joseph's deliverance and inheritance. v9 sets both cities within a 'new heaven and new earth.' vv10-11 form a deliberate doublet — 'And then cometh the New Jerusalem... blessed are they' answered by 'And then also cometh the Jerusalem of old... blessed are they' — with both peoples cleansed 'through the blood of the Lamb.' v12 closes by citing an unattributed 'scripture' about the first becoming last and the last first.
2. **Ether cast out and hidden (vv13-14).** Moroni notes he was forbidden to record more; Ether hides by day in a rock cavity and writes the remainder of his record from there.
3. **Conditional warning and refusal (vv15-22).** War begins through 'secret combinations' seeking Coriantumr's throne; the Lord offers Coriantumr and his household preservation through repentance (vv20-21), which they refuse (v22).
4. **Escalating, leaderless war (vv23-31).** Control of the kingdom passes between Shared and Coriantumr's sons; conflict widens from a contest between rulers into 'every man with his band fighting for that which he desired' (v25) and open robbery (v26); repeated battles at the valley of Gilgal and plains of Heshlon end with Shared's death and Coriantumr's wounding — after which, the text specifies, general bloodshed continued unrestrained even though Coriantumr himself did not fight for two years (v31).
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **New Jerusalem, the Abrahamic Covenant, and the Atonement.** The New Jerusalem in the Americas and the rebuilt Jerusalem of old are both tied to [[Abrahamic Covenant]] and [[Gathering of Israel]] (v11: 'the fulfilling of the covenant which God made with their father, Abraham'). Both communities' blessedness is grounded in the same image — garments made white / washed 'through the blood of the Lamb' (vv10-11) — tying covenant lineage to the [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]] rather than treating ancestry alone as salvific.
- **Type and deliverance.** vv6-7 explicitly name the Joseph-in-Egypt narrative a 'type,' comparing Joseph's preservation of his father with the Lord's preservation of a remnant of Joseph brought out of Jerusalem — an instance of [[Exodus and Deliverance]] the text identifies as intentional pattern, not merely a reader's inference.
- **Conditional prophecy and agency.** Ether's warning to Coriantumr (vv20-21) makes preservation of the kingdom and people strictly conditional on repentance; the narrative then records that refusal — not merely military weakness — precedes the unraveling (v22), affirming [[Agency]] and [[Repentance]] as operative even in a civilization's final years.
- **Secret combinations as a corrosive force.** vv15, 18, and 26 explicitly name 'secret plans of wickedness' and 'secret combinations' as the proximate cause of the war that opens the Jaredites' final collapse.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The expressions “come down out of heaven” (13:3), “should be built up” (13:6), and “then cometh the New Jerusalem” (13:10) should not be harmonized too quickly. They may describe different aspects or stages of the same hope, but this chapter does not explain their precise relationship. Similarly, “this land” is meaningful within the Book of Mormon's narrative geography, but Ether 13 itself does not supply modern coordinates.

The repeated totalizing language in verses 17, 25, 26, and 31 intensifies the depiction of social collapse. Whether every expression is intended as mathematically universal or as conventional narrative emphasis cannot be determined from the wording alone.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
- **The v10/v11 doublet.** The matching grammar of vv10-11 ('And then cometh the New Jerusalem... blessed are they... blood of the Lamb' / 'And then also cometh the Jerusalem of old... blessed are they... blood of the Lamb') is the chapter's clearest structural device, placing the two gathered peoples in literary balance.
- **Named typology.** v6's phrase 'for which things there has been a type' is a rare case of the text naming its own literary device outright, inviting the reader to see Joseph's preservation of his father (v7) as a conscious pattern rather than incidental history.
- **Holy city against seized kingdom.** The first half of the chapter anticipates a holy city built to the Lord (vv6, 8); the second depicts armed bands fighting for individual desire (v25) — a moral contrast the narrator does not label explicitly but which the sequence makes hard to miss.
- **The cavity of the rock.** Ether's daytime concealment and nighttime observation (vv13-14) functions as both plot device and image: a rejected prophet who keeps recording from hiding.
- **An unattributed 'scripture.'** v12 cites 'the scripture which saith' the first/last reversal without naming a source — it is unclear whether this is part of Ether's own prophecy or Moroni's own editorial citation of a saying familiar to him as he wrote generations later.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual/Philological *(tentative)*
> The reversal saying in v12 uses phrasing closely matching a well-known Gospel idiom ('the first shall be last, and the last first'), which in the Bible is first attributed to Jesus centuries after Ether. Whether this reflects genuine ancient prophetic content, translation idiom supplied for an English readership, or an editorial gloss by Moroni (who lived after Christ's teachings had already been recorded in 3 Nephi) cannot be settled from the text of Ether 13 alone.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.6 · disputed, well-hedged

> [!evidence] Onomastic/Textual *(tentative)*
> The battle sites in vv27-29, the valley of Gilgal and the plains of Heshlon, use place names phonetically resembling Hebrew/Israelite toponyms (Gilgal is a well-known Old Testament place name), even though the Jaredite record's own internal chronology places their departure from the Old World before the era of Abraham and the Hebrew nation.
> — strength 0.2 · confidence 0.5 · disputed, and only half-supported
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why might Moroni present the New Jerusalem and the rebuilt Jerusalem of old side by side, both blessed and both cleansed through the blood of the Lamb, rather than emphasizing one over the other?
- What is gained by v6 naming the Joseph-in-Egypt narrative explicitly as a 'type' rather than simply narrating the parallel and letting the reader notice it?
- Why does Moroni emphasize in v13 that he was forbidden to record more of Ether's prophecies — what does that constraint suggest about the record we do have?
- Ether's warning to Coriantumr (vv20-21) ties the fate of a whole people to one man's repentance. What does that suggest, and what questions does it leave open, about the relationship between individual and collective consequences?
- At what point does the war stop being a contest between rulers (Coriantumr, Shared) and become the leaderless, unrestrained bloodshed described in v31?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
For the fulfillment of Ether's conditional prophecy to Coriantumr, see [[Ether 14]] and [[Ether 15]]. The reversal saying in v12 ('first...last...last...first') also appears in [[D&C 29]] and in the Gospels (e.g. Matthew 19:30) — worth comparing as parallel wording, not assumed literary dependence in either direction. The 'new heaven and new earth' setting of the New Jerusalem (v9) and the Lamb's-blood cleansing imagery (vv10-11) both closely echo the Book of Revelation (21:1-2; 7:14); Ether 13 never identifies a source for either image, so the resemblance is worth noting without over-concluding. For the Joseph typology named in vv6-7, compare Joseph's reception and preservation of his father in Genesis 45-46.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
