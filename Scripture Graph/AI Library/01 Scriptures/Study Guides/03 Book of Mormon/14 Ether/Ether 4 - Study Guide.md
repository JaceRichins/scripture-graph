# Ether 4 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Ether 4]] · My notes: [[Ether 4 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Moroni is commanded to seal up the writings of the brother of Jared—They will not be revealed until men have faith even as the brother of Jared—Christ commands men to believe His words and those of His disciples—Men are commanded to repent, believe the gospel, and be saved.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Past custody of the vision (vv. 1–5):** the vision is written, initially withheld, later manifested, and then sealed again with its interpreters.

2. **Conditions of further disclosure (vv. 6–12):** repentance and faith like the brother of Jared's precede greater manifestations; the Spirit and the production of good are given as tests of Christ's words.

3. **Invitations to two audiences (vv. 13–17):** Gentiles and Israel are summoned to come to Christ; Israel is promised recognition of covenant remembrance, and the record becomes a sign of the Father's commencing work.

4. **Universal gospel summons (vv. 18–19):** all the earth is called to repent, believe, be baptized, and remain faithful.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter joins [[Faith]], [[Repentance]], and [[Justification and Sanctification]] to receptivity: greater knowledge is promised to a spiritually prepared audience (Ether 4:6–7). The Spirit is said to produce knowledge and witness, while persuasion toward good serves as an ethical criterion for divine influence (Ether 4:11–12), a principle developed more fully in [[Moroni 7]].

Gentiles and Israel are both invited to Christ, while Israel receives a specific assurance of covenant remembrance (Ether 4:13–17), making the chapter relevant to the [[Gathering of Israel]]. Verse 18 explicitly commands repentance, belief, and [[Baptism]].

The titles applied to Christ in verses 7 and 12 are relevant to [[The Godhead]]. The passage calls Christ both the Son of God and the Father, yet also speaks of the Father who sent him; these statements should be studied together without treating this chapter alone as a complete systematic explanation.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Ether 4:18 is a close English parallel to Mark 16:16–17 and Mormon 9:23–25. The textual relationship is clear; its historical explanation is not. Plausible accounts include direct use of King James wording, translation into familiar biblical diction, or a shared Christian commission formula.

The divine titles in verses 7 and 12 require equally careful reading. Christ calls himself both the Son of God and the Father of heaven and earth, yet also speaks of the Father who sent him. The passage clearly exalts Christ but does not, by these titles alone, provide a complete systematic account of [[The Godhead]].
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter develops a veil motif across [[Ether 3]] and Ether 4. The brother of Jared's faith carries him beyond a veil, while Ether 4:15 calls later audiences to rend a 'veil of unbelief.' Ether 4:7 also makes his faith a model for future recipients. This creates meaningful literary coherence, but it does not establish ancient authorship or historicity; common religious imagery, editorial framing, translation, or later composition could also account for the pattern.

A broader movement runs from concealment to invitation. The opening emphasizes writing, sealing, and hiding, while the conclusion repeatedly invites its audiences to come to Christ and finally addresses 'all ye ends of the earth.' Hidden knowledge is thus placed within, and made subordinate to, the chapter's public call to faithfulness.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual Intertext
> Ether 4:18 closely parallels Mark 16:16–17 in sequence and wording: belief and baptism lead to salvation, unbelief leads to condemnation, and signs follow believers. The overlap securely establishes an English-text intertext, although it does not by itself determine how or when the relationship arose.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 1.0 · English-text intertext accepted; historical explanation disputed · [[Textual Intertext in Ether 4|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary Coherence
> The recurrence and development of the veil motif across Ether 3–4 is good evidence of literary coherence between the vision narrative and its interpretation: the barrier crossed by the brother of Jared becomes a figure for the unbelief later audiences must overcome.
> — confidence 0.9 · Literary coherence accepted; origin remains disputed · [[Literary Coherence in Ether 4|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Doctrinal-Textual
> In Ether 4:12, Christ declares 'I am the Father, I am the light, and the life, and the truth of the world.' Read in isolation, this self-identification as 'the Father' raises a genuine interpretive question about the Godhead that this verse alone does not resolve.
> — strength 0.2 · confidence 1.0 · Text accepted; theological interpretation disputed · [[Doctrinal-Textual in Ether 4|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intertextual Verbal Parallel *(tentative)*
> The title given to Christ in Ether 4:7 — 'Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the Father of the heavens and of the earth' — verbally overlaps (per the verified index) with similar Father/Son language for Christ in Mosiah 3, suggesting this compound title was a recurring Nephite Christological formula rather than a one-off phrase.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.7 · Verbal recurrence accepted; formula hypothesis disputed

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
What exactly is promised to be 'manifest': public access to the sealed vision, personal revelation comparable to it, or both?

How does the ethical test that divine influence leads to good complement the experiential claim that the Spirit produces knowledge?

Does 'this record' in verse 17 refer narrowly to the Jaredite record, to the larger Book of Mormon record containing it, or to both within the narrator's perspective?

What identifiable developments, if any, constitute the promised 'unfolding' of John's revelations in verse 16?
