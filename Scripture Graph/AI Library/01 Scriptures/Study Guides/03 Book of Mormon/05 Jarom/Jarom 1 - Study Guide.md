# Jarom 1 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Jarom 1]] · My notes: [[Jarom 1 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The Nephites keep the law of Moses, look forward to the coming of Christ, and prosper in the land—Many prophets labor to keep the people in the way of truth. About 399–361 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
Jarom 1 moves through four compact movements:

- **Verses 1–4:** Jarom states his commission, purposes, space constraint, and editorial restraint, then contrasts widespread spiritual resistance with people who receive revelation according to faith.
- **Verses 5–9:** At the two-hundred-year notice, he summarizes Mosaic and Sabbath observance, strict laws, population growth, material abundance, warfare, fortification, and preparation. He interprets successful defense through the inherited promise of prosperity for obedience.
- **Verses 10–13:** Prophets, priests, and teachers warn, instruct, and repeatedly call the people to [[Repentance]]. A second notice, at 238 years, characterizes much of the interval by war, contention, and dissension.
- **Verses 14–15:** Jarom directs readers to the other plates for fuller war records and transfers his plates to Omni.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Forward-looking faith in the Messiah:** Jarom's teachers explain the [[Law of Moses]] and its intent, persuading the people to look forward to the Messiah and believe in his future coming 'as though he already was' (v. 11). The passage clearly presents a Christ-centered interpretation of Mosaic observance; it does not by itself determine the historical origin of that theology.
- **Conditional prosperity:** Jarom interprets Nephite survival through the inherited saying, 'Inasmuch as ye will keep my commandments ye shall prosper in the land' (v. 9). The narrative also identifies fortification, weapons, preparation, and capable leadership as practical factors.
- **Mercy amid serious failure:** Verse 3 joins a severe indictment of the people with God's continued mercy, illustrating [[Justice and Mercy]] without erasing either side.
- **Faith and spiritual communion:** Verse 4 associates humility and [[Faith]] with revelation and communion with the Holy Spirit while portraying the community as spiritually mixed rather than uniformly faithful.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verse 3 gathers four bodily metaphors for spiritual resistance: hardness of heart, deafness of ears, blindness of mind, and stiffness of neck. The immediate turn marked by 'nevertheless' places this indictment beside God's exceeding mercy. Verse 9 uses the recurring Book of Mormon covenant language that links keeping the commandments with prospering in the land. Its recurrence supports comparison with passages such as [[2 Nephi 1]] and [[Alma 9]], but does not by itself demonstrate direct dependence between particular passages.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Two contrasts help organize the chapter: stiffnecked people versus those who receive revelation, and threatened destruction versus continued preservation. The chronological notices at 200 and 238 years make the record's compression visible. Jarom's references to limited plate space and his referral to another record emphasize deliberate selection: these plates preserve a brief genealogical and religious account, while the other plates contain fuller war records. The opening concern for genealogy and final transfer to Omni can be read as a modest documentary frame, though the correspondence need not be a formally designed inclusio. The handoff leads directly into [[Omni 1]].
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Metallurgy Anachronism Debate *(tentative)*
> The mention of 'steel' (and, secondarily, iron and 'machinery') among Nephite goods around 400-360 BC is a long-standing point of contention: critics cite it as a metallurgical anachronism for the pre-Columbian Americas, while apologists propose alternative referents for the underlying term. The text itself does not resolve this dispute either way.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How broad is 'prosper' in this chapter—material wealth, military survival, covenant continuity, or some combination?
- How does the narrative hold faithful leadership and divine favor together with fortification, weapons, and military preparation?
- What might it mean to believe in the Messiah's future coming 'as though he already was' (v. 11)?
- Does Jarom's refusal to repeat earlier teachings chiefly reflect doctrinal continuity, limited plate space, a literary convention, or several of these factors?
- How should a careful reader distinguish Jarom's hostile description of the Lamanites from independently corroborated information about Lamanite society?
- Why does the chapter place reports of law observance beside repeated warnings and calls to repentance?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Jarom directs readers to the other plates of Nephi for fuller records of the wars (v. 14), while the custody narrative continues in [[Omni 1]]. For the recurring promise that obedience brings prosperity in the land, compare [[2 Nephi 1]] and [[Alma 9]], while recognizing that similar language appears throughout the Book of Mormon. For another explicit conjunction of Mosaic observance and forward-looking belief in Christ, compare [[2 Nephi 25]].
