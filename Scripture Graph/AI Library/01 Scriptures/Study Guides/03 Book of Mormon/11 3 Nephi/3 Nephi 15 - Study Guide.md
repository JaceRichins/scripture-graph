# 3 Nephi 15 — Study Guide

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Jesus announces that the law of Moses is fulfilled in Him—The Nephites are the other sheep of whom He spoke in Jerusalem—Because of iniquity, the Lord’s people in Jerusalem do not know of the scattered sheep of Israel. About A.D. 34.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Remember and do the sayings (v. 1):** a transition from the preceding [[Sermon at the Temple (3 Nephi)]].

2. **What has and has not ended (vv. 2-10):** the Mosaic law is fulfilled in Christ; unfulfilled prophecy and the covenant remain; discipleship is expressed through looking to Christ, [[Endurance]], and [[Obedience]].

3. **The remnant of Joseph (vv. 11-13):** the twelve are commissioned as a light to a people identified with the house of Joseph and a land of inheritance.

4. **Restricted disclosure at Jerusalem (vv. 14-20):** Jesus distinguishes what the Father commanded him to reveal and attributes Jerusalem's ignorance of separated Israel to unbelief and iniquity.

5. **Interpretation of the other sheep (vv. 21-24):** the present hearers, not the Gentiles, are directly identified as those who would hear Jesus's voice.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter resists a simple choice between abolishing everything old and preserving the Mosaic order unchanged. The law given to Moses has an end in Christ (vv. 4-8), yet commandments, covenant promises, and prophecy continue (vv. 6-10). The organizing center is therefore personal and Christological: “I am the law, and the light” (v. 9). This formulation connects [[Jesus Christ (Gospel Topic)]], [[Covenants]], [[Prophecy]], and [[Eternal Life]] without treating them as identical.

The second half joins [[Scattering of Israel]] and [[Gathering of Israel]]. Separation explains why communities do not know one another, while the one-fold, one-shepherd promise gives that separation an anticipated movement toward unity.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Three repetitions control the argument. First, “fulfilled” distinguishes the completed role of the Mosaic law from prophetic and covenant promises still awaiting fulfillment (vv. 4-8). Second, statements about what the Father “commanded” or did not command emphasize authorized disclosure (vv. 14-20). Third, “they understood me not” diagnoses successive aspects of the Jerusalem audience's misunderstanding (vv. 18, 22-23). The final contrast—Gentiles receiving manifestation through the Holy Ghost versus the present hearers hearing and seeing Jesus—clarifies how the chapter uses “hear my voice.”
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The other-sheep section is sustained scriptural interpretation rather than an incidental allusion. The saying from [[John 10]] appears in verses 17 and 21; verses 18 and 22-23 recount its misunderstanding; verse 24 applies it to the immediate audience. This makes the chapter's intended interpretation unusually explicit. The close wording is strong evidence of a literary relationship, but it cannot by itself decide whether that relationship arose from historical repetition, translation into biblical idiom, or modern dependence on the King James Bible.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Scriptural Intertextuality
> The repetition of the other-sheep saying, followed by an explicit identification and an explanation of its former misunderstanding, provides very strong textual evidence that 3 Nephi 15 deliberately engages John 10:16 as an interpretive problem.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · Intertextual engagement clear; mechanism disputed · [[Scriptural Intertextuality in 3 Nephi 15|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Explicit Internal Identification
> Within the narrative's own argument, the case that the present hearers belong to the 'other sheep' is explicit rather than merely inferred: Jesus directly identifies them and points to their sensory encounter with him.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · Conclusive concerning the narrative's own identification · [[Explicit Internal Identification in 3 Nephi 15|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual/Literary Parallel *(tentative)*
> The near-verbatim correspondence between 3 Nephi 15:17/21 and John 10:16 is cited by some as evidence of intentional intertextual fulfillment (Christ re-quoting and glossing his own earlier saying) and by others as evidence that the Book of Mormon's English text draws on King James Bible phrasing rather than, or in addition to, an independent ancient record.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.9 · Parallel accepted; authorship implications disputed

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What precisely belongs to “the law” that has ended, and what is retained under the command to keep Christ's commandments?

- Why does the chapter carefully separate the completed Mosaic law from the still-unfulfilled covenant and prophetic promises?

- Does “hear my voice” always mean a visible personal manifestation, or only within the contrast constructed in verses 22-24?

- How does the next chapter distinguish the present audience from other tribes who have also been led away?
