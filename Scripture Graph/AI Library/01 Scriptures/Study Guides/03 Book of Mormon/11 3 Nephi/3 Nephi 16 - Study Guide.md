# 3 Nephi 16 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 16]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 16 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
3 Nephi 16 continues the discourse begun in [[3 Nephi 15]]. The resurrected Christ tells the Nephites of "other sheep" — additional peoples he has not yet visited, located neither in the disciples' own land nor near [[Jerusalem]] — who have not yet heard his voice (vv. 1–3). He commands that his words be written down so that, if his disciples at Jerusalem fail to ask the Father for knowledge of the Nephites and other tribes, the record will nonetheless be preserved, later manifested to the Gentiles, and used to bring a scattered remnant of Israel to a knowledge of their Redeemer (v. 4). The bulk of the chapter (vv. 5–16) is a conditional oracle "saith the Father": Gentiles who believe are blessed (v. 6), but Gentiles who reject the fulness of the gospel and are lifted up in pride will lose it, while the Father restores it to the house of Israel (vv. 7–12); Gentiles who repent, however, are numbered among Israel and spared judgment (vv. 13–14), while the unrepentant will in turn be trodden down (v. 15). The chapter closes (vv. 17–20) with an explicit quotation of [[Isaiah 52]] (vv. 8–10) on the redemption of Jerusalem and the Lord bringing again [[Zion]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter falls into five movements: (1) vv. 1–3, the announcement of "other sheep" and the commandment that they hear Christ's voice, framed as fulfilling "one fold and one shepherd" (compare [[John 10]]); (2) vv. 4–5, the conditional commandment to write these sayings, tied to the future gathering of Israel and fulfillment of the Father's covenant; (3) vv. 6–10, alternating Gentile belief and Gentile accountability, culminating in a conditional warning against pride and rejection of the gospel; (4) vv. 11–16, the promised restoration of the gospel to the house of Israel, an explicit offer of inclusion to repentant Gentiles, and the promise of "this land" as an inheritance; (5) vv. 17–20, a direct quotation of [[Isaiah 52]] (vv. 8–10) used as a prophetic capstone — the same technique employed with the same Isaiah passage in [[Mosiah 12]] and [[Mosiah 15]].
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter ties covenant standing to response rather than fixed status: Gentile belief brings blessing through the witness of the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] (vv. 6–7), while Gentile rejection of the gospel's fulness brings its loss (vv. 7, 10) — a loss that is explicitly reversible through repentance, after which Gentiles are "numbered among" the house of Israel (v. 13). Knowledge of Christ's ministry to peoples beyond Jerusalem is here obtained through revelation in answer to prayer (v. 4), though the chapter addresses one specific situation rather than a general doctrine excluding other means of knowledge. Underlying the entire oracle is God's covenant with the house of Israel (vv. 5, 11–12), stated in more categorical terms than the surrounding conditional clauses about Gentile belief and repentance — the [[Gathering of Israel]] proceeds from that covenant regardless of the specific outcomes described for the Gentiles.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Several expressions require restraint. “Other sheep” identifies additional recipients of Jesus's ministry but does not locate them (vv. 1–3). “Fulness” refers in different clauses to the Gentiles, “these things,” and the gospel (vv. 4, 7, 10, 12); readers should not assume that every occurrence has an identical grammatical function. The images of being “trodden under feet” recur first in the description of Israel's mistreatment and later in a threatened reversal against unrepentant Gentiles (vv. 8, 14–15). This symmetry is rhetorically important, but the chapter does not explain whether the latter image should be read literally, metaphorically, or as inherited prophetic idiom.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 1–3 echo the shepherd discourse of [[John 10]], sharing the motifs of other sheep, hearing the shepherd's voice, and "one fold and one shepherd." Much of vv. 7–15 is formally an oracle of the Father, marked by repeated "saith the Father" and "thus commandeth the Father," distinguishing this material as reported divine speech within Christ's own discourse. Verse 15's image of salt "that hath lost its savor... good for nothing but to be cast out, and to be trodden under foot" echoes wording Christ had already used with the Nephite disciples in the [[Sermon at the Temple (3 Nephi)]] (3 Nephi 12:13); whether this is a deliberate authorial callback or shared dependence on common salt-of-the-earth idiom (compare [[Matthew 5]]) is not settled by the text alone. The chapter closes by explicitly naming "the prophet Isaiah" and quoting [[Isaiah 52]]:8–10 (with only a minor wording variant — "the salvation of God" here versus the KJV's "the salvation of our God") — the same passage quoted by Abinadi in [[Mosiah 12]] and [[Mosiah 15]], making it a recurring intertext across the Book of Mormon, though its function shifts between contexts (christological in Abinadi's exposition, gathering/Zion-focused here).
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Who are the "other sheep" of v. 1, and what can actually be inferred about them from this chapter alone, apart from later chapters or the modern chapter heading? How does the possibility that Gentiles may be "numbered among" Israel (v. 13) affect strictly genealogical readings of Israel/Gentile identity? Does "bring the fulness of my gospel from among them" (v. 10) describe total removal, loss of stewardship, or another form of judgment? How should the treading-down imagery (vv. 14–15) be read in light of its conditional framing and its reuse of language from the Sermon at the Temple — literally, symbolically, or as inherited prophetic idiom? What does "bring again Zion" mean in the Isaiah quotation that closes the chapter — a restored Jerusalem, a gathered latter-day covenant people, or both?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
