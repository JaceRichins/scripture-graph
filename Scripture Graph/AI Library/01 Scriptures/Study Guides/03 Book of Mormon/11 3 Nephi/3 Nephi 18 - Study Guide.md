# 3 Nephi 18 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 18]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 18 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Jesus institutes the sacrament among the Nephites—They are commanded to pray always in His name—Those who eat His flesh and drink His blood unworthily are damned—The disciples are given power to confer the Holy Ghost. About A.D. 34.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter moves through five units, with two explicit shifts in audience (marked at v. 17 and v. 26):

1. **Bread and wine, and the warning that follows (vv. 1-14).** The bread (vv. 1-7) and wine (vv. 8-11) are administered in matched units, each closing with the identical refrain 'if ye do always remember me ye shall have my Spirit to be with you' (vv. 7, 11). Jesus then warns that doing 'more or less' than commanded builds on 'a sandy foundation' (vv. 12-13), reusing the rock/sand/rain/flood imagery from his earlier sermon (compare [[3 Nephi 14]]).
2. **Watchfulness and prayer, first to the disciples (vv. 15-16), then to the multitude (vv. 17-21).** The same 'watch and pray' charge is repeated to each audience in turn, including the command to pray within families (v. 21).
3. **Open meetings and the light of example (vv. 22-25),** addressed to the multitude: no one is to be turned away, and Christ's own example — especially his prayer — is the light disciples are told to hold up (compare [[3 Nephi 12]]).
4. **Sacramental worthiness, addressed again to the disciples (vv. 26-34):** withhold the ordinance from a known-unworthy person without casting them out of the congregation.
5. **Conferral of power and ascension (vv. 35-39):** Jesus touches each disciple, who alone bear record of what he said and did; a cloud then hides him from the multitude as he ascends, witnessed only by the disciples.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
**Remembrance and the Spirit.** The ordinance's purpose is stated, not merely implied: 'if ye do always remember me ye shall have my Spirit to be with you' is repeated verbatim for bread and wine alike (vv. 7, 11), tying the sacrament to an ongoing promise of the Spirit's companionship rather than a one-time memorial. The chapter itself never uses the word 'covenant' for this act — it speaks of remembrance, witness to the Father, and obedience — so a covenant-renewal reading, while common in later teaching, goes beyond what these verses say on their own.

**Restriction without abandonment.** Verses 22-32 hold two things together that can look contradictory: no one is to be forbidden from meetings (v. 22), yet a known-unworthy person is to be forbidden the sacrament itself (v. 29). The text is explicit that restriction is not exclusion — such a person is still to be ministered to, prayed for, and welcomed back the moment they repent (vv. 30, 32) — while v. 31 is equally explicit that a persistently unrepentant person is not 'numbered among my people.' The distinction is best described as restricted standing with continued access to worship and ministry, not full, unqualified retention of fellowship.

**Qualified promise on prayer.** The promise 'whatsoever ye shall ask the Father in my name... it shall be given unto you' (v. 20) carries its own conditions in the same sentence: the request must be in Christ's name, 'right,' and offered in belief — not an unconditional guarantee.

**A commission the multitude could not witness.** The bestowal of power to give the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] (vv. 36-37) is given only to the chosen disciples; the multitude 'heard not the words' and could not bear record of them. The text does not specify the mechanism (it says only that Jesus 'touched' each disciple), so claims about priesthood ordination or a laying-on-of-hands pattern go beyond what is stated here.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Repetition organizes the chapter's argument as much as its content does: 'always' binds remembrance (vv. 7, 11) to prayer (vv. 15, 18-19, 21); 'witness' ties the sacrament and prayer back to the Father (vv. 7, 10-11, 24); and the instruction 'ye shall not cast him out' (vv. 30, 32) keeps sacramental restriction from sliding into abandonment. Two of the chapter's most distinctive images have close biblical counterparts: 'sift you as wheat' (v. 18) closely echoes Christ's warning to Simon in [[Luke 22]]:31, though there it is addressed to one disciple rather than a multitude (and the underlying Greek 'you' in Luke is plural, so the contrast should not be overstated); and the rock, sand, rain, flood, and wind of vv. 12-13 echo both Christ's own earlier Nephite sermon ([[3 Nephi 14]]:24-27) and the conclusion of the Sermon on the Mount ([[Matthew 7]]:24-27). Which of these is the more proximate source is a matter of literary judgment, not something the text settles.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The bread and wine instructions are built as matched units ending in an identical sentence (vv. 7, 11), a refrain that reinforces the doctrine of remembrance through form as well as statement. Christ also repeatedly makes his own action the explicit pattern for the disciples: 'even as I have done' governs the sacrament (v. 6), 'as I have prayed among you even so shall ye pray' governs prayer (v. 16), and 'that which ye have seen me do' defines the light they are told to hold up (v. 24) — a modeling motif that unifies ordinance, devotion, and public example under a single instruction: imitate what you have witnessed.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why is the same 'always remember me... my Spirit to be with you' refrain repeated separately for bread and wine (vv. 7, 11) rather than stated once for the whole ordinance?
- How does the distinction between admitting people to meetings (v. 22) and admitting them to the sacrament (vv. 28-29) shape what 'sacramental worthiness' is for?
- What safeguards, if any, are implied by the phrase 'if ye know' in v. 29 regarding how a disciple would ascertain someone's unworthiness — and what does the text leave unaddressed?
- Does 'more or less than these' (v. 13) refer narrowly to the sacrament instructions just given, or to the wider discourse — and what turns on that scope?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the institution of the sacrament with [[Matthew 26]]:26-28, [[Luke 22]]:19-20, and [[1 Corinthians 11]]:23-25. Compare the rock/sand warning (vv. 12-13) with its closer internal parallel in [[3 Nephi 14]]:24-27 and its Sermon-on-the-Mount source in [[Matthew 7]]:24-27, and the 'hold up your light' command (v. 24) with [[3 Nephi 12]]:16 and [[Matthew 5]]:14-16. For the chapter's continuation, see the disciples' reception of the Holy Ghost in [[3 Nephi 19]], the renewed administration of bread and wine in [[3 Nephi 20]], and the invitation to feel and see Jesus in [[3 Nephi 11]].
