# 3 Nephi 28 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 28]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 28 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
The chapter contrasts two faithful desires. Most disciples seek rest with Christ after completing a normal mortal ministry; three seek to continue bringing souls to him. Jesus blesses both choices and grants the three an extraordinary, prolonged ministry. The narrative then combines heavenly revelation, missionary work, persecution, miraculous deliverance, and Mormon's explanation of their bodily condition. Its closing clarification is crucial: their present change is not the final change associated with [[Resurrection]] and [[Final Judgment]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter has five movements:

1. **Two desires and two promises (vv. 1–12):** Jesus questions the disciples individually and responds to their differing desires.
2. **Heavenly experience (vv. 13–17):** the three are caught up, encounter undisclosable things, and return to ministry; Mormon admits uncertainty about their state.
3. **Mission and deliverance (vv. 18–23):** preaching, [[Baptism]], reception of the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]], and repeated deliverances advance their work.
4. **Mormon's testimony and future projection (vv. 24–35):** Mormon withholds their names, says they ministered to him, and anticipates a worldwide mission before judgment.
5. **Clarification after inquiry (vv. 36–40):** Mormon revisits his earlier uncertainty and distinguishes their temporary change from the greater change at the last day.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents prolonged mortal ministry as service directed toward bringing people to Christ, not as an escape from death for its own sake. It also distinguishes at least two bodily transformations: a present change that suspends ordinary death, pain, temptation, and earthly confinement, and a greater future change followed by permanent life with God. Readers should therefore be cautious about treating the three as already possessing the final state described under [[Resurrection]].

Verses 10–11 contribute to [[The Godhead]] by affirming the Father's and Son's unity while describing distinct actions of the Father, Jesus, and the Holy Ghost. Verses 18 and 23 connect [[Missionary Work]], faith, [[Baptism]], the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]], and incorporation into the church.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The word **change** develops across the chapter. Verse 8 promises a future change from mortality to immortality when Christ comes. Verse 15 says the heavenly experience seemed like a transfiguration into an immortal state, but verse 17 preserves Mormon's uncertainty. Verses 37–40 then clarify that an actual bodily change occurred while insisting that it was not equal to the greater change at the last day. This progression discourages forcing every use of "immortal" or "transfiguration" into a single, fully defined category.

Similarly, "as the angels of God" in verse 30 is grammatically comparative. The immediate context explains angel-like capacities — selective appearance and extraordinary works — without expressly redefining the disciples as angels.

Verse 8's phrase "changed in the twinkling of an eye from mortality to immortality" closely matches the King James wording of 1 Corinthians 15:51–52. Because the Book of Mormon is presented as a translation, exact KJV idiom at a doctrinally central moment like this is worth noting honestly — it shows an English-language correspondence, not by itself evidence for or against the antiquity of the underlying text.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter contains close scriptural resonances. The heavenly ascent and uncertainty about being in or out of the body parallel [[2 Corinthians 12]], while the furnace and beast-den deliverances recall [[Daniel 3]] and [[Daniel 6]]. The connection with John is explicit within the narrative, but [[John 21]] is more guarded than some retellings: it reports a saying about remaining until Christ comes while correcting the rumor that Jesus had declared John would not die.

Mormon's self-correction is also notable. He records “I know not,” later says “since I wrote, I have inquired,” and then supplies a qualified answer. This creates a narrator who differentiates prior record, personal experience, uncertainty, prohibition, and reported revelation. That is a significant literary feature, though it cannot independently authenticate the narrated events.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does Jesus call both groups blessed while calling the three "more blessed"? Is the comparison about moral worth, duration of service, promised experience, or some combination?
- What is clarified — and what remains undefined — by distinguishing the first bodily change from the "greater change" at judgment?
- Why does the chapter emphasize several kinds of restricted knowledge: an unutterable vision, unnamed disciples, missing scriptures, and an initially unresolved question?
- Does sorrow "for the sins of the world" indicate that freedom from ordinary suffering increases rather than removes compassionate participation in human need?
- How should the warnings in verses 34–35 be read alongside the chapter's missionary purpose?
- Is the age of seventy-two (v. 3) meant literally, symbolically, or as an idealized Nephite lifespan?
- Does verse 6's allusion to John presuppose that Nephite listeners already knew a tradition about his request — later spelled out in [[D&C 7]] — or is it left deliberately unexplained within this record?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the ascent account with [[2 Corinthians 12]], the deliverance scenes with [[Daniel 3]] and [[Daniel 6]], and the statement about John's desire with [[John 21]] and [[D&C 7]], which records Christ's later dialogue with John the Beloved directly. These comparisons securely identify shared motifs and language; determining literary dependence, historical relationship, or theological priority requires arguments beyond verbal resemblance. For the Godhead-unity language of verses 10–11, compare [[3 Nephi 11]]. For the distinction this chapter draws between a translated body and a resurrected body, see [[Resurrection]] and [[Final Judgment]].
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
