# 3 Nephi 9 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 9]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 9 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> In the darkness, the voice of Christ proclaims the destruction of many people and cities for their wickedness—He also proclaims His divinity, announces that the law of Moses is fulfilled, and invites men to come unto Him and be saved. About A.D. 34.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
**vv. 1-2** — A voice cries a threefold woe over 'all the inhabitants of the earth' unless they repent.
**vv. 3-11** — A catalog of sixteen destroyed cities (Zarahemla, Moroni, Moronihah, Gilgal, Onihah, Mocum, Jerusalem, Gadiandi, Gadiomnah, Jacob, Gimgimno, Jacobugath, Laman, Josh, Gad, Kishkumen), destroyed variously by fire, drowning, burial, or altered terrain. A recurring purpose clause states the destruction hid the people's wickedness 'that the blood of the prophets and the saints...shall not come up any more unto me against them' (vv. 5, 7-9), climaxing in v. 11's image of blood crying 'from the ground.' Two groups get named causes beyond general wickedness: Jacobugath for 'secret murders and combinations,' and Laman/Josh/Gad/Kishkumen for 'casting out the prophets, and stoning' them.
**v. 12** — 'Many great destructions' beyond the sixteen named cities are noted; the catalog is not presented as exhaustive.
**vv. 13-14** — The address turns to 'all ye that are spared because ye were more righteous than they' — a comparative, not absolute, standing — inviting them to return, repent, and be healed, with 'mine arm of mercy...extended.'
**vv. 15-18** — A string of divine self-identifications: 'I am Jesus Christ the Son of God,' Creator, one with the Father, fulfiller of scripture, 'the light and the life of the world,' 'Alpha and Omega.'
**vv. 19-20** — The [[Law of Moses]] and its blood sacrifices are declared fulfilled and replaced by 'a broken heart and a contrite spirit,' tied to baptism 'with fire and with the Holy Ghost.'
**vv. 21-22** — Closing invitation: come 'as a little child,' grounded in Christ having 'laid down my life, and...taken it up again.'
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **[[Law of Moses]] fulfilled**: vv. 19-20 formally end animal sacrifice ('I will accept none of your sacrifices and your burnt offerings') and replace it with an inward sacrifice, 'a broken heart and a contrite spirit' — language that recurs later in [[3 Nephi 12]] and echoes penitential vocabulary already present in [[2 Nephi 2]].
- **[[Justice and Mercy]] held together**: judgment is not the last word — vv. 13-14, 21-22 extend an unrestricted invitation ('whosoever will come, him will I receive'). Survival itself is not proof of righteousness: the spared are called only 'more righteous than they' (v. 13) and are still commanded to repent and be converted.
- **[[The Godhead]]**: vv. 15-18 assert both Christ's distinct identity ('the Son of God') and His unity with the Father ('I am in the Father, and the Father in me').
- **[[Baptism]] and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]]**: v. 20 ties the broken-heart sacrifice to being 'baptized with fire and with the Holy Ghost,' citing an unnamed prior Lamanite conversion as precedent — plausibly, though not certainly, the fire-encircled prisoners of [[Helaman 5]].
- **Consequences of rejecting prophets**: the recurring refrain frames the cities' destruction as removing a testimony (innocent blood) that would otherwise accuse the guilty before God; [[Secret Combinations]] are named specifically as the cause of Jacobugath's destruction.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verses 15-18 and 22 are notable for how closely their English wording tracks the King James New Testament: 'I came unto my own, and my own received me not... as many as have received me, to them have I given to become the sons of God' (cf. John 1:11-12); 'I am in the Father, and the Father in me' (cf. [[John 14]]:10-11); 'I am the light and the life of the world... Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end' (cf. Revelation 1:8); 'for of such is the kingdom of God' (cf. Mark 10:14); 'I have laid down my life, and have taken it up again' (cf. John 10:17-18). Readers should hold two things at once: within the narrative these are the same divine speaker using a consistent self-revelatory idiom across dispensations, while as an English text the correspondence to specific KJV verses is close enough that the relationship between translation and composition remains a genuinely open, disputed question in Book of Mormon studies.

Two other patterns are worth noting: the survivors are addressed with the comparative 'more righteous than they' (v. 13) rather than an absolute claim of righteousness, and forms of 'come' and 'receive' recur through vv. 13-14, 20, and 22, making the second half of the chapter an invitation-and-response passage.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The city catalog (vv. 3-10) is built on a repeated frame — 'And behold, that great city X...have I caused to be [destroyed]' — varied across fire, drowning, and burial, reinforcing the scale of destruction without becoming monotonous. The recurring purpose clause ('to hide their wickedness...that the blood of the prophets and the saints...shall not come up any more unto me against them') binds an otherwise list-like passage into a single indictment, resolving in v. 11's more vivid image of blood crying 'from the ground' — language that recalls the Genesis account of Abel's blood ([[Genesis 4]]:10), though the text does not make that allusion explicit. The chapter's two halves are joined by a hinge around vv. 13-14, moving from judgment-oracle mode into invitation, and then into revelation-of-self mode at v. 15.

Two verbal parallels to earlier scripture are worth studying without overclaiming their origin: the movement from Christ coming to 'his own' who 'received him not,' to granting sonship to 'as many as have received me' (vv. 16-17), closely parallels [[John 1]]:11-12. And the 'broken heart and a contrite spirit' offered as sacrifice (v. 20) echoes the Psalms' language of a broken and contrite heart — though within the Book of Mormon itself, the identical phrase already recurs at [[2 Nephi 2]]:7 and again shortly afterward at [[3 Nephi 12]]:19, which is the closer and better-evidenced parallel for this specific wording.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why are causes specified for only two of the city-groups (Jacobugath; Laman/Josh/Gad/Kishkumen) and not the other eleven — is a cause implied to be the same throughout, or were these two considered worse?
- What is the referent of 'the Lamanites...were baptized with fire...and they knew it not' (v. 20) — is it necessarily the fire-encircled prisoners of [[Helaman 5]], or a separate, unrecorded event? And what might 'they knew it not' mean, given that Helaman 5 describes their experience as vivid and unmistakable?
- Why does the text call the survivors only 'more righteous' than those destroyed rather than simply righteous (v. 13)?
- Is the coincidence between the city name 'Kishkumen' (v. 10) and the earlier Gadianton assassin of the same name significant, or an independent reuse of a name?
- How should the close KJV-parallel phrasing of vv. 15-18 and v. 22 shape one's view of the translation process, given that verbal closeness alone cannot settle the question?
