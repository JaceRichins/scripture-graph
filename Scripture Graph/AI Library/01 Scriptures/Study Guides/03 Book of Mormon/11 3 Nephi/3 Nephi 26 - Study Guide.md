# 3 Nephi 26 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 26]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 26 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Jesus expounds all things from the beginning to the end—Babes and children utter marvelous things that cannot be written—Those in the Church of Christ have all things in common among them. About A.D. 34.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Expansive teaching and judgment (vv. 1–5):** Jesus's exposition reaches from “the beginning” to cosmic dissolution and the last judgment; the synopsis includes selected content as well as scope.
2. **Mormon's editorial explanation (vv. 6–12):** Mormon distinguishes his abbreviated account from the more extensive record on the plates of Nephi and explains the withholding of greater things as a trial of faith.
3. **Continued ministry and unwritten manifestations (vv. 13–16):** Jesus teaches, appears repeatedly, breaks bread, ministers to children, and enables utterances that may not be recorded.
4. **Formation of the community (vv. 17–21):** teaching, baptism, the Holy Ghost, mutual ministry, shared goods, justice, obedience, and the name church of Christ follow.

Across these movements, abundant revelation is repeatedly set beside limits on what may be written.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Judgment and resurrection:** All peoples stand before God to be judged according to their works, with contrasting resurrection outcomes (vv. 4–5). The same verse places [[Justice and Mercy]], holiness, and Christ together without fully explaining their relationship.
- **Christ before the world:** Verse 5 explicitly describes Christ as existing before the world began and is therefore relevant to [[Premortal Life]].
- **Conditional revelation:** The written account is presented as a preliminary test; “greater things” may be manifested upon belief, but the chapter does not specify their form, timing, or recipients (vv. 8–11).
- **Community formation:** [[Baptism]] in Jesus's name is joined with the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]], teaching, mutual ministry, just conduct, obedience, and communal identity (vv. 17–21).
- **Shared goods:** Having “all things common” is relevant to [[Consecration]], but the brief statement does not define ownership or a complete economic system.
- **Breaking bread:** Verse 13 plausibly describes repeated administration of the [[Sacrament]] in light of [[3 Nephi 18]], although it does not itself name the rite.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Writing and restriction form a repeated vocabulary: Mormon writes a “lesser part,” “greater things” may be manifested, the Lord forbids further inclusion from the plates, and the children's sayings are forbidden to be written. In this chapter, therefore, *not written* does not mean *not revealed*.

“Loosed their tongues” denotes divinely enabled speech in context, but it does not establish that the children spoke previously unknown human languages.

Several English phrases closely resemble the King James Bible:

- “the elements should melt with fervent heat” (3 Nephi 26:3) is nearly verbatim with 2 Peter 3:10, apart from “should” and “shall”;
- the works-based contrast between “resurrection of everlasting life” and “resurrection of damnation” (vv. 4–5) closely parallels John 5:28–29, which says “resurrection of life”;
- “unspeakable things, which are not lawful to be written” (v. 18) more loosely echoes 2 Corinthians 12:4.

These similarities establish relationships between the English expressions, not their historical mechanism. Direct dependence, deliberate allusion, repeated teaching, and translation into familiar KJV diction remain competing explanations. Isaiah 34:4 is only a qualified comparison: Isaiah rolls the *heavens* together as a scroll, whereas 3 Nephi 26:3 says the *earth* is wrapped as a scroll.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter creates a deliberate tension between comprehensiveness and compression. Jesus expounds "all things," yet Mormon says not even a hundredth part can be included. Children speak things greater than those already disclosed, yet their words cannot be recorded. This pattern makes the silences part of the chapter's meaning: the narrator asks readers to respond faithfully to limited testimony without pretending that the written text contains the whole revelation. The children also produce a striking reversal of expected authority, becoming witnesses to their fathers rather than merely recipients of adult instruction.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Narrative And Editorial Structure *(tentative)*
> Verses 6–12 contain a sustained first-person editorial explanation: Mormon distinguishes his present book from the plates of Nephi, says his account contains a lesser part of Jesus's teachings, and reports that the Lord forbade him to include everything he found on those plates. This is strong internal evidence that the chapter is deliberately framed as an abridgment with asserted source layers.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does the chapter mean by believing "these things": accepting propositions, acting on Jesus's commandments, or the whole pattern of response shown in verses 17–21?
- Does "made manifest" in verse 9 promise a future text, experiential revelation, or either? What in the wording limits the answer?
- Why are both Mormon and the witnesses to the children's speech forbidden to write certain revelations?
- How does the pairing of common goods with just dealing shape a responsible reading of [[Consecration]]?
- Why does the narrative move directly from withheld revelation to baptism, mutual ministry, and the church of Christ?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the command to preserve scripture in [[3 Nephi 23]] with the command to withhold material here. Read the breaking of bread alongside [[3 Nephi 18]], the reception of the Holy Ghost alongside [[3 Nephi 19]], and the shared-goods language beside [[4 Nephi 1]]. For conditional disclosure, compare [[Ether 4]]; the comparison does not require the “greater things” in both passages to be the same record. For the judgment and cosmic imagery, compare [[John 5]], [[2 Peter 3]], and [[Isaiah 34]], keeping the mechanism of the English parallels open and noting that Isaiah rolls the heavens as a scroll while 3 Nephi wraps the earth.
