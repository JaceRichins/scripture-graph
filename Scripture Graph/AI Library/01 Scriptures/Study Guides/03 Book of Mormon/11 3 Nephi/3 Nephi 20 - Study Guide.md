# 3 Nephi 20 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 20]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 20 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
The chapter moves from worship to covenant exposition. Jesus commands continued inward [[Prayer]], provides bread and wine, relates them to his body and blood, and the multitude is filled with the Spirit (vv. 1–9). He then explains the Father's [[Covenants]] with Israel through conversion, gathering, conditional judgment, and restored inheritance (vv. 10–46). The chapter distinguishes a New Jerusalem in “this land” from the restoration of Jerusalem, while presenting both within a larger covenant hope. Its final portion extensively applies language from [[Isaiah 52]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Prayer and sacred meal (vv. 1–9):** the immediate prayer ends, prayer in the heart continues, and Jesus provides bread and wine before the multitude is filled with the Spirit.

2. **Gathering, judgment, and New Jerusalem (vv. 10–22):** Jesus connects Isaiah's fulfillment with the [[Gathering of Israel]], warns unrepentant Gentiles through severe prophetic imagery, and promises a New Jerusalem in “this land.”

3. **Christ and the ancestral covenants (vv. 23–33):** the prophet like Moses, the witness of the prophets, and the [[Abrahamic Covenant]] are applied to Christ's mission, Gentile blessing, Israel's scattering, conversion, and Jerusalem's restoration.

4. **Isaiah 52 applied (vv. 34–46):** Jerusalem's comfort, Zion's awakening, purification, protected departure, worldwide salvation, and the exalted yet marred servant culminate in renewed inheritance.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The sacred meal later identified with [[Sacrament]] emphasizes Christ as giver, the disciples as distributors, and spiritual filling as the narrative outcome (vv. 3–9). The sequence presents a close relationship without defining an automatic or exclusive causal mechanism.

The [[Gathering of Israel]] has both spiritual and geographic dimensions: scattered remnants are brought to knowledge of their Redeemer, while later passages describe belief in Christ and restored lands (vv. 13, 29–33). These passages may concern related but distinguishable populations or stages.

The [[Abrahamic Covenant]] includes moral transformation. Christ blesses covenant heirs by turning them from iniquity, and the Holy Ghost is poured out upon the Gentiles (vv. 25–27). Gentile blessing does not remove accountability: judgment is conditioned on failure to repent and hardening after receiving the gospel (vv. 15, 20, 28).
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Recurring words such as **covenant**, **fulfill**, **gather**, **inheritance**, **bless**, and **repent** give the discourse continuity. A particularly strong verbal link occurs between Jesus's promise that the recipient's soul “shall be filled” (v. 8) and the report that the multitude “were filled with the Spirit” (v. 9).

Collective speech also recurs: the multitude glorifies Jesus “with one voice” (v. 9), the watchmen sing together (v. 32), and Jerusalem's waste places are commanded to sing together (v. 34). This may create a literary echo between present worship and future restoration, although the recurrence could also reflect ordinary communal language and the inherited wording of Isaiah.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter is densely intertextual. [[Micah 5]] supplies the remnant-as-lion and lifted-hand imagery in verses 16–17, while [[Micah 4]] supplies the gathered sheaves, iron horn, brass hoofs, and consecrated gain in verses 18–19. Verses 23–26 closely parallel [[Acts 3]] in their sequence of Moses's prophet, prophetic witnesses, Abraham's promise, and turning people from iniquity.

The longest sequence comes from [[Isaiah 52]]. Verses 34–35 correspond to Isaiah 52:9–10; verses 36–38 return to 52:1–3; verses 39–40 use 52:6–7; and verses 41–45 continue with 52:11–15. The reordered material joins Jerusalem's comfort, Zion's purification, proclamation of salvation, protected departure, and the exalted yet marred servant.

These correspondences strongly establish deliberate biblical intertextual construction in the chapter's final English form. They do not determine whether the relationships arose through ancient source traditions, editorial activity, translation into familiar biblical diction, or modern literary dependence, and they do not independently establish the historical fulfillment of the prophecies.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How does the command to continue praying in the heart qualify the command to cease the immediate prayer?

- What distinctions does the chapter make between the New Jerusalem in "this land" and the restored land of Jerusalem?

- Which statements make judgment conditional, and how should those conditions govern interpretation of the violent prophetic imagery?

- How does the promise to Abraham move through Christ, Israel, and the Gentiles in verses 25-29?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare verses 16–19 closely with [[Micah 5]] and [[Micah 4]], noting what is retained and how the two passages are joined. Compare verses 23–27 with [[Acts 3]] to trace the clustered themes of Moses's prophet, prophetic witness, Abraham's seed, and turning from iniquity. Then read [[3 Nephi 16]] and [[3 Nephi 21]] alongside this chapter to follow the developing account of [[Jews and Gentiles]], gathering, repentance, and [[Zion]].
