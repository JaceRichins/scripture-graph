# 3 Nephi 27 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 27]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 27 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
The disciples ask how the church should be named because the issue has caused disputation. Jesus answers that his church must bear his name and also be built upon his [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]] (3 Nephi 27:3–10). He then expounds that gospel through his mission from the Father, the [[Crucifixion of Jesus Christ]], judgment, [[Repentance]], [[Baptism]], reception of the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]], and [[Endurance]] (3 Nephi 27:13–21). The chapter subsequently addresses discipleship, records, judgment, prayer, and the contrasting futures of the living and fourth generations.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The naming question and the church's foundation (vv. 1–12):** The disciples seek guidance; Jesus joins his name with the requirement that the church be built upon his gospel.
2. **The gospel expounded (vv. 13–22):** Christ describes his mission, cross, judgment, cleansing, repentance, baptism, sanctification, endurance, and the imitation of his works.
3. **Records, judgment, and Christlike character (vv. 23–27):** The disciples are commanded to write, records disclose works in connection with judgment, and Jesus becomes their standard.
4. **Promises and warnings (vv. 28–33):** Jesus teaches asking in his name, rejoices in the living generation, foretells the fourth generation's fall, and concludes with the two ways.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter places divine cleansing and accountable conduct together. People are judged according to works, yet guiltlessness and entrance into God's rest are also associated with Christ's blood, [[Faith]], repentance, baptism, reception of the Holy Ghost, sanctification, and faithfulness to the end (3 Nephi 27:14–20). This rules out a simple works-only summary but does not settle every later theory of grace, ordinances, and judgment.

Church identity likewise involves more than a designation. Bearing Christ's name is required, while verse 8 adds the condition of being built upon the [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]]. Verses 21 and 27 command disciples to do Christ's works and become like him; the text presents these as standards of discipleship without formally defining them as institutional tests or qualifications for authority.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The phrase "in my name" recurs in connection with baptism, the church's designation, action, and prayer. In this chapter it conveys allegiance to Christ and claimed relationship to him, while the additional condition "if it so be that they are built upon my gospel" prevents the name from functioning as the sole criterion.

The English expression "lifted up" connects three distinct movements: Christ is lifted upon the cross, humanity is brought before him for judgment, and faithful disciples are promised final elevation (3 Nephi 27:14–15, 22). The recurrence creates local cohesion without proving deliberate source-language wordplay. Likewise, "works" recurs with different referents: the Father's works, works of false foundations, works judged, Christ's works imitated, and human works preserved in records.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The opening prayer and the later promise concerning asking the Father in Christ's name form a possible loose echo, though the text does not state that the opening prayer was undertaken solely because of the naming dispute. The declarations "this is the gospel" in verses 13 and 21 frame the central discourse.

Verses 28–29 and 33 closely parallel sayings in [[Matthew 7]] and [[3 Nephi 14]], and the night imagery in verse 33 resembles John 9:4. These correspondences are secure at the level of the received English text. They do not by themselves identify the responsible compositional agent, establish direct dependence, recover an underlying language, or prove either ancient or modern authorship.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does Jesus answer a dispute over the church's name by expounding the gospel and its works?

- What does "shall be filled" mean in verse 16, and how—if at all—should it be related to the Holy Ghost in verse 20?

- How do judgment according to works and washing garments in Christ's blood function together in verses 14–20?

- What books are envisioned in verses 23–26, and how do human records relate to the statement that all things are written by the Father?

- How broadly should the command to be "even as I am" in verse 27 be applied beyond the immediate judging context?

- What chronological span constitutes the "fourth generation" in verse 32?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the gospel discourse with [[3 Nephi 11]] and [[2 Nephi 31]], noting both the shared elements and each passage's distinctive emphasis. Compare verses 28–29 and 33 with [[Matthew 7]] and [[3 Nephi 14]], distinguishing observable English correspondence from claims about historical dependence. Trace "lifted up" through verses 14, 15, and 22, and compare the judicial-books imagery with Revelation 20:12 without assuming direct borrowing. Study verses 23–26 alongside [[Records and Sacred Writings]], preserving the unresolved distinction between human books, divine writing, and divine knowledge.
