# 3 Nephi 29 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 29]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 29 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The coming forth of the Book of Mormon is a sign that the Lord has commenced to gather Israel and fulfill His covenants—Those who reject His latter-day revelations and gifts will be cursed. About A.D. 34–35.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Covenant sign and assurance (vv. 1–3):** The sayings come among the Gentiles; readers may recognize that Israel's restoration has begun and need not regard prophetic words as delayed or vain.
2. **Judgment and warnings (vv. 4–7):** Spurning the Lord's works is illustrated through denials of Christ, revelation, spiritual gifts, and miracles, with verse 7 specifically identifying a denial made “to get gain.”
3. **Israel and unavoidable divine action (vv. 8–9):** Contempt toward Jews and Israel's remnant is prohibited because the Lord remembers his covenant; human resistance cannot prevent covenant fulfillment and judgment.

Covenant remembrance occurs on both sides of verses 4–7, while right-hand imagery appears in verses 4 and 9. These recurrences form a plausible literary frame, though deliberate authorial design cannot be established from repetition alone.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter closely joins [[Covenants]], [[Gathering of Israel]], and [[Records and Sacred Writings]]. The record's coming forth is treated as a sign that restoration has commenced. It also rejects categorical cessation of [[Revelation]], [[Prophecy]], [[Spiritual Gifts]], and [[Miracles]].

Verse 8 adds a vital moral dimension to [[Jews and Gentiles]]: belief in Israel's restoration cannot coherently coexist with making Jews or Israel's remnant objects of ridicule. God's faithfulness to his covenant is the stated reason for rejecting such contempt.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The alternating formulas “ye may know” and “ye need not” give the chapter a pastoral as well as a warning voice. Readers may know that fulfillment has begun; therefore they need not infer delay, vanity, or covenant failure.

“Already beginning to be fulfilled” is an important temporal qualification. It permits a process with multiple stages and discourages equating an initial sign with exhaustive fulfillment.

The phrase “to get gain” narrows the immediate target of verse 7. Its harshest comparison addresses a financially motivated proclamation that Christ can perform no miracle, not every unresolved question about a particular miracle claim.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter participates in a wider network of recurring themes. [[3 Nephi 20]] and [[3 Nephi 21]] connect Israel's covenant and restoration with the future emergence of the record. [[Mormon 8]] and [[Mormon 9]] associate the record's latter-day setting with gain-oriented religion and denials of miracles, while [[Moroni 10]] gives a fuller catalogue of spiritual gifts.

These parallels demonstrate literary and theological coherence within the received text. Their evidentiary significance is limited: the same recurrence is compatible with an ancient compiler, later redaction, dependence on shared scriptural language, or unified modern composition. It therefore does not by itself establish date, authorship, or historical truth.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What distinction should be maintained between the beginning and the completion of covenant fulfillment?
- Why does the chapter move from recognizing a record to moral warnings about revelation, gain, and contempt for Jews?
- Does verse 7 condemn all critical inquiry about miracles, or the narrower act it describes: profit-motivated categorical denial?
- How does the right-hand image connect covenant faithfulness with judgment?
