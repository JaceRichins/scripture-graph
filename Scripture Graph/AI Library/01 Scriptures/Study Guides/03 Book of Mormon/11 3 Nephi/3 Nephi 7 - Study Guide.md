# 3 Nephi 7 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 7]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 7 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The chief judge is murdered, the government is destroyed, and the people divide into tribes—Jacob, an anti-Christ, becomes king of a secret combination—Nephi preaches repentance and faith in Christ—Angels minister to him daily, and he raises his brother from the dead—Many repent and are baptized. About A.D. 30–33.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Collapse and tribalization (vv. 1–8):** the chief judge is murdered, common government is destroyed, and rapid apostasy is emphasized.

2. **Jacob's rival kingdom (vv. 9–13):** the secret combination appoints a king and flees north to recruit dissenters.

3. **Limited peace without righteousness (v. 14):** separate tribal laws prevent war but do not end hostility toward God or the prophets.

4. **Nephi's witness and miracles (vv. 15–22):** angelic preparation, preaching, healing, deliverance, and the raising of his brother produce both anger and conversion.

5. **Repentance and baptism (vv. 23–26):** authorized ministers baptize repentant believers as a public witness before God and the people.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter tightly associates [[Faith]], [[Repentance]], remission of sins, and [[Baptism]] without reducing them to interchangeable acts. Nephi preaches remission through faith in Jesus Christ (v. 16); those brought to repentance receive water baptism (v. 24); and baptism witnesses that they have repented and received remission (v. 25). The wording presents an integrated covenant response, although it does not by itself settle every question about the precise temporal order of these elements.

The account also portrays [[Miracles]] as signs that invite but do not force conversion. Some witnesses become angry despite being unable to dismiss Nephi's words and power (vv. 18–21). Recognition of power is therefore not portrayed as equivalent to willing discipleship.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verse 8 uses a paired animal proverb—dog returning to vomit and sow returning to mire—that closely parallels 2 Peter 2:22, with the dog image also appearing in Proverbs 26:11. The verbal relationship is clear in English; its historical explanation is not. It may reflect translation into familiar biblical diction, modern composition, or a shared proverbial tradition.

Witness vocabulary accumulates across the second half: Nephi is an "eye-witness" (v. 15), the people "saw" and "did witness" (v. 20), converts "signify" and healed people "manifest" their experiences (vv. 21–22), and baptism becomes a "witness and a testimony" (v. 25). This pattern gives Nephi's ministry a deliberately public character.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Several contrasts carry the chapter's argument: tribes achieve peace but remain estranged from God; opponents recognize Nephi's power but respond with anger; only a few initially convert, yet many are later baptized; and the government collapses while an authorized ministry is established. The movement is not a simple transition from chaos to restoration. Political order remains fractured even as a community of repentant believers begins to form.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Intertextual Verbal Parallel
> The English wording of 3 Nephi 7:8 closely parallels the paired dog-and-sow proverb in 2 Peter 2:22; its dog-and-vomit image also appears in Proverbs 26:11. This is strong evidence of biblical intertextuality in the English Book of Mormon wording.

> [!evidence] Internal Narrative Testimony
> The chapter constructs an internal chain of testimony: Nephi is called an eye-witness, the people see and witness miracles, converts publicly signify their experience of divine power, healed people manifest what occurred, and baptism serves as a witness and testimony.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 1.0 · Literary pattern accepted; historical force limited · [[Internal Narrative Testimony in 3 Nephi 7|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intertextual/Translation-Idiom *(tentative)*
> 3 Nephi 7:8 combines two proverbial images of relapse into a single phrase — a dog returning to its vomit, and a washed sow returning to wallow in mire — that in the King James Bible appear only in two separate texts: Proverbs 26:11 (dog/vomit alone) and 2 Peter 2:22 (both images together, in wording very close to the Nephi passage).

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does the chapter gain by repeating "family, kindred and friends" when explaining tribal organization?

- Why does the narrator distinguish peace "in some degree" from righteousness in verse 14?

- In verse 18, what kind of disbelief has become impossible, given that anger and resistance continue?

- How do the witness terms in verses 15 and 20–25 shape the account's understanding of conversion and public accountability?

- Does the changing description from "few" converts in verse 21 to "many" baptisms in verse 26 indicate accelerating success, or simply different unspecified quantities?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read this chapter with [[3 Nephi 6]] and [[3 Nephi 8]] to trace the sequence from institutional corruption through fragmentation to destruction. Compare its treatment of [[Secret Combinations]] with [[Helaman 6]], and its rapid reversal of righteousness with [[Helaman 11]]. [[4 Nephi 1]] offers a useful later contrast: kinship-based division and incompatible laws give way to a society described in terms of unity.
