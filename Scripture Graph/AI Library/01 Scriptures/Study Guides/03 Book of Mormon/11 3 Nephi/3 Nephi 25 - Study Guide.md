# 3 Nephi 25 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[3 Nephi 25]] · My notes: [[3 Nephi 25 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> At the Second Coming, the proud and wicked will be burned as stubble—Elijah will return before that great and dreadful day—Compare Malachi 4. About A.D. 34.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter has three compact movements:

1. **The coming day (vv. 1-3)** — the day 'shall burn as an oven,' consuming 'the proud' and wrongdoers as stubble, while those who fear the Lord's name are healed and 'tread down the wicked.'
2. **Remember Moses (v. 4)** — a command to remember 'the law of Moses...which I commanded unto him in Horeb for all Israel.'
3. **Await Elijah (vv. 5-6)** — the promise that Elijah will be sent to 'turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers' before 'the great and dreadful day of the Lord.'

Verse 4 functions as a hinge, looking backward to the covenant given at Horeb between two forward-looking sections. Whether this arrangement is a deliberate literary echo of the 'turning' the prophecy itself describes is an interesting but speculative reading, not something the text states outright.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **[[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] and [[Final Judgment]]**: the wicked and proud are consumed ('neither root nor branch'), while the righteous are healed and empowered. The chapter does not specify whether every image (burning, ashes, calves) is literal or symbolic.
- **[[Law of Moses]]**: v. 4's command to 'remember' the law, spoken by the same Christ who elsewhere teaches its fulfillment in him, raises a real interpretive question rather than settling it (see Questions).
- **[[Family]]** and **[[Priesthood Restoration]]**: Latter-day Saints identify the Elijah promise (vv. 5-6) as fulfilled by Elijah's appearance to Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery in the Kirtland Temple ([[D&C 110]] 13-16), understood as the restoration of sealing keys. The chapter itself names neither genealogy nor a particular ordinance — that is later Restoration application, not something stated here.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The most notable textual feature of this chapter is v. 2's 'the **Son** of Righteousness,' where the King James Malachi 4:2 reads 'the **Sun** of righteousness.' Because 'sun' and 'son' are homophones in English, the difference could be a byproduct of oral dictation, a scribal or printing choice, or a deliberate Christological reading placed in the mouth of Christ himself — the text alone cannot settle which. Notably, the surrounding solar imagery is left unchanged: the figure still 'arise[s] with healing in his wings.' The 'Son of Righteousness' spelling is not unique to this chapter — the same phrase also appears elsewhere in the Book of Mormon (e.g., 2 Nephi 26:9) — so this alone cannot be read as a variant unique to Christ's own quotation.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
- **Imagery of reversal**: the wicked become 'stubble' burned to nothing ('neither root nor branch'), then 'ashes under the soles of your feet' (v. 3); the righteous 'grow up as calves in the stall' (v. 2), an image of vitality and growth, though the text does not itself specify release from confinement.
- **Solar/filial wordplay**: see Language, above.
- **Backward/forward turn**: v. 4 pivots from the forward-looking judgment of vv. 1-3 to a backward glance at the law given at Horeb, before vv. 5-6 turn forward again to Elijah's still-future mission — a suggestive but not textually explicit echo of the 'turning' the prophecy itself describes.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Cross-Canon Textual Comparison *(tentative)*
> The angel Moroni quoted this same Malachi 4:5-6 prophecy to young Joseph Smith in 1823 (recorded in Joseph Smith—History 1:38-39 and D&C 2), but with wording that differs from both the KJV and 3 Nephi 25:6 — describing Elijah planting in children's hearts 'the promises made to the fathers' and warning that the earth would otherwise be 'utterly wasted' at his coming, rather than 3 Nephi's 'turn the heart of the fathers to the children...lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.' Readers should verify the precise 1823 wording directly, as this claim reconstructs it from memory rather than mechanical lookup.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does Christ command remembrance of 'the law of Moses' (v. 4) in the same visit in which he elsewhere teaches that the law is fulfilled in him? Is this about continued ritual practice, or about honoring the law's origin and purpose?
- Is the 'Son'/'Sun' variant in v. 2 an intentional pun or an artifact of oral dictation? The text alone cannot decide.
- What does 'turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers' (v. 6) mean on its own terms, before later canonical interpretations are brought in?
- The text names 'Elijah the prophet' directly (v. 5) rather than a symbolic figure — how would Nephite readers c. 34 A.D., without the Restoration's later framework, have understood this promise?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
