# 1 Nephi 15 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Nephi 15]] · My notes: [[1 Nephi 15 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Lehi’s seed are to receive the gospel from the Gentiles in the latter days—The gathering of Israel is likened unto an olive tree whose natural branches will be grafted in again—Nephi interprets the vision of the tree of life and speaks of the justice of God in dividing the wicked from the righteous. About 600–592 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Return, grief, and renewed strength (vv. 1–6):** Nephi returns from the vision, grieves over his brothers and the destruction he has foreseen, and receives strength before speaking.

2. **How understanding is received (vv. 7–11):** The brothers identify their question; Nephi exposes their failure to inquire and states conditions for receiving [[Revelation]].

3. **Olive tree and covenant restoration (vv. 12–20):** Israel is the olive tree; Lehi's family is a broken-off branch; the gospel's movement through the Gentiles leads to covenant remembrance and restoration.

4. **Tree and rod (vv. 21–25):** The tree is the tree of life, and the rod is God's word, which must be hearkened to and held fast.

5. **River, gulf, and judgment (vv. 26–36):** The river represents filthiness, separation, hell, and divine justice; temporal works are connected to the final state of souls.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's account of spiritual learning joins [[Faith]], [[Obedience]], and an unhardened heart rather than treating revelation as mere information transfer (1 Nephi 15:8–11). Its account of Israel joins covenant identity with knowledge of forefathers, knowledge of the Redeemer, his doctrine, and coming to him for [[Salvation]] (1 Nephi 15:13–18).

The iron rod passage presents God's word as a means of endurance against [[Satan and Temptation]]. Its promise is specifically that temptation will not overpower faithful hearers into blindness and destruction; it does not say they will never experience temptation or adversity (1 Nephi 15:24–25).

The closing argument links embodied conduct during probation with [[Final Judgment]]. No unclean thing can enter the [[Kingdom of God]], so divine justice separates persistent wickedness from righteousness (1 Nephi 15:31–36). This chapter emphasizes justice and exclusion; a fuller account of repentance, cleansing, grace, and resurrection must be drawn from other passages.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Several repeated word clusters carry the argument. In verses 8–11, **inquire**, **ask**, **faith**, **believing**, **diligence**, and **known** connect spiritual posture with understanding. In verses 23–25, **hearken**, **hold fast**, **give heed**, and **remember** portray reception of God's word as sustained action. In verses 27 and 33–35, **filthiness**, **filthy**, and **unclean** connect the polluted river with the moral condition that excludes a person from God's kingdom.

The discourse also combines branch, vine, fold, and grafting language in verses 12–16. These images overlap in their themes of belonging and nourishment, but their individual details should not be forced into a single literal horticultural scheme.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter uses a repeated question-and-answer pattern: the brothers ask what Lehi meant, and Nephi identifies or expands each symbol (1 Nephi 15:7–20, 21–36). Their questions create a progression from communal history to personal destiny—from Israel's scattering and restoration, through holding to God's word, to judgment and the final state of souls.

Two trees organize the chapter's imagery. The **olive tree** signifies the house of Israel and concerns covenant membership, scattering, and grafting (vv. 12–18). The **tree of life** belongs to Lehi's dream and concerns divine gift, discipleship, and final access or exclusion (vv. 21–36). Recognizing this distinction prevents the chapter's two symbolic systems from being collapsed.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary Coherence
> The sustained question-and-answer interpretation of Lehi's dream is strong evidence that 1 Nephi 15 is literarily integrated with the earlier dream narrative: multiple symbols are revisited in sequence and incorporated into a single teaching about revelation, discipleship, judgment, and the tree of life.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 1.0 · Coherence accepted; evidentiary reach reduced · [[Literary Coherence in 1 Nephi 15|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intertextual Phrasing *(tentative)*
> The covenant formula quoted in 1 Nephi 15:18 uses the phrase 'kindreds of the earth,' which matches the wording of Acts 3:25 (KJV) rather than the 'nations of the earth' wording of Genesis 22:18. This is a documentable wording similarity between the Book of Mormon text and a specific New Testament passage, not merely the general Abrahamic covenant theme.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.8 · English wording match accepted; significance disputed

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. Verse 11 promises that difficult things can be made known. What conditions does Nephi name, and which commonly assumed conditions does he not name?

2. How does Nephi's grief in verses 4–6 affect the tone of his correction and teaching?

3. What is gained by reading the restoration sequence in verses 13–16 step by step rather than treating gathering as only geographic relocation?

4. How do the olive tree and the tree of life perform different functions in the same chapter?

5. In verse 24, what precisely are faithful hearers protected from? How does that differ from a promise of an adversity-free life?

6. What does Nephi's answer to the temporal-or-spiritual question include, and what details remain unspecified?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
