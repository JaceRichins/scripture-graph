# 1 Nephi 7 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Nephi 7]] · My notes: [[1 Nephi 7 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Lehi’s sons return to Jerusalem and invite Ishmael and his household to join them in their journey—Laman and others rebel—Nephi exhorts his brethren to have faith in the Lord—They bind him with cords and plan his destruction—He is freed by the power of faith—His brethren ask forgiveness—Lehi and his company offer sacrifice and burnt offerings. About 600–592 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The narrative has six movements:

1. **Commission and invitation (vv. 1–5):** the Lord commands the return to Jerusalem, and Ishmael's household responds.
2. **Division (vv. 6–7):** members of both families rebel and desire to return.
3. **Remember and choose (vv. 8–15):** Nephi appeals to prior revelation and deliverance, then warns about Jerusalem.
4. **Violence and release (vv. 16–18):** Nephi is bound, prays, and is freed.
5. **Mediation and reconciliation (vv. 19–21):** members of Ishmael's family intervene; the offenders become sorrowful, seek forgiveness, and pray.
6. **Arrival and worship (v. 22):** the reunited company reaches Lehi's tent and offers thanks and sacrifice.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents [[Faith]] through remembered dealings with God, present trust, and Nephi's prayer for deliverance. It also distinguishes two dimensions of [[Repentance and Forgiveness]]: Nephi forgives the offenders for what they did to him, then directs them to pray to God for forgiveness. Their reconciliation permits the journey to resume, but this chapter alone does not establish permanent conversion.

Divine help and human mediation appear in sequence. Nephi prays before his bonds are loosed; after the threat resumes, three members of Ishmael's household plead with the attackers until the conflict ends. The narrative therefore gives meaningful roles to both prayer and interpersonal peacemaking.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Nephi's repeated “How is it” and “how is it that ye have forgotten” questions make forgetfulness the center of his diagnosis. The speech moves from remembered signs to a principle: because the Lord has already demonstrated power, the brothers should exercise [[Faith]] now. The narration also consistently describes travel as going “up” to Jerusalem and “down” into the wilderness, although that conventional directional language alone cannot establish provenance.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Nephi's references to the angel, deliverance from Laban, and acquisition of the record recall [[1 Nephi 3]] and [[1 Nephi 4]], giving his rebuke continuity with earlier narrated events. The concluding sacrifice also parallels the family's worship after the earlier safe return in [[1 Nephi 5]]. These connections support literary coherence, but they do not distinguish ancient narration from modern composition or later editorial shaping. The later binding episode in [[1 Nephi 18]] is also a useful comparison, provided its different sequence and outcome are not obscured.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Historical Correlation *(tentative)*
> Nephi's aside that the Jerusalem establishment 'rejected the prophets, and Jeremiah have they cast into prison' is consistent with the biblical account of Jeremiah's imprisonment (Jeremiah 37-38), but this correlation is weak evidence for the text's antiquity, since Joseph Smith had direct access to that same biblical material, and the precise timing of Jeremiah's imprisonment (associated with Zedekiah's later reign, near Jerusalem's 587 BC fall) sits uneasily against the traditional ~600 BC dating of this episode, shortly after Lehi's departure.
> — strength 0.2 · confidence 0.7 · textual correlation accepted; chronology disputed
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does Nephi frame rebellion principally as forgetting rather than as lack of evidence?
- What does the intervention of Ishmael's wife, daughter, and son add to a reading focused only on Nephi's miraculous release?
- Why does Nephi distinguish his own forgiveness from forgiveness sought through prayer to God?
- Does the rebels' sorrow represent lasting conversion, temporary remorse, or something between them? What can and cannot be concluded from this chapter alone?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the angel and record references with [[1 Nephi 3]] and [[1 Nephi 4]], and compare the concluding offerings with [[1 Nephi 5]]. Jeremiah's imprisonment in [[Jeremiah 37]] is relevant to 1 Nephi 7:14, but any chronological argument should distinguish the clear textual parallel from disputed assumptions about when this journey occurred and whether Nephi's statement is contemporaneous or retrospective.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
