# 1 Nephi 22 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Nephi 22]] · My notes: [[1 Nephi 22 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Nephi interprets the prophecy read in [[1 Nephi 21]] as concerning matters that are both temporal and spiritual. The chapter moves rhetorically from [[Scattering of Israel]] through complex Gentile involvement and a marvelous work, then to covenant disclosure, [[Gathering of Israel]], recognition of the Redeemer, judgment, and the reign of the Holy One. This sequence should not be treated as a fully specified timetable. Nephi concludes with personal application: all peoples may find safety through [[Repentance]], while [[Obedience]] and [[Endurance]] are joined to salvation.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The interpretive question (vv. 1-3):** Nephi's brothers ask whether the prophecy is spiritual rather than embodied. Nephi answers that it is revealed by the Spirit but concerns matters both temporal and spiritual and occurring according to the flesh.

2. **Scattering and Gentile involvement (vv. 4-12):** Israel is dispersed; a Gentile nation scatters Nephi's descendants; afterward a marvelous work among the Gentiles brings blessing, covenant knowledge, the gospel, and gathering.

3. **Judgment and preservation (vv. 13-19):** Powers opposing Israel and Zion suffer reversal and self-defeating violence, while the righteous are promised preservation. The exact scope of that preservation remains uncertain.

4. **The Holy One, corrupt kingdoms, and the gathered flock (vv. 20-26):** Nephi identifies the prophet like Moses as the Holy One of Israel, describes the corrupt aims of the devil's kingdom, and portrays one shepherd reigning over a righteous people among whom Satan has no power.

5. **Conclusion (vv. 27-31):** Nephi reaffirms fulfillment according to the flesh, extends conditional safety to all repentant peoples, and appeals to the brass-plate writings as witnesses of obedience and endurance.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter tightly associates [[Gathering of Israel]] with territorial return, release from captivity, emergence from obscurity, reception of covenants and the gospel, and recognition of the Lord as Savior and Redeemer. It therefore portrays gathering as more than migration, though it does not erase its geographic dimension.

Verse 26 connects Satan's lack of power over human hearts with communal righteousness under the Holy One's reign. This resembles later teachings about the [[Millennium]], but the chapter itself says only that Satan cannot be loosed for many years. Verse 28 extends safety to every nation, kindred, tongue, and people while making [[Repentance]] an explicit condition.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
“According to the flesh” appears at strategic points in verses 2, 18, and 27. It counters an exclusively spiritual reading, but it need not turn every prophetic image into literalistic reportage. The chapter itself says the matters are “both temporal and spiritual.”

The repeated “soon,” “shortly,” and “speedily” language creates urgency without giving a calendar. Readers should distinguish the textual observation—that imminence language is present—from proposed explanations of its prophetic horizon.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The repetition of “according to the flesh” near the beginning and in verse 27 frames the main exposition as an answer to the opening question, whether by deliberate literary design or thematic restatement. The prophet-like-Moses passage parallels Deuteronomy 18, [[Acts 3]], and [[3 Nephi 20]]. The ordered images of wicked stubble and stalled calves closely parallel Malachi 4:1-2. The known sheep, one fold, one shepherd, feeding, and pasture language parallels John 10 and [[3 Nephi 15]], while also belonging to a broader biblical shepherd tradition. These are strong English textual correspondences. They do not establish direction of dependence, an original language, a date of composition, or ancient authorship; ancient synthesis, translation into familiar biblical idiom, common imagery, and modern KJV-mediated composition remain materially distinct explanations.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How does “both temporal and spiritual” differ from either a strictly literal or an exclusively symbolic reading?

- What does this chapter itself identify as the content of the “marvelous work,” and which later identifications require evidence from other texts?

- Why are Gentiles associated first with scattering Nephi's descendants and later with a work that nourishes them?

- What does verse 12 add to definitions of gathering focused only on land?

- Does the promise that the righteous will be saved describe physical protection, collective preservation, ultimate salvation, or some combination?

- Does verse 23 identify one historical institution, or does its catalog of corrupt motives support a broader moral application?

- What human agency is implied when verse 26 connects Satan's loss of power with the righteousness of the people?

- Why does Nephi conclude a prophecy about nations and judgment with repentance, obedience, and enduring to the end?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[1 Nephi 21]] and Isaiah 49 before this chapter to identify which details Nephi interprets and which he adds. Compare Jacob's related exposition in [[2 Nephi 6]] and the earlier treatments of Gentiles and Israel in [[1 Nephi 13]], [[1 Nephi 14]], and [[1 Nephi 15]]. Then compare verses 20-21 with Deuteronomy 18, [[Acts 3]], and [[3 Nephi 20]]; verses 15 and 24 with Malachi 4; and verse 25 with John 10, Ezekiel 34 and 37, and [[3 Nephi 15]]. Track exact wording separately from thematic similarity, and do not infer a direction or historical mechanism of dependence from resemblance alone.
