# 1 Nephi 5 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Nephi 5]] · My notes: [[1 Nephi 5 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Sariah complains against Lehi—Both rejoice over the return of their sons—They offer sacrifices—The plates of brass contain writings of Moses and the prophets—The plates identify Lehi as a descendant of Joseph—Lehi prophesies concerning his seed and the preservation of the plates. About 600–592 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Fear, reassurance, and return (vv. 1–8):** Sariah's complaint is recalled, Lehi's answer is given, and the sons' return produces her declaration of sure knowledge.

2. **Worship after deliverance (v. 9):** The family rejoices, offers sacrifices and burnt offerings, and gives thanks.

3. **The records searched (vv. 10–16):** Lehi identifies scriptural history, prophetic material, and genealogy on the plates.

4. **Prophecy and lasting value (vv. 17–22):** Lehi prophesies about his descendants and the plates; the narrator closes by explaining why carrying the records was wise.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter depicts [[Testimony]] as capable of growth through reassurance and experienced deliverance. Lehi's words are said to comfort Sariah (v. 6), and after the sons return she declares sure knowledge concerning both Lehi's command and the Lord's aid (vv. 7–8). The text does not isolate a single cause for her change.

[[Obedience]] includes more than finishing the immediate errand. The family obtains the records, searches them, recognizes their worth, and carries them onward. [[Records and Sacred Writings]] serve an intergenerational purpose because the commandments are to be preserved for the family's children (v. 21).

The family's sacrifices and thanksgiving connect deliverance with [[Gratitude]] and [[Sacrifice]] (v. 9). The text does not identify the officiant or give enough procedural detail to reconstruct the ritual setting.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Two verbal echoes help bind the reunion account together. Lehi is initially “filled with joy” (v. 1), and after the intervening account of fear and reassurance, “their joy was full” (v. 7). Likewise, Sariah twice says “I know of a surety” (v. 8), first concerning the command to Lehi and then concerning the protection and empowerment of her sons. The repetition marks a comprehensive change in her stated judgment of both the departure and the plates mission.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter repeatedly returns to preservation: the sons are delivered from Laban, Joseph preserves Jacob's household, Israel is led from Egypt, and the records preserve commandments for Lehi's children. This clustering supports a reading centered on [[Exodus and Deliverance]] and intergenerational continuity, although such themes also arise naturally from the Genesis and Exodus history being summarized.

A second movement runs from possession to use. Obtaining the plates does not end the episode: Lehi searches them, discovers genealogy and history, is filled with the Spirit, and prophesies. The narrative thus presents sacred records as objects to be studied and transmitted, not merely acquired.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Why does the narrator preserve Sariah's complaint in direct speech rather than report only the successful return?

What precisely changes Sariah's judgment: Lehi's reassurance, the sons' return, their report of divine aid, or the convergence of all three?

How does the Joseph story shape the family's understanding of its recent deliverance and future migration?

Why is the mission considered complete only after the records have been searched and their intergenerational value recognized?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
