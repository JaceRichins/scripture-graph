# 1 Nephi 2 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Nephi 2]] · My notes: [[1 Nephi 2 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
The chapter presents Lehi's departure as revelation followed by costly [[Obedience]]. He abandons property, travels with his [[Family]], worships through [[Sacrifice]] and [[Gratitude]], and urges Laman and Lemuel toward righteousness and steadfastness. The family's responses then divide: Laman and Lemuel reject Lehi's claims, while Nephi seeks God in [[Prayer]], experiences a softened heart, and speaks to Sam. The closing revelation introduces a promised land and a series of conditional consequences tied to obedience and rebellion (1 Nephi 2:19–24).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Command and departure (vv. 1–4):** Lehi receives a warning in a dream and leaves Jerusalem with his family and basic provisions.

2. **Journey, worship, and exhortation (vv. 5–10):** The family camps near the Red Sea; Lehi offers sacrifice and uses the river and valley as moral images.

3. **Laman and Lemuel's resistance (vv. 11–15):** Their material and theological objections are stated, followed by a forceful confrontation with Lehi.

4. **Nephi and Sam's responses (vv. 16–18):** Nephi prays, believes, tells Sam, and then intercedes for the resistant brothers.

5. **Promise and warning (vv. 19–24):** The Lord blesses Nephi and describes conditional consequences for obedience and rebellion.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter associates receptive faith with active seeking: Nephi desires to know, prays, and is described as having a softened heart (1 Nephi 2:16). His experience combines [[Prayer]], [[Faith]], [[Humility]], and [[Revelation]] rather than portraying belief as effortless.

The land promise is governed by conditional language. Prosperity and Nephi's future role are connected with keeping commandments, while separation from God's presence and conflict are connected with rebellion (1 Nephi 2:20–24). Because the chapter does not define "prosper," it should not automatically be reduced to wealth or ease.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Several contrasts organize the narrative: retained wealth versus abandoned wealth, murmuring versus prayer, outward compliance versus a softened heart, and rebellion versus commandment-keeping. Lehi's appeals to Laman and Lemuel also use the physical setting symbolically — the flowing river for movement toward righteousness and the valley for steadfastness (1 Nephi 2:9-10). The hopeful tone of these exhortations sits uneasily beside the murmuring reported immediately afterward (vv. 11-13); readers may notice an irony here, though the text never states that the brothers became "immovable" in unbelief — that connection is a reader's inference, not a stated device.

The Lord's blessing to Nephi ("Blessed art thou, Nephi," v. 19) echoes the opening address to Lehi ("Blessed art thou Lehi," v. 1). The repetition invites comparison between father's and son's revelations, though the two experiences differ in occasion and content, so the echo is suggestive rather than a stated claim of equivalence.

The departure, wilderness journey, sacrifice, and promised land also resemble an [[Exodus and Deliverance]] pattern. That comparison can illuminate the narrative, but the chapter never explicitly names Moses or declares itself a reenactment of the biblical exodus.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How should "prosper" be understood when the family's first act of obedience involves losing property and accepting hardship?
- What distinction does the narrative draw between the brothers doing what Lehi commands after their bodies shake (v. 14) and Nephi believing after his heart is softened (v. 16)?
- Does being "cut off from the presence of the Lord" describe spiritual alienation, geographic separation, covenant exclusion, or several of these? This chapter alone does not resolve the scope.
- How does Nephi's position as both participant and narrator shape the presentation of the family dispute?
- What does Nephi's description of himself as "exceedingly young, nevertheless...large in stature" (v. 16) suggest about his age and role at this point, and why does the record leave it ambiguous?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[1 Nephi 1]] for the visions, preaching, and threat that precede the departure. Compare [[1 Nephi 3]] for the continuing family conflict over difficult commands. [[2 Nephi 1]] revisits the promised-land and prosperity language, while [[2 Nephi 5]] narrates a later separation relevant to the warnings in 1 Nephi 2:21-24. The conditional "choice land" language of vv. 20-24 also recurs in Ether 2 and is later invoked by Alma (Alma 9); treat these as thematic echoes within the Book of Mormon's own text rather than as established evidence of a formal ancient covenant genre.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
