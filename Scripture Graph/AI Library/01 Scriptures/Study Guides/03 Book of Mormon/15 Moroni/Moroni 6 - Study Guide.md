# Moroni 6 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Moroni 6]] · My notes: [[Moroni 6 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Repentant persons are baptized and fellowshipped—Church members who repent are forgiven—Meetings are conducted by the power of the Holy Ghost. About A.D. 401–21.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Admission (vv. 1–3):** repentance, contrition, public witness, covenant identity, and enduring service.

2. **Incorporation (v. 4):** cleansing by the Holy Ghost, recorded membership, nourishment by God's word, watchful [[Prayer]], and reliance on Christ.

3. **Communal worship (vv. 5–6):** frequent meetings for [[Fasting]], prayer, mutual spiritual concern, and the [[Sacrament]].

4. **Discipline and restoration (vv. 7–8):** witnessed accountability, possible removal, and renewed forgiveness whenever repentance is sincere.

5. **Spiritual direction (v. 9):** the Holy Ghost guides the forms taken by the meeting.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter refuses to separate covenant commitment from [[Grace]]. Candidates must show repentance and determination to serve, but members are then told to rely "alone upon the merits of Christ" (v. 4). Their own resolve matters, yet Christ remains the ground of faith.

Membership is also relational rather than merely numerical. Taking names enables people to be remembered and nourished. The repeated phrase "meet together oft" links care for souls with sacramental remembrance, suggesting that fellowship, teaching, prayer, and the [[Sacrament]] belong to one continuing practice of discipleship.

Discipline is bounded by mercy. Verse 7 treats unresolved iniquity seriously; verse 8 prevents exclusion from becoming irrevocable by extending forgiveness as often as repentance is accompanied by real intent.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated restrictive formulas "not ... save" and "none ... save" in verses 1–3 give the baptismal requirements unusual rhetorical force. These are presented as community-recognized signs of repentance, not as casual preferences.

The expression "author and the finisher of their faith" in verse 4 closely parallels [[Hebrews 12]]. This is a clear English intertext, but the wording alone cannot determine whether it reflects translation into biblical language, conscious scriptural allusion, or modern literary dependence.

Verse 8 uses the passive "they were forgiven." Because the agent is not named, interpreters should avoid forcing a choice between divine forgiveness and restoration by the church when the immediate context can accommodate both.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 5 and 6 begin with nearly the same clause—"the church did meet together oft" and "they did meet together oft." The repetition unites two dimensions of worship: caring for the welfare of souls and remembering Jesus through bread and wine.

The chapter also forms a movement from boundaries to belonging: requirements guard entry, names secure remembrance, meetings sustain fellowship, discipline protects the community, forgiveness restores, and the Spirit directs the whole. This coherence supports reading the chapter as an integrated ecclesiology, though it does not by itself establish when or how that system originated.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Literary And Institutional Coherence *(tentative)*
> The progression from admission requirements to membership records, pastoral nourishment, worship, discipline, forgiveness, and Spirit-led meetings is strong evidence that the chapter intentionally presents an integrated model of church community rather than a miscellaneous list of rules.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.8 · Coherence observable; evidentiary force overstated

> [!evidence] Textual Parallel Across Restoration-Era Texts *(tentative)*
> The baptismal prerequisites in Moroni 6:1-3 (broken heart and contrite spirit, witnessed repentance, willingness to take the name of Christ with a determination to serve him to the end) closely resemble baptismal language later recorded in D&C 20, a revelation Joseph Smith received shortly after completing the Book of Mormon translation; the index's mechanical parallel detection independently flags meaningful overlap between Moroni 6 and D&C 20 (4 verses).
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.8 · Overlap verified; historical framing disputed

> [!evidence] Textual/Legal Parallel *(tentative)*
> The requirement of "three witnesses" to condemn a member for iniquity (Moroni 6:7) is consistent with the broader biblical legal principle requiring multiple witnesses to establish a matter (compare Deuteronomy 19:15; Matthew 18:16), which some readers cite as a mark of Hebraic legal patterning in the text.
> — strength 0.2 · confidence 0.6 · General parallel accepted; historical significance disputed

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Why does Moroni place reliance on Christ's merits immediately after demanding visible fruits and determination from baptismal candidates? How might that sequence correct both permissiveness and self-righteousness?

What practical responsibilities follow from taking members' names "that they might be remembered"? Does recordkeeping fulfill the verse if no nourishment follows?

How should the strictness of verse 7 be held together with the repeated forgiveness of verse 8? What safeguards are explicit in the passage, and what safeguards should not be read into it?

What does verse 9 imply about the relationship between recognizable meeting activities and responsiveness to revelation?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
- Compare Moroni 6:1–3 with [[D&C 20]], especially verse 37, and Moroni 6:6 with verse 75. The English correspondences are extensive, but they do not by themselves determine direction of dependence, authorship, or revelatory mechanism.
- Read [[Moroni 4]], [[Moroni 5]], and [[3 Nephi 18]] alongside Moroni 6:6 to connect the sacrament prayers, Jesus's instructions, and recurring church practice.
- Compare Moroni 6:7–8 with [[Mosiah 26]] for another treatment of discipline, removal for refusal to repent, and forgiveness as often as repentance occurs.
