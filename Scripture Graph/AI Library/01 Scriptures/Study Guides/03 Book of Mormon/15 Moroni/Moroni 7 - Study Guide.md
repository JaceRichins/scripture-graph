# Moroni 7 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Moroni 7]] · My notes: [[Moroni 7 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> An invitation is given to enter into the rest of the Lord—Pray with real intent—The Spirit of Christ enables men to know good from evil—Satan persuades men to deny Christ and do evil—The prophets manifest the coming of Christ—By faith, miracles are wrought and angels minister—Men should hope for eternal life and cleave unto charity. About A.D. 401–21.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Setting and audience (vv. 1–4):** Moroni introduces Mormon's synagogue sermon, and Mormon addresses peaceful followers of Christ.
2. **Works, character, and intent (vv. 5–11):** Gifts and prayers without real intent are not counted as righteousness.
3. **Judging good and evil (vv. 12–19):** The Spirit or light of Christ supplies the governing test, accompanied by a warning against wrongful judgment.
4. **Faith and laying hold on good (vv. 20–39):** Angelic and prophetic testimony leads to faith; Christ's mercy, miracles, and angelic ministry remain operative.
5. **Hope (vv. 40–42):** Hope looks through Christ's atonement and resurrection toward eternal life.
6. **Meekness and charity (vv. 43–48):** Faith and hope require humility and charity, and the sermon ends with prayer for transforming love.

The repeated expression "lay hold upon every good thing" helps connect the discernment section to the discussion of faith.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter treats [[Faith]], [[Hope]], and [[Charity]] as mutually related. Faith lays hold upon good things and trusts Christ; hope has the defined object of resurrection to eternal life through the [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]]; charity is enduring Christlike love that must fill the disciple. [[Humility]] is not an optional addition: without meekness and lowliness of heart, Mormon calls faith and hope vain.

Mormon also joins moral discernment to discipleship. The test is directional and Christ-centered: influences from God invite good, love and service toward God, and belief in Christ. Yet the warning against judging wrongfully and the command to search diligently discourage treating one's first impression as automatically infallible.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
"Real intent" occurs in the discussion of both giving and praying, placing inward purpose alongside outward action. "Inviteth and enticeth" describes competing moral influence rather than coercion. The paired expressions "Spirit of Christ" and "light of Christ" occur in the same explanation of discernment, supporting the link to [[Light of Christ]].

"The pure love of Christ" is grammatically open to more than one nuance: Christ's love for others, love directed toward Christ, or love resembling Christ's. The surrounding prayer to be filled with love and become like him favors a transformative, Christlike sense but does not eliminate every nuance.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Moroni 7:45-46 closely tracks the wording and clause order of [[1 Corinthians 13]]'s description of charity, a strong English-level parallel whose historical mechanism (translation idiom, direct literary use, or independent revelation) remains disputed and unsettled by the parallel alone. Moroni 7:48 ("we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is") similarly parallels 1 John 3:2. Moroni 7:18's teaching that "with that same judgment which ye judge ye shall also be judged" echoes Matthew 7:2 and the Nephite Sermon at the Temple (3 Nephi 14:2). Mormon also uses a vivid natural image—a bitter versus a good fountain (v.11)—to argue that one cannot simultaneously serve Christ and the devil.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] English-Language Intertextuality
> Moroni 7:45-46 has a close verbal and sequential correspondence with the description of charity in 1 Corinthians 13:4-8. This strongly supports a literary relationship at the level of the English texts, while the direction and historical mechanism of that relationship remain disputed.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 0.9 · strong parallel, verse-range needs tightening · [[English-Language Intertextuality in Moroni 7|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Narrative/Contextual Inference *(tentative)*
> Mormon's cessationism polemic (miracles, angels, and the Holy Ghost's power have not ceased, and their absence signals unbelief rather than divine withdrawal of possibility) functions rhetorically within the narrative as commentary on the Nephites' own final apostasy and destruction, since Moroni compiled and preserved this sermon while witnessing the collapse of Nephite society and its associated loss of faith.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · speculative narrative inference, honestly labeled

> [!evidence] Textual/Philological *(tentative)*
> The near-verbatim overlap between Moroni 7:45 and 1 Corinthians 13:4-7 (KJV) is verifiable textual evidence of literary dependence between the two passages, given Joseph Smith's documented use of KJV language throughout the Book of Mormon translation; it is disputed among scholars whether this reflects (a) direct translation influence from a KJV Mormon possessed/echoed in translation, (b) authentic ancient teaching independently paralleling Paul via shared Hebraic/Christian moral vocabulary, or (c) a modern compositional choice.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.7 · disputed, unsourced historical assertion
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does "real intent" (vv.6-9) mean in practice, and how does it relate to the outward "good works" test stated in v.5?
- Mormon says the Spirit of Christ lets every person judge good from evil with "perfect knowledge" (vv.16-17) — how does this square with his own warning against judging wrongfully (v.18) and the command to "search diligently" (v.19)?
- Why does Mormon tie the cessation of miracles and angels so tightly to unbelief (vv.37-38) rather than to changing dispensational needs?
- Does "the pure love of Christ" (v.47) mean Christ's love for us, our love for Christ, or a Christlike love we receive and extend — and how does the closing prayer to be "filled with this love" (v.48) shape that reading?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
