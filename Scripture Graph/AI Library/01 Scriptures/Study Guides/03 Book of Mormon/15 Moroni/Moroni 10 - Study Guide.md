## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A restrained outline is:

1. Final address and sealing of the record (10:1-2)
2. Invitation to remember, ponder, ask, and receive by the Holy Ghost (10:3-7)
3. Catalog and theology of spiritual gifts (10:8-19)
4. Faith, hope, charity, and the consequences of unbelief (10:20-26)
5. Witness from the dead and fulfillment of prophecy (10:27-29)
6. Come to Christ; covenant restoration, grace, and sanctification (10:30-33)
7. Farewell, paradise, resurrection, and judgment (10:34)

This outline follows explicit topic changes and repeated exhortations; it should not be treated as proof of a hidden formal pattern.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Moroni's closing doctrine is strongly Christ-centered. Knowledge is attributed to the power of the Holy Ghost (10:4-7), good gifts come through Christ and the Spirit (10:8-19), and salvation requires faith, hope, and charity (10:20-23). In 10:32-33, discipleship and grace are coordinated rather than set against each other: the reader is commanded to reject ungodliness and love God, while perfection, sanctification, remission, and holiness are attributed to God's grace and Christ's blood. The passage therefore supports both serious moral response and dependence on divine action, without supplying a complete systematic account of their relationship.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The phrase “if these things are not true” (10:4) is a negative question. Its practical force is an invitation to ask concerning the truth of the received record, but its precise scope remains unstated. Verse 5 broadens the discussion with the maxim that truth may be known by the Holy Ghost; it should not automatically be read as saying that every truth claim must be resolved through one identical kind of spiritual experience.

“Severally” in 10:17 means individually or separately in context: gifts are distributed among persons according to divine will, not necessarily all given in identical form to everyone.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter interweaves several verified intertextual echoes with a KJV-era vocabulary. Verses 8-17 organize spiritual gifts along much the same template as [[1 Corinthians 12]] — including near-verbatim language ('severally... as he will,' cf. 1 Corinthians 12:11) — while adapting rather than reproducing it: verse 14 substitutes 'the beholding of angels and ministering spirits' for Paul's 'discerning of spirits,' though the surrounding sequence of gifts is largely preserved. Verse 19's 'the same yesterday, today, and forever' matches the three-part formula of Hebrews 13:8. Verse 31 recombines Isaianic restoration language — 'awake, and arise from the dust,' 'put on thy beautiful garments,' and 'strengthen thy stakes' echo Isaiah 52:1-2 and 54:2 — though Moroni reassigns some epithets between 'Jerusalem' and 'daughter of Zion' rather than quoting either passage intact, and 'enlarge thy borders' resembles Deuteronomic covenant language (e.g., Deuteronomy 12:20) as much as Isaiah 54. Verse 27's image of Moroni's words 'speaking out of the dust' resonates with the broader 'voice from the dust' idiom found elsewhere in the Book of Mormon (compare 2 Nephi 33:13's 'voice of one crying from the dust') and in Isaiah 29:4 — a recurring motif of a buried record addressing a future audience, though the trope is common enough in prophetic literature that a single source should not be assumed. Verses 20-22 build faith, hope, and charity into a cumulative, step-building chain, though describing it as a fully reversed formal sorites overstates what vv.21-22 actually do. These parallels establish real textual relationships in the English text; they do not by themselves settle questions of composition, translation, or authorship, and are equally consistent with ancient prophetic reuse of earlier scripture or a translator's/author's reliance on familiar biblical idiom.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Verbal/Textual Echo
> Verse 31's call to 'awake, and arise from the dust, O Jerusalem...put on thy beautiful garments, O daughter of Zion; and strengthen thy stakes and enlarge thy borders' verbally echoes two distinct Isaiah passages: Isaiah 52:1-2 ('put on thy beautiful garments, O Jerusalem'; 'shake thyself from the dust; arise...O Jerusalem') and Isaiah 54:2 ('strengthen thy stakes'), which Moroni appears to weave together and re-apply, notably shifting which figure ('Jerusalem' vs. 'daughter of Zion') receives the 'beautiful garments' language relative to Isaiah 52:1.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation

> [!evidence] Verbal/Textual Echo
> Verse 19's description of Christ as 'the same yesterday, today, and forever' verbally matches the formula in Hebrews 13:8 ('Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever'), an identical three-part temporal formula applied to Christ's unchanging nature.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation · [[Verbal/Textual Echo in Moroni 10|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual Intertextuality
> Moroni 10:8-17 closely parallels the organization and several items of the spiritual-gift catalog in 1 Corinthians 12:4-11: diverse gifts arise from the same Spirit and include wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, tongues, and interpretation.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation · [[Textual Intertextuality in Moroni 10|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Scriptural Allusion
> Moroni 10:31 combines recognizable language from Isaiah's calls for Jerusalem or Zion to awake, arise from dust, put on beautiful garments, enlarge her borders, and strengthen her stakes.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation · [[Scriptural Allusion in Moroni 10|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual/Literary Comparison *(tentative)*
> Moroni's list is not a verbatim copy of Paul's: verse 14 lists 'the beholding of angels and ministering spirits' where 1 Corinthians 12:10 instead lists 'discerning of spirits,' a distinct gift. The lists overlap substantially in content and structure but diverge in specific terms and ordering.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.6 · partially inaccurate as worded

> [!evidence] Recurring Motif / Verbal Echo *(tentative)*
> Verse 27's image of Moroni's words being read 'like as one crying from the dead, yea, even as one speaking out of the dust' draws on the same 'voice from the dust' idiom found elsewhere in Isaiah-influenced Book of Mormon passages (e.g., Isaiah 29:4's 'thy speech shall be low out of the dust...whisper out of the dust'), forming a recurring motif of ancient/buried records speaking to a future audience.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.6 · disputed

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does 'real intent' add to sincerity and faith in Moroni 10:4, and how would a reader recognize its absence?
- How broadly should 'these things' (10:3-4) be understood — the whole record, its central claims, or something narrower — and what does the passage itself leave unspecified?
- Why does Moroni tie the loss of spiritual gifts specifically to unbelief (10:19, 24), and how could that explanation be distinguished from other possible causes in a given case?
- How do divine grace and commanded human response interact in 10:32-33, and does the text resolve their relative priority?
- What might be signified by Moroni substituting 'the beholding of angels and ministering spirits' (10:14) for Paul's 'discerning of spirits' (1 Corinthians 12:10) while keeping the surrounding list order intact?
- What is gained by Moroni's combination of imagery from Isaiah 52 and Isaiah 54 in verse 31, including his reassignment of epithets between Jerusalem and the daughter of Zion?
