# Alma 50 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Alma 50]] · My notes: [[Alma 50 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Moroni fortifies the lands of the Nephites—They build many new cities—Wars and destructions befell the Nephites in the days of their wickedness and abominations—Morianton and his dissenters are defeated by Teancum—Nephihah dies, and his son Pahoran fills the judgment seat. About 72–67 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Fortifications and territorial strategy (vv. 1–16):** earthworks, timber defenses, towers, removal of Lamanite positions, border garrisons, settlement, and the founding of Moroni, Nephihah, and Lehi.
2. **Prosperity interpreted covenantally (vv. 17–24):** population growth, wealth, happiness, and peace are followed by the narrator's application of the conditional promise given to Lehi and an explanation of earlier wars.
3. **The Morianton crisis (vv. 25–36):** a boundary dispute escalates to an armed threat and attempted flight northward; Morianton beats a maidservant, whose report alerts Moroni; Teancum intercepts the group; the survivors covenant to keep peace and are restored.
4. **Records and government (vv. 37–40):** Nephihah dies, the earlier transfer of sacred records to Helaman is recalled, and Pahoran assumes the judgment-seat by oath.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's theology of prosperity is explicitly conditional, not an unconditional promise of wealth or safety. Alma 50:19–22 applies Lehi's words to collective Nephite history, linking [[Obedience]] with prosperity and rebellion with loss. Because this is the narrator's theological interpretation of history, it should not be turned into a universal claim that every faithful individual will always avoid suffering.

The settlement of the Morianton crisis joins accountability with restoration. Armed resistance is defeated, yet the prisoners are returned to their land after covenanting to keep the peace (vv. 35–36). This makes the episode relevant to [[Justice and Mercy]], though the text does not reveal how freely the covenant was made.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Alma 50:32 contains a visible correction: “the people who were in the land Bountiful, or rather Moroni, feared.” The phrase preserves a narrator who revises the subject while speaking or composing. It is a real literary observation, but competing explanations include dictation, scribal or editorial correction, and deliberate imitation of record-like narration; the wording alone cannot decide among them.

The repetition of “commence” in verse 1 and “caused” throughout verses 1–15 gives the opening a procedural quality. Moroni is repeatedly presented as initiating coordinated work carried out by armies and inhabitants.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter moves from extensive preparation against external enemies to a crisis arising among the Nephites themselves. Verses 19–22 attribute earlier wars to wrongs “among themselves,” and verses 25–35 then narrate an internal dispute that threatens the peace. This movement provides useful context for the political division in [[Alma 51]], though it does not prove that the sequence was deliberately constructed as irony.

An unnamed maidservant occupies a pivotal place in the plot. After Morianton beats her, she flees to Moroni and discloses the planned migration, prompting the interception. The abuse therefore has public consequences within the narrative, but the text offers no explicit general teaching about abuse and does not establish that the violence and Morianton's political ambitions arise from a single stated motive.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does Moroni's occupation of the east wilderness read solely as defense, or does the language of driving out inhabitants and possessing land also invite questions about expansion?
- What legal process, if any, lay behind the narrator's judgment that the people of Lehi “were not in the wrong”?
- How should readers balance Moroni's stated concern for [[Freedom and Liberty]] with his use of military force against a migrating dissident population?
- Why does the record pause during Pahoran's accession to explain that sacred records had passed to Helaman rather than to the chief judge?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
