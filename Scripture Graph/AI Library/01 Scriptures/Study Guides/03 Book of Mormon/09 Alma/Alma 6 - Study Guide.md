# Alma 6 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Alma 6]] · My notes: [[Alma 6 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The Church in Zarahemla is cleansed and set in order—Alma goes to Gideon to preach. About 83 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Ordering the Zarahemla church (vv. 1–4):** Alma ordains priests and elders (v.1); repentant nonmembers are baptized and received (v.2); persistently unrepentant, prideful members are rejected and their names blotted out (v.3); "the order of the church" is established (v.4).
2. **Access and intercession (vv. 5–6):** hearing the word of God remains open to all (v.5), while the church gathers, fasts, and prays for the welfare of those who do not know God (v.6).
3. **Transition to Gideon (vv. 7–8):** Alma travels east of the river Sidon to the valley of Gideon — named for the judge slain by Nehor (see [[Alma 1]]) — and begins preaching there, grounding his message in revealed truth from his fathers, the spirit of prophecy, and testimony of Jesus Christ (v.8).

The chapter functions as a hinge: it completes the Zarahemla narrative from [[Alma 4]]-[[Alma 5]] and opens the ministry continued in [[Alma 7]].
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Priesthood ordination by the laying on of hands**, "according to the order of God" (v.1) — see [[Priesthood]].
- **Membership tied to ongoing repentance, not a one-time event**: baptism admits the repentant (v.2), but unrepented pride can still result in a member's name being "blotted out" (v.3). See [[Repentance]], [[Pride]], [[Humility]].
- **Discipline and invitation coexist**: exclusion from membership (v.3) does not mean exclusion from hearing the word, which remains open to all (v.5).
- **Intercessory fasting and prayer for those outside the faith** "who knew not God" (v.6), a communal rather than purely individual discipline — see [[Fasting]] and [[Prayer]].
- Alma's preaching in Gideon is grounded in testimony of the future redemptive mission of [[Jesus Christ (Gospel Topic)]] and in the spirit of [[Prophecy]].

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
In verse 5, “liberal” is best read through its own explanatory clause: none were denied the privilege of assembling to hear the word. The verse therefore concerns broad access or generosity, not a modern political position. “Blotted out” and “not numbered” in verse 3 evoke recordkeeping, but the text does not explain the physical form of the record or every consequence of removal.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Alma 6 is transitional rather than sermonic. Verse 7 preserves a brief etiological note tying the place name Gideon to the man slain by Nehor's sword, recalling the earlier narrative in [[Alma 1]] — a real internal connection, though the text does not indicate whether this is a deliberate cross-reference or simply a repeated biographical detail. The closing line, "And thus it is written. Amen" (v.8), marks the end of this narrative unit before the sermon recorded in [[Alma 7]]; whether it reflects a genuine editorial or source-boundary marker cannot be determined from the text alone.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Institutional Detail
> The combination of designated officers, ordination by laying on hands, procedures for admission and exclusion, and a numbered record is strong internal evidence that the narrative depicts a formally regulated religious community.

> [!evidence] Textual/Theological Parallel *(tentative)*
> The phrase "their names were blotted out" in Alma 6:3 uses covenant-curse imagery attested in the Hebrew Bible (e.g., a name being blotted out of a book), which is consistent with, but does not independently prove, the Book of Mormon's claim to be an ancient Israelite record.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How should the openness of verse 5 shape our reading of the disciplinary action in verse 3? What distinguishes attending an assembly, belonging to the church, and being "numbered among the righteous"?
- Does the "blotted out" language of verse 3 refer only to a local membership roll, or is it meant to evoke the "book of life" theology raised in [[Alma 5]] (5:57-58)? The text does not say.
- Why does verse 6 direct fasting and prayer specifically toward those who "knew not God" rather than the material needs of the poor within the church?
- What does verse 8 claim for Alma's authority, and which details of that authority remain unstated?
