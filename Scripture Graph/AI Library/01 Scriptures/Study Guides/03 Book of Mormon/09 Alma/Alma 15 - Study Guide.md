# Alma 15 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Alma 15]] · My notes: [[Alma 15 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Alma and Amulek go to Sidom and establish a church—Alma heals Zeezrom, who joins the Church—Many are baptized, and the Church prospers—Alma and Amulek go to Zarahemla. About 81 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Displaced believers and unresolved loss (vv. 1–2):** Alma and Amulek meet those expelled from Ammonihah, and the events involving their families and the missionaries' deliverance are related.
2. **Zeezrom's restoration (vv. 3–12):** guilt and a burning fever give way to courage, professed faith, healing, baptism, and preaching.
3. **Contrasting communal responses (vv. 13–17):** a church is established at Sidom, many are baptized, and the people begin to humble themselves; Ammonihah remains hard-hearted and unrepentant.
4. **Care for Amulek and chronological close (vv. 16–19):** after noting Amulek's material and relational losses, the narrative records Alma housing and strengthening him, then closes the tenth year of the judges.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Faith, mercy, and healing:** Alma connects possible healing with belief in Christ's power and redemption, then petitions God to have mercy and heal Zeezrom according to his faith (vv. 6–10). The passage presents faith as an expressly named condition, not as leverage that controls God. It does not imply that continuing illness proves deficient faith. See [[Faith]], [[Miracles]], and [[Prayer]].
- **Conversion expressed through covenant and conduct:** Zeezrom's story does not end with recovery. Alma baptizes him, and he begins preaching (v. 12). Repentance is not explicitly named for Zeezrom, but his changed allegiance and conduct support a conversion reading. See [[Conversion]], [[Baptism]], and [[Missionary Work]].
- **Contrasting responses to repentance:** Ammonihah remains hard-hearted and rejects repentance, whereas people at Sidom begin to humble themselves and gather for worship (vv. 13–17). See [[Repentance]] and [[Pride]].
- **The cost of discipleship and care for sufferers:** Amulek forsakes valuables and is rejected by former friends, his father, and kindred. Alma then takes him home, ministers to him, and strengthens him in the Lord (vv. 16–18). See [[Adversity]] and [[Service]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Zeezrom's story is organized through linked reversals: he supposes Alma and Amulek dead, then learns they live; his heart takes courage while his mind remains sore; he lies very low, then leaps to his feet; and the former opponent of the preaching becomes a preacher. The dialogue in verses 6–10 slows the narrative around one question about belief, Zeezrom's affirmations, Alma's conditional declaration, and a prayer for divine mercy.

The combination of taking Zeezrom by the hand and his subsequently leaping to his feet and walking resembles the healing sequence and English diction of [[Acts 3]]. The resonance is worth noting, but the wording alone cannot determine whether it reflects KJV influence on translation, deliberate literary dependence, a conventional healing pattern, or another relationship.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What changes in Zeezrom when he learns that Alma and Amulek are alive, and what changes only after his healing?
- How does Alma's petition for mercy shape the relationship among faith, Christ's redemption, and healing in verses 6–10?
- What does the contrast between Sidom and Ammonihah suggest about the communities' differing responses without erasing possible diversity within either population?
- Why might Alma's quiet act of housing and strengthening Amulek appear after the public healing and mass baptisms?
- How do the continuing tribulations of the displaced believers and Amulek caution against assuming that faith immediately removes every consequence of violence or discipleship?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
- Read [[Alma 14]] to identify what Alma and Amulek witnessed, what happened to the expelled believers' families, and what 'their power of deliverance' most naturally denotes in Alma 15:2.
- Compare Zeezrom's reversal with [[Mosiah 27]], noting both the shared movement toward preaching and the different agents, sequence, and circumstances.
- Compare Alma 15:6,11 with [[Acts 3]] at the level of English wording. Treat the resemblance as a literary observation while keeping its origin and historical significance unresolved.
