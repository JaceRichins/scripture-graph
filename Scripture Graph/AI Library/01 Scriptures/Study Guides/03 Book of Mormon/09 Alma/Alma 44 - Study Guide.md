# Alma 44 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Alma 44]] · My notes: [[Alma 44 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Moroni commands the Lamanites to make a covenant of peace or be destroyed—Zerahemnah rejects the offer, and the battle resumes—Moroni’s armies defeat the Lamanites. About 74–73 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Moroni's ultimatum and theological explanation (vv. 1–7):** Moroni offers life and release if the Lamanites surrender their weapons and cease making war.
2. **Failed weapons-only surrender (vv. 8–11):** Zerahemnah rejects both the oath and Moroni's explanation of the victory. Moroni returns the weapons and reiterates the oath-or-death terms.
3. **Attack, scalp sign, and divided response (vv. 12–16):** Zerahemnah attacks; a soldier wounds and scalps him, then turns the scalp into a warning. Many covenant and depart, while Zerahemnah rallies the remainder.
4. **Renewed battle and conclusion (vv. 17–24):** the remaining force suffers severe losses, accepts the covenant, and is released. The dead are cast into Sidon, the armies return home, and Alma's record closes.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Moroni interprets preservation through [[Faith]] in Christ and describes divine protection as conditional upon continued faithfulness (vv. 3–5). Zerahemnah rejects that interpretation and credits Nephite cunning, breastplates, and shields (v. 9). The narrator later notes the tactical effect of exposed Lamanite bodies (v. 18). Material preparation is therefore part of the narrated causation, although the text does not independently prove or disprove Moroni's providential interpretation.

The chapter also develops [[Covenants]] through a political covenant of peace. Zerahemnah treats the oath as sufficiently serious that he initially refuses to make a promise he expects to break. Because the agreement is demanded under threat of death, it should be distinguished from voluntary religious covenant-making.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Moroni's speech accumulates religious and civic loyalties—faith, worship, church, wives and children, liberty, lands, country, and the sacred word of God—before appealing to the enemy's desire for life (vv. 5–6). Verse 8 lists Zerahemnah's “sword,” “cimeter,” and “bow,” but the chapter does not define the precise physical form intended by “cimeter”; historical or cultural arguments require evidence beyond this occurrence.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The severed scalp becomes an enacted comparison: as the chief's scalp fell, his followers are warned that they too will fall unless they surrender (vv. 13–14). The narrator later describes their rapid defeat as occurring “even as the soldier of Moroni had prophesied” (v. 18). This establishes the narrator's retrospective label but does not by itself prove that the warning came through supernatural revelation.

The chapter also preserves opposing explanations of victory. Moroni claims divine deliverance; Zerahemnah denies it and credits equipment and cunning. This creates literary tension, although the subsequent defeat may be intended to discredit Zerahemnah rather than leave both explanations equally authoritative.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does Moroni's promise that the faithful will not be destroyed concern collective survival rather than protection of every individual (vv. 4, 21)?
- How should restraint and coercion be distinguished in Moroni's successive offers of peace?
- What function does Zerahemnah's material explanation serve within a narrative that also presents Moroni's providential interpretation?
- Does verse 18 call the soldier's warning prophecy because it was revealed, or because events fulfilled an immediate battlefield prediction?
- In context, does “we will end the conflict” in verse 10 announce peace or the decisive resumption of combat?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the equipment descriptions in [[Alma 43]] with the competing explanations in Alma 44:3–9 and the narrator's tactical comment in verse 18. Then compare this coerced surrender covenant with the voluntary weapons covenant in [[Alma 24]] and Moroni's later liberty language in [[Alma 46]]. These comparisons clarify the chapter's treatment of [[War and Peace]], [[Freedom and Liberty]], and [[Covenants]] without assuming that every covenant scene serves the same function.
