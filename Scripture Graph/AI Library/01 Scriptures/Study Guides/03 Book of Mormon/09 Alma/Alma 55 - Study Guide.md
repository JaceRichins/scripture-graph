# Alma 55 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Alma 55]] · My notes: [[Alma 55 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Moroni refuses to exchange prisoners—The Lamanite guards are enticed to become drunk, and the Nephite prisoners are freed—The city of Gid is taken without bloodshed. About 63–62 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Refusal and resolve (vv. 1–3):** Moroni condemns Ammoron's cause and rejects an exchange that would increase Ammoron's power.
2. **Deception and intoxication (vv. 4–15):** Laman approaches Gid with a false escape account and strong wine; the guards drink until deeply asleep.
3. **Arming and encirclement (vv. 16–22):** The prisoners are armed within while Moroni's soldiers surround the Lamanite force from without.
4. **Surrender and liberation (vv. 23–24):** The Lamanites plead for mercy, surrender their weapons, and are captured; the Nephite prisoners are freed.
5. **Consolidation and vigilance (vv. 25–32):** Captured Lamanites strengthen Gid, and the Nephites resist later wine-based snares.
6. **Next objective (vv. 33–35):** The narrative turns to the fortified city of Morianton.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter contributes to [[War and Peace]] through narrative rather than a complete theory of warfare. Alma 55:18–24 portrays restraint favorably: Moroni possesses the opportunity to kill incapacitated enemies but instead uses his advantage to secure surrender and liberate captives. This supports an episode-specific preference for avoiding unnecessary bloodshed; it does not establish pacifism or approve every tactic in the operation.

The references to avoiding injustice and pleading for mercy make the account relevant to [[Justice and Mercy]]. Yet mercy here means sparing the surrendering force from immediate death, not release from captivity: the prisoners are subsequently assigned labor.

In Alma 55:31–32, remembrance of God during [[Adversity]] accompanies practical vigilance. No miraculous detection is reported. The passage associates faithfulness with caution, while leaving unresolved the ethics of testing potentially dangerous wine on prisoners.

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The Gid account contrasts ability with desire: Moroni's forces could kill the drunken Lamanites, but the narrator says Moroni desires the rescue of his people rather than bloodshed. The unrealized possibility makes his restraint narratively visible.

Wine also links the main episode with the campaign summary. It first enables the Nephite operation; later, Lamanite forces make similar attempts using poison or drunkenness, but Nephite caution defeats them. This recurrence gives the chapter coherence, although it does not establish conscious imitation, symbolic meaning, a particular source history, historicity, or date of composition.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does the narrator's approval of Moroni's rescue and restraint imply approval of the entire stratagem, or only of his refusal to kill incapacitated enemies?
- How should verse 19's concern about injustice be weighed alongside deception, the arming of children, compulsory prisoner labor, and testing suspect wine on captives?
- What does “remember the Lord” add to verses 31–32 when the actions described are caution and practical testing?
- Does “reclaim their rights and their privileges” establish an objective political fact, or express the narrator's Nephite-centered interpretation?
- What important information about the ages, choices, and combat roles of the armed women and children is not supplied?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
