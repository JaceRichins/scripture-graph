# Alma 43 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Alma 43]] · My notes: [[Alma 43 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Alma and his sons preach the word—The Zoramites and other Nephite dissenters become Lamanites—The Lamanites come against the Nephites in war—Moroni arms the Nephites with defensive armor—The Lord reveals to Alma the strategy of the Lamanites—The Nephites defend their homes, liberties, families, and religion—The armies of Moroni and Lehi surround the Lamanites. About 74 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter moves through six stages:

1. **Transition from preaching to war (vv. 1–3).**
2. **Coalitions and motives (vv. 4–17).** Dissenters strengthen and lead the Lamanite coalition; the Nephites state defensive aims.
3. **Armor redirects the invasion (vv. 18–22).** The Lamanites avoid Jershon after seeing Moroni's preparations.
4. **Intelligence and deployment (vv. 23–35).** Spies and Alma's revelation disclose the route; Moroni positions forces around Sidon and Riplah.
5. **Battle, crisis, and renewed morale (vv. 36–50).** Armor reduces Nephite losses but does not prevent them; remembrance and prayer help the Nephites stand.
6. **Encirclement and restraint (vv. 51–54).** The larger army is trapped, and Moroni halts the bloodshed.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents a conditional defense ethic within its own narrative. It approves defending [[Family]], land, worship, rights, and [[Freedom and Liberty]]. Verses 46–47 attribute to the Lord sayings about the first and second offense and defending families even unto bloodshed, though the precise source and wider scope of those sayings are not identified here. Moroni’s order to halt the bloodshed once the enemy is contained adds an instance of restraint, but the chapter does not supply a complete universal theory of war.

The people of Ammon embody a different obligation. Their [[Covenants]] prevent them from bearing arms, yet they provide substantial material support while the Nephites protect them (vv. 11–13). Alma 43 explicitly describes the people of Ammon’s covenant; it does not describe the Nephites’ corresponding protection as a parallel covenant.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The account repeatedly contrasts attributed motives. Zerahemnah is said to preserve hatred and stir anger in pursuit of power (vv. 6–8), while Moroni recalls his soldiers to homes, family, liberty, worship, and freedom from bondage (vv. 45–50). This is the moral contrast presented by the Nephite narrator, whose account does not independently establish every combatant’s actual motives.

Material contrasts also shape the battle: armor and exposed bodies, numerical superiority and tactical containment, and fierce courage on both sides. The striking simile “they did fight like dragons” marks the Lamanites’ most forceful resistance (v. 44). The repeated phrase “work of death” and Moroni’s closing command to stop shedding blood can be read as giving the account a grim frame, though the phrase may be conventional war diction and the final order also prepares for negotiation in [[Alma 44]].
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Narrative Temporal Association
> The narrative associates united prayer for freedom with an immediate reversal in battle, stating that the Lamanites began to flee in the same hour that the Nephites cried to the Lord.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 1.0 · temporal association explicit; causation disputed · [[Narrative Temporal Association in Alma 43|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal Ethical Framing
> Alma 43 provides strong internal textual evidence for a bounded defensive-war ethic in this narrative: defense is justified by protection of families, liberty, land, and worship; the Nephites are told not to initiate repeated offenses; and Moroni stops the killing when the encircled enemy is terrified.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · strong internal framing; wider scope disputed · [[Internal Ethical Framing in Alma 43|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual-Cultural Correlation *(tentative)*
> Book of Mormon apologetic scholarship has proposed that the described contrast (quilted/thick-clothing and shield-based Nephite armor vs. largely unarmored Lamanite combatants) is broadly consistent with attested Mesoamerican military material culture, in which quilted cotton armor (ichcahuipilli) and wooden/hide shields were used by some combatants while others fought lightly clad.
> — strength 0.2 · confidence 0.5 · believing scholarship; nondiagnostic correlation
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- The narrator has direct access to the attributed designs of Zerahemnah and the desires of the Nephites. How should readers distinguish the narrative's moral judgment from an independently recoverable account of each combatant's motives?
- Why does the text place spies and prophetic revelation side by side rather than presenting either as sufficient alone?
- What limits do the references to the "first offense" and "second" offense place on the permission to defend families?
- How do [[Alma 24]], [[Alma 27]], and [[Alma 43]] together portray the costs and communal consequences of the people of Ammon's covenant?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the people of Ammon’s covenant background in [[Alma 24]] and their settlement arrangement in [[Alma 27]] with Alma 43:11–13. Compare Moroni’s equipment, motives, and preparations here with [[Alma 48]] and [[Alma 49]]. [[Alma 2]] offers an earlier comparison involving dissenters, spies, maneuver, and the river Sidon. A study of [[Book of Mormon Geography]] can trace the internal relationships among Jershon, Antionum, Manti, Riplah, and Sidon while keeping proposed modern identifications separate from what the text itself establishes.
