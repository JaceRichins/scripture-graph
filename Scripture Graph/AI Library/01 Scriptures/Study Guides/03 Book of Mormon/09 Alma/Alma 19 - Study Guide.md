# Alma 19 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Alma 19]] · My notes: [[Alma 19 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Lamoni receives the light of everlasting life and sees the Redeemer—His household falls into a trance, and many see angels—Ammon is preserved miraculously—He baptizes many and establishes a church among them. About 90 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Waiting and fulfillment (vv. 1-12):** Lamoni's apparent death is disputed; the queen believes Ammon and watches until the appointed rising.
2. **Household spiritual experience (vv. 13-16):** Lamoni testifies of the Redeemer; the queen, Ammon, and the servants are overcome.
3. **Abish and the public crisis (vv. 17-28):** Abish gathers the people, but the unexplained scene produces accusation, fear, and sharp contention.
4. **Rising, witness, and division (vv. 29-34):** Abish raises the queen, the queen raises Lamoni, and multiple participants teach; some hear and some depart.
5. **Community outcome (vv. 35-36):** believers receive [[Baptism]], become a righteous people, and establish a church.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Lamoni's testimony centers salvation on the coming Redeemer and belief on his name (v. 13). The queen's response makes salvation personal—Jesus has saved her—yet immediately turns outward in a plea for mercy on her people (v. 29). Conversion is described both inwardly, as changed hearts and the loss of desire for evil (v. 33), and communally, through teaching, [[Baptism]], and an established church (vv. 35-36). The final verse joins divine initiative with human response: God's arm is extended to all, and the stated response is repentance and belief.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Four recurring word-fields organize the account. **Death, sleep, falling, and rising** make bodily posture carry spiritual and narrative meaning. **Darkness and light** describe Lamoni's inner passage from unbelief to everlasting life (v. 6). **Witness, word, hearing, and declaring** trace how knowledge moves from servants to the queen and then to the wider people. **Hands** connect the risings: Lamoni reaches to the queen, Abish raises the queen, and the queen raises Lamoni (vv. 12, 29-30).
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter repeatedly separates an event from its meaning. The prostrate household is one visible scene, but observers call it evil, punishment, the work of the Great Spirit, or a Nephite attack. This narrative polyphony makes interpretation—and not mere exposure to marvels—the decisive issue. Abish is structurally pivotal: her faithful action creates the public opportunity, her grief registers its initial failure, and her second intervention enables the testimony that redirects the gathering.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does the queen regard as sufficient grounds for trust, and why does Ammon praise her response as exceptional [[Faith]]?
- Why does the same scene move Abish toward public action but move members of the crowd toward mutually hostile explanations?
- Does the repeated hand-taking signify relationship or restoration, or is it simply the practical and conventional act of helping someone rise?
- Verse 30 says that the queen spoke words that were not understood but does not identify their nature. What, if anything, warrants comparison with the [[Gift of Tongues]]?
- The queen names Jesus in verse 29. How should that wording be read within the chapter's pre-Christian chronology, and what room remains for revelation, record transmission, or translation standardization as explanations?
- What was the 'remarkable vision of her father' associated with Abish's earlier conversion, and who experienced that vision? The grammar and narrative leave both questions unresolved.
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[Alma 18]] with this chapter to follow the movement from Ammon's service and teaching to Lamoni's household transformation. Trace the chapter's testimony chain: servants → queen → Lamoni → Abish → gathered multitude → Lamoni, Ammon, and servants → baptized community. Also compare every occurrence of falling and rising to determine when the narrator supplies a spiritual explanation and when the observing characters remain uncertain.
