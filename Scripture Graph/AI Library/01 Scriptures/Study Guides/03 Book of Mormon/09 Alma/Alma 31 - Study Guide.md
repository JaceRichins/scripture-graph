# Alma 31 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Alma 31]] · My notes: [[Alma 31 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Alma heads a mission to reclaim the apostate Zoramites—The Zoramites deny Christ, believe in a false concept of election, and worship with set prayers—The missionaries are filled with the Holy Spirit—Their afflictions are swallowed up in the joy of Christ. About 74 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The crisis and response (31:1-7):** Alma grieves, the Nephites perceive a strategic danger, and a mission team is assembled.
2. **Description of Zoramite apostasy (31:8-12):** The narrator lists neglected commandments, religious practices, and prayer.
3. **The Rameumptom prayer (31:13-23):** Architecture, ritual, creed, and the people's conduct after worship are described.
4. **Alma's diagnosis and prayer (31:24-35):** He identifies [[Pride]] and wealth, confesses his own infirmity, and intercedes for both missionaries and Zoramites.
5. **Commission and answer (31:36-38):** The missionaries receive the Holy Spirit, separate to preach, and are sustained amid [[Adversity]].
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter portrays the Zoramites’ election claim together with denial of Christ, disobedience, material [[Pride]], and condemnation of outsiders. This supports reading the target as presumptuous and exclusionary certainty of [[Salvation]], though the text does not present a complete theory of election or explicitly evaluate every possible doctrine bearing that name.

Alma’s counterexample places hope in Christ while treating the dissenters as precious brethren rather than disposable enemies. [[Prayer]] is presented as continuing devotion rather than merely a weekly performance. Alma asks for strength, patience, comfort, wisdom, and success amid anticipated affliction; the answer emphasizes faithful endurance and joy, not immunity from hardship.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter’s principal literary feature is the juxtaposition of two prayers. The Zoramite prayer moves toward self-congratulation, separation, and condemnation of others; Alma’s prayer moves toward confessed weakness, concern for companions, and reconciliation in Christ. Repetitions of “comfort,” “strength,” and “in Christ” connect Alma’s petitions with the narrated answer in Alma 31:36-38. The nearby treatment of costly apparel and chosen status also presents material and theological [[Pride]] together.

The Rameumptom episode is morally analogous to [[Luke 18]], where confident religious self-assessment and contempt for others appear in prayer. The wording about taking no thought for food, drink, or clothing closely parallels [[Matthew 6]]. These comparisons are useful literary observations, but they do not determine dependence, source language, historicity, or date of composition.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Narrative Political Theology
> Alma 31:4-5 is strong evidence for the narrative's preference for moral persuasion over coercive force in this crisis. It is not, by itself, empirical proof that preaching is always more effective than military or political action.
> — confidence 1.0 · Broad agreement · [[Narrative Political Theology in Alma 31|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary Design
> The sustained oppositions between the Zoramite prayer and Alma's prayer are strong evidence that the chapter has been composed or edited to invite comparison between two models of worship.
> — confidence 0.9 · Literary contrast accepted; compositional mechanism disputed · [[Literary Design in Alma 31|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intertextual/Thematic Parallel *(tentative)*
> Alma 31:38's description of the Lord giving the missionaries strength to "suffer no manner of afflictions" echoes a similar motif elsewhere in the Alma narrative in which the Lord eases the burdens of a suffering group of Alma's people (Mosiah 24, concerning Alma the Elder's people under Amulon). This is offered only as a loose thematic/intergenerational echo, not a demonstrated literary dependency.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · Speculative thematic parallel

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does verse 1 mean by "dumb idols"? The rest of the chapter emphasizes wealth, status, and distorted prayer but does not identify a physical idol.
- Is the selfsame prayer criticized because it is fixed, because it is insincere, or principally because of its proud and exclusionary content? The chapter most clearly supports the latter two concerns.
- How does Alma's political concern in verses 3-5 coexist with his sincere declaration that Zoramite souls are precious in verse 35?
- Does the description of God as spirit receive independent criticism, or is it reported as one element within a larger rejected creed? The chapter does not explicitly isolate that clause for evaluation.
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[Alma 32]] for the mission's reception among people excluded from the synagogues because of poverty and clothing. That continuation gives social consequences to the material pride diagnosed in Alma 31. Read [[Alma 35]] for the expulsion of believers and the eventual Zoramite alignment with the Lamanites, which develops the danger anticipated in Alma 31:4.
