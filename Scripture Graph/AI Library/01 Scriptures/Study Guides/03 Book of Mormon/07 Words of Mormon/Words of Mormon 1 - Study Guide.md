# Words of Mormon 1 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Words of Mormon 1]] · My notes: [[Words of Mormon 1 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Mormon abridges the large plates of Nephi—He puts the small plates with the other plates—King Benjamin establishes peace in the land. About A.D. 385.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Mormon's historical setting (1–2):** He prepares to transfer his record to Moroni amid national destruction.
2. **Discovery and selection of plates (3–8):** He explains what he found, why it pleased him, and why he included it despite not knowing the full purpose.
3. **Custody and preservation (9–11):** He traces the plates from Amaleki through Benjamin and subsequent generations to himself.
4. **Return to Benjamin's reign (12–18):** The narrative summarizes war, religious and political disorder, prophetic labor, and restored peace.

The transition at verse 12 is abrupt in subject matter but purposeful in editorial function: after explaining the inserted record, Mormon resumes at the same king where his earlier abridgment had arrived.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter connects [[Revelation]] with responsible human action. Mormon reports spiritual prompting but also admits limited understanding (verse 7), a combination that discourages treating revelation as equivalent to exhaustive knowledge. His prayers in verses 8 and 11 connect preservation of records with hoped-for restoration, redemption through Christ, and [[Final Judgment]].

Verses 13–18 also complicate a simplistic account of [[War and Peace]]. Benjamin's peace follows both armed defense and intensive prophetic labor. The text praises Benjamin and the prophets, but it supplies too little detail to evaluate every punishment or political measure.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter is unusually self-referential: its narrator pauses the historical account to explain how and why part of that account was assembled. Repeated references to plates, records, abridgment, writing, and preservation make editorial process a principal subject rather than invisible background.

The wording in verses 13–14 juxtaposes Benjamin's own strength with the strength of the Lord. This may express cooperation between exertion and divine aid, although the text does not explicitly formulate a general doctrine from the juxtaposition.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Statement Of Editorial Motivation
> The text supports the limited claim that Mormon believed a divine purpose guided his inclusion of the plates. Because the chapter never specifies the later circumstance that would constitute that purpose, it does not by itself identify a particular historical fulfillment.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · best-calibrated claim in the set · [[Internal Statement Of Editorial Motivation in Words of Mormon 1|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal Literary Structure
> The chapter explicitly explains the joining of records and then resumes the historical narrative with King Benjamin. This strongly supports reading the chapter as a deliberate literary bridge between the account ending with Amaleki and the succeeding Benjamin narrative.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · supported, honestly hedged on authorship implications · [[Internal Literary Structure in Words of Mormon 1|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal-Textual-Correspondence *(tentative)*
> Mormon's stated stopping point for his abridgment — "down to the reign of this king Benjamin" — closely matches the stopping point later given to Joseph Smith for translating the large-plates abridgment after the 1828 loss of the 116-page manuscript, as recorded in Doctrine and Covenants 10, which instructs him to translate the plates of Nephi down to the reign of king Benjamin. Latter-day Saint commentary has long treated this correspondence as an internal-coherence argument for providential design in how the record reached its current form.
> — strength 0.2 · confidence 0.6 · disputed interpretation; weak evidentiary weight
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What exactly does Mormon know, and what does he expressly leave unknown, about the "wise purpose" in verse 7?
- Which conclusions about later manuscript history come from this chapter, and which require information outside it?
- How does the admission that Mormon cannot record even a hundredth part affect interpretation of omissions elsewhere in his history?
- What kinds of authority are exercised by Benjamin and the prophets in verses 15–18, and what information would be needed to assess those actions historically or ethically?
- Who are the intended referents of "my brethren" and "their brethren" in verses 8 and 11? The immediate chapter does not define those expressions with complete precision.
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read alongside [[Omni 1]] (Amaleki's account of delivering the plates to Benjamin) and [[Jacob 1]] (the starting point of the small-plates record Mormon describes finding). Compare Mormon's first-person editorial interjection here with his other self-identifying comments elsewhere in his abridgment (traditionally compared to [[3 Nephi 5]], though that comparison has not been independently verified). Those curious about the Restoration-era translation history may compare the boundary Mormon names in verse 3 — "down to the reign of this king Benjamin" — with Doctrine and Covenants 10:41; the correspondence is worth noting but should not be overread, since the small plates were translated after the Mosiah-through-Moroni portion was already complete.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
