# Mormon 8 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Mormon 8]] · My notes: [[Mormon 8 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Mormon 8 shifts the record into Moroni's own voice. Writing after Cumorah, with Mormon and all his kinsfolk dead, Moroni describes himself as isolated — without ore, without friends, uncertain how much longer he will live (v.5) — yet still bound by his father's commandment to finish the record before hiding it in the earth. The chapter falls into two uneven parts: a terse first-person report of the Nephites' final destruction and Moroni's own isolation (vv.1-11), and an extended address to future readers about the record's worth, its promised coming forth 'by the power of God,' and the wickedness of the generation that will receive it (vv.12-41). It continues the project [[Mormon 5]] already anticipated, and its themes of [[Pride]], [[Secret Combinations]], and [[Records and Sacred Writings]] recur through the rest of the book.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
- vv.1-11: First-person report. Moroni states he writes only 'few things,' commanded by his father; recounts the Nephites' final destruction and Mormon's death; cites lack of room and ore as reasons for brevity (v.5); and reports — with the explicit hedge that 'no man knoweth' where they now are — that the disciples of Jesus who once ministered among the people had withdrawn, though he and Mormon personally 'have seen them.'
- vv.12-22: Address about the record itself: the plates are worthless as metal but the record is of great worth (v.14); a blessing is pronounced on whoever brings it to light 'with an eye single to his glory'; and readers are warned against rash condemnation and coercive demands for a sign, since judgment and vengeance belong to the Lord (vv.17-20).
- vv.23-26: A pivot on Isaiah ('search the prophecies of Isaiah... I cannot write them') into a meditation on the faith and enduring prayers of 'saints who have gone before,' anchoring the record's future recovery in covenant promise.
- vv.27-32: A sixfold prophetic oracle built on the anaphora 'it shall come in a day when...' (vv.26, 27, 28, 29, 31, 32), cataloguing latter-day secret combinations, prideful and profit-seeking churches, natural disasters, and war.
- vv.33-41: Direct apostrophe — 'O ye wicked and perverse and stiffnecked people' — turning the oracle into personal accusation against the very generation that will read the book, closing with a warning that 'the sword of vengeance hangeth over you.'
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- The record's coming forth is framed as an act of God, not human effort: it will be 'brought out of the earth... by the power of God' (v.16; see [[Book of Mormon (Gospel Topic)]]), and no one can bring it forth 'save it be given him of God' (v.15).
- Verses 17-20 reserve final judgment and vengeance to God and warn specifically against rash condemnation and coercive demands ('Show unto me, or ye shall be smitten') — a warning against violent or hasty responses, not a blanket prohibition on careful examination of the record. See [[Final Judgment]] and [[Justice and Mercy]].
- [[Pride]] is diagnosed as a specific mechanism of latter-day apostasy: polluted, ornamented churches, fine apparel, and — concretely — loving money and status more than 'the poor and the needy, the sick and the afflicted' (vv.36-39). The moral test is neglect of vulnerable people, not wealth by itself; see [[Service]].
- [[Secret Combinations]] and 'works of darkness' (v.27) are named as a recurring precursor to societal collapse, echoing the destruction just narrated in vv.1-9.
- The record's purpose is tied to 'the ancient and long dispersed covenant people of the Lord' (v.15), situating it within [[Covenants]] and, more tentatively, [[Gathering of Israel]] promises.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Two phrases closely track King James New Testament wording: v.20's 'judgment is mine... and vengeance is mine also, and I will repay' most closely matches Romans 12:19 ('Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord'), which itself draws on Deuteronomy 32:35 — though the KJV wording there ('To me belongeth vengeance, and recompence') is a looser match; and v.31's 'gall of bitterness... and... bonds of iniquity' echoes Acts 8:23 (Peter's rebuke of Simon Magus), allowing for the singular/plural difference ('bond' vs. 'bonds'). These are real English-wording echoes, but the exact match is most simply explained by a KJV-saturated translation or composition process; whether it also reflects a genuinely shared ancient idiom (the brass plates carried the law of Moses) is a separate, unresolved question. Moroni's instruction to 'search the prophecies of Isaiah' (v.23) and the 'speak from the dead' image of v.26 also echo Isaiah 29:4's voice 'out of the dust' — an echo available to any writer steeped in the King James Bible, not proof of a particular mode of composition.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 26-32 are built on a sixfold anaphora — 'it shall come in a day when...' — a prophetic-oracle form that piles up parallel latter-day evils (secret combinations, prideful churches, natural disasters, war, greed-based religion) before the voice suddenly turns from third-person prediction to second-person accusation in v.33 ('O ye wicked and perverse and stiffnecked people'). That rhetorical pivot collapses the distance between the predicted future audience and the reader actually holding the book. Verse 24's catalogue of deliverance-motifs — mountains removed, earth shaken, prison walls fallen, fire and beasts and serpents rendered harmless — evokes biblical deliverance imagery, but it also has a close Book of Mormon antecedent in the three disciples' furnace- and beast-survival in [[3 Nephi 28]]; Moroni may be summarizing that earlier account together with broader scriptural motifs rather than describing a wholly separate incident. The chapter's argument also runs on sustained contrast: plates of no worth versus a record of great worth, darkness versus light, and fine apparel and adorned churches versus unnoticed sufferers.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Are 'the disciples of Jesus' Mormon and Moroni saw (v.11) the same three Nephites who chose to tarry in [[3 Nephi 28]], or a larger, unnamed group of translated ministers? Mormon 8 alone does not say.
- What does 'search the prophecies of Isaiah... I cannot write them' (v.23) imply about which Isaiah material Moroni assumed his future readers would already have?
- Does Moroni's insertion 'it is the hand of the Lord which hath done it' (v.8) reframe the destruction as covenant judgment, or simply assert divine sovereignty over an otherwise military defeat?
- Is the primary sin condemned in vv.36-39 wealth itself, display, misplaced love, or neglect of the poor and afflicted — or some combination of all four?
- How should the warning against rash condemnation and coercive sign-demanding (vv.17-20) be balanced against responsible, careful examination of the record's historical and textual claims?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Moroni's more extended reflection on human weakness in record-keeping appears in [[Ether 12]], not in this chapter; read the two together. Compare the record's promised 'coming forth... by the power of God' with the faith-and-record themes of [[Ether 4]]. The latter-day oracle in vv.27-32 pairs naturally with the signs-of-the-times language of [[D&C 45]], with Nephi's own warnings about gain-seeking churches in [[2 Nephi 26]] and [[2 Nephi 28]], and with the definition of priestcraft in [[Alma 1]] (compare Nehor). The warning against those who would fight the Lord's latter-day work anticipates [[3 Nephi 29]]. For continuity of the record-keeping project across a change of narrator, read this chapter alongside [[Mormon 5]].
