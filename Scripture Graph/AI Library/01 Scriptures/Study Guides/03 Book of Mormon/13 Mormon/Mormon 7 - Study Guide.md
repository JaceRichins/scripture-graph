# Mormon 7 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Mormon 7]] · My notes: [[Mormon 7 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Mormon invites the Lamanites of the latter days to believe in Christ, accept His gospel, and be saved—All who believe the Bible will also believe the Book of Mormon. About A.D. 385.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Audience and identity (vv. 1-2):** Mormon addresses a remnant of the house of Israel.
2. **Four things to know (vv. 2-5):** Israelite identity, the necessity of repentance, renunciation of bloodshed, and knowledge of the fathers joined with belief in Christ.
3. **Christ's saving work (vv. 5-7):** death, resurrection, victory over death, resurrection of the dead, judgment, redemption, and endless happiness in God's presence.
4. **Response and records (vv. 8-9):** repent, be baptized, and receive the gospel presented through two records.
5. **Covenant conclusion (v. 10):** identity as Jacob's remnant is joined to faith, baptism with water and with fire and the Holy Ghost, Christ's example, and the day of judgment.

The fourfold “Know ye that ye” refrain gives verses 2-5 their clearest formal structure. Discussions of covenant identity also frame the chapter's Christ-centered proclamation.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter does not present lineage as sufficient for salvation. Covenant identity gives the appeal its setting, while repentance, faith in Christ, and baptism remain required responses.

Verses 5-7 connect the [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]], [[Resurrection]], and [[Final Judgment]]. Christ rises by the Father's power and gains victory over the grave; he brings about the resurrection of the dead, after which humanity stands before his judgment seat. The guiltless are given a place in God's presence and a state of happiness without end. Verse 7 names the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost as “one God,” but does not define the metaphysical nature of that unity.

Verse 4 addresses disposition as well as conduct: the audience must lay down weapons and cease delighting in bloodshed. Because the verse includes an exception for divine command, it should not be treated as an unqualified statement of absolute pacifism.

Verse 10 places baptism with water before baptism with fire and the Holy Ghost. This is a clear sequence, though the verse alone does not settle whether the latter expression describes a separately administered ordinance, a divine spiritual experience, or both.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated “Know ye that ye” openings in verses 2-5 create a solemn, emphatic rhythm. The form may be described as catechetical, but the text does not identify it as a formal catechism or liturgy.

Verses 8-9 distinguish “this record” from a record coming from the Jews to the Gentiles and then from the Gentiles to the audience. In context, “that” most naturally refers to the Jewish record and “this” to Mormon's record. The traditional identification is the Bible and the Book of Mormon respectively, but neither modern title appears in the chapter, and the pronoun reading remains inferential.

Verse 5 combines language resembling [[Isaiah 25]] and [[1 Corinthians 15]]: death is swallowed up, death has a sting, and the grave is defeated. The English verbal relationship is strong. By itself it does not determine whether the relationship arose through ancient scriptural tradition, translation using familiar biblical diction, or modern composition.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Knowledge of the fathers binds the chapter together. Mormon hopes the remnant will learn “the things of their fathers” (v. 1), commands them to come to that knowledge (v. 5), and says belief in the record will disclose both their fathers and God's marvelous works among them (v. 9). Verse 10 completes the movement by identifying the audience as Jacob's seed. The record therefore functions as an instrument of testimony and recovered covenant memory.

The two-record teaching can profitably be compared with [[2 Nephi 29]], where records associated with different covenant peoples come together as witnesses. This is a meaningful internal theological and literary connection, but the records' claim to confirm one another is not independent historical proof of either record's authenticity.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Who is “the remnant of this people” in verse 1, and what does the chapter itself establish about that audience?
- Why is the command to relinquish weapons placed between the calls to repentance and belief in Christ?
- How do the four “Know ye” statements connect ancestry, conduct, and discipleship?
- What assumptions about “this” and “that” are required when identifying the records in verse 9?
- What might “the first covenant” emphasize: ancestry, divine promises, covenant obligations, or some combination?
- How does the baptismal sequence compare with [[2 Nephi 31]] and [[3 Nephi 12]]?
- What does verse 7 explicitly affirm about the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, and which later metaphysical questions does it leave unanswered?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the resurrection-and-judgment sequence with [[Mormon 9]], and the baptismal pattern with [[2 Nephi 31]] and [[3 Nephi 12]]. For the theology of multiple records, read [[2 Nephi 29]]. Study Mormon 7:5 alongside [[Isaiah 25]] and [[1 Corinthians 15]], distinguishing the observable English wording from competing explanations of its compositional or translation history. Matthew 27 also supplies useful context for the compressed statement that Jesus “was slain by the Jews,” showing why that phrase must not be extended into collective blame against Jewish people.
