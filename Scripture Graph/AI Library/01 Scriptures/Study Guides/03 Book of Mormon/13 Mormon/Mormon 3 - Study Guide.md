# Mormon 3 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Mormon 3]] · My notes: [[Mormon 3 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Mormon cries repentance unto the Nephites—They gain a great victory and glory in their own strength—Mormon refuses to lead them, and his prayers for them are without faith—The Book of Mormon invites the twelve tribes of Israel to believe the gospel. About A.D. 360–62.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter has four movements:

1. **Reprieve and rejected call (Mormon 3:1–4):** ten years without battle allow preparation, but the renewed call to repentance fails.
2. **Defense and escalation (3:5–10):** fortification and two victories are followed by boasting and an oath to invade in vengeance.
3. **Withdrawal from command (3:11–16):** Mormon recalls his love, prayers, and repeated deliverances, refuses further leadership, and becomes an “idle witness” to what follows.
4. **Address to future readers (3:17–22):** Mormon writes to Gentiles, Israel, the remnant, and ultimately all humanity about witness, repentance, and judgment.

Verses 16–17 form the hinge from autobiographical war narrative to direct audience address.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Repentance during reprieve:** The period of preservation is described as a “chance for repentance” (Mormon 3:3), showing divine patience as an opportunity for change rather than proof of approval.
- **Coming to Christ and [[Baptism]]:** The conditional promise in verse 2 joins repentance, coming to Christ, baptism, rebuilding the church, and being spared.
- **Deliverance and [[Pride]]:** Repeated rescue does not validate the people's conduct. Their victories lead to boasting in their own strength and vengeance rather than repentance (3:7–15).
- **Love with moral withdrawal:** Mormon's refusal to command does not erase his love or prayer for the people (3:11–12).
- **Collective and individual judgment:** A collective sentence is announced against the Nephites (3:15), while the closing address teaches that every soul must face [[Final Judgment]] according to works (3:18–22).
- **Two groups of twelve:** Verses 18–19 distinguish the twelve chosen in Jerusalem from twelve chosen “in this land.” The precise antecedent of the final “they” in verse 19 is grammatically disputed, so the passage should not be forced into an overly technical account of separate tribunals.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated verb **write** in Mormon 3:17–20 shifts attention from the events being narrated to the purposes of the record. The widening sequence—Gentiles, house of Israel, twelve tribes, remnant, and all—turns a localized war account into universal admonition.

The phrase **without faith** in 3:12 is qualified by “because of the hardness of their hearts.” Its precise force is uncertain, but the context favors lack of confident expectation for the people's repentance or deliverance, not an absence of Mormon's love, prayer, or belief in God.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter is organized around reversals. Preservation intended to foster repentance is followed by boasting, while Mormon's withdrawal from military action leads into an active written witness to future readers. His phrase “idle witness” (Mormon 3:16) is best understood as inactivity in the conflict, since he continues observing and writing.

The contrast between “their own strength” (3:9) and “Vengeance is mine” (3:15) opposes human self-assertion and retaliation to judgment claimed by God. The latter wording closely parallels Romans 12:19 in English and reflects the theology of Deuteronomy 32:35. That parallel may arise from biblical translation or compositional idiom and does not establish that the historical Mormon quoted Paul's epistle.

Verses 18–20 widen from particular covenant groups to every soul before Christ. They clearly distinguish two groups of twelve, although the final pronoun in verse 19 leaves part of their proposed judicial hierarchy open to interpretation. Compare [[Matthew 19]] and [[3 Nephi 27]].
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Authorial Purpose And Audience
> Mormon's repeated 'I write unto you' statements are strong textual evidence that this passage is consciously composed for future audiences and organized around persuasion, witness, repentance, and judgment rather than functioning as a bare military chronicle.

> [!evidence] Narrative And Ethical Contrast *(tentative)*
> The sequence supplies strong internal evidence that Mormon distinguishes defending territory from undertaking an offensive campaign motivated by pride and vengeance: he commands the earlier defense but refuses the later attack.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.7 · Plausible contrast; causal emphasis disputed

> [!evidence] Textual/Phraseological Parallel *(tentative)*
> Mormon 3:20's phrase "stand before the judgment-seat of Christ" closely parallels Romans 14:10's "we shall all stand before the judgment seat of Christ" (a verified text-overlap between this chapter and Romans 14). This is a genuine verbal echo, but it does not by itself establish that the historical Mormon (4th century Nephite record-keeper) was quoting or aware of Paul's epistle, since Paul wrote after both Lehi's departure from Jerusalem (c. 600 B.C.) and Christ's resurrection.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.9 · English parallel accepted; origin unresolved

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does “chance for repentance” in Mormon 3:3 identify the entire ten-year reprieve as divine patience, or only the people's recent preservation more generally?
- Is Mormon's refusal directed against all offensive war, specifically revenge warfare, the forbidden oaths, or the people's total wickedness?
- What expectation was absent when Mormon prayed “without faith” in 3:12?
- How does Mormon's continuing love for the people qualify the meaning of withdrawing from leadership?
- Why does the narrative move from the judgment of particular covenant groups to “the whole human family of Adam” in 3:20?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Mormon 3:15 with Romans 12:19 and Deuteronomy 32:35, distinguishing the close English parallel with Romans from the broader theological parallel with Deuteronomy. Compare Mormon 3:18–19 with Matthew 19:28 and 3 Nephi 27:27 concerning the two groups of twelve, while noting the pronoun ambiguity in Mormon 3:19. Read [[Mormon 4]] for the immediate consequences of the offensive campaign and [[Mormon 5]] for Mormon's continuing explanation of his record and its future audiences.
