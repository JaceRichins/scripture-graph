## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The question after entry (vv. 1–2):** What follows entrance into the way?
2. **Words and Spirit as guidance (vv. 3–5):** Christ's words tell, and the Holy Ghost shows, what disciples should do.
3. **A boundary and a lament (vv. 6–7):** Nephi points toward Christ's manifestation, reports that the Spirit stops him, and mourns resistance to plainly given knowledge.
4. **Prayer as the immediate practice (vv. 8–9):** The Spirit teaches people to pray; disciples should pray always and seek consecration of their actions.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter describes post-entry discipleship as responsive dependence on both revealed words and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]]. These are mutually reinforcing: the Spirit empowers speech of Christ's words, and both provide direction. This is a theology of active reception—disciples feast, ask, knock, search, hearken, pray, and do.

Verse 9 adds a significant purpose for [[Prayer]]. Prayer does not merely request that an intended act succeed; it asks the Father, in Christ's name, to consecrate the performance for the disciple's spiritual welfare. The wording allows divine transformation or redirection of the act, not simply endorsement of whatever the petitioner has already chosen.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verses 3 and 5 repeat the same practical promise: the words of Christ “will tell you all things what ye should do,” while the Holy Ghost “will show unto you all things what ye should do.” The pairing suggests complementary guidance, although “tell” and “show” may be ordinary rhetorical variation rather than technical terms for instruction and application.

“Feast” portrays abundant reception rather than minimal engagement. The movement from asking and knocking, darkness and light (v. 4), to searching, plainness, and knowledge (v. 7) associates understanding with willing inquiry without proving that every uncertainty results from insufficient effort.

Verse 9 also resembles Luke 18:1 in joining continual prayer with not fainting. The similarity is clear in the received English text, but it does not by itself distinguish direct literary dependence, a King James-shaped translation register, shared scriptural idiom, or theological explanations.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter is tightly joined to [[2 Nephi 31]] by repeated language: entering the way, receiving the Holy Ghost, and the doctrine of Christ. It is therefore best read as the continuation of one argument. A second progression runs through the chapter: angels speak by the Holy Ghost, Christ's words tell disciples what to do, the Holy Ghost shows them what to do, the Spirit may stop prophetic speech, and the Spirit teaches individuals to pray. The sequence moves from heavenly speech to personally enacted discipleship.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How do “tell” in verse 3 and “show” in verse 5 illuminate the relationship between [[Scripture Study]] and personal [[Revelation]]?
- Why does Nephi answer a question about post-entry discipleship by emphasizing both knowledge and prayer?
- What safeguards are needed before treating a personal impulse as guidance from the Holy Ghost?
- Does the request for God to consecrate a performance imply confirmation, transformation, redirection, or some combination of these?

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[2 Nephi 31]], [[2 Nephi 32]], and [[2 Nephi 33]] as a continuous discourse, marking every occurrence of “Holy Ghost,” “words,” “speak,” “understand,” and “do.” Compare the teachings on persistent prayer with [[Alma 34]] and the promise of knowledge through the Holy Ghost with [[Moroni 10]]. These comparisons clarify recurring Book of Mormon patterns without by themselves determining literary dependence or historical origin.
