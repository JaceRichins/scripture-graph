# 2 Nephi 1 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[2 Nephi 1]] · My notes: [[2 Nephi 1 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Lehi's farewell begins with remembered mercy and moves toward warning, exhortation, and blessing. He reviews the family's deliverance from Jerusalem and the sea, then describes their inheritance as both gift and covenant responsibility. Liberty and prosperity are conditional in his speech: rejecting the Messiah and abandoning the commandments reverse the promised blessings into dispossession, scattering, and captivity. The final movement narrows from future generations to the immediate family conflict, defending Nephi's ministry and addressing the succession of Lehi's blessing.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Remembered deliverance and promised land (vv. 1–9):** Lehi rehearses the journey and states the land covenant.
2. **Covenant reversal (vv. 10–12):** rejection of the Messiah leads to judgment, dispossession, and scattering.
3. **Awakening appeal (vv. 13–23):** the dying parent calls his sons to escape spiritual chains, unite, and put on righteousness.
4. **Defense of Nephi and first blessing (vv. 24–29):** Lehi answers complaints about Nephi and makes the blessing conditional upon hearkening to him.
5. **Blessing of Zoram (vv. 30–32):** faithfulness joins Zoram's future prosperity and security with Nephi's descendants.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter holds [[Justice and Mercy]] together. Deliverance from Jerusalem and preservation at sea are remembered as mercy, while rejection of the Messiah invites the judgments of a just God. Its prosperity formula belongs to a multigenerational covenant discourse; it should be applied cautiously rather than treated as a guarantee that every obedient person will avoid poverty, persecution, or suffering.

Lehi's treatment of [[Freedom and Liberty]] also reaches beyond government. Iniquity can produce conquest and dispossession, but Lehi also speaks of the chains of hell and captivity to the devil. His proposed release is awakening, unity, [[Obedience]], and the armor of righteousness.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated imperatives—“awake,” “arise,” “shake off,” “put on,” and “come forth”—give Lehi's appeal urgency and movement. Sleep, dust, chains, obscurity, and captivity depict his sons' danger; awakening, rising, armor, unity, and liberty depict the desired reversal (vv. 13–14, 21, 23).

This cluster closely parallels [[Isaiah 52]], especially Isaiah 52:1–2, where Zion is commanded to awake, put on strength, shake off dust, arise, and loose the bonds of captivity. The verbal relationship is strong in the English text, but its mechanism remains uncertain: it could reflect conscious allusion, broader scriptural diction, Nephi's presentation, or the language of translation. The parallel alone does not establish ancient authorship or a particular original wording.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Lehi's impending death gives the address the form of a farewell discourse. The expression “the way of all the earth” also appears in the biblical farewells of Joshua and David. Within this setting, memory serves exhortation: earlier deliverance shows both God's mercy and the seriousness of renewed rebellion. The speech then turns into a family succession scene as Lehi defends Nephi and conditionally places his first blessing upon him.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Narrative Characterization
> Lehi's defense supplies direct narrative evidence that authority, motive, and manner of speech were contested within the family. It supports the interpretation that the later division was preceded by a genuine conflict over leadership, but Lehi's testimony is partisan evidence within the narrative and cannot independently settle every accusation.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · Balanced narrative interpretation · [[Narrative Characterization in 2 Nephi 1|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary Intertextuality
> The concentration of dust, rising, bonds, and captivity language is good evidence of a literary relationship between Lehi's exhortation and Isaiah 52:2. It is less certain whether the passage presents a conscious quotation, an allusive reuse by the narrator, or biblical language supplied through translation.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · Literary relationship supported; mechanism disputed · [[Literary Intertextuality in 2 Nephi 1|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Language/Translation Idiom *(tentative)*
> The phrase 'put on the armor of righteousness' (v. 23) uses militarized-virtue idiom of the kind common in the KJV-flavored English of the Book of Mormon translation; this is worth flagging as a possible translation-idiom dependency rather than as evidence bearing on ancient authorship one way or the other.
> — strength 0.2 · confidence 0.5 · Speculative translation-language observation

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How does remembering mercy in verses 1–3 prepare for the warning in verses 10–12?
- Does “prosper in the land” mean wealth, continued possession, divine presence, or a combination of these within Lehi's discourse?
- What changes when the imagery of political captivity in verses 7–12 becomes imagery of spiritual chains in verses 13 and 23?
- What does Lehi establish about Nephi's leadership, and what details about the “first blessing” remain unspecified?
- Which modern geographic conclusions, if any, can actually be drawn from a chapter that never names a continent or nation?
