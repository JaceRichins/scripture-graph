# 2 Nephi 21 — Study Guide

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The stem of Jesse (Christ) will judge in righteousness—The knowledge of God will cover the earth in the Millennium—The Lord will raise an ensign and gather Israel—Compare Isaiah 11. About 559–545 B.C.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Righteous ruler (vv. 1–5):** A figure associated with Jesse receives the Spirit and judges the poor and meek with righteousness.

2. **Transformed creation (vv. 6–9):** Predators, prey, livestock, and children coexist without harm; the climax is an earth filled with knowledge of the Lord.

3. **Ensign and gathering (vv. 10–12):** The root of Jesse becomes an ensign sought by Gentiles, and dispersed Israel and Judah are assembled.

4. **Reunification and new Exodus (vv. 13–16):** Ephraim and Judah are reconciled, surrounding nations are subdued, and a highway from Assyria recalls Israel's deliverance from Egypt.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The ruler's justice is measured by righteousness and equity rather than surface appearance (vv. 3–5), linking divine rule with concern for the poor and meek. The gathering in verses 10–16 joins the [[Gathering of Israel]] to the older pattern of [[Exodus and Deliverance]]: God removes barriers and makes a way home for the remnant. Verse 9 also presents knowledge of the Lord as central to the cessation of harm, a significant connection between revealed knowledge, transformed conduct, and [[War and Peace]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter repeatedly uses paired expressions: wisdom/understanding, counsel/might, knowledge/fear, sight/hearing, righteousness/faithfulness, Ephraim/Judah, and outcasts/dispersed. These pairings give the oracle a balanced, elevated cadence. Plant imagery also frames its principal figure: a rod and branch emerge in verse 1, while the root of Jesse returns in verse 10. The repeated “ensign” in verses 10 and 12 links the Jesse figure to the gathering of nations and Israel.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The peaceful-animal sequence expands from pairs of natural enemies to vulnerable children confronting dangerous creatures. Verse 9 then interprets the scene through the absence of harm and the abundance of divine knowledge. The closing verses shift to exodus imagery: sea, river, dry passage, highway, remnant, Assyria, and Egypt. This makes an earlier act of deliverance the pattern for a promised future one. [[2 Nephi 30]] later reuses the judgment and peace language, showing how Nephi incorporates this Isaianic vision into his own prophecy.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. What contrast does the text create between judgment based on sight and hearing and judgment shaped by the Spirit, righteousness, and equity?

2. Does verse 9 portray knowledge of the Lord as the cause of peace, the defining feature of peace, or both?

3. How should the non-harm of verses 6–9 be read alongside the slaying and conquest language of verses 4 and 14?

4. How does [[D&C 113]]'s distinction among the stem, rod, and root complicate an interpretation that assigns every Jesse image to the same person?

5. Which elements of verses 15–16 most clearly evoke [[Exodus and Deliverance]], and what does that analogy imply about the gathering?
