# 2 Nephi 31 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[2 Nephi 31]] · My notes: [[2 Nephi 31 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
2 Nephi 31 is Nephi's concluding doctrinal teaching, delivered as he "make[s] an end" of his prophesying (v. 1) and turns to speak "plainly" about what he calls "[[Baptism|the doctrine of Christ]]" (v. 2). Using the [[Jesus Christ (Gospel Topic)|Lamb of God]]'s own baptism as his starting example, Nephi explains why the sinless Christ was baptized (vv. 4-10), then sets out an ordered path of [[Repentance]], [[Baptism]], the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]], and [[Endurance]] to the end (vv. 11-20). He is explicit that entering this path through baptism is a beginning, not a completion — "is all done? ... Nay" (v. 19) — and closes by naming this the one "doctrine of Christ" and identifying Father, Son, and Holy Ghost as "one God" (v. 21).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Introduction and purpose (vv. 1-3):** Nephi ends his prophesying and turns to speak plainly of the doctrine of Christ.
2. **Why Christ was baptized (vv. 4-10):** though holy, Christ is baptized to fulfil all righteousness, witnessing obedience to the Father and setting an example — "Follow thou me" (v. 10).
3. **Instruction from the Father and the Son (vv. 11-16):** the Father commands repentance and baptism (v. 11); the Son promises the Holy Ghost and warns against later denial (vv. 12, 14); the Father confirms the Son's words and ties salvation to enduring to the end (vv. 15-16).
4. **The gate and the path (vv. 17-20):** repentance and baptism by water are "the gate"; remission by fire and the Holy Ghost follows; disciples must then press forward in faith, hope, and love.
5. **Closing declaration (v. 21):** Nephi names this the only true doctrine of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost, "which is one God."
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Nephi sets out an ordered process — [[Repentance]], [[Baptism]] by water, the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost|baptism of fire and of the Holy Ghost]], and [[Endurance|enduring to the end]] (vv. 13, 16, 17, 20) — while insisting this is not merely mechanical: baptism is a conscious witness of "real intent" and "full purpose of heart" (v. 13), and progress on the path depends on "unshaken faith" and "relying wholly upon the merits of him who is mighty to save" (v. 19). Christ's own baptism is presented not as needed for remission of sin (the text never uses that word of him) but to "fulfil all righteousness" — an act of humility and obedience that sets the example his followers are commanded to follow (vv. 5-10). The chapter closes with one of the Book of Mormon's more direct statements naming [[The Godhead]] — the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, "which is one God" (v. 21) — alongside an exclusive claim about [[Salvation]]: "none other way nor name given under heaven whereby man can be saved" (v. 21).
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The controlling metaphor is movement through a **gate** and along a **strait and narrow path** (vv. 17-20): repentance and baptism are entry, not arrival. Nephi's phrases **full purpose of heart**, **no hypocrisy**, **no deception**, and **real intent** focus attention on the integrity of the participant rather than outward performance alone (v. 13). The metaphor **feasting upon the word of Christ** portrays continuing discipleship as sustained nourishment (v. 20), leading naturally into [[2 Nephi 32]]. Notably, "to fulfil all righteousness" (v. 5) matches the King James wording of Matthew 3:15, where John the Baptist says the same of Jesus at his own baptism — an echo worth noting for readers comparing the two accounts, independent of which explanation (a shared idiom, translation into familiar language, or direct textual borrowing) one finds persuasive.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Nephi's gate-and-path imagery ("the straitness of the path, and the narrowness of the gate," v. 9; "this strait and narrow path," v. 18) recalls the language of his earlier vision of the [[Tree of Life]] (1 Nephi 8); whether this chapter deliberately reinterprets that vision or independently uses common gate/path imagery (compare Matthew 7:13-14) is not settled by the text itself. The chapter also layers quotations attributed to the Father and the Son (vv. 11, 12, 14, 15) — though only these two voices speak, while the Holy Ghost appears as a promised gift rather than a speaker — giving the teaching the weight of multiple witnesses without amounting to a fully worked-out statement about the relationship named in v. 21, where Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are called "one God."
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does Nephi treat Christ's holiness as the reason baptism needs explaining rather than as a reason to skip it — what does his answer (obedience, example, fulfilling "all righteousness") suggest about baptism's purpose for everyone else (vv. 5-10)?
- What is the significance of warning specifically about someone who has already received the baptism of fire and spoken with the tongue of angels, and only then denies Christ (v. 14)?
- How does Nephi's question "is all done?" (v. 19) reframe baptism as a beginning rather than an endpoint, and what does it mean in practice to "press forward... feasting upon the word of Christ" (v. 20)?
- The chapter narrates the Father and the Son each speaking, then calls them "one God" (v. 21) — what does the text itself establish about this relationship, and what does it leave for later scripture to clarify?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
