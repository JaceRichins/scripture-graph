## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Accession and evaluation (verses 1–2):** age, reign, ancestry, and separate assessments of king and people.
2. **Building works (verses 3–4):** the high gate, Ophel wall, mountain cities, fortified places, and towers.
3. **War and payments (verse 5):** victory over the Ammonites and payments reported for three years.
4. **Theological culmination (verse 6):** Jotham's might is attributed to preparing his ways before God.
5. **Source notice and regnal close (verses 7–9):** written-source reference, repeated regnal data, death, burial, and succession.

The repetition in verses 1 and 8 gives the account a framing effect, although it may also reflect conventional regnal formulas.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter is relevant to [[Obedience]] because the narrator explicitly connects Jotham's prepared conduct before God with his might (2 Chronicles 27:6). This is an interpretation of Jotham's particular reign, not an exceptionless promise that faithful people always receive political or material success. Verse 2 also distinguishes a ruler's positive evaluation from the moral condition of the population: Jotham does right while the people continue corruptly. The chapter does not describe a reform program or explain the causes of the people's condition.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The clause "Jotham became mighty, because he prepared his ways before the LORD his God" states the narrator's causal interpretation explicitly. "Prepared his ways" can reasonably be read as purposeful ordering or orientation of conduct, though the chapter does not specify every action included in that description. By contrast, "the people did yet corruptly" is broad: it reports continuing wrongdoing without identifying its particular practices, extent, or causes.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The statement that Jotham "entered not into the temple" gains significance from its position after [[2 Chronicles 26]], where Uzziah unlawfully enters the temple to burn incense. Jotham resembles his father in doing right but is distinguished from that particular transgression. The contrast is strong, although 2 Chronicles 27:2 does not explicitly retell the earlier event or describe Jotham's motives.

The repeated accession age and reign length in verses 1 and 8 frame the achievement report. Verse 6 then functions as its theological culmination. These features are compatible with deliberate presentation, but the regnal formulas are also conventional.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Comparative Literary Evidence
> The parallel Jotham notices in 2 Kings and 2 Chronicles share the central regnal data, maternal identification, positive evaluation, high-gate construction, death, and succession. Chronicles additionally reports the Ophel and regional fortifications, Ammonite tribute, and the explicit explanation of Jotham's might in verse 6. This comparative pattern supports the conclusion that the Chronicler's presentation has a distinctive thematic emphasis on building, strength, and devotion.

> [!evidence] Internal Narrative Causation
> Verse 6 is direct evidence that the narrator interprets Jotham's might as resulting from his having prepared or ordered his ways before the LORD. It strongly establishes the narrator's stated theological explanation, but not the complete historical causation of Jotham's power.

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare this chapter with [[2 Kings 15]]. Both accounts contain central regnal data, Jotham's positive evaluation, construction at the temple's high gate, death, and succession. Chronicles additionally reports work at Ophel and elsewhere in Judah, the Ammonite campaign and payments, and the explicit explanation of Jotham's might in verse 6. These differences show distinctive emphasis in the extant accounts but do not by themselves determine historicity, literary dependence, or source relationships.

Reading [[2 Chronicles 26]] clarifies the likely contrast with Uzziah's temple transgression, while [[2 Chronicles 28]] continues the succession with Ahaz. Verse 7 is relevant to [[Records and Sacred Writings]], but its cited work cannot automatically be identified with the present biblical book of Kings, an official archive, or an independent witness.
