## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Accession and evaluation (verses 1–2):** Jotham's age, reign, ancestry, positive evaluation, avoidance of temple entry, and the people's continuing corruption.
2. **Construction (verses 3–4):** work at the temple, Ophel, the mountains of Judah, and forested areas.
3. **War and payments (verse 5):** victory over the Ammonites followed by payments reported across three years.
4. **Theological interpretation (verse 6):** Jotham's might is attributed to preparing his ways before God.
5. **Source notice and regnal close (verses 7–9):** reference to a written record, repeated regnal data, death, burial, and Ahaz's succession.

The repetition of Jotham's age and reign length in verses 1 and 8 frames the central report, though such repetition is also conventional in royal notices.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter contributes to [[Obedience]] by explicitly connecting Jotham's prepared ways before God with his might (2 Chronicles 27:6). This is the narrator's interpretation of one particular reign, not an exceptionless promise that obedience always produces political power, wealth, or military success — the text does not claim the pattern holds universally.

Verse 2 also prevents a simplistic equation of righteous leadership with a righteous population: Jotham receives a positive evaluation while the people continue corruptly. The chapter neither identifies their particular offenses nor claims Jotham reformed them. The note that Jotham "entered not into the temple" (v. 2) touches [[Temples]] indirectly — it records restraint regarding sacred space rather than describing temple ordinances or worship practice.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The clause "Jotham became mighty, because he prepared his ways before the LORD his God" (v. 6) states the narrator's causal interpretation explicitly. "Prepared his ways" can reasonably be read as purposeful ordering or orientation of conduct, though the chapter does not specify every action included in that description. By contrast, "the people did yet corruptly" (v. 2) is broad: it reports continuing wrongdoing without identifying its particular practices, extent, or causes.

The twice-given accession age is not verbatim: verse 1 has "twenty and five years old" while verse 8 has "five and twenty years old" — the same number with reversed word order, alongside a nearly identical reign-length clause. This shows the repetition is a formulaic echo rather than an exact duplicate; whether it reflects stylistic variation or a textual seam cannot be determined from wording alone.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The statement that Jotham "entered not into the temple" gains significance from its position after [[2 Chronicles 26]], where Uzziah unlawfully enters the temple to burn incense and is struck with leprosy. Jotham resembles his father in doing right but is distinguished from that particular transgression. The contrast is strong, although 2 Chronicles 27:2 does not explicitly retell the earlier event or state Jotham's motives, so the connection rests on narrative placement rather than an explicit cross-reference.

Verse 6 functions as a theological culmination of the reports in verses 3-5. The repeated accession age and reign length in verses 1 and 8 loosely bracket this material, though such repetition is unusual compared to the neighboring regnal notices in [[2 Chronicles 26]] and [[2 Chronicles 28]], which do not restate age and reign length near their close — so a deliberate literary frame remains only one possible explanation alongside an ordinary textual seam or source doublet.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Narrative Theological Causation
> Verse 6 strongly supports the conclusion that this narrator interprets Jotham's might as connected to his ordered conduct before God. Because the statement concerns Jotham's particular reign, it provides weak evidence for—and does not establish—an exceptionless rule that obedience always produces political, military, or material success.

> [!evidence] Internal Narrative Causation
> Verse 6 is direct evidence that the narrator interprets Jotham's might as resulting from his having prepared or ordered his ways before the LORD. It strongly establishes the narrator's stated theological explanation, but not the complete historical causation of Jotham's power.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->

- Which aspects of Jotham's conduct might be included in "he prepared his ways," and which remain unstated?
- How does [[2 Chronicles 26]] clarify the statement that Jotham did not enter the temple?
- What does verse 6 claim about this particular reign, and what universal claims does it not make?
- Why is the accession-age formula repeated with reversed word order in verses 1 and 8 — stylistic variation or a textual seam?

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare [[2 Kings 15]] with this chapter. Both accounts include Jotham's regnal data, positive evaluation, construction at the temple's high gate, death, and succession by Ahaz. Chronicles additionally records work at Ophel and elsewhere in Judah, the Ammonite campaign and payments, and the explicit explanation in verse 6. These differences demonstrate selection and emphasis in the extant narratives but do not alone resolve source relationships or historicity.

Read [[2 Chronicles 26]] for the probable background to Jotham's avoidance of temple entry and [[2 Chronicles 28]] for the contrasting reign of Ahaz. Verse 7 also belongs with [[Records and Sacred Writings]], provided its cited work is not automatically equated with the present canonical book of Kings or treated as an independently recoverable witness.
