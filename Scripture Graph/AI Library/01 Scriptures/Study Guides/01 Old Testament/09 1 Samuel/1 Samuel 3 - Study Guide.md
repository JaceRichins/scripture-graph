# 1 Samuel 3 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Samuel 3]] · My notes: [[1 Samuel 3 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
The chapter recounts more than a private call. It marks a transition from scarce revelation to a newly established prophetic ministry. Samuel initially needs Eli's help to recognize the voice, receives an unwelcome judgment against Eli's house, tells Eli everything despite fear, and is subsequently recognized throughout Israel as a prophet. The closing statement that the LORD appeared again at Shiloh answers the opening report that there was no open vision.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Scarcity and setting (vv. 1–3):** Samuel serves at the sanctuary while revelation is rare.
2. **Call and recognition (vv. 4–10):** Three mistaken responses lead Eli to recognize the divine call; Samuel then answers as instructed.
3. **The oracle (vv. 11–14):** The LORD confirms irreversible judgment against Eli's house for known and unrestrained iniquity.
4. **Disclosure to Eli (vv. 15–18):** Samuel fears the task but conceals nothing.
5. **Prophetic establishment (vv. 19–21):** Samuel's dependable words, national recognition, and continuing revelations confirm his role.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter contributes to [[Revelation]] and [[Prophets]] by depicting a call initiated by God but recognized with human mentoring. Prophetic fidelity includes delivering an unwelcome message rather than merely receiving one. Samuel's complete report exemplifies [[Honesty]]. The judgment on Eli's house also places a sharp limit on ritualism: [[Sacrifice]] is not portrayed as a substitute for confronting knowingly tolerated sin. Because the oracle concerns a specified house and judgment, it should not be expanded into a universal denial of repentance or mercy.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The “word of the LORD” provides the chapter's clearest verbal thread: it is scarce in verse 1, not yet revealed to Samuel in verse 7, delivered as judgment in verses 11–14, validated through Samuel in verse 19, and named as the means of renewed revelation in verse 21. “Here am I” occurs five times, marking Samuel's prompt availability before he understands the caller and again when Eli requests the message. The statement that the LORD let none of Samuel's words “fall to the ground” portrays those words as effective or reliably fulfilled. Samuel's hearing in verse 10 and the ears that tingle in verse 11 create a possible verbal echo, but the latter is also a conventional idiom for shocking news, so deliberate wordplay should not be assumed.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter contrasts Samuel's initial inexperience with Eli's ability to recognize the call, even though Eli's house stands under judgment. Its strongest literary frame moves from scarce vision and the rare word of the LORD in verse 1 to renewed appearance and revelation at Shiloh in verse 21. Eli's dim eyes and the lamp not yet extinguished may contribute to a secondary contrast between decline and continuity, but both details also serve ordinary characterization and nighttime scene-setting. The chapter continues the oracle of [[1 Samuel 2]] and anticipates its narrative realization in [[1 Samuel 4]].
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary Characterization And Authorization
> The chapter supplies cumulative internal evidence for its characterization of Samuel as an authorized prophet: divine initiative, receipt of a difficult oracle, complete delivery, continuing divine presence, dependable words, national recognition, and renewed revelation.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · Broadly accepted narrative characterization · [[Literary Characterization And Authorization in 1 Samuel 3|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intra-Canonical Narrative Correspondence
> Within the canonical narrative, later episodes correspond to the judgment oracle: Eli's sons are killed and Eli dies after the ark is captured, while a still later narrator explicitly associates Abiathar's removal with fulfillment of the word concerning Eli's house at Shiloh. These are strong literary links within the narrative but are not independent historical corroboration of supernatural prediction.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 1.0 · Canonical correspondence accepted; historical inference disputed · [[Intra-Canonical Narrative Correspondence in 1 Samuel 3|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary/Symbolic Reading *(tentative)*
> The juxtaposition of Eli's dimming eyes with the not-yet-extinguished 'lamp of God' in the temple is sometimes read as a deliberate literary contrast between a declining old order and renewing revelation, but this symbolic reading goes beyond what the text states outright and cannot be confirmed as authorial intent from the verse alone.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · Plausible but speculative symbolism

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the narrative prolong recognition of the voice through three calls, rather than presenting immediate understanding?
- How does Eli's useful guidance complicate a simple contrast between a rejected old leader and a faithful new one?
- Does Eli's final response express faith, resignation, repentance, or some combination? What does the text leave unstated?
- How do hearing, sight, light, and the divine word interact across the chapter?
- Why is Samuel's first reported oracle directed against the household of the person who raised and instructed him?
