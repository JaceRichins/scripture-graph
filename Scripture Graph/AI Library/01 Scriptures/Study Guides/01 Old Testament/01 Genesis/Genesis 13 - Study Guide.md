14–17 The LORD renews the covenant: land and seed "as the dust of the earth"

The chapter opens and closes with an act of worship (vv. 4, 18), loosely framing the conflict and covenant renewal in between.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **[[Abrahamic Covenant]]**: vv. 14–17 restate and expand the land and posterity promises first given in Genesis 12:7, offered "for ever" and compared to the innumerable "dust of the earth" — an image echoed in Jacob's [[Genesis 28|Bethel vision]] (28:14) and reused with different imagery in Genesis 15:5 and 22:17. The text does not state this renewal was a reward for Abram's conduct toward Lot; it simply follows the separation in sequence.
- **[[Family]] and [[War and Peace]]**: Abram resolves the herdmen's strife (v. 7) through voluntary separation, appealing to kinship ("we be brethren," v. 8) rather than asserting seniority. The text does not spell out Abram's motives beyond the proposal itself, so readings of humility or self-denial remain interpretive.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
- **Lift up thine eyes**: the same idiom appears twice — Lot "lifted up his eyes" to choose land by what looked good (v. 10), and the LORD tells Abram to "lift up now thine eyes" before promising him land (v. 14). The juxtaposition invites comparison between choosing by sight and receiving by promise, though "lift up eyes" is also a common idiom elsewhere in Genesis (18:2; 22:4; 24:63), so an intentionally engineered contrast is not certain.
- **Prolepsis**: v. 10's aside that the plain was lush "before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah," and v. 13's note that Sodom's men were "wicked and sinners...exceedingly," anticipate [[Genesis 19]], coloring Lot's choice with dramatic irony.
- **Altars at both ends**: vv. 4 and 18 bracket the chapter with worship; whether this is deliberate design or simple itinerary notice is not something the text states.
- **Verbal parallel with Genesis 28**: the seed-as-dust image paired with a four-point compass survey (v. 14) recurs in Jacob's covenant at Beth-el (28:14) — the only mechanically verified text-overlap parallel to this chapter.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual/Historical *(tentative)*
> The parenthetical note that Canaanites and Perizzites already dwelled in the land (13:7) is consistent with the patriarchal narratives generally depicting Abram as an immigrant pastoralist among established populations (compare the similar aside in Genesis 12:6), though this brief detail alone cannot independently establish historical settlement patterns or the date of the underlying tradition.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does Abram, not Lot, initiate the separation and offer first choice of land?
- Is the "lift up eyes" echo between vv. 10 and 14 an intentional narrative contrast, or a common idiom used twice?
- Why does the narrator flag Sodom's wickedness (v. 13) well before Lot's own story with the city unfolds?
- What connects this chapter to [[Genesis 12]] and anticipates [[Genesis 19]]?
