# Genesis 3 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Genesis 3]] · My notes: [[Genesis 3 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The serpent (Lucifer) deceives Eve—She and then Adam partake of the forbidden fruit—Her Seed (Christ) will bruise the serpent’s head—The roles of woman and of man are explained—Adam and Eve are cast out of the Garden of Eden—Adam presides—Eve becomes the mother of all living.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Genesis 3:1–5 — Disputed speech:** The serpent questions and contradicts the divine warning.
2. **Genesis 3:6–7 — Choice and recognition:** Seeing, desiring, taking, eating, giving, and opened eyes form a rapid action sequence.
3. **Genesis 3:8–13 — Hiding and interrogation:** Fear and concealment are followed by questions, admissions, and redirected responsibility.
4. **Genesis 3:14–19 — Consequences:** The speeches address the serpent, woman, and man and concern hostility, childbirth, relationship, labor, food, and death. This reverses the man-then-woman order of interrogation, although the serpent was not questioned.
5. **Genesis 3:20–24 — Life outside Eden:** Eve is named, God supplies clothing, and access to perpetual life is barred.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter supplies the biblical narrative foundation for the [[Fall of Adam and Eve]], but several later doctrinal expressions are not stated in its own vocabulary. Genesis explicitly presents disobedience, knowledge of good and evil, return to dust, and loss of access to the tree of life. [[Alma 42]] interprets these conditions as temporal and spiritual death and as a probationary opportunity for repentance. [[Romans 5]] and [[1 Corinthians 15]] develop the Adam-Christ contrast central to the [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]] and [[Resurrection]]. Keeping the narrative and its later interpretations distinct makes those connections clearer rather than weaker.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
A Hebrew sound play joins [[Genesis 2]]:25 to Genesis 3:1: the humans are commonly transliterated *arummim* (naked), while the serpent is *arum* (subtil or crafty). The immediate juxtaposition makes deliberate literary shaping likely, although it does not establish a date, author, or genre.

The woman's account of the command also differs from the wording narrated in [[Genesis 2]]:17: it adds "neither shall ye touch it" and uses "lest ye die" in place of "thou shalt surely die." The text does not explain whether these differences reflect paraphrase, transmission, protective expansion, or an unrecorded instruction. The serpent then denies the warning while emphasizing opened eyes and knowledge—effects the narrative partly affirms. This permits a selective-truth reading of the temptation, but it does not eliminate readings in which the serpent's denial is understood as directly false.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Irony drives the chapter. The humans seek opened eyes, but their first recognition is nakedness; they seek elevated knowledge, but respond by hiding among created trees; Adam redirects blame toward the woman God gave him, and the woman redirects it toward the serpent. Earth imagery binds creation, labor, and death together: the man taken from the ground must struggle with the cursed ground until he returns to it. The final guarding of the tree of life gives the narrative a spatial image for the transition from garden life to mortality.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How much of the serpent's persuasiveness depends on saying things that later prove partly true? Why does God question the humans before announcing consequences? Does Genesis 3:16 prescribe male rule, or describe a damaged relationship—and what evidence from the grammar and wider creation narrative bears on that question? What is contributed by God's act of clothing the humans after their disobedience? How do the interpretations in [[Alma 42]] and [[Romans 5]] extend, rather than merely repeat, Genesis 3?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the command in [[Genesis 2]] with the three versions of it in Genesis 3:1-4. Trace the ground and dust vocabulary through Genesis 2:7, 2:15, 3:17-19, and 3:23. Then compare the guarded tree of life with [[Alma 42]], the theological interpretation of the Fall in [[2 Nephi 2]], and Paul's Adam-Christ argument in [[Romans 5]] and [[1 Corinthians 15]]. [[Moses 4]] is especially useful for identifying which details belong to Genesis's wording and which come from Latter-day Saint revelatory expansion.
