# Genesis 7 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Genesis 7]] · My notes: [[Genesis 7 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Genesis 7 narrates the execution of the instructions begun in [[Genesis 6]]. Noah obeys; his household and the animals enter the ark; the LORD shuts him in; and the waters rise until dry-land life outside the ark is destroyed. The chapter holds catastrophic judgment beside the preservation of a household and a living remnant. Its closing statement that the waters prevailed for 150 days leads directly into [[Genesis 8]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Instruction and response (vv. 1-5):** Noah's household is summoned, animal quantities are specified, a seven-day warning is given, and Noah obeys.

2. **Entry and onset (vv. 6-16):** Noah's age, the family and animals, and the precise flood date are given through overlapping entry notices; the LORD shuts Noah in.

3. **Waters prevail and dry-land life perishes (vv. 17-24):** Repeated increase-and-prevail language carries the account from the ark's being lifted to comprehensive coverage and death.

The recurring entry notices may be read as summary and expansion, recapitulation, or evidence of compositional coordination; Genesis 7 does not specify their relationship.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The clearest connection to [[Obedience]] is explicit: Noah does according to all that the LORD commanded (v. 5), and later notices repeat the command-fulfillment pattern (vv. 9, 16). The chapter also places [[Family]] within the account of preservation, but it attributes righteousness explicitly to Noah rather than individually to every relative.

The juxtaposition of destruction and preservation can inform reflection on [[Justice and Mercy]], but "mercy" is an interpretation of the preservation rather than the chapter's own label. Genesis 7 reports the judgment without answering every moral question it raises; [[Genesis 6]] supplies more of the stated rationale.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Time markers give the narrative a measured progression: seven days, Noah's six hundredth year, a precise month and day, forty days and nights, and 150 days. Such precision is a textual feature, not independent proof of historicity.

"By sevens" may mean seven animals or seven pairs; the KJV wording alone is not decisive. "Two and two" can describe paired entry rather than the total number of each clean kind, although Genesis 7 does not explicitly reconcile the formulations.

The phrases "under the whole heaven" and "all flesh" are strongly comprehensive within the narrative. Moving from that literary scope to a modern geological conclusion requires evidence beyond this chapter. Verse 22's qualification "in the dry land" also means the text does not explicitly report the death of aquatic life.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter's dominant movement is escalation: the waters increase, prevail, lift the ark, and culminate in comprehensive coverage. The same waters that carry the ark bring death to dry-land life outside it.

The catalog of animals after their kinds, male and female, creeping things, birds, humanity, and the breath of life recalls [[Genesis 1]] and supports a reading of the flood as an undoing of [[Creation]]. The preserved remnant anticipates renewed habitation in [[Genesis 8]]. Shared subject matter can explain some of this vocabulary, so intentional literary reversal is supported rather than proved.

The repeated entry notices, animal-number formulations, and alternation between LORD and God are genuine compositional data. Source-critical coordination, final-form recapitulation, rhetorical variation, and oral-style repetition remain viable explanations; the chapter alone does not establish a particular reconstruction.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Textual Scope
> Genesis 7 uses strongly comprehensive language to present the flood's scope within the narrative: all high hills under the whole heaven are covered, all dry-land creatures with the breath of life die, and Noah's company alone survives. This is strong evidence for the text's portrayal of a universal catastrophe, but the wording alone cannot determine the event's geological extent.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · strong textual evidence; referential scope disputed · [[Internal Textual Scope in Genesis 7|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Narrative-Scope
> The chapter's English narrative depicts the catastrophe in comprehensive terms: all hills under the whole heaven are covered, all moving flesh on earth dies, and only Noah and those with him remain alive. This is strong evidence for the totalizing scope of the narrated judgment, but internal narrative language alone cannot settle the event's physical geography or its relationship to modern geological reconstruction.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why is Noah explicitly called righteous when his whole household is preserved?

- Does "by sevens" denote individuals or pairs, and does "two and two" describe quantity or arrangement?

- Are the repeated entry notices successive stages, recapitulations, or signs of compositional coordination?

- How does the creation vocabulary shape the account of destruction and preservation?

- How should readers distinguish comprehensive narrative scope from claims about physical or geological extent?

- What account of the judgment is supplied by [[Genesis 6]], and what moral questions remain unanswered?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[Genesis 6]], Genesis 7, and [[Genesis 8]] continuously, tracking commands, fulfillment notices, dates, durations, and changes in the waters. Compare Genesis 7:14-16 and 21-23 with [[Genesis 1]] and Genesis 2:7 to test the creation-reversal reading. Compare the clean/non-clean distinction with Leviticus 11 while keeping narrative chronology separate from claims about composition date. For later reception, examine Hebrews 11:7, Matthew 24:37-39, and 1 Peter 3:20-21, noting their distinct emphases on faith, sudden judgment, and baptismal typology rather than automatically treating them as exhaustive statements of Genesis's original meaning.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
