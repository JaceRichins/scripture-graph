## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Material-Cultural Fit *(tentative)*
> The plain of Shinar and the explicit substitution of fired brick for stone and “slime” for mortar are consistent with southern Mesopotamian building conditions and techniques. This is meaningful geographical and cultural fit, but it is not independent proof that the narrated tower existed.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.9 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation

> [!evidence] Linguistic-Literary Wordplay *(tentative)*
> Genesis 11:9 presents an intentional Hebrew sound-play between the name Babel and the verb translated “confound.” This functions as a narrative explanation of the name, even though the historically accepted Akkadian derivation of Babylon's name is different.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · broadly accepted

> [!evidence] Historical/Chronological *(tentative)*
> The chapter twice locates the family's origin as 'Ur of the Chaldees.' The term 'Chaldees/Chaldeans' is elsewhere associated with a people and designation more prominent in the first millennium BC, which raises a disputed question about how this designation relates to the much earlier traditional dating of the patriarchal period.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · disputed

> [!evidence] Philological/Literary *(tentative)*
> Genesis 11:9 gives an explicit in-text folk etymology linking the name 'Babel' to God having there 'confounded' (Hebrew balal, 'to confuse') the language of the earth. This is a recognized Hebrew wordplay (paronomasia) rather than the name's actual historical-linguistic derivation.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.8 · broadly accepted
<!-- SG:END evidence -->
