# Genesis 18 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Genesis 18]] · My notes: [[Genesis 18 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Genesis 18 joins three scenes: Abraham's hospitality to three visitors (vv. 1–8), the renewed promise that Sarah will bear a son (vv. 9–15), and Abraham's intercession concerning Sodom (vv. 16–33). The chapter connects the [[Abrahamic Covenant]] with both divine power and human ethical responsibility. Abraham is promised descendants and worldwide blessing, but he is also expected to teach his household to practice justice and judgment.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Hospitality at Mamre (vv. 1–8):** Abraham receives and feeds three men.
2. **Promise and laughter (vv. 9–15):** Sarah hears that she will bear a son; ordinary impossibility is answered by the question, “Is any thing too hard for the LORD?”
3. **Disclosure and vocation (vv. 16–21):** The LORD relates Abraham's promised future to his duty to form a just household, then announces an investigation of Sodom and Gomorrah.
4. **Intercession (vv. 22–33):** Abraham asks whether the righteous will perish with the wicked and successively lowers the proposed number from fifty to ten.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter holds [[Justice and Mercy]] together rather than treating them as opposites. Abraham does not deny the seriousness of Sodom's reported sin; he questions indiscriminate judgment and asks whether a righteous minority might bring mercy to the whole place. The LORD accepts every numerical proposal Abraham actually makes. The exchange therefore presents intercession as meaningful, but it does not explain whether Abraham changes a settled divine decision or is being drawn into understanding divine justice.

Genesis 18:19 also makes family instruction part of covenant life. Abraham is to direct his children and household toward the way of the LORD, specifically “to do justice and judgment.” This connects [[Family]], [[Obedience]], and the [[Abrahamic Covenant]] without reducing the covenant either to ancestry alone or to a simple system of earned blessing.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter's identity language is deliberately fluid: 18:1 says the LORD appeared, 18:2 introduces three men, dialogue shifts between plural ('they said,' v.5) and singular ('the LORD said,' vv.9-10,13), and v.22 splits 'the men' (bound for Sodom) from Abraham 'yet before the LORD.' The text itself never assigns a specific identity to each of the three; readers should preserve this ambiguity rather than resolve it. The recurring verb 'laughed' (vv.12-15) continues wordplay on Isaac's name begun when Abraham laughed at the same news in [[Genesis 17]] (17:17), later made explicit when Sarah says 'God hath made me to laugh' at his birth ([[Genesis 21]] 21:6). 'Time of life' (vv.10,14) marks the promised season without further explanation, and 'go down now, and see' (v.21) is investigative narrative language whose implications for divine knowledge this chapter alone cannot settle.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 1-8 move quickly: Abraham runs, hastens, and has a calf 'hasted' to prepare, conveying urgent hospitality. Verses 9-15 follow a birth-announcement pattern (an aged woman told she will bear a son, responding with doubt) also present in Genesis 17. Verses 23-32 shift to a slower, formal, repeated petition-and-response structure descending through fifty, forty-five, forty, thirty, twenty, and ten, each exchange varying its deferential formula ('dust and ashes,' v.27; 'let not the Lord be angry,' vv.30,32) without a strictly increasing escalation. The ordered descent highlights Abraham's persistence and the LORD's repeated willingness to spare the city for a shrinking righteous minority.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the narrator move between “the LORD,” “three men,” plural speech, and a singular speaker without identifying every visitor explicitly?
- What does Genesis 18:19 suggest about the relationship between covenant promises and the practice of justice?
- Is Abraham attempting to change God's intention, discovering its limits, or participating in a disclosure meant to form his judgment?
- Why does Abraham stop at ten? The text records no explanation, so should ten be treated as a universal threshold?
- How does [[Genesis 19]] answer Abraham's concern about the righteous perishing with the wicked, and how does it leave his request for the whole city unresolved?
