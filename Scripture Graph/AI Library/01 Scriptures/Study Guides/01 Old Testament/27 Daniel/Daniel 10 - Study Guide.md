## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Setting and preparation (10:1-4):** Cyrus's third year, three weeks of mourning and abstinence, and the river setting.
2. **The glorious appearance (10:5-9):** Daniel sees the linen-clothed figure, his companions flee, and Daniel collapses.
3. **Commission and reported delay (10:10-14):** Daniel is raised, called greatly beloved, and told of the twenty-one-day resistance and Michael's assistance.
4. **Renewed incapacity and strengthening (10:15-19):** Daniel again becomes unable to speak or continue in his own strength; touch and reassurance restore him.
5. **Transition to the disclosure (10:20-21):** Persia, Grecia, Michael, and the 'scripture of truth' establish the horizon for [[Daniel 11]] and [[Daniel 12]].

Daniel's recovery is recurrent rather than a simple uninterrupted progression: he stands in verse 11 but later returns to a face-down, speechless condition.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Daniel 10 distinguishes being heard from receiving an immediately visible answer. The messenger says Daniel's words were heard from the first day he set his heart to understand and chastened himself before God, yet the messenger reports twenty-one days of resistance (10:12-13). This supports trust amid delayed answers to [[Prayer]], but it does not teach that every delay has the same cause.

The chapter also portrays strength as received. Daniel cannot continue until heavenly touch, peace, and the command to be strong restore him (10:18-19). This can inform reflection on [[Grace]], while remaining a theological application rather than Daniel's own terminology. The matched three-week and twenty-one-day periods connect Daniel's sustained seeking with the unseen conflict in the narrative; they do not establish that his abstinence caused or altered that conflict.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated address 'a man greatly beloved' frames correction and reassurance with divine regard (10:11, 19). Likewise, 'fear not,' 'peace be unto thee,' and 'be strong' are performative within the story: after the words are spoken, Daniel says, 'thou hast strengthened me.'

The chapter repeatedly uses comparison rather than simple identification: 'like the beryl,' 'as the appearance of lightning,' 'as lamps of fire,' 'like in colour to polished brass,' and figures 'like' human beings. This language reports perceived likenesses while preserving the strangeness of the vision.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Daniel 10 develops encounter patterns also found in [[Daniel 8]]: a humanlike heavenly appearance, a face-down seer, touch, restoration, interpretation concerning a distant horizon, and physical exhaustion. The linen-clothed figure also resembles the figure in [[Revelation 1]] through golden clothing, fiery eyes, brasslike limbs or feet, a powerful voice, the seer's collapse, touch, and 'fear not.' These are strong literary and thematic correspondences, but they do not establish that the two passages identify the same being.

The three weeks of Daniel's mourning equal the messenger's twenty-one days of resistance. This creates a tight internal correspondence and integrates the earthly and unseen scenes, though it does not independently verify the conflict's metaphysical nature. The chapter also leaves open whether the glorious figure, the later speaker, and the humanlike figures who touch Daniel are one being or several.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Chronological Correspondence
> Daniel's three full weeks of mourning correspond exactly to the messenger's twenty-one days of resistance by the prince of Persia. This is strong internal evidence that the narrative deliberately connects Daniel's sustained seeking with an unseen delay in the response.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · strong internal correspondence; significance bounded · [[Internal Chronological Correspondence in Daniel 10|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intertextual Descriptive Parallel
> The figure in Daniel 10:5-6 and the figure in Revelation 1:13-16 share a golden girdle, fiery eyes, brasslike limbs or feet, and an immense voice; both visions also include the seer's collapse, a strengthening touch, and 'fear not.' These correspondences strongly support a literary relationship or shared visionary idiom and help explain a Christological reading of Daniel's figure, but they do not settle his identity within Daniel 10.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · strong descriptive parallel; identity disputed · [[Intertextual Descriptive Parallel in Daniel 10|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intertextual/Literary Analysis *(tentative)*
> The physical description of the being in Daniel 10:5-6 (linen garment, golden girdle, glowing body, fiery eyes, burnished-metal limbs, thunderous voice) closely parallels John's description of the glorified Christ in Revelation 1, a resemblance widely noted by commentators as literary/thematic dependence of later apocalyptic theophany imagery on Daniel.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.7 · real parallel; dependence and identity disputed

> [!evidence] Comparative Biblical Theology / Second Temple Angelology *(tentative)*
> Daniel's language of national 'princes' contending in a heavenly realm behind the visible conflict of Persia and Greece reflects an ancient Israelite/Second Temple concept of spiritual powers standing behind earthly kingdoms (compare the divine allotment of nations in Deuteronomy 32:8-9), rather than describing merely human political resistance.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does the chapter present one principal heavenly visitor throughout, or move among the glorious linen-clothed man and one or more humanlike messengers?
- What does 'prince' signify when applied to Persia, Grecia, and Michael? Does it describe personal heavenly beings, symbolic personifications, or some relationship between heavenly and earthly rule?
- Why does the narrative stress that Daniel's words were heard on the first day even though the messenger arrived only after twenty-one days?
- What is the 'scripture of truth'—a heavenly record or decree, the authoritative disclosure that follows, or both?
- How does [[Daniel 12]] clarify the description of Michael as 'your prince'?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Daniel's collapse, touch, restoration, and distant-horizon commission in [[Daniel 8]]; the repeated address 'greatly beloved' in [[Daniel 9]]; Michael's relationship to Daniel's people in [[Daniel 12]]; and the glorious figure, collapse, touch, and reassurance in [[Revelation 1]]. Ezekiel 1 offers a broader comparison for radiant glory imagery and a prophet's face-down response, while Ezekiel 3 provides a more limited comparison involving prophetic speechlessness.
