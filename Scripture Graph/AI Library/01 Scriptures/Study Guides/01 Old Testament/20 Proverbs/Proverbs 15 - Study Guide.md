# Proverbs 15 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Proverbs 15]] · My notes: [[Proverbs 15 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> A soft answer turns away wrath—A wise son makes a glad father—The thoughts of the wicked are an abomination to the Lord—Before honor comes humility.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter is better read as a collection with recurring motifs than as a continuous argument. Verses 1-12 emphasize speech, divine scrutiny, righteousness, and responses to correction. Verses 13-24 turn toward the heart, contentment, anger, family, counsel, and contrasting ways. Verses 25-33 revisit pride, speech, greed, prayer, reproof, wisdom, and humility. These boundaries are interpretive rather than explicit, since several themes cross them.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verses 3 and 11 portray the LORD’s knowledge as extending to every place and to human hearts; verse 26 applies divine moral judgment to thoughts. Verses 8 and 29 associate accepted [[Prayer]] with upright or righteous character. Verse 8 suggests that [[Sacrifice]] does not compensate for wicked conduct, but it does not condemn sacrifice itself. Verses 16–17 prefer fear of the LORD and love over abundance accompanied by trouble or hatred. The chapter repeatedly commends correction and understanding, while verse 25 condemns [[Pride]] and verse 33 places [[Humility]] before honor.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Speech is evaluated by both source and effect. Wise lips disperse knowledge (verse 7), a timely answer brings joy (verse 23), and a wholesome tongue gives life (verse 4). By contrast, foolish or wicked mouths repeatedly 'pour out' foolishness or evil (verses 2 and 28). That repeated verb creates an image of uncontrolled output, while the righteous heart 'studieth to answer,' suggesting deliberation before speech.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Antithetical parallelism drives much of the chapter: soft and grievous speech, wise and foolish mouths, righteous and wicked houses, love and hatred, pride and the widow, and accepting or refusing reproof. Verses 16-17 form a compact pair of comparative sayings beginning with 'Better,' and verses 31-33 supply a closing sequence from hearing reproof, to gaining understanding, to wisdom and humility. The image of the [[Tree of Life]] in verse 4 makes speech a source of either vitality or inward rupture.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How do verses 1, 18, 23, and 28 distinguish gentleness from merely saying whatever will avoid conflict? Why does the chapter repeatedly connect wisdom with receiving correction rather than only possessing information? How do verses 6, 16, 17, and 27 qualify simplistic readings of prosperity? What does the contrast between sacrifice and prayer in verse 8 imply about worship and character? How should verse 29 be read without turning suffering or delayed answers to prayer into evidence of wickedness?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Proverbs 15:16-17 with [[Proverbs 17]], especially Proverbs 17:1, on peace and modest provision. Compare Proverbs 15:18 with [[Proverbs 16]], especially Proverbs 16:32, on restraint of anger. Study the instruction and reproof sayings alongside [[Education and Learning]], and read verses 25 and 33 together when studying [[Pride]] and [[Humility]].
