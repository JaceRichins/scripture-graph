# Proverbs 19 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Proverbs 19]] · My notes: [[Proverbs 19 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Proverbs 19 gathers sayings about integrity, teachability, anger, family life, work, rulers, and treatment of the poor. A particularly clear thread contrasts social preference for wealth with the superior value of integrity and kindness (verses 1, 4, 6-7, 17, 22). The chapter also gives unusual emphasis to truthful testimony by repeating the judgment against a false witness in verses 5 and 9. Its statements are compact wisdom sayings rather than a continuous argument, so apparent themes should not be forced into a rigid system.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious thematic outline is: integrity, knowledge, and wealth-shaped relationships (verses 1-9); authority, anger, and household life (verses 10-14); diligence, obedience, care for the poor, and correction (verses 15-20); divine counsel, fear of the LORD, and further warnings about sloth and resistance to instruction (verses 21-29). This outline describes clusters, not an unmistakable formal division. The repeated false-witness saying in verses 5 and 9 is the clearest local structural marker.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Several sayings carry doctrinal weight beyond ordinary practical advice. Verse 17 treats kindness to the poor as a loan extended to the LORD who will repay it, connecting practical compassion to [[Charity]] — though the text does not specify the form or timing of repayment and should not be read as a guaranteed formula of material return. Verses 2, 8, 20, 25, and 27 tie wisdom to receptivity — hearing counsel, keeping understanding, and rejecting instruction that leads away from knowledge — relevant to [[Education and Learning]]. Verse 11 commends restrained anger and passing over an offense, touching on [[Justice and Mercy]] without requiring that every grievance be ignored. The repeated warning against false witnesses (vv.5, 9) and the 'ungodly witness' of v.28 make [[Honesty]] a structural concern of the chapter. Verse 21 sets the many 'devices' of a human heart against 'the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand' — affirming that human planning is real while final outcomes rest with divine purpose, without resolving how the two interact philosophically. Verse 3 quietly resists the impulse to blame God for troubles actually caused by one's own foolishness, a theme relevant to [[Agency]] and moral accountability. Verse 14 sets a prudent wife apart from ordinary inherited 'house and riches' as something 'from the LORD,' touching on [[Family]] and [[Marriage]].

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter frequently uses comparative sayings: the poor person of integrity is better than a perverse fool (verse 1), and a poor person is better than a liar (verse 22). These comparisons establish moral priority without saying that poverty itself makes a person righteous. Verse 17's lending language is metaphorical: the LORD is represented as accepting an obligation created by generosity to the poor. Verse 24 uses deliberate exaggeration to make sloth appear absurdly self-defeating.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Proverbs 19 relies heavily on antithesis: wealth and poverty, wisdom and folly, diligence and sloth, human plans and divine counsel. It also uses vivid comparisons—the king's anger as a roaring lion, his favor as dew, and household contention as continual dripping. The near repetition of verses 5 and 9 deserves attention, but by itself it supports only a local emphasis on false testimony, not a claim that the entire chapter has an intricate hidden design.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How do verses 4, 6, and 7 expose the instability of relationships organized around material advantage? What limits should be placed on verse 11's counsel to pass over a transgression? Does verse 17 promise repayment in material terms, or does its unspecified language resist that conclusion? How should the general promise of verse 23 be read alongside the observable suffering of God-fearing people? What distinction does verse 25 draw between correction that warns an observer and correction that teaches its direct recipient?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Proverbs 19:1 with [[Proverbs 28]], especially Proverbs 28:6, noting both the shared better-poor formula and the different descriptions of perversity. Study verses 5, 9, and 28 together when examining truthful witness and judgment. For thematic study, trace the chapter's contributions to [[Honesty]], [[Charity]], [[Education and Learning]], [[Family]], and [[Work and Self-Reliance]].
