# Joshua 1 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Joshua 1]] · My notes: [[Joshua 1 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The Lord speaks to Joshua—He is commanded to be of good courage, to meditate upon the law, and to keep the commandments—He prepares Israel to enter Canaan.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
- **vv.1-2** — Notice of Moses' death; the Lord speaks directly to Joshua.
- **vv.3-5** — The land grant, described in maximal boundary terms, with the promise of the Lord's unfailing presence.
- **vv.6-9** — Charge to be strong and courageous, with success conditioned on keeping and meditating on the law (the phrase recurs in vv.6, 7, 9, and again in v.18).
- **vv.10-11** — Joshua's first command as leader: prepare provisions to cross the Jordan in three days.
- **vv.12-15** — Joshua reminds the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh of Moses' earlier instruction ([[Deuteronomy 3]]) that their armed men cross ahead of their brethren until those tribes also receive rest.
- **vv.16-18** — The response of these tribes: a pledge of obedience to Joshua, conditioned on the Lord being with him as he was with Moses, closing with a final 'be strong and of a good courage.'
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **[[Law of Moses]]** and **[[Obedience]]** — Joshua's own authority is repeatedly subordinated to 'all the law which Moses my servant commanded'; his success is conditioned on fidelity to it, not granted unconditionally.
- **[[Scripture Study]]** — v.8 ties recitation ('shall not depart out of thy mouth'), meditation ('day and night'), and action ('observe to do') together as the stated condition for prosperity and good success. This promise is addressed to Joshua's specific commission, not a general guarantee of material reward for scripture readers.
- **[[Covenants]]** — the land is given 'which I sware unto their fathers,' tying the conquest to the patriarchal promise rather than to Israel's own merit; the tribes' pledge in vv.16-18 functions as a loyalty commitment to the new leader, conditioned on the Lord's presence with him.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Several phrases in this chapter recur across Deuteronomy and Joshua rather than being unique to this scene: 'be strong and of a good courage' and 'I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee' (both in [[Deuteronomy 31]]), and 'turn not...to the right hand or to the left' (Deuteronomy 17; [[Joshua 23]]). Whether this reflects independently preserved speeches, a single author or editor shaping both texts, or simply a conventional idiom for undeviating obedience is disputed; the repetition itself is a verified textual feature that ties Joshua 1 into the wider Deuteronomy-Joshua language.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The repeated 'be strong and of a good courage' (vv.6, 7, 9, 18) links the Lord's commission to the tribes' response, and the recurring 'as I was with Moses' / 'as he was with Moses' frames Joshua's authority as continuous with Moses' while grounding it in the Lord's presence rather than Joshua's status alone. This close parallel with Moses' own charge to Joshua in [[Deuteronomy 31]] is a strong literary and thematic connection; it does not by itself establish whether the two passages preserve independent historical speeches or reflect a single editorial hand shaping both.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary And Intertextual
> Joshua 1 closely parallels Moses's earlier public commissioning of Joshua: both associate courage with entering and inheriting the land, and both ground Joshua's confidence in the Lord's unfailing presence. This is strong internal evidence that the narrative deliberately presents Joshua as Moses's authorized successor.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · strong parallel, alternatives stated honestly · [[Literary And Intertextual in Joshua 1|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Historical-Geography *(tentative)*
> The land boundaries given in verse 4 (from the wilderness and Lebanon to the Euphrates, and to the Mediterranean) describe a maximal, aspirational extent of territory rather than land Israel is known to have controlled continuously; this bears on how literally the promise in this chapter should be read as a historical land grant versus a rhetorical/covenantal ideal.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.6 · boundary text solid; historical claim uncited
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the narrative make Joshua's authority explicitly contingent on his own obedience to 'the book of the law,' rather than granting it unconditionally?
- What is the effect of having the Transjordan tribes' obligation (vv.12-15) stated as a reminder of Moses' earlier command rather than as a new demand from Joshua?
- The land boundaries in v.4 reach to the Euphrates — what would be needed, beyond this chapter, to know how fully that grant was ever realized historically?
- Why might 'be strong and of a good courage' need to be repeated four times in eighteen verses?
- The death sanction in v.18 is proposed by the tribes themselves, not commanded by the Lord in this chapter — how should that distinction shape how the verse is read?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
