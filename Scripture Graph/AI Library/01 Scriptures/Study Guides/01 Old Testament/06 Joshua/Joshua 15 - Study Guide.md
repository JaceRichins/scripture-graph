## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Boundary description** (1-12): south, east, north, and west borders traced landmark by landmark, closing with "the coast of the children of Judah round about according to their families" (12).
2. **Caleb-Achsah-Othniel narrative** (13-19): framed by the same closing formula in vv. 12 and 20, setting it apart as its own unit — Hebron's conquest, Othniel's capture of Debir/Kirjath-sepher, and Achsah's request for springs of water.
3. **City catalog by district** (20-62): the Negev/south (21-32, stated total "twenty and nine"); the lowland "valley" in three sub-lists (33-44, totals 14+16+9); a separate, untotaled notice of Philistine coastal territory — Ekron, and the district near Ashdod and Gaza (45-47); the hill country in five sub-lists (48-60, totals 11+9+10+6+2); and the wilderness (61-62, total 6).
4. **Closing note** (63): the unfinished conquest of Jerusalem.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter juxtaposes two notes on covenant faithfulness without fully resolving the tension between them. Caleb's inheritance is explicitly tied to "the commandment of the LORD to Joshua" (13); read alongside [[Joshua 14]]'s account of Moses' earlier oath, this presents Hebron as a promise kept, connected to Caleb's earlier [[Faith]] as one of the twelve spies. Against this stands the closing note (63) that Judah "could not drive" the Jebusites from Jerusalem — an unresolved gap between assigned inheritance and actual possession. The text does not explain the failure or moralize about it; it simply distinguishes what Judah was given from what Judah controlled, worth noting before reading this chapter as teaching a settled doctrine of [[Obedience]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Several explanatory formulas preserve alternate place names: Baalah is Kirjath-jearim, Kirjath-sannah is Debir, and Kirjath-arba is Hebron. Such equivalences help readers follow a landscape known by multiple names. They may preserve different naming periods or source traditions, but the equivalences alone cannot determine when the chapter was composed.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The most notable literary feature is that verses 12 and 20 both close with "...according to their families," setting the Caleb-Achsah-Othniel unit (13-19) apart from the surrounding boundary and city material — a pattern consistent with, but not proof of, a separately circulating tradition incorporated by the compiler. The three Anakim named as driven out by Caleb in v. 14 (Sheshai, Ahiman, Talmai) are the same three the spies reported seeing at Hebron in [[Numbers 13]] 13:22, a striking recurrence, though whether it functions as a deliberate literary bookend or simply reflects a fixed local tradition about Hebron's inhabitants is an open question. The Caleb-Achsah-Othniel account (13-19) closely parallels [[Judges 1]] 1:10-15; given how closely the wording matches, this is better read as one tradition preserved in two places than as two independent witnesses to the same event. The city lists read as an administrative document rather than a literary composition, most sub-lists closed by an explicit numeric total — though the Negev sub-list's stated total of "twenty and nine" (32) does not match the number of names actually given in vv. 21-32, a long-noted textual crux (compare the compound-looking entries "Hazor, Hadattah" and "Kerioth, and Hezron" in v. 25), and the Ekron/Ashdod/Gaza notice (45-47) has no total at all.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the compiler set the Caleb-Achsah story apart within the boundary-and-city record rather than placing it beside [[Joshua 14]]?
- What accounts for the mismatch between the stated total of 29 towns in v. 32 and the longer list of names preceding it?
- Does Achsah's request in vv. 18-19 reflect a broader legal custom of granting land to daughters, or is it a singular, personal episode?
- How should the frank admission in v. 63 (Jebusites not driven out) be read alongside its near-twin in [[Judges 1]] 1:21, which attributes the same failure to Benjamin rather than Judah?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Historical geographers have long used the density of named landmarks in vv. 1-12 and the district lists in vv. 21-62 to attempt reconstructions of ancient Judahite boundaries (e.g., Yohanan Aharoni, *The Land of the Bible*), though the date and original administrative purpose of the underlying list is disputed, with proposals ranging from a conquest-era record to a later monarchic administrative document. Compare the near-duplicate Caleb-Achsah-Othniel account in [[Judges 1]] 1:10-15, the southern boundary parallel in [[Numbers 34]], Benjamin's overlapping boundary in [[Joshua 18]], and the reappearance of Hebron and Debir as Levitical cities in [[Joshua 21]] (which explicitly retains Hebron's fields and villages for Caleb, v. 12).
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
