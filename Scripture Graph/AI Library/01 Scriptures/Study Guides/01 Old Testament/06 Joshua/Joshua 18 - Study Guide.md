## Overview

<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Shiloh and the unfinished allotment (vv. 1–3):** The land is called subdued, yet seven tribes remain without inheritances.
2. **Survey and allocation procedure (vv. 4–10):** Representatives divide the territory into seven descriptions, record it by cities in a book, and return for the casting of lots.
3. **Benjamin's boundary (vv. 11–20):** The territory lies between Judah and Joseph and is traced by four boundary segments.
4. **Benjamin's cities (vv. 21–28):** Two groups are counted as twelve and fourteen cities, repeatedly ordered according to families.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Joshua's question in verse 3 places [[Agency]] beside divine promise: Israel regards the land as given by the LORD but is still accountable to act. The repeated phrase "before the LORD" gives the allotment a sacred setting, though it does not independently verify every human decision as divine. Verse 7 also distinguishes territorial wealth from Levi's stated inheritance in the [[Priesthood]].

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The KJV repeatedly uses **coast** for a boundary, including Benjamin's inland and Jordan-side limits; readers should not assume that every occurrence means a seacoast. The text also preserves changing or alternate place names: Luz/Beth-el, Kirjath-baal/Kirjath-jearim, and Jebusi/Jerusalem. Such explanatory identifications help readers correlate the boundary description with the city list but do not by themselves reveal when the explanations were added.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
A productive tension governs the chapter: the land is already "subdued" (v. 1), but seven tribes have not received inheritances and are rebuked for delay (vv. 2–3). The narrative therefore distinguishes broad subjugation from completed allotment and possession. This distinction becomes clearer when Joshua 18:28 is read with [[Joshua 15]] and [[Judges 1]], where Jerusalem remains associated with Jebusite inhabitants.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. What different stages might be covered by the words **subdue**, **receive an inheritance**, and **possess**?
2. Why does the narrative place both the tabernacle and the allotment procedure at Shiloh?
3. What is gained by recording the survey "by cities" in a book before casting lots?
4. How do Joshua 18:28, Joshua 15:63, and Judges 1:21 complicate a simple equation between territorial assignment and actual control?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the allotment-by-lot rule in [[Numbers 26]] with its implementation here and continuation in [[Joshua 19]]. For Levi's exceptional inheritance, compare [[Numbers 18]] and [[Joshua 13]]. Read Jerusalem's placement alongside [[Joshua 15]] and [[Judges 1]]. Shiloh's later importance and fate can be traced through [[1 Samuel 1]] and [[Jeremiah 7]]. These comparisons illuminate the narrative's categories but do not settle archaeological questions or the precise date represented by every boundary list.
