# Joshua 4 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Joshua 4]] · My notes: [[Joshua 4 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Joshua 4 completes the crossing begun in [[Joshua 3]]. At the Lord's command, twelve tribal representatives carry twelve stones from the Jordan to Gilgal as a memorial designed to prompt future children to ask about the crossing. Verse 9 also reports Joshua placing twelve stones where the priests stood in the river; whether this reflects a distinct monument or a compositional doublet remains disputed. After the ark-bearing priests leave the Jordan, the waters return. The Lord magnifies Joshua before Israel, and the chapter explicitly compares the crossing with the Red Sea deliverance.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter unfolds in five movements:

1. **Command and execution (vv. 1–8):** twelve tribal representatives remove twelve stones from the priests' standing place.
2. **Completion of the crossing (vv. 9–18):** Joshua places stones in the Jordan, the people and armed eastern tribes cross, Joshua is magnified, and the priests leave before the waters return.
3. **Arrival at Gilgal (vv. 19–20):** the date and camp are specified, and the carried stones are erected.
4. **Instruction for children (vv. 21–23):** future questions about the stones are answered by recounting the dry-ground crossing and comparing it with the Red Sea.
5. **Theological purpose (v. 24):** all peoples are to know the Lord's might, and Israel is to fear him continually.

The repeated question about the stones in verses 6–7 and 21–23 binds the command to its later explanation. It is a thematic repetition, not a precise chapter-wide inclusio.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter joins divine deliverance with disciplined remembrance. Israel is commanded to preserve and teach the meaning of God's act through a visible memorial and a repeated explanation to children, making [[Family]] and [[Education and Learning]] especially relevant. The repeated title "ark of the covenant" also connects the account with [[Covenants]], although the stones are explicitly said to memorialize the crossing rather than a covenant-renewal ceremony. Verse 24 gives the event a wider theological purpose: all peoples are to know the might of the Lord's hand, while Israel is to fear the Lord continually.

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Repetition shapes the account. The statement that all the people had crossed appears near the opening and completion of the event (vv. 1, 11), while the children's question about the stones appears in verses 6–7 and again in verses 21–23. The later explanation expands the first by emphasizing dry ground and explicitly comparing the Jordan with the Red Sea. Joshua's magnification in 4:14 also completes the promise in [[Joshua 3]]. Verse 9's stones in the Jordan and verse 20's stones at Gilgal naturally read as two placements in the final text, but some readers understand verse 9 as a doublet or variant tradition; the chapter alone cannot settle its compositional history. The phrase "unto this day" marks a retrospective viewpoint but cannot establish a date or author.
<!-- SG:END literary -->
