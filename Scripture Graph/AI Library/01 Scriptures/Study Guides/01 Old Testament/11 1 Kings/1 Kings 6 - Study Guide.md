# 1 Kings 6 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Kings 6]] · My notes: [[1 Kings 6 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
[[1 Kings 6]] dates, describes, and completes the construction of Solomon's temple. Its extensive architectural detail is interrupted by a divine message concerning Solomon's conduct, God's word to David, and God's promised dwelling among Israel (1 Kings 6:11–13). This supports the interpretation that the building is not presented as an automatic substitute for [[Obedience]], while the chapter's sustained attention to sacred space and costly materials also prevents minimizing the importance of the temple itself.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious outline is:

- 1 Kings 6:1: chronological opening.
- 1 Kings 6:2–10: dimensions, porch, windows, side chambers, prepared stone, and roof.
- 1 Kings 6:11–13: the divine word concerning obedience, the promise to David, and God's dwelling among Israel.
- 1 Kings 6:14–22: interior paneling, the most holy place, the intended location of the ark, and gold overlay.
- 1 Kings 6:23–36: cherubim, carved imagery, doors, floors, and the inner court.
- 1 Kings 6:37–38: foundation and completion dates.

Finishing language recurs in verses 9, 14, 22, and 38, but its precise literary function is disputed. It may mark stages, resume interrupted material, or reflect the joining of descriptive units. The chapter has chronological framing and a broad movement inward, but it does not demonstrate a formal concentric structure.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter brings [[Temples]], [[Covenants]], and [[Obedience]] into close relationship. The LORD conditions fulfillment of his word to David on Solomon's walking in the statutes, executing the judgments, and keeping the commandments (1 Kings 6:11–12), then promises to dwell among Israel and not forsake the people (1 Kings 6:13). A responsible interpretation is that temple construction does not replace covenant faithfulness. Verse 19 says that the inner sanctuary was prepared to receive the ark; the ark's actual installation is narrated later in [[1 Kings 8]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The KJV term “oracle” denotes the innermost sanctuary, not a spoken pronouncement: verse 16 identifies it as “the most holy place,” and verse 19 says it was prepared for the covenant ark. “House” usually concerns the temple building in this chapter, though its exact architectural scope must be judged from context. Zif and Bul are identified respectively as the second and eighth months. Repeated forms of “finished” are observable, but whether they formally divide the account into construction stages is an interpretive question.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The divine speech in verses 11–13 conspicuously interrupts the architectural description and places covenant conduct beside the temple project. The interior account repeatedly emphasizes cedar or olive wood, gold, cherubim, palms, and open flowers, producing a coherent visual vocabulary. The description moves broadly toward the inner sanctuary before returning to doors and the court. Comparison with [[Genesis 3]] permits an Edenic reading of the vegetation and cherubim, but the chapter never identifies the temple as Eden; conventional sanctuary or royal decoration is an important alternative explanation. Likewise, the increasing prominence of wood and gold may suggest intensified sacred space, royal magnificence, or both.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. What effect does the placement of the divine message in verses 11–13 have on the surrounding construction account?
2. What does verse 7 explicitly say about prepared stone and tool noise, and which practical, ritual, or symbolic explanations remain conjectural?
3. How do the ark and cherubim in this chapter compare with [[Exodus 25]] and [[Exodus 37]]?
4. Should the 480-year figure be read as exact elapsed chronology, a schematic total, or some combination of the two?
5. Which architectural features can be reconstructed confidently, and which remain uncertain because of ancient terminology?
6. Where does [[2 Chronicles 3]] agree with this chapter, and where do its measurements or emphases differ?
7. How does [[1 Kings 8]] clarify the eventual spatial relationship between the ark and the large cherubim?

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the sanctuary, ark, and cherubim with [[Exodus 25]], [[Exodus 26]], [[Exodus 29]], and [[Exodus 37]]. Read [[2 Samuel 7]] and [[1 Kings 2]] as context for the word to David recalled in verse 12. Compare the temple description with [[2 Chronicles 3]], noting both correspondence and difference, and continue through [[1 Kings 7]] and [[1 Kings 8]] for furnishings, ark installation, and dedication. [[1 Kings 9]], [[Jeremiah 7]], and [[2 Kings 25]] provide later developments and canonical counterpoints to confidence in the temple, but they should not be treated as meanings mechanically explicit in every detail of chapter 6.
