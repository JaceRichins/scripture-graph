# 1 Kings 4 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Kings 4]] · My notes: [[1 Kings 4 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
The chapter presents Solomon's kingdom through four connected portraits: central officials, twelve provisioning districts, national security and royal abundance, and divinely given wisdom with international fame. Its dominant tone is affirmative: the administration functions, the court lacks nothing, and Judah and Israel dwell safely. Yet the quantities of food, fodder, horses, and district obligations also reveal the substantial organization and resources required to sustain the monarchy. The chapter is therefore especially relevant to [[Government and Laws]], [[Education and Learning]], and [[War and Peace]], while not proving that prosperity was equally experienced by every subject.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Central administration (vv. 1–6):** priests, scribes, military leadership, household management, and labor or tribute.

2. **Provisioning districts (vv. 7–19):** twelve officers supply the royal household in monthly rotation.

3. **Dominion, abundance, and security (vv. 20–28):** population, territorial reach, provisions, horses, and peace are described expansively.

4. **Wisdom and reputation (vv. 29–34):** God gives Solomon wisdom expressed through proverbs, songs, discourse about living things, comparison with noted sages, and international fame.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter explicitly attributes Solomon's wisdom to God and portrays that wisdom broadly through sayings, songs, learning, reputation, and discourse about living things. This makes the chapter relevant to [[Education and Learning]], though it does not define Solomon's discussions as modern science or directly attribute every feature of his government to wisdom. Peace is portrayed at the household level through the vine-and-fig-tree image. The large horse establishment should also be read beside [[Deuteronomy 17]]: the comparison creates a genuine canonical tension, although 1 Kings 4 itself gives no explicit condemnation.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Three spatial and numerical expressions shape the chapter's rhetoric. “From Dan even to Beer-sheba” presents the land from north to south; the vine-and-fig-tree image expresses settled household security; and repeated references to “all” kingdoms, nations, people, and kings magnify Solomon's reach and fame. Such expressions communicate comprehensiveness within the narrative, but they need not function as modern statistical or cartographic claims.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Lists and expansive expressions turn Solomon's greatness into several forms of abundance: a numerous people, a fully supplied court, extensive military resources, and exceptional wisdom. Repeated terms such as “all Israel,” “all kingdoms,” and “all people” create an idealizing portrait of scope without requiring modern universal or statistical precision. The vine-and-fig-tree image makes national security concrete at the household level and closely parallels [[Micah 4]]. The two seashore-sand comparisons may link demographic and intellectual abundance, but deliberate structural coordination is not certain. The population comparison may also resonate with the [[Abrahamic Covenant]], though ordinary abundance imagery is an equally plausible explanation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Intertextual Numerical Discrepancy
> The KJV text of 1 Kings 4:26 reports forty thousand stalls of horses, whereas 2 Chronicles 9:25 reports four thousand stalls and the same total of twelve thousand horsemen. This is firm evidence of a numerical difference between the canonical passages, but it does not determine which number, if either, reflects the earliest recoverable wording or historical total.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · firm canonical numerical discrepancy · [[Intertextual Numerical Discrepancy in 1 Kings 4|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Parallel-Account Numerical Variation
> First Kings 4:26 reports forty thousand stalls of horses, whereas the parallel notice in 2 Chronicles 9:25 reports four thousand stalls; both report twelve thousand horsemen. The tenfold difference is direct evidence of a numerical textual or traditional problem that should be acknowledged rather than silently harmonized.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What relationship, if any, did the twelve provisioning districts have to Israel's tribal organization?

- How should “they lacked nothing” be weighed against the amount each district supplied?

- Does the narrator intend the horse establishment to recall [[Deuteronomy 17]], or does that critical reading arise chiefly from the wider canon?

- How should the chapter's positive portrait be held together with the later complaint about Solomon's heavy yoke?

- What explains the forty-thousand versus four-thousand stall figures in [[1 Kings 4]] and [[2 Chronicles 9]]?

- How much historical weight can the detailed administrative roster bear without evidence outside the text?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the official list with [[2 Samuel 20]] and [[1 Chronicles 18]]. Read the horse and wealth notices alongside [[Deuteronomy 17]] and [[1 Kings 10]], then consider Solomon's later evaluation in [[1 Kings 11]]. [[1 Kings 12]] supplies important evidence about the remembered burden of Solomon's reign, though it does not prove that every provisioning obligation in chapter 4 was oppressive. Compare the stall total directly with [[2 Chronicles 9]] and leave the original number unresolved unless textual evidence beyond these two passages is supplied.
