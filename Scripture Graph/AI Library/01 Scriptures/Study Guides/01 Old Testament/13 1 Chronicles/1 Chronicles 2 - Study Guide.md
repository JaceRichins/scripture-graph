## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
- **vv.1-2** — The twelve sons of Israel named.
- **vv.3-8** — Judah's sons: Er, Onan, Shelah by Bath-shua; Tamar's sons Pharez and Zerah; Achar 'the troubler of Israel.'
- **vv.9-17** — Hezron's son Ram's line to David (Amminadab → Nahshon → Salma → Boaz → Obed → Jesse → David); David's sisters and their sons.
- **vv.18-24** — Caleb son of Hezron, his wives, Hur, Uri, Bezaleel; Hezron's late marriage to Machir's daughter and the Jair/Gilead note.
- **vv.25-33** — Jerahmeel's descendants.
- **vv.34-41** — Sheshan's line preserved through his daughter's marriage to his servant Jarha.
- **vv.42-55** — Further descendants of Caleb (son of Hezron, brother of Jerahmeel - not Caleb son of Jephunneh) and the towns and clans associated with them, ending with the Kenite scribal families of Jabez and the house of Rechab.

The repeated Caleb headings (vv.18, 42, 50) resist reduction to one simple linear pedigree; the chapter appears to assemble related family and regional traditions rather than a single unbroken chain.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter has few doctrinal statements as such, but two narrative asides carry moral weight: Er 'was evil in the sight of the LORD; and he slew him' (2:3), and Achar 'transgressed in the thing accursed' (2:7) - brief but pointed notices that ancestry does not erase individual accountability. The chapter also contributes to [[Family]] and [[Records and Sacred Writings]] by preserving descent, marriage, childlessness, and collateral kinship, including cases where a family's line continues through a daughter (Tamar, Sheshan's daughter) rather than a son. These are limited observations - the text does not offer a systematic teaching about covenant status, salvation, or the eternal significance of those named.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The KJV repeatedly uses 'begat' for direct descent, while 'sons of' and 'father of' can carry broader social meanings. When a person is called 'father of' a place - Shobal father of Kirjath-jearim, Salma father of Beth-lehem, Hareph father of Beth-gader (2:50-51), Shammai's descendant father of Beth-zur (2:45) - the phrase likely denotes an ancestor, founder, or leading clan of that settlement rather than ordinary biological parentage alone; the exact relationship cannot always be recovered from the English wording. The name Achar (2:7) is widely read as a wordplay on Achan ([[Joshua 7]]), sharing a Hebrew root with 'trouble' that also names the Valley of Achor (Joshua 7:26) - though this links two different Hebrew name-forms, which should be preserved rather than silently harmonized. Chronicles likewise has 'Salma' where [[Ruth 4|Ruth 4:20]] has 'Salmon'; such variants are worth documenting rather than smoothing over.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Though Reuben is named first among Israel's sons (2:1), the Chronicler moves immediately to Judah in verse 3 and gives it by far the fullest treatment in the chapter - consistent with the Chronicler's later explanation that though Reuben was firstborn, the birthright passed to Joseph while the ruling line came through Judah (1 Chronicles 5:1-2). Short narrative notices punctuate the lists throughout: Er's death, Achar's offense, Hezron's late marriage, sons who died without children, and Sheshan's arrangement for his daughter briefly turn names into remembered stories. The chapter's movement from individual descent into cities, regional holdings, and clan or ethnic affiliations (vv.42-55) also shifts its function partway through - genealogy becomes a way of mapping Judah's social geography, not just its family tree.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Intertextual Numerical Discrepancy
> Chronicles lists seven sons of Jesse and calls David the seventh, whereas 1 Samuel 16 presents seven sons before David and 1 Samuel 17 calls Jesse the father of eight sons. The passages therefore preserve a real numerical tension in Jesse's family list.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · real, well-documented discrepancy · [[Intertextual Numerical Discrepancy in 1 Chronicles 2|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Genealogical Identification
> Uri's son Bezaleel, named in 1 Chronicles 2:20 as a great-grandson of Caleb through Hur, is generally identified with the Bezaleel of Exodus 31:2 and 35:30, the Spirit-filled craftsman who built the tabernacle, since both share the same name and the same father (Uri) and grandfather (Hur).
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.8 · broadly accepted identification · [[Genealogical Identification in 1 Chronicles 2|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual/Numerical Discrepancy
> 1 Chronicles 2:13-15 names Jesse's sons as Eliab, Abinadab, Shimma, Nethaneel, Raddai, Ozem, and David the seventh - seven sons total - while 1 Samuel 16:10-11 and 17:12-14 describe Jesse as having eight sons with David the youngest, a numerical discrepancy between the two accounts.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.8 · real, well-documented discrepancy · [[Textual/Numerical Discrepancy in 1 Chronicles 2|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual-Genealogical Parallel
> The sequence from Ram to David closely parallels Ruth 4:19-22, with the minor name-form difference Salma/Salmon. This strongly supports a shared genealogical tradition or literary relationship between the passages.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · broadly accepted, properly hedged · [[Textual-Genealogical Parallel in 1 Chronicles 2|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Genealogical/Textual Comparison *(tentative)*
> Zerah's sons Zimri, Ethan, Heman, Calcol, and Dara (1 Chronicles 2:6) closely resemble the four wise men Solomon is said to have surpassed in 1 Kings 4:31 - Ethan the Ezrahite, Heman, Chalcol, and Darda - though 1 Kings calls them 'sons of Mahol' rather than sons of Zerah, a discrepancy long noted by commentators.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.6 · disputed

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does 1 Chronicles 2:13-15 list only seven sons of Jesse when 1 Samuel 16:10-11 and 17:12 imply eight?
- How should the differing accounts of Amasa's father - Jether the Ishmeelite (2:17) versus Ithra the Israelite (2 Samuel 17:25) - be read without forcing a harmonization?
- What does 'father of' mean in each settlement-oriented notice: biological ancestor, founder, clan chief, or representative population?
- Is the 'house of Rechab' in 2:55 meant to be read as ancestors of the Rechabites later praised in Jeremiah 35, or simply a shared clan name?
- Why are some women in this genealogy named while Sheshan's daughter, through whom a long line continues, remains unnamed?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
