## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Historiographic Source Notice *(tentative)*
> The closing notice attributes information about David's reign to writings associated with Samuel, Nathan, and Gad. This supports the limited conclusion that the narrator presents the history as grounded in earlier prophetic records.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 1.0 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation

> [!evidence] Literary And Thematic Parallel *(tentative)*
> The emphasis on willing hearts, voluntary offerings, skilled work, and precious materials parallels the collection for the tabernacle in Exodus 35. This is meaningful evidence that the Chronicler's temple account evokes Israel's earlier sanctuary tradition, although literary intention cannot be proven from shared motifs alone.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation

> [!evidence] Internal Source Attribution *(tentative)*
> The closing source notice is evidence that the narrative presents itself as drawing on earlier prophetic records, but the notice alone cannot demonstrate that those sources survive, are independently recoverable, or verify every reported event.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 1.0 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation

> [!evidence] Narrative Sequence And Repeated Language *(tentative)*
> Within the narrative sequence, David's personal preparation and invitation are followed by willing gifts from leaders and joyful participation by the people. This provides moderate literary support for reading exemplary leadership as prompting communal generosity.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation
