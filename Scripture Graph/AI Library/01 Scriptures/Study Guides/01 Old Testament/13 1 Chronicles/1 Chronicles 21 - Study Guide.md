# 1 Chronicles 21 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Chronicles 21]] · My notes: [[1 Chronicles 21 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> David sins by numbering Israel—The Lord sends pestilence upon the people—David offers sacrifices and the plague is stayed.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The census and its condemnation (vv. 1–8):** Satan provokes David; Joab objects; the census is taken; David confesses.
2. **Judgment chosen and restrained (vv. 9–17):** David chooses to fall into the LORD's hand; pestilence follows; the angel is halted at Jerusalem; David intercedes for the people.
3. **The commanded altar (vv. 18–27):** David buys Ornan's threshing floor, offers costly sacrifices, and receives an answer by fire.
4. **Transition to the temple site (vv. 28–30):** David continues sacrificing where God answered, despite the tabernacle and older altar remaining at Gibeon. [[1 Chronicles 22]] and [[2 Chronicles 3]] complete this transition.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter holds several themes together without resolving every difficulty. [[Satan and Temptation]] does not erase [[Agency]]: although Satan provokes David, David repeatedly calls the census his own sin. [[Justice and Mercy]] appear together when severe judgment falls but God commands the angel to stop. David's confession, intercession, and obedience provide a strong narrative of [[Repentance]], while the deaths of the people leave the problem of collective suffering unresolved. David also refuses to offer another person's property without payment, showing that his act of [[Sacrifice]] must involve a real cost to himself (v. 24).

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The drawn sword organizes the second half of the account. It threatens Jerusalem (v. 16), prompts David's intercession (v. 17), explains his fear of approaching Gibeon (v. 30), and is finally returned to its sheath after the offering (v. 27). The threshing floor likewise changes meaning: an ordinary workplace becomes the point where destruction stops, sacrifice is accepted, and the future [[Temples|temple]] site is established. Comparison with [[2 Samuel 24]] shows both close dependence or shared tradition and significant differences in presentation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Comparative Literary Evidence *(tentative)*

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why is the census sinful? Does the military wording suggest pride or reliance on armed strength, or does [[Exodus 30]] supply a legal background? The chapter condemns the act but does not explicitly state the decisive reason.
- How should Satan's provocation be read alongside David's responsibility and the different opening of [[2 Samuel 24]]?
- What does David's description of the people as sheep imply about a king's responsibility for harm caused by royal decisions?
- Why does the account preserve collective suffering while emphasizing that David ordered the census? Verse 17 raises this moral problem inside the narrative rather than resolving it abstractly.
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[2 Samuel 24]] beside this chapter, noting the different initiating agent, census totals, famine duration, purchase terms, and treatment of the sanctuary site. Then continue into [[1 Chronicles 22]] and [[2 Chronicles 3]] to see how Ornan's threshing floor is identified with the temple. [[Exodus 30]] is a useful possible background for the otherwise unexplained association between census and plague, but it should not be treated as an explanation explicitly supplied by Chronicles.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
