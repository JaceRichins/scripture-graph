## Overview

<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Installation and celebration (vv. 1-3):** ark, offerings, blessing, and food.
2. **Initial appointments (vv. 4-7):** named Levites, priests, and musicians.
3. **Thanksgiving song (vv. 8-36):** covenant memory (vv. 8-22), worldwide proclamation and divine kingship (vv. 23-33), then thanksgiving and a gathering prayer (vv. 34-36).
4. **Continuing worship (vv. 37-42):** service before the ark and sacrifices at Gibeon.
5. **Return home (v. 43):** the people disperse and David blesses his house.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The song places [[Covenants]] at the center of faithful memory. The promise to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob anchors Israel's identity in the [[Abrahamic Covenant]] (vv. 15-18), while the following verses portray God's protection of a small and vulnerable covenant family (vv. 19-22). Israel's particular history opens outward as all peoples are summoned to hear of the LORD's salvation and acknowledge his reign (vv. 23-31). The contrast between idols and the maker of the heavens connects worship with [[Creation]] (vv. 25-26). Creation's rejoicing before the LORD who comes to judge the earth supplies a genuine judgment theme (vv. 31-33), without by itself specifying the timing or full doctrine of the [[Final Judgment]]. Verse 35 also gives the chapter a clear [[Gathering of Israel]] theme.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The song has an anthological arrangement: verses 8-22 closely parallel [[Psalm 105]], verses 23-33 parallel [[Psalm 96]], and verses 34-36 correspond to material in [[Psalm 106]]. The textual relationship is strong, but the parallels alone do not establish the direction of borrowing, the date of shared material, or that David composed every line in its received form. The refrain about enduring mercy in verses 34 and 41 bridges the recited song and the continuing ministry. A smaller narrative frame appears in David's blessing of the people near the beginning and his return to bless his household at the end.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual Interdependence
> The thanksgiving song has sustained, sequential correspondence with Psalm 105, Psalm 96, and Psalm 106. This is strong evidence of a literary or shared-liturgical relationship among these texts.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · Textual relationship accepted; direction of dependence disputed · [[Textual Interdependence in 1 Chronicles 16|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Source-Critical/Textual *(tentative)*
> The three-way overlap between 1 Chronicles 16 and Psalms 105, 96, and 106 supports the conclusion that this psalm is a composite/anthological arrangement, but does not by itself establish whether the Chronicler assembled excerpts from already-existing psalms or whether the separate psalms were later drawn out of an originally unified composition.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.7 · Anthological form accepted; literary history disputed

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Why does the song move from Israel's covenant history to an appeal addressed to all the earth? What is gained by placing the gathering prayer of verse 35 in David's mouth and liturgical setting? How do the simultaneous ministries before the ark and at Gibeon prepare for later temple worship? What distinction should readers maintain between the narrative's statement that David "delivered" the psalm and a stronger claim that he authored every line?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare 1 Chronicles 16:8-36 line by line with [[Psalm 105]], [[Psalm 96]], and [[Psalm 106]], noting both agreements and differences. Then compare the surrounding ark narrative with [[2 Samuel 6]]. These comparisons clarify what Chronicles preserves, rearranges, or emphasizes without requiring an unsupported conclusion about the direction of literary dependence.
