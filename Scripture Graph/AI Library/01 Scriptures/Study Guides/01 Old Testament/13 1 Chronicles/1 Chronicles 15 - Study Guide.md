# 1 Chronicles 15 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Chronicles 15]] · My notes: [[1 Chronicles 15 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
The chapter recounts a corrected attempt to bring the ark into the city of David. After preparing its place, David gathers Israel and assigns the transport to sanctified Levites. The ark is carried on shoulders with staves according to the command associated with Moses, while named singers, instrumentalists, trumpeters, and doorkeepers turn the procession into an ordered communal celebration. God helps the bearers, sacrifices are offered, and Israel rejoices. The final image complicates the joyful scene: Michal watches from a window and despises David, though this chapter does not explain her motive.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Preparation and assembly (vv. 1–11):** David prepares the ark's place, gathers Israel, and assembles priests, Levites, chiefs, and contingents.

2. **Diagnosis and correction (vv. 12–15):** David recalls the earlier breach; priests and Levites sanctify themselves, and the ark is carried as commanded.

3. **Musical and custodial assignments (vv. 16–24):** Named personnel receive responsibilities for singing, instruments, trumpets, leadership, and care of the ark.

4. **Successful procession (vv. 25–28):** Leaders accompany the ark, God helps the bearers, sacrifices are offered, and Israel celebrates with music and shouting.

5. **Contrasting reaction (v. 29):** Michal watches David's celebration and despises him.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter makes a substantial contribution to [[Obedience]]: good intentions and communal enthusiasm do not replace the prescribed handling of the ark. Its connection to the [[Law of Moses]] is explicit in the appeal to what Moses commanded. Yet order is not presented as the opposite of joy; sanctification and careful assignments accompany music, sacrifice, shouting, and celebration.

The repeated expression "ark of the covenant" supports a direct connection with [[Covenants]], though the chapter does not develop covenant theology in detail. The procession also includes [[Sacrifice]] and differentiated service by priests and Levites. A link to [[Priesthood]] is useful only with caution: the chapter distinguishes priests from other Levites and does not define later theological categories. The successful outcome is attributed to God's help as well as to corrected human preparation.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
"Due order" in verse 13 refers at minimum to procedures neglected during the earlier attempt; verse 15 identifies shoulder-bearing with staves as part of the correction. The precise scope of the phrase may also include authorized personnel and sanctification.

"Alamoth" and "Sheminith" in verses 20–21 appear to be technical musical designations, but this chapter does not define their precise meanings. Verse 22 is also translation-sensitive: the supplied KJV describes Chenaniah as instructing "about the song" because he was skilful, while other translations understand him as supervising the carrying or transport. The phrase "all Israel" gives the event a corporate-national presentation but need not mean every Israelite was physically present.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The narrative moves from preparation through correction and detailed assignment to successful celebration. The long lists slow the action but display the organized Levitical service behind the public joy. The phrase "all Israel" appears near the beginning and end of the public action (vv. 3, 28), giving the procession a corporate frame.

Verse 29 then sharply narrows the view from national sound and movement to Michal watching through a window. Her inward contempt contrasts with David's visible dancing and playing. Calling her "the daughter of Saul" while naming "king David" permits a possible dynastic dimension, but the verse does not establish that reading or explain her motive.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
What procedures are included in seeking God "after the due order," and how does [[1 Chronicles 13]] clarify the phrase? Why does the narrator devote so much space to named personnel and assignments? What might the statement that God "helped the Levites" mean, and what does the text leave unspecified? How does careful order coexist with shouting, music, sacrifice, and dancing? What additional information about Michal appears in [[2 Samuel 6]], and which explanations of her motive remain interpretive rather than explicit in Chronicles?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the failed transport in [[1 Chronicles 13]] with the corrected procedure here, then follow the ark's installation and worship in [[1 Chronicles 16]]. Read [[2 Samuel 6]] as the principal parallel, noting both shared events and Chronicles' expanded Levitical detail. For legal background, compare [[Deuteronomy 10]], [[Exodus 25]], [[Numbers 4]], and especially the explicit shoulder-bearing statement in [[Numbers 7]]. Later ark processions appear in [[1 Kings 8]] and [[2 Chronicles 5]].
