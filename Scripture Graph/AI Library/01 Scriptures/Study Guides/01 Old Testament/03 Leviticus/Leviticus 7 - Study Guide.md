# Leviticus 7 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Leviticus 7]] · My notes: [[Leviticus 7 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Leviticus 7 concludes a larger collection of sacrificial instructions. Verses 1-10 regulate the trespass offering and several priestly portions. Verses 11-21 distinguish thanksgiving, vow, and voluntary peace offerings, establish consumption deadlines, and address contamination of both sacred food and participants. Verses 22-27 prohibit eating specified fat and any blood, while verses 28-36 assign the breast and right shoulder of peace offerings to the priests. The closing summary attributes these laws to the LORD's command through Moses at Mount Sinai.

The chapter presents [[Sacrifice]] as serving more than one purpose: the text mentions atonement, thanksgiving, vows, and voluntary offerings. It also coordinates altar worship, sacred meals, and institutional support for the [[Priesthood]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Trespass offering and other priestly portions (vv. 1-10):** The trespass offering's altar procedure and priestly consumption are followed by allocation rules for burnt- and grain-offering portions.
2. **Peace-offering meals (vv. 11-21):** Thanksgiving, vow, and voluntary offerings have different consumption periods; contaminated flesh and unclean participants receive different treatment.
3. **Fat and blood prohibitions (vv. 22-27):** Specified fat and all blood are forbidden as food, with a limited non-food allowance for fat from dead or torn animals.
4. **Offerer participation and priestly portions (vv. 28-36):** The offerer brings the fat and breast; the altar receives the fat, and the priests receive the breast and right shoulder.
5. **Closing inventory and Sinai attribution (vv. 37-38):** The chapter summarizes the offering laws and identifies their stated setting.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter depicts holiness through ordered access: sacred food has designated recipients, places, times, and purity conditions. Verse 18 shows that mishandling a peace offering can affect its stated acceptance, so prescribed handling belongs to the rite rather than being incidental.

Leviticus 7 also resists reducing every sacrifice to one function. Atonement is explicit in the sin-and-trespass rule, while peace offerings may express [[Gratitude]], accompany a vow, or be voluntary. These purposes are related within the sacrificial system but are not identical.

The portions assigned to Aaron and his sons are presented as an institutional provision connected with their anointing and ministry. The text does not explain the arrangement in terms of individual merit.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The KJV expression “meat offering” in verses 9-10 uses older English. The references to flour, oil, cakes, wafers, ovens, and pans show that this is a grain-based offering rather than animal flesh.

“Most holy” is a ritual classification associated here with restricted handling and consumption; it should not automatically be converted into a judgment of moral superiority. “Cut off from his people” states a serious sanction, but this chapter does not define whether the mechanism is human, divine, social, or lethal.

“Wave offering” and “heave offering” identify designated presentations or portions, but Leviticus 7 does not fully explain the precise mechanics of the actions.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Repeated formulas such as “this is the law,” “the LORD spake unto Moses,” and “by a statute” organize and authorize the material. The phrase “cut off from his people” occurs four times in verses 20, 21, 25, and 27, covering three broad offense categories: unclean consumption of sacred flesh, prohibited fat, and blood.

Verses 37-38 function as a formal closing inventory for the sacrificial instructions in their present literary form. The summary supports reading Leviticus 7 as a conclusion to the collection, but it does not by itself establish single authorship, composition date, or uniform historical practice.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Why does a thanksgiving offering require same-day consumption while vow and voluntary offerings permit a second day? Does the tighter deadline intensify communal sharing, protect sanctity, address spoilage, or serve several purposes at once?

What does the command that the offerer's “own hands” bring the fat and breast contribute to the meaning of participation?

How should “cut off from his people” be understood in each legal setting, and what additional biblical passages clarify the sanction?

What cautions arise when moving from a prescribed ritual system to claims about how consistently it was practiced historically?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

- Compare [[Leviticus 2]] with Leviticus 7:9-14 for grain-offering preparations and the inclusion of leavened bread with a thanksgiving offering.
- Compare [[Leviticus 17]] with Leviticus 7:26-27 for the fuller explanation connecting blood with life and altar atonement.
- Read [[Exodus 29]] for canonical context concerning priestly consecration and the breast and shoulder portions.
- Distinguish throughout between what the legal text prescribes and what can be established about the frequency or uniformity of historical practice.
