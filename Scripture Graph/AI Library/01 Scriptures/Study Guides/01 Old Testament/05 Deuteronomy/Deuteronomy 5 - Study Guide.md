# Deuteronomy 5 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Deuteronomy 5]] · My notes: [[Deuteronomy 5 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Moses tells of the covenant God made with Israel in Horeb—He reviews the Ten Commandments—Sabbath observance also commemorates the deliverance from Egypt—God talks with man—Blessings flow from obedience.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Covenant summons (5:1-5):** Israel is called to hear, learn, keep, and do; Moses recalls the encounter at Horeb.
2. **The covenant words (5:6-21):** Deliverance from Egypt introduces commands governing worship, sacred time, family, life, marriage, property, testimony, and desire.
3. **The public revelation (5:22-27):** God speaks from fire and darkness; the people acknowledge surviving the encounter but request mediation.
4. **God's response and Moses's commission (5:28-31):** God approves their words, desires an enduring obedient heart, and appoints Moses to teach.
5. **Concluding exhortation (5:32-33):** Israel must not turn aside but walk fully in the commanded way.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
God identifies himself as Israel's deliverer before giving the commandments (5:6-7), supporting a reading of covenant obedience as a response to prior rescue rather than the means of earning that rescue. The [[Sabbath Day]] command makes [[Exodus and Deliverance|deliverance]] a recurring social practice: servants, resident strangers, and animals are also given rest (5:14-15), although the day remains explicitly consecrated to the LORD.

The chapter places public revelation and prophetic mediation together. Israel hears God's voice, Moses stands between God and the people, and after the people request mediation, God commissions Moses to receive and teach further commandments (5:4-5, 22-31). God's approval of their request is followed by a desire that their fear and promised [[Obedience|obedience]] endure (5:28-29); the concern is lasting covenant faithfulness, not necessarily the request for mediation itself.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter's framing verbs form a practical sequence: Israel must **hear**, **learn**, **keep**, and **do** (5:1), then **observe**, refuse to turn aside, and **walk** in God's ways (5:32-33). Obedience is portrayed as reception followed by sustained action.

Deuteronomy 5 names the covenant location “Horeb” (5:2); “Sinai” does not occur in the chapter's wording. The phrase “face to face” (5:4) describes an unusually immediate corporate encounter, but its precise sense is uncertain. Verse 5 simultaneously assigns Moses an intermediary role, and Deuteronomy 4:12, 15 emphasizes that Israel heard a voice without seeing a form.

Compared with [[Exodus 20]], the canonical English text gives different rationales for Sabbath observance—creation there and deliverance here—and varies the wording and order of the final prohibition concerning coveting. These differences are textual observations; by themselves they do not establish authorship, date, contradiction, or literary priority.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The divine self-identification in 5:6 functions as the theological premise of the commands: the speaker is Israel's liberator. Verse 22 then closes the command sequence by reporting that God “added no more” and wrote the words on stone. The narrative after the commandments tests the issue introduced before them—whether Israel will truly hear and do. The people's promise in 5:27 is answered by God's desire for an enduring obedient heart in 5:29.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How does Moses's statement that the covenant was made “with us” make Horeb binding upon the present community (5:2-3)? Must every listener have personally witnessed the original event?
- What changes when the commandments are read as a response to deliverance rather than as a means of earning it?
- Why does Deuteronomy connect the [[Sabbath Day]] to slavery and deliverance, while [[Exodus 20]] connects it to creation?
- How should “face to face” in 5:4 be read alongside Moses's mediating role in 5:5?
- Why does God approve the people's words in 5:28 yet immediately desire an enduring obedient heart in 5:29?
- Does 5:9 describe inherited guilt, continuing consequences, a hostile family line, or some combination? What does the contrast with mercy in 5:10 clarify, and what does it leave unresolved?
- What does the prohibition of coveting add to commandments governing outward conduct?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the Decalogue here with [[Exodus 20]], attending especially to the Sabbath rationale and the wording of the final commandment. Compare the command sequence preserved in [[Mosiah 13]] and examine how Abinadi uses it in his prophetic indictment. Then continue into [[Deuteronomy 6]], where hearing, loving, remembering, teaching, and obeying develop the covenant response introduced here.
