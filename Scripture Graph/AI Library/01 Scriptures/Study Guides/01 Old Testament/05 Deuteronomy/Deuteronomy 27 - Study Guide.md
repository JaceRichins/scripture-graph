# Deuteronomy 27 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Deuteronomy 27]] · My notes: [[Deuteronomy 27 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The children of Israel are to cross the Jordan, build an altar, and worship the Lord—They are the Lord’s people but will be cursed if they do not obey Him.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Verses 1–8 — Inscription, altar, and offerings:** After crossing the Jordan, Israel is to write the law on plastered stones and worship at an altar of whole stones untouched by iron tools.

2. **Verses 9–10 — Identity and obedience:** Israel is declared the Lord's people and therefore commanded to hear and obey him.

3. **Verses 11–14 — Two-mountain arrangement:** Six tribes are assigned to Gerizim and six to Ebal; the Levites address all Israel with a loud voice.

4. **Verses 15–26 — Twelve responsive curses:** Each curse receives the people's “Amen,” and the final declaration broadens from particular offenses to failure to uphold the law as a whole.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Israel's declaration as the Lord's people is immediately followed by the command to obey his voice (Deuteronomy 27:9–10). The chapter therefore places covenant identity and covenant obligation beside one another, without explaining whether this moment creates or reaffirms Israel's identity. Its commands join [[Sacrifice]] and rejoicing with duties concerning worship, [[Family]], property, sexual conduct, vulnerable people, and innocent life. This breadth makes the chapter important for studying [[Covenants]], [[Obedience]], and justice under the [[Law of Moses]], while leaving broader claims about how covenant membership is acquired or lost unanswered.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The phrase “all the words of this law” appears in verses 3, 8, and 26. The first two occurrences concern writing; the final occurrence concerns confirming the law's words by doing them. This recurrence creates a literary connection between inscription and obedience. The repeated “Amen” after each curse gives verses 15–26 a responsive, liturgical sound and makes the assembly audible participants. The chapter does not define the response's precise force or establish the inward agreement of every participant.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter coordinates visible inscription, sacrifice, assigned locations, loud proclamation, repeated formulas, and communal responses. These features strongly support reading the passage as a formal ritual prescription rather than an unstructured legal list. Written words and oral responses function as complementary parts of the ceremony, though the text does not state how durable the inscription was. Two curses explicitly concern concealed acts—an image placed “in a secret place” and secretly striking a neighbor—but secrecy should not be made the organizing principle of the entire list. [[Joshua 8]] presents a closely corresponding narrative, while [[Deuteronomy 28]] continues the broader blessing-and-curse material; neither connection by itself establishes historical performance or compositional priority.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What body of material is meant by “all the words of this law” in verses 3, 8, and 26?

- Why are these particular tribes assigned to Gerizim and Ebal, and how does Levi's tribal assignment relate to the Levites' speaking role?

- Why does the chapter assign a blessing role to Gerizim but preserve only the responsive curse formulas?

- What did the people's “Amen” express: affirmation, acceptance of obligation, acknowledgment of justice, or some combination of these?

- How does the narrated ceremony in [[Joshua 8]] clarify or differ from the command in [[Deuteronomy 27]]?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare [[Deuteronomy 11]] for the earlier Gerizim–Ebal instruction, [[Deuteronomy 26]] for the reciprocal declarations concerning the Lord and Israel, and [[Joshua 8]] for the corresponding narrative ceremony. [[Deuteronomy 28]] extends the broader blessing-and-curse material but should not automatically be identified as the missing Gerizim responses. [[Exodus 20]] provides parallels concerning images, parents, and unworked altar stones. Galatians 3:10 later applies the comprehensive curse principle of Deuteronomy 27:26 within a Christian argument about law and curse.
