## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Setting and enacted scene (vv. 1–5):** Jeremiah assembles the Rechabites in a chamber of the house of the Lord and offers them wine.

2. **Refusal and explanation (vv. 6–11):** They recount Jonadab's commands and explain why warfare brought them to Jerusalem.

3. **Application to Judah (vv. 12–17):** Their obedience becomes the basis of an explicit contrast with Judah's failure to hear the Lord and his prophets.

4. **Promise to the Rechabites (vv. 18–19):** Their fidelity is acknowledged, and Jonadab's line receives a continuing promise.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's central concern is [[Obedience]], specifically the contrast between hearing and refusing to hear. The comparison is deliberately unequal: the Rechabites maintained one ancestor's instructions, while Judah rejected repeated messages from God through [[Prophets]]. Verse 15 clarifies that the required response included [[Repentance]]—turning from evil, amending conduct, and abandoning other gods.

The passage should not be converted without qualification into a universal law of abstinence. The text attributes the rule to Jonadab and places it beside prohibitions on houses, fields, seed, and vineyards. Its strongest doctrinal contribution is therefore the demand for faithful responsiveness, not an independent formulation of the [[Word of Wisdom]].

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verses 14-17 accumulate expressions of failed reception: Judah does not “hearken,” does not “incline” the ear, does not “hear,” and does not “answer.” By contrast, the Rechabites say they have “obeyed,” “done according to,” and “performed” Jonadab's command. The repeated expression “rising early” presents God's speaking and sending of prophets as persistent; determining the idiom's fuller force requires comparison with its use elsewhere in Jeremiah.

The promise that a man will “stand before” God is plainly favorable, but Jeremiah 35 does not define a particular office or settle whether the expression emphasizes descendants, accepted standing, service, or some combination. Claims about a priestly office or securely identified modern descendants go beyond this chapter.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The wine scene functions as an enacted prophetic comparison whose meaning is then explained. Wine is placed before the Rechabites, their refusal prompts an account of Jonadab's command, and their obedience becomes evidence in God's case against Judah. The comparison does not equate the authority or content of Jonadab's rules with divine law; its force lies in descendants obeying an ancestral instruction while Judah refuses God's repeated appeals.

The chapter ends with corresponding but unequal outcomes: announced judgment upon Judah and a favorable promise to Jonadab's house. [[Jeremiah 34]] supplies an illuminating canonical contrast between Judah's reversal of a commitment and the Rechabites' sustained fidelity, but adjacency alone does not establish chronology or intentional pairing.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why was the demonstration staged in a chamber of the house of the Lord, and what audience beyond the Rechabites may have been intended?

- Does the chapter commend the content of every Rechabite practice, or primarily their fidelity to the tradition they received?

- How should the emergency move to Jerusalem qualify descriptions of the Rechabites as permanently nomadic?

- What range of meanings can "stand before me" bear in Jeremiah 35:19, and can the chapter alone decide among them?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare [[Jeremiah 7]] and [[Jeremiah 25]], where the same themes of repeatedly sent prophets, refusal to hearken, turning from evil, and abandoning other gods occur. Those parallels place Jeremiah 35 within the book's sustained explanation of why judgment comes upon Judah. Second Kings 10 provides the principal biblical narrative involving an earlier Jehonadab son of Rechab, but the precise history of the later Rechabite community requires evidence beyond Jeremiah 35.
