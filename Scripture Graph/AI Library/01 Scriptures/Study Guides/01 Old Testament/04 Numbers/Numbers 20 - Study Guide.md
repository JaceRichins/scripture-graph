# Numbers 20 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Numbers 20]] · My notes: [[Numbers 20 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Numbers 20 pairs the deaths of two of Israel's three founding wilderness leaders around a water crisis and a diplomatic failure. Miriam dies and is buried at Kadesh (v.1). Lacking water, the congregation contends bitterly with Moses and Aaron, idealizing what they left behind in Egypt (vv.2-5). The LORD instructs Moses to speak to the rock before the people; instead Moses addresses the assembly as rebels and strikes the rock twice, and water still comes forth abundantly for the people and their animals (vv.6-11). Immediately afterward the LORD tells Moses and Aaron that, because they did not believe him or sanctify him before Israel, neither will lead the congregation into the land (vv.12-13). Israel then appeals to Edom, "thy brother," for peaceful passage along the King's Highway; Edom refuses and comes out with an armed force, and Israel turns away rather than force passage (vv.14-21). At Mount Hor, Aaron's priestly garments are commanded to be transferred to his son Eleazar in full view of the congregation before Aaron dies there; Israel mourns him for thirty days (vv.22-29).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter has a loose envelope shape: Miriam's death (v.1) and Aaron's death (vv.28-29) frame two central episodes.

1. **Miriam's death at Kadesh (v.1).** Brief, with no account of a successor.
2. **The Meribah crisis (vv.2-13).** Complaint, divine instruction, water from the rock, and judgment form a single episode. The text names unbelief and failure to sanctify the LORD as the reason for judgment but does not reduce the offense to one single gesture.
3. **Edom refuses passage (vv.14-21).** Israel appeals to kinship, recounts the exodus, proposes restricted peaceful transit, and withdraws after Edom mobilizes an armed force.
4. **Aaron's death and Eleazar's succession (vv.22-29).** Unlike Miriam's death notice, Aaron's death is preceded by a commanded, visible transfer of his garments to his son.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The Meribah episode is a stringent statement about [[Obedience]] and prophetic accountability: even Moses and Aaron, after decades of faithful service, are held to a standard for how they represent God before the people (v.12). Crucially, the miracle's success does not double as approval of how it was performed — water comes abundantly in v.11, and divine censure follows immediately in v.12; the text distinguishes God's provision for the people from his assessment of the leaders who mediated it.

Aaron's death scene contributes to the biblical picture of the [[Aaronic Priesthood]]: authority is not self-assumed. The transfer to Eleazar is commanded by the LORD, enacted by Moses, represented through Aaron's garments, and witnessed by the congregation — an orderly, public succession rather than an office ending with its holder. Numbers 20 alone should not be read as a complete theology of priesthood succession, but it models continuity of office as a deliberate, visible act.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
“Meribah” is associated within the chapter with striving or contention: verse 13 explains the name by saying that Israel “strove with the LORD.” The wording “sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel” in verse 12 concerns how Moses and Aaron publicly represented the LORD, not merely whether water was produced.

Aaron's death is described as being “gathered unto his people” before verse 26 also says that he will “die there.” The expression clearly denotes death in context, but Numbers 20 alone does not explain its full theological implications.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Death notices frame the chapter: Miriam's at the opening (v.1), Aaron's at the close (vv.28-29), with the Meribah judgment and Edom's refusal set between them. The chapter also turns on a contrast between speech and action: Moses is told to speak to the rock but instead speaks to the people and strikes it — an important contrast, though it does not by itself prove the offense had only one component (see Doctrines, above).

The rock-and-water miracle closely echoes the earlier episode at Rephidim in [[Exodus 17]], also called Meribah/Massah, though the two divine commands differ (there Moses is told to strike; here, to speak). Whether these represent two separate historical events decades apart, or two literary tellings of one underlying tradition, is disputed among scholars; the text itself narrates them as two distinct occasions. [[Numbers 27]] later cites this chapter almost verbatim as the reason Moses himself will not enter the land, and [[Psalm 106]] and [[Deuteronomy 32]] both retrospectively recall the failure to sanctify the LORD at Meribah.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What exactly did Moses (and Aaron) do wrong at the rock — striking rather than speaking, claiming credit ("must we fetch you water"), losing composure, or something the text leaves unspecified as a single cause?
- Why is Aaron judged alongside Moses (v.12) despite playing a more passive role at the rock itself?
- Are [[Exodus 17]] and this chapter two distinct historical episodes, or two tellings of one underlying tradition — and how would each reading change what the parallel teaches?
- Why does Israel withdraw from Edom rather than force passage, despite having significant numbers of its own? Is this restraint, or simply prudence in the face of superior force?
- What is communicated by transferring Aaron's garments before his death, in full view of the congregation, rather than after?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
