# Numbers 28 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Numbers 28]] · My notes: [[Numbers 28 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Sacrifices are to be offered each morning and evening, on the Sabbath, on the first day of each month, at Passover, on each day of the Feast of Unleavened Bread, and at the Feast of Firstfruits.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Daily offering (vv. 1–8):** two unblemished year-old lambs, morning and evening, with flour, oil, and wine.
2. **Sabbath offering (vv. 9–10):** two additional lambs with their accompaniments.
3. **Monthly offering (vv. 11–15):** two bullocks, one ram, seven lambs, and a goat for a sin offering.
4. **Passover and the seven-day feast (vv. 16–25):** Passover falls on the fourteenth; the feast begins on the fifteenth, with daily sacrifices and convocations on its first and seventh days.
5. **Day of firstfruits (vv. 26–31):** a holy convocation and another prescribed set of offerings after the weeks are completed.

The same animal pattern—two bullocks, one ram, and seven lambs—appears in the monthly, feast, and firstfruits sections.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Within the [[Law of Moses]], worship is portrayed as regular, ordered, and communal. [[Sacrifice]] accompanies ordinary days and special seasons, while atoning offerings remain present even during festivals (Numbers 28:15, 22, 30). Christian readers may relate sacrificial atonement typologically to [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]], but that identification comes from broader Christian interpretation; Numbers 28 itself does not explicitly identify these offerings with Jesus.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
In the KJV, “meat offering” here denotes an offering of flour mingled with oil, as the chapter itself makes clear (Numbers 28:5, 12–13, 20–21, 28–29). “Continual” emphasizes recurrence, while “beside” means that Sabbath and festival sacrifices are additional to the daily offering. The repeated phrase “sweet savour unto the LORD” is cultic language expressing an accepted offering; the chapter does not require a literalistic conclusion that God depends upon food or aroma.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter's principal literary feature is cumulative calendrical progression: day, week, month, and annual feast. Repeated quantities and formulas give the legislation a patterned character and make deviations easy to notice. The sequence continues into [[Numbers 29]], while [[Exodus 29]] supplies the earlier Sinai setting for the continual offering and [[Leviticus 23]] provides a closely related festival calendar.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why are special-day offerings explicitly added to, rather than substituted for, the continual offering?
- What might the progression from daily to annual observance communicate about the ordering of communal life?
- Why do atoning offerings accompany occasions otherwise associated with festal worship?
- How does Numbers 28 complement the descriptions of sacred time in [[Leviticus 23]] and the continuation in [[Numbers 29]]?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Numbers 28:3–8 with Exodus 29:38–42 for the Sinai instructions behind the continual offering. Compare Numbers 28:16–31 with Leviticus 23:5–21 to distinguish each chapter's emphasis: Leviticus foregrounds the occasions and assemblies, whereas Numbers specifies recurring sacrificial requirements. Then read [[Numbers 29]] as the continuation of the calendar.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
