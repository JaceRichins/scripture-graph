# Numbers 18 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Numbers 18]] · My notes: [[Numbers 18 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Aaron and his sons are called to minister in the priest’s office—Levites are called to minister in the service of the tabernacle—Levites receive no land inheritance but are supported by the tithes of the people.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Verses 1–7 — Custody and boundaries:** Aaron's house bears responsibility for the sanctuary and priesthood; the Levites assist without approaching the sacred vessels or altar.

2. **Verses 8–19 — Priestly portions:** The text allocates offerings, firstfruits, devoted property, and firstborn animals, with differing rules for redemption and consumption.

3. **Verses 20–24 — Inheritance through service:** Aaron's portion is the Lord, and the landless Levites receive Israel's tithes for tabernacle work.

4. **Verses 25–32 — The Levites' offering:** The Levites give Aaron the best tenth of the tithe and may then consume the remainder as compensation for service.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Within the [[Law of Moses]], [[Priesthood]] is portrayed as both gift and accountable [[Service]]. Those granted privileged access also bear heightened responsibility for protecting holy things. Material provision is not detached from labor: offerings and [[Tithing]] support ministers who receive no territorial inheritance, while those ministers remain obligated to consecrate the best portion of what they receive.

The chapter also joins holiness to ordered participation. Priests, Levites, and other Israelites do not have interchangeable assignments. In the chapter's own theological framework, these boundaries protect the congregation from wrath as well as preserve the sanctuary.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
“Bear the iniquity” occurs with both Aaron's house and the Levites (18:1, 23). In context it includes liability for properly discharging sacred custody; the wording alone does not determine whether punishment, responsibility, expiation, or some combination is intended in every occurrence.

“Stranger” in 18:4 and 18:7 denotes someone outside the authorized group in context. It should not automatically be reduced to the modern sense of an unknown person or treated as an unambiguous ethnic label.

“Covenant of salt” in 18:19 clearly accompanies repeated permanence language. [[Leviticus 2]] connects salt and covenant in sacrificial law, but Numbers 18 itself does not define the salt metaphor.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter repeatedly balances grant with obligation: Levites are given but must guard; priesthood is given but bears liability; offerings are given but remain holy; tithes are given for service, yet the recipients must offer a tenth. This patterned reciprocity is the chapter's strongest organizing idea.

The movement from dangerous approach to ordered access also connects the chapter tightly to [[Numbers 17]]. Israel's fear that proximity to the tabernacle means death is answered by a system of authorized mediation and guarded boundaries.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How does the chapter's repeated language of “gift,” “service,” “inheritance,” and “reward” define the relationship between ministry and material support?

- Why must the Levites offer from the tithes they receive, and why must that offering be the best portion?

- What distinctions does the text make among holiness, ritual cleanliness, authorized office, and kinship?

- Does “bearing iniquity” here describe guilt, responsibility for preventing violations, consequences of failure, or several of these ideas together?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
