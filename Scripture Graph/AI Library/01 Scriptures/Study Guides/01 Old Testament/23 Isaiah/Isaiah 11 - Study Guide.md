## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious outline is:

- **Verses 1-5:** The Jesse-linked ruler, his spiritual endowment, and his righteous judgment.
- **Verses 6-9:** Peace among animals and children, culminating in universal knowledge of the LORD.
- **Verses 10-12:** An ensign, Gentile response, and the [[Gathering of Israel]].
- **Verses 13-14:** Reconciliation between Ephraim and Judah followed by victory over neighboring peoples.
- **Verses 15-16:** A return from Assyria modeled on the earlier [[Exodus and Deliverance]].
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The ruler's justice gives priority to righteousness and equity rather than appearances (Isaiah 11:3-5), relevant to [[Justice and Mercy]]. Christian and Latter-day Saint tradition identifies this Davidic figure with [[Jesus Christ (Gospel Topic)]]; the chapter's heading names the "stem of Jesse" as Christ, though Doctrine and Covenants 113 treats the "rod" and "root" as related but distinct figures rather than every image being a direct title for Christ. The chapter links peace with knowledge of the LORD (v.9) and portrays gathering as both geographic recovery and the healing of hostility between Ephraim and Judah (vv.11-13), a key text for [[Gathering of Israel]]. Its mention of Gentiles seeking the root of Jesse (v.10) broadens the vision beyond Israel alone, connecting to [[Jews and Gentiles]]. The peaceable-kingdom imagery (vv.6-9) is applied by the chapter heading to the [[Millennium]]; see also [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] with the caution that this chapter itself supplies no explicit chronological marker.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter's opening metaphor plays on agricultural terms: a "stem" (a cut-down stock or stump) and a "rod"/"Branch" (new growth from that stump or its roots). The underlying Hebrew word rendered "Branch" (netzer) has been proposed by some scholars as wordplay behind Matthew 2:23's statement that Jesus "should be called a Nazarene" — an intriguing but linguistically disputed connection that Matthew does not explicitly attribute to Isaiah by name. Verse 9's climactic clause, "the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea," uses "knowledge of the LORD" in a covenantal sense — recognition of and submission to God — rather than as a general endorsement of education or learning.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Isaiah 11 reads as a deliberate reversal of [[Isaiah 10]]'s closing image: proud, towering trees are hewn down in 10:33-34, while a humble stump sprouts new life in 11:1. Verses 1 and 10 both invoke Jesse ("stem" then "root"), a repetition plausibly read as a loose framing device around the ruler's portrait before the chapter turns to the nations, though the two images are related rather than verbally identical. The formula "they shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain" (v.9) recurs verbatim at Isaiah 65:25, linking this oracle to Isaiah's climactic new-heavens-and-new-earth vision. The chapter's conclusion (vv.15-16) draws on the Exodus pattern explicitly, comparing the remnant's future return to Israel's departure from Egypt.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Several interpretive questions should remain open:

- Are the peaceful animals intended as literal transformed creation, political symbolism, or imagery capable of both senses?
- Is the root of Jesse in verse 10 identical with the shoot in verse 1, and what accounts for the shift from descendant-like shoot imagery to root imagery?
- How should the universal peace of verses 6-9 be related to the military violence of verse 14?
- What is meant by the Lord acting a "second time" in verse 11? Verse 16 supplies an exodus analogy, but it does not unambiguously define the numbered first recovery.
- Does "all my holy mountain" primarily denote Zion/Jerusalem, an ideal sacred realm, or a more universal condition?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the near-full reproduction of this chapter in [[2 Nephi 21]] and Nephi's own commentary reusing its language in [[2 Nephi 30]]. The "Branch" title recurs elsewhere among the prophets (e.g., Jeremiah 23:5; Zechariah 3:8), and Paul quotes the "root of Jesse" prophecy of v.10 in Romans 15:12.
