# Exodus 35 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Exodus 35]] · My notes: [[Exodus 35 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Exodus 35 moves from sacred rest to communal preparation for sacred work. Moses first commands Israel to observe the [[Sabbath Day]] (vv. 1–3), then requests materials and skilled makers for the tabernacle (vv. 4–19). Men and women respond with willing offerings and textile work (vv. 20–29), after which Bezaleel and Aholiab are identified as divinely enabled artisans and teachers (vv. 30–35). The chapter joins divine command with willing participation while distinguishing among the resources and abilities people bring.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Sabbath command (vv. 1–3):** The seventh day is declared holy rest before the tabernacle appeal begins.

2. **Materials and makers requested (vv. 4–19):** Moses lists offerings, furnishings, structures, garments, and needed workers.

3. **The congregation responds (vv. 20–29):** Willing men and women bring the requested materials, and skilled women spin textiles.

4. **Named artisans and their capacities (vv. 30–35):** Bezaleel is called by name, Bezaleel and Aholiab are associated with teaching, and the artisans are filled with wisdom for specialized work.

The chapter prepares for actual construction in [[Exodus 36]].
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter places divine command and willing response together. The LORD specifies the project, while the offerings are repeatedly associated with willing or stirred hearts (vv. 5, 21–22, 29). This provides a useful connection to [[Consecration]] and [[Agency]], provided those later categories are recognized as interpretations rather than the chapter's own terminology.

Exodus 35 also attributes practical capacities to divine enabling. Bezaleel's design, metalwork, stonecutting, and woodworking are associated with the spirit of God, wisdom, understanding, and knowledge (vv. 30–33); teaching is added in verse 34. This supports connections to [[Service]], [[Spiritual Gifts]], and [[Education and Learning]], but it does not establish that every talent or instance of technical excellence is direct revelation.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated KJV expressions “willing heart,” “wise hearted,” and “wisdom of heart” connect disposition with ability. Context cautions against reading “wise hearted” as moral goodness alone: the phrase repeatedly introduces concrete technical competence.

Likewise, “curious works” and “cunning work” describe sophisticated or skillful craftsmanship in this context; the modern senses of merely strange or deceitful would misread the passage.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The clearest literary correspondence is between request and response. Metals, textiles, skins, wood, oil, spices, and stones requested in verses 5–9 recur in the contribution report in verses 22–28. This presents the response as fulfillment of the announced command and demonstrates careful textual organization, though routine reporting or editorial alignment could also account for the repetition.

Heart language recurs in descriptions of willingness, technical competence, and teaching. This creates a suggestive verbal connection among donors and artisans, but the expressions do not all mean precisely the same thing and do not establish a formal literary structure. Likewise, the placement of Sabbath legislation before the work material supports—but does not prove—the interpretation that sacred work remains bounded by sacred rest.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why might the [[Sabbath Day]] command be placed immediately before the tabernacle appeal, and what alternative explanations could account for that placement?

- How does the chapter hold together divine command and willing human response?

- What different contributions are associated with men, women, rulers, textile workers, artisans, teachers, and priests?

- Why are teaching and technical craftsmanship described alongside wisdom and the spirit of God?

- How can readers value these forms of service without claiming that every talent proves divine inspiration or that the chapter grants identical responsibilities to every participant?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare [[Exodus 25]] for the earlier sanctuary offering and construction instructions, [[Exodus 31]] for the artisan material followed by Sabbath legislation, and [[Exodus 36]] for the beginning of actual construction and the continued abundance of offerings. The golden-calf crisis and covenant renewal in [[Exodus 32]], [[Exodus 33]], and [[Exodus 34]] intervene between Exodus 31 and Exodus 35; Exodus 35 therefore resumes implementation after rupture and renewal rather than following the instructions immediately. [[Exodus 28]] gives fuller context for the priestly garments, and [[Exodus 39]] reports their completion.
