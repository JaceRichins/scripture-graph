# Exodus 38 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Exodus 38]] · My notes: [[Exodus 38 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Exodus 38 reports the construction of the burnt-offering altar, brass laver, and surrounding court, then turns to the workers and an itemized reckoning of materials. Its repeated reports that objects were “made” correspond to earlier instructions in [[Exodus 27]], [[Exodus 30]], and [[Exodus 31]], emphasizing fulfillment of command. The chapter is therefore especially relevant to [[Sacrifice]], [[Obedience]], [[Service]], and [[Temples]]. Its named personnel, measurements, and balanced figures establish careful literary and administrative presentation, but they do not independently verify the account's date, authorship, census, or material quantities.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Altar and equipment (vv. 1–7):** dimensions, horns, brass vessels, grate, rings, and carrying staves.

2. **Laver (v. 8):** a brass laver and base made from the women's lookingglasses.

3. **Court (vv. 9–20):** hangings, pillars, sockets, entrance, and pins. The intended enclosure is 100 by 50 cubits; on the east, 15 + 20 + 15 cubits accounts for the full width.

4. **Personnel and reckoning (vv. 21–23):** Ithamar is associated with the Levitical reckoning, while Bezaleel and Aholiab receive artisan attribution.

5. **Material totals and allocations (vv. 24–31):** gold, silver, and brass are totaled; the uses of silver and brass are then specified.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's clearest theological emphasis is [[Obedience]]: Bezaleel is credited with making all that the Lord commanded Moses (v. 22). The altar directly relates the chapter to [[Sacrifice]], while the laver's washing function is explained in [[Exodus 30]]. Skilled labor and Levitical work also provide concrete examples of [[Service]].

The offered materials may invite comparison with [[Consecration]], and the ordered sanctuary provides background for [[Temples]], but these are broader applications rather than doctrines explicitly formulated in Exodus 38. The chapter does not say that every contribution was voluntary or that later sacred projects must reproduce its organization, measurements, or materials.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The KJV term “lookingglasses” in verse 8 means mirrors—reflective objects associated here with women assembling at the tabernacle entrance. The verse does not explain the women's function, how the mirrors were obtained, why they were selected, or whether symbolism was intended.

“The sum of the tabernacle” in verse 21 introduces a reckoning or inventory. KJV “brass” is an ancient-metal translation term and should not be used to claim a precisely known modern alloy from this chapter alone.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Exodus 38 participates in a clear command-and-fulfillment pattern. The altar and court correspond closely to [[Exodus 27]], the laver to [[Exodus 30]], and the artisan notice to [[Exodus 31]]. The statement that Bezaleel “made all that the LORD commanded Moses” (v. 22) makes conformity to the instructions an explicit literary emphasis, continued in [[Exodus 39]].

The silver reckoning is also internally coherent: 603,550 half-shekels equal 301,775 shekels, corresponding to 100 talents plus 1,775 shekels under the account's implied ratio. This supports careful numerical composition or editing. Neither the verbal correspondence nor the arithmetic determines whether the material originated in contemporaneous records, later compilation, or both, and neither independently proves the historical totals.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the account preserve the source of the laver's brass without explaining the function of the assembled women?

- Why are the uses of silver and brass itemized while only an aggregate total is given for gold?

- Is the differentiation of materials primarily practical, symbolic, literary, or some combination?

- How does the command-and-fulfillment pattern shape the chapter's portrayal of obedience?

- What does the internal consistency of the silver figures establish, and what historical conclusions remain unavailable?

- What purpose is served by naming Ithamar, Bezaleel, and Aholiab alongside the material reckoning?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Exodus 38:1–20 with [[Exodus 27]], the laver and half-shekel material with [[Exodus 30]], and the artisan notices with [[Exodus 31]] and [[Exodus 35]]. Then follow the completion formula into [[Exodus 39]]. Compare the reported total of 603,550 with [[Numbers 1]], while remembering that an identical figure demonstrates textual coordination more directly than independent corroboration. Track both exact correspondences and added or omitted details, especially the source of the laver's brass and the laver's absence from the brass allocation in Exodus 38:29–31.
