# Exodus 40 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Exodus 40]] · My notes: [[Exodus 40 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Exodus 40 concludes the book with construction giving way to presence and guidance. The LORD commands Moses to erect and consecrate the tabernacle; Moses carries out the setup; the cloud covers the tent; and the glory of the LORD fills it. The sanctuary then becomes the visible center of Israel's journeys. The ending therefore joins [[Obedience]], worship, divine presence, and continuing [[Exodus and Deliverance]] without claiming that Israel's journey is finished.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Instructions (vv. 1-15):** erect and arrange the tabernacle, consecrate its furnishings, and prepare Aaron and his sons for priestly ministry.
2. **Compliance summary (v. 16):** Moses does what the LORD commanded.
3. **Setup (vv. 17-33):** the structure, furnishings, offerings, laver, and court are put in place. Seven action units end with a command-fulfillment formula.
4. **Divine manifestation (vv. 34-35):** cloud and glory fill the completed sanctuary, preventing Moses from entering while the cloud abides there.
5. **Guidance (vv. 36-38):** cloud and fire regulate Israel's movement throughout its journeys.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents holiness as ordered approach rather than casual access. Space is screened and arranged; objects are anointed; priests are washed, clothed, and sanctified; and washing precedes entry or approach to the altar. These details make the chapter relevant to [[Priesthood]], [[Ordinances]], [[Sacrifice]], and [[Temples]]. Care is needed, however: the text describes Israel's tabernacle and should not automatically be treated as a complete blueprint for later temple practice.

The glory arrives only after the work is finished, making divine presence the narrative culmination of obedient construction. Yet Moses's inability to enter prevents a simplistic conclusion that ritual preparation gives human beings control over God.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The most secure literary observation is repetition. Verse 16 gives a comprehensive statement of compliance, and verses 19, 21, 23, 25, 27, 29, and 32 repeat variants of "as the LORD commanded Moses." This strongly emphasizes [[Obedience]]. The sevenfold count may be literarily suggestive, but symbolic intent is not stated and should remain tentative.

The closing scene also changes the chapter's mode: detailed human actions cease, and the cloud and glory become the active elements. The structure built by Moses becomes the means by which Israel knows whether to move or remain.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the narrative report some setup actions in detail while the anointing and priestly investiture commanded in verses 9-15 are not explicitly narrated in the same execution sequence? Compare [[Leviticus 8]].
- Does the glory's arrival function as approval, occupation, or both? Which part is stated, and which part is inferred from sequence?
- What is the significance of Moses being unable to enter in verse 35, especially after he has served as the principal mediator throughout [[Exodus 40]]?
- How does the stationary sanctuary become the instrument for directing a mobile people in verses 36-38?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the cloud and fire in [[Exodus 13]], the Sinai cloud in [[Exodus 24]], and the tabernacle cloud in [[Numbers 9]]. Study the priestly commands beside their enactment in [[Leviticus 8]]. For the recurring sanctuary-filling pattern, compare [[1 Kings 8]] and [[2 Chronicles 7]]. These comparisons clarify both continuity and development without requiring that every passage have the same immediate purpose.
