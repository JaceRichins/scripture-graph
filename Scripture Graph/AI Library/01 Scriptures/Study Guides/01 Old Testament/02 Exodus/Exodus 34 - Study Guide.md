# Exodus 34 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Exodus 34]] · My notes: [[Exodus 34 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Moses hews new tables of stone—He goes up into Mount Sinai for forty days—The Lord proclaims His name and attributes and reveals His law—He makes another covenant with Israel—The skin of Moses’ face shines, and he wears a veil.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter falls into five movements:

1. **Replacement tablets and ascent (vv. 1-4):** Moses hews tablets like the first and ascends alone.
2. **Divine proclamation and intercession (vv. 5-9):** The LORD proclaims His name and attributes; Moses worships and pleads for God's presence and pardon.
3. **Covenant terms and exclusive loyalty (vv. 10-17):** God promises marvels and warns against covenants, intermarriage, and idolatry with the land's inhabitants.
4. **Ritual and calendar stipulations (vv. 18-26):** feasts, firstborn redemption, sabbath rest, and sacrifice regulations are restated, largely paralleling [[Exodus 23]].
5. **Descent, radiance, and veil (vv. 27-35):** the covenant words are written, and Moses returns with a shining face, adopting a recurring pattern of veiling before Israel and unveiling before the LORD.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verses 6-7 proclaim God as 'merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth,' immediately after Israel's golden-calf apostasy — mercy-related predicates are named first and are more numerous. The same declaration insists God 'will by no means clear the guilty' and describes consequences reaching to the third and fourth generation, so [[Justice and Mercy]] are held together rather than one replacing the other; the text does not state that either quality outweighs the other in length or force. Moses immediately applies this proclamation in prayer, asking God to pardon Israel's iniquity and take them as His inheritance (34:9) — the proclamation shapes intercession, not just abstract theology.

The renewed [[Covenants|covenant]] (v. 10) again demands exclusive worship: Israel is warned against covenants, intermarriage, and religious syncretism with the land's inhabitants (vv. 12-16), and the LORD names Himself 'Jealous' (v. 14). The chapter places this demand for exclusive loyalty alongside the mercy of vv. 6-7 without explaining how the two relate; readers must supply that harmonization.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The Hebrew verb behind 'shone' in verse 29 (qaran) shares its consonantal root with the noun for 'horn' (qeren). This is the likely source of Jerome's Vulgate rendering of Moses' face as 'horned' (cornuta), which shaped centuries of Western art, including Michelangelo's horned Moses. Whether Jerome was making a translation error or deliberately using horn imagery to convey emitted rays of light is disputed; the surrounding narrative (fear, then veiling) supports 'shone,' but the history is a caution about how translation choices can outlive their original basis.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The narrative moves from tablets broken in [[Exodus 32]] to replacement tablets carried down radiant in this chapter; speech becomes writing, and writing becomes public instruction, with Moses mediating each stage. The proclamation of 34:6-7 became a formula reused elsewhere in the Old Testament, most directly when Moses himself re-quotes it in [[Numbers 14]] to intercede for Israel again.

The closing scene also repeats a pattern: Moses goes before the LORD, removes the veil, comes out, delivers the LORD's words to Israel unveiled, then veils his face again until his next encounter with God (vv. 33-35). Exodus does not explain the veil's purpose directly. The apostle Paul later develops it typologically, contrasting Moses' veiled and fading glory with an unveiled glory in Christ ([[2 Corinthians 3]]) — a later Christian reading, not a claim the Exodus text makes about itself.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How should mercy, forgiveness, and the refusal to 'clear the guilty' be held together in verses 6-7, without letting either cancel the other?
- Why does Moses hew the replacement tablets himself, rather than receiving tablets already 'the work of God' as in the first giving ([[Exodus 32]])?
- Why does Moses mention Israel's stiffneckedness while asking God to accompany and pardon the people (34:9) — is this a reason for favor or an obstacle to overcome?
- Do verses 1, 27, and 28 describe a single act of writing or leave the writer's identity genuinely unresolved?
- What function does the veil serve within Exodus 34 itself, before considering Paul's later interpretation in [[2 Corinthians 3]]?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the breaking of the first tablets in [[Exodus 32]], Moses' request to see divine glory in [[Exodus 33]], and the replacement-tablet account in [[Deuteronomy 10]]. For the later reuse of the divine proclamation, compare [[Numbers 14]]. The close legal parallels with [[Exodus 23]] help identify which covenant stipulations are repeated. [[2 Corinthians 3]] is essential for Christian reception of the radiant face and veil, but its theological application should be distinguished from a strictly historical-literary reading of Exodus.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
