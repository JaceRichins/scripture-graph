# Exodus 7 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Exodus 7]] · My notes: [[Exodus 7 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Exodus 7 moves from commission to confrontation. Moses and Aaron receive defined speaking roles, obey the command to approach Pharaoh, and present a court sign that Egyptian magicians imitate but do not overcome. The first plague then turns Egypt's water to blood, kills the fish, and deprives the population of drinkable river water. Throughout the chapter, the announced goal is both [[Exodus and Deliverance]] and recognition of the LORD's identity and authority.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Commission and forecast (7:1-7):** Moses and Aaron are assigned their roles; resistance, judgments, deliverance, and recognition of the LORD are announced.

2. **The palace sign (7:8-13):** Aaron's rod becomes a serpent; the magicians imitate the act; Aaron's rod swallows theirs; Pharaoh remains unresponsive.

3. **Warning at the river (7:14-19):** Pharaoh's refusal is named, the demand to release Israel is repeated, and the water judgment is announced.

4. **The river struck (7:20-25):** The warning is fulfilled, the magicians imitate the sign, Pharaoh withdraws, and the Egyptians dig for water.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Exodus 7 portrays prophetic authority as delegated and communicative: the LORD commands Moses, and Aaron speaks as Moses's prophet. This supports a functional reading of Moses as God's authorized representative without asserting that Moses becomes divine in being. See [[Prophets]].

The hardening passages require care. The chapter announces that the LORD will harden Pharaoh's heart while also describing Pharaoh as refusing, failing to hear, and declining to take the sign to heart. The final text therefore retains both divine action and human resistance, but it does not explain their philosophical relationship or settle debates about [[Agency]]. Source-critical readers may explain variations in hardening language through the combination or editing of different textual traditions rather than a single deliberately constructed theory of concurrent agency.

The signs also serve more than spectacle: they accompany the demand for Israel's release and are intended to produce recognition of the LORD. Because the magicians imitate the initial acts, the narrative does not treat an impressive act alone as sufficient identification of divine authority. Its contrast instead includes command, announced purpose, control, and outcome. See [[Miracles]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The KJV renders the palace creature as “serpent,” but Exodus 7:9–12 uses the Hebrew noun *tannin*, whereas Exodus 4:3 uses *nachash*. *Tannin* has a broader semantic range that can include a large serpent or dragon-like creature. This lexical difference is verifiable; claims that it deliberately invokes royal symbolism, cosmic conflict, or a particular Egyptian deity remain plausible but unproven interpretations.

Exodus 7:4 calls Israel the LORD's “armies,” and related host language appears when Israel leaves Egypt in Exodus 12:41. The wording presents Israel as a people belonging to the LORD, but it does not by itself establish a literal military organization or formation.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Two repeated patterns organize the chapter. First, formulas stating that Moses and Aaron acted as commanded emphasize their obedience in contrast with Pharaoh's refusal to hear. Second, statements about knowing the LORD interpret the signs for the reader before narrating them.

The rod episode is structured as comparison rather than simple uniqueness: Aaron acts, the magicians imitate, and Aaron's rod swallows theirs. The first plague similarly moves from announcement to exact consequences—blood, dead fish, stench, and inability to drink—before contrasting Pharaoh's withdrawal with the Egyptians' search for water.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the narrative use *tannin* in the palace scene but *nachash* in Exodus 4:3?

- How do the statements that God hardens Pharaoh relate to the statements describing Pharaoh's refusal and disregard?

- What does the magicians' ability to imitate the initial signs contribute to the chapter's theology of [[Miracles]]?

- Why does the narrative show ordinary Egyptians digging for water immediately after describing Pharaoh's indifference?

- Does Exodus 7:25 mark the duration of the water plague, the interval before the next warning, or both? The verse itself says only that seven days were fulfilled after the river was struck.
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the related but variant rod signs in [[Exodus 4]] and Exodus 7, noting the different performers, audiences, purposes, and Hebrew creature terms. Trace the plague motifs into [[Exodus 8]], including the changing role of the magicians and the different formulations of Pharaoh's hardening. Numbers 33:38–39 provides a modest final-form chronological comparison for Aaron's age. Psalm 78:44 and Psalm 105:29 preserve later poetic memories of the water-to-blood plague. Second Timothy 3:8 names Jannes and Jambres as opponents of Moses, but identifying them specifically as Exodus 7's unnamed magicians belongs to later reception history and requires additional documentation.
