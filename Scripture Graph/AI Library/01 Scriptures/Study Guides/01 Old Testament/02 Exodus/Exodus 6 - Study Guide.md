# Exodus 6 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Exodus 6]] · My notes: [[Exodus 6 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Exodus 6 answers the preceding setback with renewed divine promises. The LORD recalls the [[Abrahamic Covenant]], promises to bring Israel out of bondage, take them as his people, and give them the promised land. Israel does not yet heed Moses because of anguish and cruel bondage, while Moses doubts that Pharaoh will hear him. A selective genealogy then locates Aaron and Moses within Levi before the narrative resumes the commission leading into [[Exodus 7]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A useful outline is:

1. **Assurance after the setback (6:1):** Pharaoh will ultimately drive Israel out.
2. **Divine identity, covenant, and promises (6:2-8):** The LORD recalls the patriarchal covenant and declares what he will do.
3. **Nonresponse and renewed commission (6:9-13):** Israel does not heed Moses, and Moses questions whether Pharaoh will hear him.
4. **Selective genealogy (6:14-27):** Reuben and Simeon introduce an extended treatment of Levi that identifies Aaron and Moses.
5. **Narrative resumption (6:28-30):** The command and Moses's objection reappear.

The repetition gives the genealogy an identity-establishing effect in the received chapter, but it does not by itself determine whether the unit originated with the surrounding narrative or was incorporated later.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents [[Exodus and Deliverance]] as more than escape from forced labor. In fulfillment of [[Covenants]], God promises release, redemption, covenant belonging, and inheritance (6:6-8). These elements are closely joined without becoming interchangeable. Verse 9 also contributes a sober perspective on [[Adversity]]: a promise of hope does not instantly remove the effects of anguish and cruel bondage, and the text explains Israel's nonresponse through those conditions rather than explicitly calling it rebellion.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated declaration "I am the LORD" marks the speech in 6:2-8 and returns in 6:29. Seven principal first-person commitments in 6:6-8 give the promise cumulative force, though the text does not explicitly assign symbolic meaning to the number. "Uncircumcised lips" (6:12, 30) probably expresses Moses's perceived inadequacy or impediment as a speaker, but its precise nuance remains uncertain.

Exodus 6:3 distinguishes the patriarchal disclosure as God Almighty from being known by the name JEHOVAH. An experiential reading of "known" fits verse 7, but Genesis also portrays patriarchal figures invoking the LORD's name. Retrospective narrative usage, fuller experiential disclosure, and distinct textual traditions are all serious explanations; the chapter alone does not decide among them.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The genealogy is selective in its received form. It begins with Reuben and Simeon, then gives sustained attention to Levi, including Aaron's sons and Phinehas. Verses 26-27 explicitly reconnect the genealogical figures with the commission of Aaron and Moses. Afterward, verses 28-30 resume the command and objection found before the genealogy.

This arrangement gives the genealogy a coherent identity-establishing function. It also supplies material used in later Levitical and Aaronide narratives. That literary effect does not establish whether the genealogy was composed with the surrounding account, incorporated from an inherited record, or shaped through later priestly interests.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary And Source-Critical Evidence
> Exodus 6:3 says that God appeared to the patriarchs as God Almighty but was not known to them by the name JEHOVAH. This stands in prima facie literary tension with Genesis passages in which the narrative uses LORD and portrays earlier figures invoking the LORD's name. The observation is secure, while explanations of the relationship are disputed.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 1.0 · accepted literary observation; explanations disputed · [[Literary And Source-Critical Evidence in Exodus 6|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary-Compositional *(tentative)*
> The selective expansion of Levi's line, the explicit identification of Aaron and Moses, and the resumption of Moses's objection after the genealogy provide substantial internal evidence that the genealogy was positioned to establish the agents' identities and lineage in the chapter's received form.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.8 · literary effect accepted; editorial intention uncertain

> [!evidence] Compositional And Literary Evidence *(tentative)*
> The repeated commission and objection surrounding a selectively Levitical genealogy are consistent with a text shaped through literary insertion, compilation, or redaction. This is meaningful compositional evidence, but intentional narrative suspension and resumption can explain the same surface features without reconstructing particular documentary sources.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What kind of knowledge is denied or deferred by "by my name JEHOVAH was I not known to them" in 6:3?
- How does the explanation in 6:9 affect moral judgments about Israel's failure to listen?
- Why does the genealogy expand Levi's line while stopping after the first three tribes?
- What might Moses mean by "uncircumcised lips," and why is the expression repeated around the genealogy?
- How are release, covenant belonging, knowledge of God, and land inheritance related without becoming interchangeable?
- How does the promise in 6:1 reframe Pharaoh's apparent power?
