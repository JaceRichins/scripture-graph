# Exodus 28 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Exodus 28]] · My notes: [[Exodus 28 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Exodus 28 gives Moses detailed instructions for the sacred vestments by which Aaron and his sons are set apart to minister in the priest's office (v. 1, 3, 41). The garments—ephod, breastplate, robe, coat, mitre, and girdle—are made 'for glory and for beauty' (v. 2, 40) from gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen, materials that echo the veil and coverings of the tabernacle (compare [[Exodus 25]], [[Exodus 26]]). Two features stand out: the onyx stones on Aaron's shoulders and the twelve stones of the breastplate, both engraved with Israel's names 'for a memorial' (v. 12, 29), and the Urim and Thummim placed within the breastplate (v. 30). The chapter commands, but does not narrate, Aaron's anointing and consecration (v. 41; the ceremony itself follows in [[Exodus 29]]). It closes with the bell-and-pomegranate hem of the robe, heard when Aaron enters and leaves the holy place (v. 35), and linen breeches required for all officiating priests (v. 42-43).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Appointment and commission (vv. 1–5):** Aaron and his sons are selected, and gifted artisans receive the garment commission.
2. **Ephod and shoulder memorials (vv. 6–14):** the ephod carries two stones engraved with Israel's names.
3. **Breastplate of judgment (vv. 15–30):** twelve tribal stones, gold fastenings, and the Urim and Thummim are placed over Aaron's heart.
4. **Blue robe (vv. 31–35):** its reinforced opening and bell-and-pomegranate hem are specified.
5. **Headpiece and remaining garments (vv. 36–40):** the gold holiness plate, coat, mitre, girdle, and garments for Aaron's sons are prescribed.
6. **Investiture and safeguards (vv. 41–43):** anointing and consecration are commanded, and linen breeches are required for sanctuary service.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents [[Aaronic Priesthood]] ministry as a divine commission rather than a self-appointed role (v. 1). Garments do not operate as mere decoration: they mark [[Consecration]], carry Israel's names before the Lord, and regulate approach to holy space. Aaron's bearing of the tribal names on his shoulders and heart makes representative priesthood a strong reading of the text, while the forehead plate joins holiness with responsibility for sacred gifts. The chapter also treats skilled workmanship as compatible with divine endowment: the makers are filled with a 'spirit of wisdom' for their task (v. 3).

Care is needed applying these themes beyond the chapter. Exodus 28 concerns a particular Aaronic sanctuary order within the [[Law of Moses]]; it does not by itself define every later form of priesthood, sacred clothing, or temple practice, nor does it establish that sincere worship in general requires priestly mediation.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Several KJV terms can obscure the instructions. In context, **ouches** are gold settings or mounts for stones, **habergeon** denotes a mail-like garment whose reinforced opening resists tearing, and **curious work** means skillful or elaborate workmanship rather than something merely unusual. The repeated verb **bear** is especially important: Aaron bears Israel's names on his shoulders (v. 12), their names and judgment on his heart (vv. 29–30), and the iniquity of their holy things at his forehead (v. 38).
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter moves across the priest's clothed body: shoulders, heart, robe, forehead, and loins. Israel's names appear twice—first collectively on two shoulder stones, then individually on twelve breastplate stones—and the repeated verb 'bear' ties these placements together (vv. 12, 29-30, 38). The recurring phrase 'for glory and for beauty' (v. 2, 40) is applied to both Aaron and his sons, framing the whole garment-making project even though the sons' list is far shorter. The text explicitly calls the stones memorials and states where they are worn; it does not decode shoulders as strength or the heart as compassion, so such symbolic readings should remain labeled interpretation rather than observation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. Why are Israel's names represented both on the shoulders and over the heart, and how much symbolism does the text itself authorize for those locations?
2. What does it mean for Aaron to "bear the iniquity of the holy things" while enabling the gifts to be accepted (v. 38)?
3. Why does the chapter associate divinely given wisdom with textile work and engraving (v. 3)?
4. What can be concluded about the Urim and Thummim from verse 30 alone, and what requires evidence from other passages?
5. How do the warnings of death in verses 35 and 43 shape the chapter's understanding of holiness and accountability?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the instructions here with their extensive implementation parallel in [[Exodus 39]]. Read [[Exodus 29]] to study the consecration ritual that follows the clothing command in Exodus 28:41, and [[Exodus 40]] for the establishment of tabernacle service. When tracing the Urim and Thummim or the expression "bear iniquity," distinguish information supplied by those other texts from what Exodus 28 itself actually explains.
