## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Altar and equipment (vv. 1–8):** dimensions, horns, brass overlay, working vessels, grate, rings, staves, and hollow construction.
2. **Court and gate (vv. 9–19):** linen boundaries, pillars and sockets, a symmetrical eastern gate, and the court's overall dimensions.
3. **Oil and priestly service (vv. 20–21):** Israel supplies the oil; Aaron and his sons maintain the lamp as a continuing statute.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter connects [[Sacrifice]], ordered sacred space, and priestly responsibility. Israel supplies the oil, while Aaron and his sons tend the lamp before the LORD (vv. 20–21), showing coordinated participation in worship. Verse 8 also presents the altar as conforming to what was shown on the mount, making [[Revelation]] part of the narrative's account of its design. Connections to later temple symbolism or the [[Light of Christ]] may be considered as theological applications, but they are not explanations supplied by Exodus 27 itself.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The phrase “burn always” stands beside “from evening to morning.” The safest reading is that the light is an enduring and regularly maintained institution; the wording does not by itself settle every question about daytime practice. Likewise, “as it was shewed thee in the mount” presents the instructions as conformity to a disclosed pattern. The repeated “thou shalt make” gives the first nineteen verses the cadence of a construction mandate, while “thou shalt command” in verse 20 begins an ongoing communal and priestly obligation.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The court's arithmetic is notably coherent: 100-cubit long sides correspond to twenty pillars each, the 50-cubit west side to ten pillars, and the eastern frontage divides exactly into 15 + 20 + 15 cubits with 3 + 4 + 3 pillars. This supports a deliberately coordinated description, although it does not prove historical execution or hidden numerical symbolism. [[Exodus 38]] supplies the strongest literary comparison because it reports these items as made, turning the commands of [[Exodus 27]] into a command-and-fulfillment sequence. [[Leviticus 24]] similarly restates the lamp obligation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Architectural Coherence
> The measurements and pillar counts constitute strong internal evidence of a coordinated court plan: each one-hundred-cubit side has twenty pillars, the fifty-cubit west side has ten, and the fifty-cubit east side is partitioned into fifteen-, twenty-, and fifteen-cubit sections with three, four, and three pillars respectively.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 1.0 · Arithmetic affirmed; larger significance limited · [[Internal Architectural Coherence in Exodus 27|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Command-Fulfillment Literary Correspondence
> Exodus 38:1-7 and 38:9-20 closely follow the altar and court specifications of Exodus 27:1-19 as construction reports. This correspondence is strong evidence that Exodus 38 is literarily designed to present fulfillment of the earlier commands.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · Broadly accepted literary correspondence · [[Command-Fulfillment Literary Correspondence in Exodus 27|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary/Theological Pattern *(tentative)*
> The court's brass sockets with silver fittings, set against the wholly-brass altar of burnt offering at the court's entrance, is often read by commentators as part of a graduated use of materials (brass outermost, silver and eventually gold moving inward toward the most holy place) that visually marks increasing sanctity closer to the presence of God.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.4 · Observed materials; disputed and insufficiently sourced symbolism
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Exodus 27:1–19 with Exodus 38:1–20, attending to what the fulfillment account repeats, condenses, or adds. Compare Exodus 27:20–21 with Leviticus 24:2–4 for the lamp's oil, schedule, and priestly oversight. Exodus 26:31–35 clarifies the veil and testimony that locate the lamp in Exodus 27:21, while Exodus 25:40 provides the closest parallel to the pattern shown on the mount.
