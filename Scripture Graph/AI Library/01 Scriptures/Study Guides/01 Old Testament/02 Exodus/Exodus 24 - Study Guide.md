# Exodus 24 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Exodus 24]] · My notes: [[Exodus 24 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Exodus 24 narrates Israel's public acceptance and ritual ratification of the Sinai covenant. The people hear the Lord's words and twice promise to do them; Moses writes and publicly reads the covenant record; sacrifices and blood solemnize the agreement. Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders then ascend, see the God of Israel, and eat and drink. Finally, Moses proceeds toward the cloud-covered summit to receive written law and commandments. The chapter brings together [[Covenants]], [[Obedience]], [[Sacrifice]], [[Records and Sacred Writings]], and [[Revelation]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Approach prescribed (24:1–2):** The people, Israel's representatives, and Moses receive differentiated instructions.
2. **Covenant ratified (24:3–8):** Moses reports and writes the words; Israel assents; sacrifices are offered; the book is read; the people renew their assent; and blood is applied.
3. **Representatives behold and eat (24:9–11):** Israel's leaders ascend, see God, are not struck, and eat and drink.
4. **Moses approaches the cloud (24:12–18):** Moses and Joshua ascend, arrangements are made for matters below, and Moses ultimately enters the cloud for forty days and nights.

The chapter moves from the assembled people to representatives and then to Moses, while also moving from spoken words to written instruction.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter portrays covenant commitment as a response to disclosed words: the people hear, assent, and undertake to obey. It also links [[Revelation]] with preservation and teaching. Moses writes the words, reads the book publicly, and is promised stone tables containing law and commandments so that he may teach them (24:4, 7, 12).

The blood is explicitly called "the blood of the covenant" (24:8). In this chapter it solemnizes the Lord's covenant with Israel. Later Christian texts reuse that language, but their applications should not be substituted for Exodus 24's immediate Sinai setting or treated as proof that every later meaning was consciously predictive.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Forms of "all" recur around the covenant words and Israel's response: Moses reports "all the words" and "all the judgments"; the people promise to do "all the words"; Moses writes "all the words"; and the blood concerns "all these words" (24:3–4, 8). The second response adds "and be obedient" (24:7), reinforcing the movement from hearing to action.

The vision language also uses explicit comparisons. What is under God's feet is "as it were" sapphire and heavenlike in clarity, while the visible glory is "like" devouring fire (24:10, 17). These comparisons convey the manifestation without providing a detailed description of God's form.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The narrative intensifies spatially and visually. The people remain below; selected representatives ascend and see sapphire-like brilliance; Moses ultimately enters the cloud as the glory appears like devouring fire. This strongly supports a pattern of differentiated access, although it may express narrative and leadership hierarchy rather than a complete later temple system. Joshua accompanies Moses during part of the ascent, but the chapter does not specify his final stopping point.

The vision report is restrained but direct: the leaders "saw God," yet the description focuses on what was under his feet and notes that he did not lay his hand upon them (24:10–11). This creates a real tension with [[Exodus 33]], where seeing the Lord's face is said to be fatal. A mediated manifestation is one possible explanation, not an explicit resolution supplied by the text.

The six days of cloud followed by a call on the seventh (24:16) may recall creation-week rhythm, but the chapter uses no creation, rest, or Sabbath language; the connection remains tentative.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why is Israel's commitment recorded twice, and what does the public reading add to the first response?
- What is included in "all the words" and the "book of the covenant"? The chapter gives no internal table of contents.
- Does the meal express covenant fellowship, emphasize survival in God's presence, or serve another function the narrator leaves unexplained?
- How should the elders' sight of God be held together with Exodus 33:20–23?
- How should Moses's exclusive nearness in verse 2 be related to the elders' vision and Joshua's accompaniment in verses 13–14?
- Does the six-day wait and seventh-day summons deliberately evoke creation, or is it simply chronological?
- How does this ratification prepare for the covenant's breach and renewal in [[Exodus 32]]–[[Exodus 34]]?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare [[Exodus 19]] for Sinai's boundaries, cloud, fire, and preparation; [[Exodus 20]]–[[Exodus 23]] for the covenant laws preceding this ceremony; and [[Exodus 32]]–[[Exodus 34]] for the breach, renewal, replacement tables, and another forty-day period. Compare [[Deuteronomy 9]] for a later recollection of the ascent. [[Matthew 26]], [[Luke 22]], [[Hebrews 9]], and [[Hebrews 12]] show how later Christian writers received covenant-blood and Sinai language. Those texts demonstrate canonical reception and theological development, not necessarily conscious prediction by the original human author or identical meaning among all the rituals.
