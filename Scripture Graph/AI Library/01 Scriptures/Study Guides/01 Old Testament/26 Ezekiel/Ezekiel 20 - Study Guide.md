# Ezekiel 20 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Ezekiel 20]] · My notes: [[Ezekiel 20 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Ezekiel 20 answers an attempted inquiry with a prosecutorial history of Israel. From Egypt through the wilderness and settlement in the land, each generation receives divine disclosure and obligation yet turns toward idols. The repeated cycle is rebellion, threatened judgment, and restraint for the LORD's name. Verses 32-44 then turn from review to promise: Israel will be gathered, judged, brought into the covenant bond, purged of rebels, and accepted in renewed worship. This makes the chapter central to both [[Scattering of Israel]] and [[Gathering of Israel]], while refusing to separate restoration from repentance and judgment.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The inquiry refused (20:1-4):** Elders sit before Ezekiel, but the LORD orders an indictment rather than an oracle favorable to them.
2. **Israel in Egypt (20:5-9):** Election and promised deliverance are answered by attachment to Egyptian idols; judgment is restrained for the divine name.
3. **Two wilderness generations (20:10-26):** Statutes, judgments, and Sabbaths are given; both generations rebel, and the second is handed over to death-producing practices.
4. **Rebellion in the land and in Ezekiel's day (20:27-32):** High-place worship and child sacrifice continue, making the elders' inquiry unacceptable.
5. **Gathering, judgment, and restored worship (20:33-44):** A new wilderness encounter brings Israel under the covenant rod, excludes rebels, and culminates in accepted worship and remorse.
6. **The southern forest oracle (20:45-49):** An unquenchable fire is announced in imagery that Ezekiel's audience calls parabolic.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents the [[Sabbath Day]] as a sign of belonging to and being sanctified by God. It also gives a demanding account of [[Covenants]]: election does not remove accountability, and gathering does not bypass judgment. Divine action for the LORD's name joins [[Justice and Mercy]] without treating Israel's conduct as meritorious. Restoration leads Israel to remember and loathe its former evil, linking [[Gathering of Israel]] with [[Repentance]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The refrain “that ye may know that I am the LORD” links Sabbath observance, judgment, gathering, and return to the land. Knowledge here is more than information: it is recognition produced through the LORD's acts. Repeated references to the LORD's “name” likewise give the chapter an outward horizon—Israel's history displays the divine reputation before the nations. The phrase “mighty hand” and “stretched out arm” applies exodus language to the future gathering, but “fury poured out” makes the renewed exodus an encounter with judgment.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The historical review is built from repetition with variation. Commands, rebellion, threatened fury, and restraint recur across generations, making continued disobedience the controlling pattern. Verses 33-44 then reuse earlier motifs in transformed form: scattering is answered by gathering, the first wilderness by the “wilderness of the people,” and the original covenant by passage under the rod into its bond. The closing forest-fire oracle changes imagery sharply, while the audience's complaint about parables foregrounds the problem of hearing prophetic language without accepting its warning.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why do the elders expect an answer while continuing the practices condemned in verses 30-31?
- How does action for the LORD's name differ from declaring Israel innocent?
- What details make the gathering in verses 33-38 an act of judgment as well as deliverance?
- How should verses 25-26 be read alongside the life-giving statutes of verses 11, 13, and 21?
- Why might hearers call the fire oracle a parable rather than respond to its warning?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the Sabbath-sign language with Exodus 31:13 and the promise of life through obedience with Leviticus 18:5. Compare the rejected inquiry with [[Ezekiel 14]], where elders and concealed idolatry appear together. The concern for the LORD's name, gathering, and sanctification can be followed into [[Ezekiel 36]], while [[Ezekiel 37]] develops gathering and covenant restoration further.
