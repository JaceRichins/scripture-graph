# Ezekiel 36 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Ezekiel 36]] · My notes: [[Ezekiel 36 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> In the last days, all the house of Israel will be gathered to their own lands—The Lord will give them a new heart and a new spirit—They will have His gospel law.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The land's humiliation and vindication (36:1–15):** The mountains and ruined cities are addressed as witnesses to foreign seizure, shame, and promised renewal.
2. **Why Israel was scattered (36:16–21):** Israel defiled the land; judgment dispersed the people; exile occasioned the profaning of God's name.
3. **Restoration for God's holy name (36:22–30):** God promises gathering, cleansing, a new heart, his Spirit, obedience, covenant fellowship, and abundance.
4. **Remembrance without boasting (36:31–32):** Restored Israel remembers and repudiates its former conduct.
5. **New-Eden renewal before the nations (36:33–38):** Ruins become inhabited, desolation becomes fruitful, and the nations recognize God's work.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter holds divine judgment and restorative mercy together. Israel's [[Scattering of Israel]] is presented as judgment for defiling the land through bloodshed and idolatry (36:17-19), while the promised [[Gathering of Israel]] is explicitly not a reward for national merit (36:22, 32).

Restoration includes cleansing, a new heart, God's spirit, and enabled obedience (36:25-27). These themes are relevant to [[Grace]], [[Conversion]], and [[Repentance]], but the passage's immediate setting is the corporate renewal of Israel rather than a complete later system of those doctrines. The narrated order places remembrance and remorse after the promises of renewal, though this literary order does not establish a universal chronology of repentance.

The covenant formula in 36:28—Israel will be God's people and he will be Israel's God—connects the chapter directly with [[Covenants]]. Compare the closely related wording in [[Ezekiel 11]] and the inwardly written law in [[Jeremiah 31]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The contrast between a **stony heart** and a **heart of flesh** is metaphorical. In context, stone suggests an unresponsive moral condition, while flesh suggests a living heart responsive to God; “flesh” is not being used here as a synonym for sinfulness.

The repeated divine first-person promises—“I will take,” “I will gather,” “I will cleanse,” “I will give,” and “I will put”—make God the principal agent of restoration. Yet 36:37 also says that Israel will inquire of God, preserving a role for petition within the promised divine action.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter repeatedly reverses key terms and conditions: possession by enemies becomes Israel's inheritance; desolation becomes cultivation; ruined cities become inhabited; famine and reproach become abundance; uncleanness becomes cleansing; a stony heart becomes a heart of flesh; scattering becomes gathering. These reversals bind the restoration of people and land into one argument.

The land is personified throughout the first oracle: it hears, bears shame, yields fruit, receives inhabitants, and ceases to bereave them. At the conclusion, observers compare the renewed land to Eden (36:35). The comparison functions as a compact image of creation-like renewal, not an explicit claim that the geographical garden itself has returned.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does restoring God's name require both Israel's return and Israel's moral transformation?
- What changes when 36:25–27 is read first as a promise to a dispersed people rather than only as a description of an isolated individual's conversion?
- How does the order of cleansing, inward renewal, obedience, and remorse shape the chapter's account of [[Grace]] and [[Repentance]]?
- Why does God invite inquiry in 36:37 after repeatedly declaring what he will certainly do?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare 36:24–28 with [[Ezekiel 11]] and 37:21–28 in [[Ezekiel 37]]. All three passages connect gathering with covenant restoration, but each emphasizes different features. Compare also [[Jeremiah 31]], where the law is written in the heart. Deuteronomy 30:1–10 is an earlier and especially useful parallel because it joins return from dispersion, heart transformation, obedience, fertility, and renewed enjoyment of the land.
