# D&C 46 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 46]] · My notes: [[D&C 46 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 46 connects three concerns: access to church meetings, the proper purpose of spiritual gifts, and protection against deception. Public meetings are not to exclude anyone, and earnest nonmembers are not to be cast out of sacrament or confirmation meetings (vv. 3-6). Verse 4 nevertheless distinguishes attending a sacrament meeting from partaking after an unresolved trespass. The chapter then places [[Spiritual Gifts]] within a framework of prayer, thanksgiving, holiness, right motives, and discernment (vv. 7-10, 27). Its repeated governing emphasis is that gifts are given for the benefit and profit of others (vv. 9, 12, 16, 26, 29).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A useful outline is:

1. **Meetings and access (vv. 1-6):** written instruction, spiritual direction, welcome for outsiders, and a distinction between attending and partaking.
2. **Safeguards against deception (vv. 7-10):** prayer, thanksgiving, holiness, right motives, and earnest pursuit of the best gifts.
3. **Distribution and catalogue of gifts (vv. 11-26):** differing gifts are given to different people so all may benefit.
4. **Discernment and coordinated order (vv. 27-30):** appointed leaders discern claims, while asking in the Spirit is defined by God's will.
5. **Christ-centered practice (vv. 31-33):** action in Christ's name, [[Gratitude]], virtue, and holiness.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents giftedness as communal and interdependent. Because no one has every gift (v. 11), another person's gift is not evidence of one's own spiritual failure; diversity is part of how the whole community profits. Verses 13-14 are especially pastorally significant: both direct knowledge that Jesus is the Christ and faithful belief in another's witness are recognized as gifts. The passage therefore honors [[Testimony]] without requiring identical spiritual experience from everyone.

The distinction between faith to be healed and faith to heal (vv. 19-20) discourages collapsing every healing claim into a single person's faith. Even so, the verses do not supply a complete explanation for why healing does or does not occur.

The chapter's openness is disciplined rather than credulous. Gifts come from God (v. 26), yet professed gifts may not be of God (v. 27). [[Revelation]] consequently requires discernment, moral practice, and accountability.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
D&C 46:7 closely recalls James 1:5 in its invitation to ask God, who gives liberally, while verse 9 recalls James 4:3 in warning against asking in order to consume upon lusts. These are two separate passages in James that D&C 46 brings into close proximity. The catalogue in verses 17-25 follows [[1 Corinthians 12]] especially closely in vocabulary and order, while [[Moroni 10]] provides a substantial Book of Mormon counterpart. These parallels establish intertextual relationships; they do not by themselves determine whether the wording reflects conscious quotation, remembered scriptural idiom, revelatory accommodation, literary adaptation, or another process.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Repetition gives the gift catalogue its rhythm: “to some,” “to another,” and “to others” distribute gifts among different recipients, while recurring benefit and profit language explains their purpose. The movement is from diversity toward coordination: members receive differing gifts, appointed officers discern professed gifts, and verse 29 speaks of “a head” so that every member may profit. That final phrase may faintly echo the body-and-members imagery of [[1 Corinthians 12]], but D&C 46 offers only a compressed head/member expression and does not reproduce Paul's extended anatomical metaphor. The precise identity and institutional meaning of “a head” remain uncertain.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Catalogue And Structural Parallel
> The catalogue has an extensive textual and conceptual relationship with 1 Corinthians 12: both distribute different gifts through the Spirit for common benefit and include wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, discernment of spirits, tongues, and interpretation. D&C 46 also uses head-and-member language when explaining the gifts' coordination.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · Strong comparative consensus · [[Catalogue And Structural Parallel in D&C 46|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Verbal Intertextuality *(tentative)*
> D&C 46:7 and 46:9 closely echo the distinctive diction and sequence of James: asking of a God who gives liberally, followed by a warning about asking in order to consume upon lusts. A literary relationship with the King James wording of James 1:5 and James 4:3 is highly likely.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.8 · Verbal parallels strong; sequence claim overstated

> [!evidence] Intertextual-Comparative *(tentative)*
> The set of gifts named in D&C 46:13-26 (wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, discerning of spirits, tongues, interpretation of tongues) closely overlaps in category and sequence with the spiritual-gift enumerations in Moroni 10 and in 1 Corinthians 12, which supports reading D&C 46 as consciously drawing on a shared biblical/Nephite framework of charismata rather than presenting an unrelated, novel taxonomy.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.7 · Parallel accepted; compositional inference disputed
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How should communities preserve the distinction in verse 4 between welcoming someone into a meeting and deciding who should partake of an ordinance?
- What practical criteria do verses 7-9 and 27 provide for distinguishing divine gifts from deception?
- Why does the text call both knowing Christ and believing another's witness gifts (vv. 13-14)?
- Do “differences of administration” and “diversities of operations” refer to offices, methods, effects, circumstances, or some combination? The chapter does not define the terms fully.
- How does verse 30 qualify readings of verse 28 that might otherwise treat spiritual asking as an unconditional promise?
