# D&C 60 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 60]] · My notes: [[D&C 60 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 60 records instructions to elders who had traveled to the land of [[Zion]] for its dedication in August 1831. The Lord approves their arrival (v.1) but rebukes those among them who have let fear of other people silence their preaching (v.2), warning that an unused gift can be taken away (v.3). The bulk of the section then coordinates two groups: elders already commanded to return home, given specific routes, preaching assignments (Sidney Rigdon, Joseph Smith Jr., and Oliver Cowdery to Cincinnati), and travel funds administered by Edward Partridge; and a second group still coming to Zion, told to preach both before and after arriving. Practical logistics are paired throughout with instruction on manner — bold but not contentious — and a closing verse defers further word about Rigdon and Cowdery to a future revelation.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter has four movements:

1. Verses 1–4: commends the completed journey to Zion, rebukes those who have stayed silent out of fear of man, and warns that an unfaithful, unused gift can be taken away.
2. Verses 5–11: directs the return journey — a craft to St. Louis, Sidney Rigdon, Joseph Smith Jun., and Oliver Cowdery assigned onward to Cincinnati, the rest sent two by two toward the churches they came from, and Edward Partridge charged to fund the return (repayment required only of those able).
3. Verses 12–16: turns to "the residue who are to come unto this land," commanding them to preach both en route to Zion and on their own return, without haste, wrath, or strife, and to answer rejection with a private, non-provocative testimony (shaking off dust, washing feet).
4. Verse 17: closes by deferring further instruction concerning Sidney Rigdon and Oliver Cowdery to a future revelation delivered through Joseph Smith — a promise later taken up in [[D&C 61]], though this chapter does not itself specify how or when.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verses 2–3 tie [[Missionary Work]] to personal stewardship: hiding one's commission to preach out of "fear of man" risks losing it, language that echoes the Parable of the Talents (Matthew 25) without the text explicitly naming that parable. Bold declaration ("with loud voices, without wrath or doubting," v.7) and restrained manner ("not in haste, neither in wrath nor with strife," v.14) are presented as complementary rather than opposed — conviction without contention. Verse 7 also links the Cincinnati assignment to [[Repentance and Forgiveness]] ("I am able to make you holy, and your sins are forgiven you"). The private dust-shaking and foot-washing of verse 15 frame unaccepted [[Testimony]] as evidence to be reckoned with at the [[Final Judgment]], with the requirement of secrecy notably limiting the act from becoming provocation rather than witness.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The metaphor of the hidden or buried "talent" is controlled by its immediate context. In verses 2 and 13–14, the opposite of hiding the talent is opening the mouth and proclaiming the word. This strongly favors a missionary-stewardship meaning here, though it need not exclude broader application by later readers. The chapter also distinguishes "speedily" from "in haste": the mission is urgent, but its message is not to be delivered with agitation, wrath, or strife.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Several phrases draw on King James idiom. Verse 4's "in the day when I shall make up my jewels" closely echoes Malachi 3:17. Verse 7's charge to preach "without wrath or doubting, lifting up holy hands upon them" closely echoes 1 Timothy 2:8 ("lifting up holy hands, without wrath and doubting") — the clearest near-verbatim borrowing in the chapter. The hidden/buried "talent" language (vv.2–3, 13) recalls the Parable of the Talents (Matthew 25:14-30), and the two-by-two travel paired with a rejection-testimony formula (vv.8, 15) recalls the Gospels' missionary charges (Mark 6:7-11; Matthew 10:14; Luke 9:5) as well as D&C 24:15. None of these parallels by itself settles whether the borrowing was a deliberate quotation or simply the revelator's habitual biblical vocabulary.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the text permit discretion about obtaining a craft while prescribing destinations and assignments in detail?
- How should "speedily" and "not in haste" be distinguished in practice?
- Does "talent" refer narrowly to the elders' commission to preach, or does the chapter invite a broader application?
- What does the requirement that the dust-shaking and foot-washing occur in secret reveal about the difference between testimony and retaliation?
- Verse 7's "lifting up holy hands ... without wrath or doubting" closely echoes 1 Timothy 2:8's language for prayer — does this suggest the holy-hands gesture is a posture of prayer over the hearers, and does that help explain who "them" refers to?
