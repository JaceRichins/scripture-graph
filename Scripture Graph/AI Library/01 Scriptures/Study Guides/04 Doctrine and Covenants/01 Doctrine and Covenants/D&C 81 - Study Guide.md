# D&C 81 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 81]] · My notes: [[D&C 81 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 81 is a compact commission to a counselor of Joseph Smith. The present canonical text addresses Frederick G. Williams, but the surviving revelation manuscript dated 15 March 1832 names Jesse Gause. The difference in names is a secure documentary fact; the documents do not disclose every detail of the later editorial decision.

The chapter moves from calling and institutional authority (vv. 1–2), through counsel, [[Prayer]], proclamation, and [[Service]] (vv. 3–5), to a conditional promise and identification of Jesus Christ as the speaker (vv. 6–7).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Calling and keys (vv. 1–2):** the counselor is called as a high priest; keys given to Joseph Smith are said to belong always to the Presidency of the High Priesthood.
2. **Practices and purpose (vv. 3–4):** faithful counsel, prayer, gospel ministry, good to fellow beings, and glory to the Lord.
3. **Supportive service (v. 5):** standing in the appointed office includes succoring, lifting, and strengthening others.
4. **Promise and speaker (vv. 6–7):** faithfulness to the end is followed by a promise of immortality and [[Eternal Life]], and Jesus Christ identifies himself as Alpha and Omega.

“Faithful” recurs in verses 3, 5, and 6; “office” appears in verses 3 and 5.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter joins [[Priesthood]] authority to accountable service. Verse 2 says that the keys given to Joseph Smith belong always to the Presidency of the High Priesthood. This is compatible with institutional continuity, but the verse alone does not define later succession procedures or every feature of the First Presidency.

Faithfulness is expressed through counsel, [[Prayer]], gospel ministry, and care for the weak. Verse 6 makes its promised “crown of immortality, and eternal life” conditional on remaining faithful to the end, giving the chapter a clear connection to [[Endurance]] and [[Eternal Life]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verse 3 uses paired expressions to make its charge comprehensive: prayer is “vocally and in thy heart” and “in public and in private.” Verse 5 follows with three support verbs: “succor,” “lift up,” and “strengthen.” Its bodily imagery can naturally describe spiritual and communal aid, though the text does not restrict it to one kind of need.

In verse 7, “Alpha and Omega” identifies Jesus Christ through a biblical divine title also found in [[Revelation 22]]. The title conventionally conveys comprehensiveness and divine sovereignty.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
D&C 81:5 closely reuses biblical English found in [[Isaiah 35]] and [[Hebrews 12]]. Isaiah speaks of strengthening weak hands and confirming feeble knees; Hebrews speaks of lifting hands that hang down and feeble knees. D&C 81 combines these elements and adds “succor the weak.” This strongly supports dependence on familiar KJV language, but it does not determine whether Isaiah and Hebrews were separately targeted or whether the wording came through Hebrews, memory, preaching, or general scriptural idiom.

Verse 6 also closely echoes [[John 14]] by combining mansions, preparation, and the Father's house. The echo does not by itself define the nature of the mansions or their relationship to later teachings about the afterlife.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] New Testament Allusion
> The promise of eternal life “in the mansions” prepared “in the house of my Father” closely echoes John 14:2, where Jesus speaks of mansions in his Father's house.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 1.0 · Strong verbal echo; wider implications limited · [[New Testament Allusion in D&C 81|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Biblical Verbal Dependence
> The clustered wording in D&C 81:5 corresponds closely to Isaiah 35:3 and Hebrews 12:12. Isaiah supplies weak hands and feeble knees, while Hebrews supplies hands that hang down; D&C 81 combines these expressions and adds “succor the weak.” This strongly supports literary dependence on, or conscious reuse of, the English Bible's language.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · Strong verbal dependence; immediate source disputed · [[Biblical Verbal Dependence in D&C 81|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intertextual/Literary Parallel
> The near-verbatim overlap between D&C 81:5 and Hebrews 12:12/Isaiah 35:3 is evidence of literary dependence on KJV phrasing, but does not by itself establish that the connection was a deliberate theological allusion (e.g., to the endurance-under-chastening context of Hebrews 12) rather than incidental reuse of familiar biblical idiom.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · Strong KJV dependence; deliberate contextual allusion disputed · [[Intertextual/Literary Parallel in D&C 81|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual And Documentary History
> The Joseph Smith Papers transcription of the 15 March 1832 revelation addresses Jesse Gause, whereas the present canonical text addresses Frederick G. Williams. The documentary comparison therefore supports the historical observation that the named recipient was changed in the transmitted, canonical form.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · Authoritatively documented textual history · [[Textual And Documentary History in D&C 81|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Manuscript/Textual History *(tentative)*
> According to Joseph Smith Papers manuscript scholarship, the earliest extant copy of this revelation (in Kirtland Revelation Book 1) named Jesse Gause, not Frederick G. Williams, as the addressee; Gause was serving as a counselor in the Presidency when the revelation was received (March 1832) but left the Church in December 1832, after which Williams took his place and the text was edited for publication to bear Williams's name.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.8 · Central name change established; bundled chronology needs precision

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. How does verse 5 shape the meaning of “stand in the office”?
2. What is communicated by describing prayer as both vocal and inward, public and private?
3. What does verse 2 establish about keys and the Presidency, and which conclusions about later succession require additional sources?
4. What is securely established by the Jesse Gause/Frederick G. Williams documentary history, and what remains unknown about the editorial rationale?
5. Does the wording shared with [[Isaiah 35]] and [[Hebrews 12]] invite their larger contexts, or can it be adequately explained as familiar biblical idiom?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare D&C 81 with [[D&C 90]] and [[D&C 107]] for later descriptions of presiding councils and counselors. Read [[Isaiah 35]], [[Hebrews 12]], and [[John 14]] in context to evaluate the verbal parallels and their limits. Compare D&C 81:3 with [[Alma 34]] on vocal and inward prayer, and D&C 81:5 with [[Mosiah 18]] on covenantal care for burdened people. [[D&C 92]] also merits comparison because its canonical text addresses Frederick G. Williams, although it dates to 1833 rather than one week after this 1832 revelation.
