# D&C 112 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 112]] · My notes: [[D&C 112 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
[[D&C 112]] joins personal correction, apostolic discipleship and governance, worldwide mission, and apocalyptic warning. Thomas's prayers and service are acknowledged while faults are also named; forgiveness and commission follow his self-abasement. The address then expands to the Twelve and First Presidency, combining institutional authority with [[Humility]], fidelity to the word, attention to the Spirit, and personal purification. Its sternest warning begins within the Lord's own house, showing that religious profession and office are not presented as immunity from accountability. The chapter closes by placing its [[Priesthood]] claims within a claimed continuity of dispensations and by echoing [[Revelation 22]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Thomas acknowledged, corrected, forgiven, and commissioned (vv. 1-10):** His prayers and alms are remembered, unspecified faults are identified, and humility is joined with guidance and worldwide witness.

2. **Love, admonition, and possible healing (vv. 11-13):** Thomas must reject partiality, pray for the Twelve, admonish them, and seek their conditional conversion and healing.

3. **The Twelve's discipleship and authorized mission (vv. 14-22):** Cross-bearing and care for Christ's sheep accompany differentiated responsibilities, authorized sending, humility, fidelity to the word, and attention to the Spirit.

4. **Judgment, purification, and proclamation (vv. 23-29):** Judgment begins within the Lord's house; the commissioned servants must purify their hearts and then preach throughout the world.

5. **Claimed dispensational continuity and final accountability (vv. 30-34):** Priesthood power is connected with earlier dispensations, leaders are commanded to cleanse themselves and remain faithful, and the chapter closes with Christ's coming and recompense.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter holds calling and accountability together. Keys and authorization matter, but authorized ministers are still commanded to humble themselves, love without partiality, accept admonition, heed the Spirit, and cleanse their hearts and garments. The warning that judgment begins within the Lord's house makes it difficult to treat office as a blanket guarantee of faithfulness or divine approval.

The movement from purification to proclamation is significant for [[Missionary Work]]: the messengers are not portrayed as standing outside the need for the message they preach. At the same time, the chapter affirms defined relationships of leadership and authorized sending; its moral conditions do not erase those institutional claims.

D&C 112:29 joins belief and [[Baptism]] with [[Salvation]], closely paralleling [[Mark 16]]. It is a compressed missionary formula and does not by itself resolve questions about ignorance, opportunity, exceptional cases, postmortal response, or the complete grounds of final judgment.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The expression “my house” in verses 25-26 most naturally refers to a community of professing believers in the immediate context, especially because verse 27 then mentions “my church in this place.” The chapter does not formally define whether the phrase denotes the institutional church generally, a particular covenant community, its leaders, or another referent; it should not automatically be reduced to a physical temple.

The chapter also uses a dense King James biblical register. Prayers and alms rising as a memorial recall [[Acts 10]]; taking up the cross recalls [[Matthew 16]]; feeding the sheep recalls [[John 21]]; darkness covering the earth recalls [[Isaiah 60]]; judgment beginning at God's house recalls [[1 Peter 4]]; worldwide preaching and the belief-baptism formula closely parallel [[Mark 16]]; and the conclusion follows [[Revelation 22]]. These connections establish verbal or thematic intertextuality with varying strength. They do not determine whether the language arose through revelation, deliberate composition, remembered scriptural idiom, scribal shaping, or some combination.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter contains an evident reversal motif: Thomas is abased and then exalted, and his words are said to bring high ones low and low ones high. The warning that judgment begins within the Lord's house gives this concern with accountability an institutional dimension, although the text does not explicitly identify every person first judged as a high officeholder.

A related movement runs from inward condition to outward service: humility precedes guidance, softened hearts condition healing, and purification precedes preaching. These repetitions form a coherent thematic pattern, but they do not by themselves establish an elaborate or intentionally formal literary structure.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What can responsibly be inferred from the text leaving Thomas's faults unspecified?

- How should sharp admonition in verse 12 be read alongside impartial love, prayer, and the conditional promise of healing in verses 11-13?

- Which responsibilities belong specifically to Thomas, to the Twelve collectively, and to the First Presidency in verses 15-22 and 30-32?

- What does “my house” designate in verses 25-26, and how does the reference to “my church in this place” in verse 27 constrain the possibilities?

- Does “thy path lieth among the mountains” describe literal travel, figurative difficulty, or both?

- How much doctrine should be inferred from the compressed salvation formula in verse 29 when it does not address opportunity, ignorance, or exceptional cases?

- Does “I come quickly” primarily communicate chronological imminence, covenantal urgency, or both?
