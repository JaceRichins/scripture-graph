# D&C 112 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 112]] · My notes: [[D&C 112 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
[[D&C 112]] joins personal correction and restoration, apostolic discipleship and governance, worldwide mission, and apocalyptic warning. Thomas's prayers and service are acknowledged while unspecified faults are also noted; forgiveness and renewed commission follow his self-abasement. The address then expands to the Twelve and First Presidency. Keys and authorized sending matter, but office is not presented as immunity from accountability: commissioned leaders must practice [[Humility]], love without partiality, heed the Spirit, and purify themselves. The chapter's sternest warning begins within the Lord's own house. It closes by presenting [[Priesthood]] power within a claimed continuity of dispensations and by closely paralleling [[Revelation 22]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Thomas acknowledged, corrected, forgiven, and commissioned (vv. 1-10):** His prayers and alms are remembered, unspecified faults are acknowledged, and humility is joined with forgiveness, guidance, and worldwide witness.

2. **Love, admonition, and possible healing (vv. 11-13):** Thomas must avoid partiality, pray for the Twelve, admonish them sharply, and seek their conditional conversion and healing.

3. **Discipleship, keys, and authorized mission (vv. 14-22):** Cross-bearing and care for Christ's sheep precede a sustained keys-and-doors description of responsibility abroad. Authorized service is conditioned on humility, fidelity to the word, and attention to the Spirit.

4. **Judgment, purification, and proclamation (vv. 23-29):** Judgment begins within the Lord's house; the commissioned servants must purify their hearts and then preach throughout the world.

5. **Claimed dispensational continuity and final accountability (vv. 30-34):** Priesthood power is connected with prior dispensations, leaders are commanded to cleanse themselves and remain faithful, and the chapter closes with Christ's coming and recompense.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter holds calling and accountability together. It affirms keys, differentiated leadership, and authorized sending, yet commissioned ministers are still commanded to humble themselves, love without partiality, heed the Spirit, and cleanse their hearts and garments. Judgment beginning within the Lord's house makes it difficult to treat office as a blanket guarantee of faithfulness or divine approval.

Verses 11-13 place sharp admonition beside expansive love, prayer, faithfulness, and hoped-for healing. This context supplies no warrant for severity detached from love or restoration, although the promised conversion and healing remain conditional on the recipients not hardening their hearts.

The movement from purification to proclamation is significant for [[Missionary Work]]: the messengers do not stand outside the moral demands of the message they preach. Their need for purification does not erase the chapter's institutional claims about authorization.

D&C 112:29 joins belief and [[Baptism]] with [[Salvation]], closely paralleling [[Mark 16]]. It is a compressed missionary formula and does not by itself resolve questions about ignorance, opportunity, exceptional cases, postmortal response, or the complete basis of [[Final Judgment]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter uses a dense King James biblical register. Prayers and alms rising as a memorial closely parallel [[Acts 10]]; taking up the cross recalls [[Matthew 16]]; feeding the sheep recalls [[John 21]]; darkness covering the earth recalls [[Isaiah 60]]; judgment beginning at God's house recalls [[1 Peter 4]]; worldwide preaching and the belief-baptism formula closely parallel [[Mark 16]]; and the conclusion follows [[Revelation 22]]. These relationships establish verbal or thematic intertextuality of varying strength. They do not determine whether the wording arose through revelation expressed in familiar language, deliberate composition, remembered scriptural idiom, editorial shaping, or some combination.

The expression “my house” in verses 25-26 most naturally has an ecclesial or covenant-community dimension: people profess, fail to know the Lord, and blaspheme within it, and verse 27 then refers to “my church in this place.” The exact referent remains undefined; it could have a narrower local, leadership-focused, institutional, or physical association and should not automatically be reduced either to a building alone or to the entire church.

Verses 16-21 repeatedly use “keys,” “unlock the door,” “an effectual door,” and “open the door.” The pattern connects the chapter's authority claims with missionary access and reception of the word, while not supplying a complete technical definition of jurisdiction.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter contains visible reversal language: Thomas is abased and then exalted, and his word is said to bring high ones low and low ones high. These statements can be read together as a motif, although they perform different functions and also draw on common biblical idiom.

A related movement runs from inward condition to outward service: humility precedes guidance, softened hearts condition healing, and purification precedes preaching. These repetitions contribute to thematic coherence, but the sequence is common in religious exhortation and does not establish a formal chiasm or elaborate intentional structure.

The shifts in audience also matter. The discourse moves from Thomas individually, to all the Twelve, back to Thomas's particular responsibility, and later to the Twelve together with the First Presidency. Instructions should therefore be assigned with attention to their stated recipients.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What interpretive limits follow from the chapter's refusal to specify Thomas's faults?

- How do impartial love, prayer, sharp admonition, voluntary softening of heart, and healing qualify one another in verses 11-13?

- Does the keys-and-doors imagery emphasize jurisdiction, missionary opportunity, reception of the word, or all three?

- What precisely does “my house” designate in verses 25-27, and what readings are excluded by the immediate context?

- Does “thy path lieth among the mountains” anticipate literal travel, figurative difficulty, or both?

- How should the strong institutional claims be read alongside the equally strong insistence that leaders remain subject to repentance and judgment?

- Does “I come quickly” specify chronology, express covenantal urgency, or intentionally leave the timing open?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare D&C 112:1 with [[Acts 10]]; verse 14 with [[Matthew 16]] and [[John 21]]; verses 23-26 with [[Isaiah 60]] and [[1 Peter 4]]; verses 28-29 with [[Mark 16]]; and verse 34 with [[Revelation 22]]. Study differences as well as similarities. Verbal dependence or shared diction is easier to establish than the theological or historical mechanism behind it.

Two details merit especially careful comparison. D&C 112:29 adds “and is not baptized” to the negative clause, where Mark 16:16 mentions unbelief alone. Verse 15 also coordinates keys given “unto him” and “to youward” but promises that they will not be taken “from him,” leaving the precise scope of that retention promise grammatically uncertain.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
