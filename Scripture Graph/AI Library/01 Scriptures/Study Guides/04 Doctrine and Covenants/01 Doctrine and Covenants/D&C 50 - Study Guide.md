# D&C 50 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 50]] · My notes: [[D&C 50 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 50 addresses a community struggling to distinguish divine manifestations from deception. Its response is simultaneously corrective and pastoral. It condemns hypocrisy, questions the elders about experiences they accepted without understanding, supplies criteria centered on the Spirit of truth and mutual edification, and prescribes prayerful and restrained opposition to manifestations judged not to be of God. The ending shifts toward reassurance: the hearers belong to Christ, must grow in [[Grace]] and truth, and are commanded to watch and be ready.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious outline is:

1. **Danger and accountability (vv. 1–9):** false spirits, deception, hypocrisy, endurance, judgment, and possible reclamation.
2. **Reasoned examination (vv. 10–16):** the Lord questions the elders about their ordination and their uncritical reception of incomprehensible spirits.
3. **Preaching, reception, and edification (vv. 17–25):** both speaker and hearer must participate through the Spirit of truth; darkness is contrasted with progressively increasing light.
4. **Authority under moral restraint (vv. 26–35):** service, purification, prayer, discernment, and non-boastful opposition to false manifestations.
5. **Local assignments and correction (vv. 36–39):** forgiveness, missionary commissions, and Edward Partridge's call to repent.
6. **Pastoral assurance and readiness (vv. 40–46):** growth, belonging to Christ, shepherd and rock imagery, promised encounter, and watchfulness.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents revelation as relational and morally accountable. Teaching is not validated merely by a preacher's claim to inspiration: the receiver also participates through the Spirit of truth, and both parties are expected to understand and be edified (vv. 17–22). This gives religious communication a communal dimension relevant to [[Revelation]], [[Truth]], and [[Spiritual Gifts]].

Authority is paired with [[Service]] and purification. The one appointed to be greatest is also the least and servant of all (v. 26), while the promised power of verses 27–32 is expressly connected to cleansing from sin and divine appointment. The passage therefore supplies poor support for detached or self-aggrandizing claims of spiritual power.

The final reassurance does not erase the need for growth. The hearers are Christ's, yet they remain little children who cannot presently bear everything and must grow in [[Grace]] and knowledge (vv. 40–42). Belonging and development are presented together.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The dominant contrasts are truth/falsehood, light/darkness, understanding/confusion, edification/non-edification, and faithful endurance/deception. These contrasts make discernment the chapter's controlling concern.

The invitation to "reason together" recalls Isaiah 1:18, while verses 41–44 concentrate language familiar from the Gospel of John: overcoming the world, those given by the Father to the Son, reciprocal indwelling, and the good shepherd. These are strong literary parallels, but they do not by themselves decide whether the language arose through direct quotation, memory, common biblical idiom, or a theological understanding of continuing revelation.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 13–21 form a compact question-and-answer sequence. The repeated alternatives—by the Spirit of truth or "some other way"—lead to the paired conclusion of verse 22. This is better described as rhetorical and logical parallelism than as a demonstrated formal chiasm.

The chapter also changes tone deliberately. Severe warnings in verses 2–9 yield to patient reasoning in verses 10–16, procedural instruction in verses 17–35, named administrative directions in verses 37–39, and intimate reassurance in verses 40–46. The movement prevents either warning or consolation from standing alone.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Canonical Intertextual Parallel
> D&C 50:41–44 contains a cluster of Johannine themes and expressions: Christ overcoming the world, people given to the Son by the Father, reciprocal indwelling, and the good shepherd. This strongly supports conscious or culturally mediated use of biblical language, while the precise route of dependence remains uncertain.

> [!evidence] Verbal Biblical Parallel
> The invitation to come and "reason together" closely parallels Isaiah 1:18. D&C 50 expands that inherited phrase into a face-to-face, question-driven explanation designed to produce understanding.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 1.0 · strong, specific, well-hedged · [[Verbal Biblical Parallel in D&C 50|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary/Thematic Parallel *(tentative)*
> The edification/intelligibility test of D&C 50:21-23 has a thematic (not verbal) parallel in 1 Corinthians 14, where Paul similarly regulates spiritual gifts (tongues, prophecy) by whether they build up the hearer — 'let all things be done unto edifying' — and insists that manifestations attributed to God must be orderly and comprehensible.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- V7 says 'such shall be reclaimed' — does 'such' refer to those deceived, to the hypocrites, or to a broader group?
- Is non-edification meant only as grounds to reject a manifestation, or can edification alone positively authenticate one? What other controls — truth, the Comforter, prayer, purification, humility — does the chapter also require?
- What practically distinguishes 'proclaiming against' a false spirit (v32) from the forbidden 'railing accusation' (v33)?
- How should the expansive promises of vv27-30 be read alongside the warning against boasting and rejoicing in v33?
- What does 'mutual understanding' (v22) require when sincere participants disagree about whether an experience was genuinely spiritual?
- How do the rebuke of error in vv1-9 and the assurance that none of Christ's people will be lost (v42) function together in the same revelation?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
