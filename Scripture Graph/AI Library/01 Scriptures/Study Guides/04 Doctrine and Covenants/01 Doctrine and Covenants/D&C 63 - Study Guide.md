# D&C 63 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 63]] · My notes: [[D&C 63 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 63 joins warning, correction, gathering, administration, and eschatological promise. Its repeated contrast is between profession and endurance: merely calling oneself one of God's people is insufficient (vv. 1, 19, 63), while faithful endurance leads to overcoming and inheritance (vv. 20, 47-49, 66). The chapter addresses [[Faith]], [[Repentance]], [[Zion]], [[Resurrection]], and the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] without separating spiritual claims from practical obligations.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A useful topical outline is:

1. Verses 1-6: summons to hear and warning of judgment.
2. Verses 7-12: signs, [[Faith]], and divine will.
3. Verses 13-19: adultery, inward lust, repentance, and judgment.
4. Verses 20-23: overcoming, the transfigured earth, and promised mysteries.
5. Verses 24-46: orderly gathering, land purchase, funding, and administrative assignments for [[Zion]].
6. Verses 47-54: endurance, inheritance, [[Resurrection]], transformation, and final separation.
7. Verses 55-66: Sidney Rigdon's correction, authorized warning, reverent speech, [[Prayer]], and patient overcoming.

This division is an organizational aid rather than a claim that the revelation has a rigid formal structure. General teaching and immediate administration remain interwoven.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's theology of signs is carefully ordered: [[Faith]] does not arise from demanded signs; signs follow belief and occur according to God's will (vv. 7-12). Its theology of [[Zion]] is likewise disciplined: gathering must avoid haste and confusion, land should be purchased, and bloodshed is forbidden (vv. 24-31). Its eschatology promises inheritance on a transfigured earth, resurrection for the faithful dead, transformation for faithful survivors, and eventual separation of righteous and wicked (vv. 20-21, 47-54). These promises are repeatedly conditioned on endurance, obedience, and doing God's will.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter is densely intertextual with the King James New Testament. Compare signs following believers with [[Mark 16]], the well springing to everlasting life with [[John 4]], rendering to Caesar with [[Matthew 22]], the transformed living with [[1 Corinthians 15]], the blessed dead with [[Revelation 14]], the virgins with [[Matthew 25]], and the eternal weight of glory with [[2 Corinthians 4]]. These parallels illuminate how the revelation communicates, but they do not alone decide whether the language is quotation, conscious imitation, shared religious idiom, or divine reuse of prior scripture.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Several patterns connect the chapter's varied subjects. Claimed identity is tested by conduct: the audience calls itself the Lord's people, yet retained sin leaves it unjustified and in need of [[Repentance]] (vv. 1, 19, 63). Endurance and faithfulness repeatedly lead to overcoming, inheritance, or glory (vv. 20, 47-49, 66). The temporal language also creates a notable juxtaposition: divine intervention is 'not yet, but by and by' (v. 35), while later events are both 'nigh at hand' and 'in a time to come' (v. 53). The wording supports watchfulness but supplies no calendar date.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary Intertextuality
> D&C 63 repeatedly reuses recognizable New Testament language: signs following believers, faith pleasing God, a well springing to everlasting life, rendering to Caesar, blessing those who die in the Lord, and an eternal weight of glory. These correspondences are strong evidence that the chapter is deliberately framed in a King James biblical register and invites intertextual reading.

> [!evidence] Internal Textual/Structural Pattern *(tentative)*
> As an evidentiary matter, the chapter's abrupt shift from universal doctrinal warning (vv. 1-23) to occasion-specific administrative directives naming individuals, a store, and a farm (vv. 24-66) is consistent with the pattern of early Restoration revelations that blend timeless doctrine with immediate organizational instruction for a nineteenth-century Ohio/Missouri context.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How does the explicit prohibition on shedding blood in verse 31 control the interpretation of the stark purchase-or-blood formulation in verse 29? What distinction do verses 7-12 make between demanding a sign for faith and receiving a sign that follows faith? Why are warning, ordination, authority, prayer, and restraint in speech placed together in verses 57-64? What does the chapter gain by placing detailed financial and property instructions amid its descriptions of final judgment and the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]]?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
