# D&C 6 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 6]] · My notes: [[D&C 6 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–6, Laborers in the Lord’s field gain salvation; 7–13, There is no gift greater than the gift of salvation; 14–27, A witness of the truth comes by the power of the Spirit; 28–37, Look unto Christ, and do good continually.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter's official heading identifies four movements, which the text's content generally supports:

1. **vv. 1–6** — A call to labor ("the field is white already to harvest"), addressed broadly ("whoso desireth") though already anticipating a specific respondent (v. 6, "as you have asked").
2. **vv. 7–13** — A meditation on [[Spiritual Gifts]], climaxing in "there is no gift greater than the gift of salvation" (v. 13).
3. **vv. 14–27** — The address becomes explicitly personal at v. 20 ("thou art Oliver"), confirming a private spiritual witness (vv. 22–24) before granting a gift to translate (v. 25) and describing hidden [[Records and Sacred Writings|records]] (vv. 26–27).
4. **vv. 28–37** — The "two or three witnesses" principle (v. 28), consequences of rejection or acceptance (vv. 29–31), and a closing charge to "look unto me" and "do good" (vv. 32–37), ending with Christ displaying his wounds (v. 37).

Six "Verily, verily, I say unto..." formulas (vv. 8, 14, 22, 26, 29, 32) mark recurring turns of emphasis within this outline, though not every instance functions identically.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
**Personal revelation is confirmed by remembered experience, not defined exhaustively.** Vv. 14–24 ask Oliver to read his own inquiry, enlightenment, and inward peace — "did I not speak peace to your mind concerning the matter?" (v. 23) — as a witness. The chapter treats this as valid evidence for Oliver specifically; it does not offer "peace" as a complete, generally applicable test for every claimed revelation, and the private nature of the experience limits what an outside reader can independently verify (see [[Testimony]]).

**Spiritual gifts are sacred stewardships, subordinate to salvation.** Vv. 10–12 treat Oliver's gift as sacred and purposeful — to be exercised for others' benefit, disclosed only with discretion ("make not thy gift known unto any save it be those who are of thy faith"). V. 13 then ranks salvation above it: "there is no gift greater than the gift of salvation." The same hierarchy frames the specific gift to translate granted in v. 25.

**Truth is established through multiple witnesses.** V. 28's "in the mouth of two or three witnesses shall every word be established" is applied here to Joseph and Oliver's joint work; it is best read as invoking a general biblical evidentiary principle for their ministry rather than a specific prediction of the later [[Witnesses of the Book of Mormon]].

**Discipleship includes mutual correction.** Vv. 18–19 instruct Oliver both to "stand by" Joseph faithfully and to "admonish him in his faults, and also receive admonition of him" — loyalty paired with reciprocal accountability, relevant to how [[Sustaining Church Leaders]] is understood.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter's diction draws heavily on the King James New Testament. V. 2 closely paraphrases Hebrews 4:12's "quick and powerful, sharper than a two-edged sword"; v. 3 quotes John 4:35's harvest imagery almost verbatim; v. 21 combines clauses from John 1:5 and 1:11; v. 32 explicitly frames itself as repeating Christ's earlier words to the disciples, matching Matthew 18:19–20; vv. 33–34 draw on Galatians 6:7, Luke 12:32, and Matthew 16:18; and v. 28's witnesses formula is a near-exact match to 2 Corinthians 13:1, with Deuteronomy 19:15 supplying the older, more loosely worded legal antecedent. This density of biblical language is a strong, well-attested literary observation; it does not by itself decide whether individual instances reflect direct quotation, revelation expressed through the recipient's own scriptural vocabulary, or human compositional reuse of memorized scripture — readers disagree on that question, and the text alone cannot settle it.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Two recurring patterns organize much of the chapter. Agricultural imagery frames the opening call to reap a ready harvest (vv. 3–4) and returns in the closing maxim that "whatsoever ye sow, that shall ye also reap" (v. 33) — likely two independently common biblical idioms applied to different points (missionary urgency, then moral consequence) rather than a deliberately engineered inclusio. Near the end, images of Christ's presence and protection accumulate: encircling arms (v. 20), presence among the gathered (v. 32), security "built upon my rock" (v. 34), and finally his displayed wounds (v. 37) — a scene that echoes the doubting-Thomas episode (John 20:27) and Christ's later invitation to the Nephites (3 Nephi 11:14–15). The six "Verily, verily" markers (vv. 8, 14, 22, 26, 29, 32) give the chapter a visible rhythm, though calling each one an "escalation" overstates what can be shown; they are best described as recurring markers of emphasis.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Comparative Textual Pattern *(tentative)*

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What is the relationship between the unnamed gift described in vv. 10–12 and the gift "to translate" granted in v. 25 — are they the same gift, or does the text leave that connection implicit?
- Is the "night that you cried unto me in your heart" (v. 22) the same episode Oliver Cowdery later described in his own writings, or a separate, unrecorded experience? The text alone cannot say.
- How should a reader distinguish the "peace" described in v. 23 from ordinary relief or expectation? The chapter treats it as a valid witness for Oliver without offering a general test that applies to every claim of revelation.
- Why does D&C 6 share such extensive language with [[D&C 12]] (and, by unverified resemblance, [[D&C 11]] and [[D&C 14]]) — a shared template adapted for different recipients, a recurring genre, or independent deliveries of similar counsel?
- How do vv. 18–19 reshape the meaning of loyalty to a leader? Standing by Joseph and admonishing his faults are commanded together, resisting a reading of support as uncritical agreement.
<!-- SG:END questions -->
