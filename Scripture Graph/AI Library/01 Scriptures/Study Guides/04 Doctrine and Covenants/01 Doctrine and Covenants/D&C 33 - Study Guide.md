# D&C 33 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 33]] · My notes: [[D&C 33 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 33 commissions Ezra and Northrop to participate in [[Missionary Work]]. Its movement is from hearing God's penetrating word to speaking that word, gathering believers, teaching the response of faith and ordinances, and remaining ready for the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]]. The chapter's language is densely biblical, but its biblical echoes serve the particular commission rather than appearing as isolated ornaments.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Hear and proclaim (vv. 1–2):** The servants first hear the divine word and then lift their own voices.
2. **Harvest and gathering (vv. 3–9):** White fields, the eleventh hour, sickles, and sheaves portray urgent labor; the promised harvest extends to the four quarters of the earth.
3. **The message and its means (vv. 10–16):** The proclamation calls for [[Repentance]], [[Faith]], [[Baptism]], and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]], while church covenants, confirmation, scripture, and the Spirit support the work.
4. **Readiness (vv. 17–18):** The lamps, oil, and Bridegroom shift from immediate missionary labor to sustained preparation for Christ's coming.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verses 10–15 give a compact account of the [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]]: repentance, faith in Christ, baptism in water, the baptism of fire and of the Holy Ghost, and confirmation by laying on of hands. The close parallel with [[2 Nephi 31]] helps explain why these are presented as connected responses rather than unrelated teachings.

Verse 16 joins the Book of Mormon and other scriptures with the quickening power of the Spirit. This pairing suggests that [[Scripture Study]] and spiritual illumination belong together, without implying that a claimed spiritual impression makes careful reading unnecessary.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
D&C 33 repeatedly uses familiar scriptural language. Verse 1 closely parallels [[Hebrews 4]], while reversing the order of “soul and spirit” and “joints and marrow.” Verse 10 combines the repentance and kingdom proclamation of [[Matthew 3]] with its command to prepare the Lord’s way, ultimately drawn from Isaiah 40:3. Verse 13 echoes [[Matthew 16]] but applies the promise directly to the hearers without defining the metaphorical “rock.” Verse 17 strongly recalls [[Matthew 25]] through its combined lamps, oil, readiness, and Bridegroom imagery, and verse 18 echoes the “I come quickly” language of [[Revelation 22]]. These parallels demonstrate extensive use of biblical wording; they do not by themselves establish a particular human or divine mechanism of composition.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The three successive commands to “open your mouths” in verses 8–10 form a clear progression: a promise of inspired speech, a command to speak without restraint accompanied by promised harvest and divine presence, and finally the message to proclaim. Harvest imagery also shapes the chapter: fields already white, eleventh-hour laborers, sickles, and sheaves express urgency. The white-field and eleventh-hour language has close biblical antecedents in [[John 4]] and [[Matthew 20]], but similar wording recurs in several other early missionary commissions, including [[D&C 6]], [[D&C 11]], [[D&C 12]], and [[D&C 14]]. It may therefore reflect both biblical imagery and a recurring commissioning formula. The vineyard corruption and “last time” language may also recall [[Jacob 5]], though that connection is thematic and remains uncertain.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What is the precise antecedent of 'this rock' in verse 13: Christ, faith in Christ, the declared gospel, revelation, or some combination?
- Does the Nephi comparison in verse 8 concern his wilderness journey, his willingness to obey, his inspired speech, or the whole pattern?
- How should 'eleventh hour' and 'I come quickly' shape conduct without turning figurative urgency into a speculative calendar?
- How do the divine promise to fill the servants' mouths and the instruction supplied by scripture in verse 16 work together?
