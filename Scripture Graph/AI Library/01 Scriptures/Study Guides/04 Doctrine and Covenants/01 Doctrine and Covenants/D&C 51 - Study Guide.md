# D&C 51 — Study Guide

## Overview

> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–8, Edward Partridge is appointed to regulate stewardships and properties; 9–12, The Saints are to deal honestly and receive alike; 13–15, They are to have a bishop’s storehouse and to organize properties according to the Lord’s law; 16–20, Ohio is to be a temporary gathering place.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter moves through four movements that track the official heading:

- **vv.1–8** — Commission and standard: Partridge is to organize the people and appoint portions 'equal according to... family... circumstances and... wants and needs' (v.3), secured by a written deed (v.4) that is forfeit only upon loss of good standing (v.5), all 'according to the laws of the land' (v.6).
- **vv.9–12** — Honest dealing and unity: the people are to 'deal honestly... and receive alike, that ye may be one' (v.9); resources belonging to this people are not to pass to another church without agreed repayment (vv.10–12).
- **vv.13–15** — Storehouse: the bishop is to maintain a storehouse for surplus, drawing his own family's needs from his labor in the work (vv.13–14), and the whole system is granted as 'a privilege of organizing themselves according to my laws' (v.15).
- **vv.16–20** — Temporary tenure and closing testimony: the land is consecrated only 'for a little season' with the timing of change withheld (vv.16–17); the arrangement is declared a general 'example... in all churches' (v.18) with a promise to faithful stewards (v.19); the chapter closes with Christ's own voice, naming himself and warning of an hour 'you think not' (v.20).
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
**Stewardship, not simple ownership.** A recipient's right to a portion is secured by a formal writing (v. 4), but continuing in it is bound up with remaining in church fellowship (vv. 4–5). Verse 5 draws a careful distinction, though: someone no longer 'accounted worthy... to belong to the church' loses his claim to what he *consecrated* to the bishop for the poor, but keeps his claim to whatever portion was *deeded* to him. The ecclesiastical gift and the civilly deeded portion are not the same thing.

**Equality defined by need, not sameness.** Portions are 'equal according to... family... circumstances and... wants and needs' (v. 3) — a need-adjusted standard, not identical shares. The chapter supplies no formula for weighing these factors, and does not explicitly reconcile this with v. 9's call to 'receive alike' (see Questions).

**Honesty and oneness as prerequisites for order.** v. 9 ties [[Honesty]] directly to becoming one, making honest dealing a condition for the whole administrative system to function.

**Temporal order and the unknown hour.** The land is consecrated only 'for a little season' (v. 16), with the timing of any change withheld (v. 17); similar withheld-timing language recurs in Christ's declaration that he 'cometh quickly, in an hour you think not' (v. 20). This echo is suggestive of a link between faithful temporal stewardship and readiness for the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]], but the chapter does not itself state that connection — it remains an interesting, unconfirmed reading rather than an established doctrine.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Two qualifications are especially important. First, "equal" in verse 3 is immediately qualified by family, circumstances, wants, and needs; the chapter therefore should not be cited as an unambiguous command for identical distributions. Second, the repeated distinction between what is "deeded" to a person and what is "consecrated unto the bishop" indicates differentiated claims over property. The precise legal implementation requires historical documentation beyond the wording of this chapter.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter opens with instruction from 'the Lord your God' (v. 1) and closes with Christ naming himself outright, 'I am Jesus Christ' (v. 20) — a loose frame of divine self-identification around the property instructions. Such formulas are also a conventional way for these revelations to open and close, so reading this as a deliberately constructed inclusio is a possible but unconfirmed literary reading.

A verbal echo is also worth noting: 'the hour and the day is not given' (v. 17, concerning the land) and 'an hour you think not' (v. 20, concerning Christ's return) both use 'hour' for withheld timing. Whether this is an intentional pairing of temporal and eschatological uncertainty, or simply shared ordinary vocabulary for an unknown time, cannot be settled from the text alone.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How should "equal" in verse 3 be understood alongside the instruction to "receive alike" in verse 9?
- What protections and obligations did the required writing in verse 4 create in practice?
- Why does the text require conformity to the laws of the land rather than treating ecclesiastical allocation as sufficient by itself?
- What does acting "as for years" teach about planning when the duration of an assignment is unknown?
- How do the qualities "faithful," "just," and "wise" constrain the exercise of administrative authority?
