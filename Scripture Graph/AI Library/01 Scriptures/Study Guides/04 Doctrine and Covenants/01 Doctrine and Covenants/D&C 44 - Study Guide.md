# D&C 44 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 44]] · My notes: [[D&C 44 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–3, Elders are to assemble in conference; 4–6, They are to organize according to the laws of the land and to care for the poor.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Gathering and communication (v. 1):** elders are summoned from four directions by letter or another means.
2. **Spiritual preparation and mission (vv. 2–3):** faithfulness precedes the promised Spirit; assembly precedes preaching.
3. **Conversion, organization, and protection (vv. 4–5):** missionary success is connected with civil capacity, preservation, and obedience.
4. **Immediate relief (v. 6):** the poor and needy must be visited and sustained while fuller implementation of the received law remains pending.

The movement is therefore inward gathering → divine empowerment → outward proclamation → communal organization and care.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The promise of the Spirit is conditional: the elders must be faithful and exercise [[Faith]] (v. 2). Spiritual empowerment is directed toward [[Missionary Work]] and [[Repentance]], not merely private experience (v. 3).

Verses 4–5 place [[Government and Laws]] in a supporting role. Organization under human law is sought so that hostile power may be limited and divine laws may be kept. The text neither identifies civil law with God's law nor treats lawful civic organization as spiritually irrelevant.

Verse 6 joins institutional preparation with concrete [[Service]]. Visiting and relieving the poor is required before every larger arrangement is complete.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The phrase “power to organize yourselves according to the laws of man” (v. 4) gives “power” a practical and legal emphasis in context; reading it automatically as priesthood power would go beyond what the verse specifies.

“My laws” in verse 5 and “my law which ye have received” in verse 6 may have related referents, but the chapter does not identify the prior law by section or title. “Every bond” in verse 5 is similarly broad: it depicts hostile restraint but does not specify whether every contemplated bond is legal, economic, social, or figurative.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verse 1’s four-direction list conveys comprehensive geographic scope. Psalm 107:3 and Luke 13:29 contain close east-west-north-south gathering formulas. 3 Nephi 20:13 uses the related but textually distinct image of gathering from the “four quarters of the earth.” These comparisons illuminate a shared scriptural idiom, but they do not establish direct quotation, dependence on one source, or identity among the gatherings.

Repeated future and purpose clauses—“it shall come to pass,” “I will,” and successive uses of “that”—connect faithfulness, the Spirit, preaching, conversion, organization, protection, obedience, and relief. This rhetorical sequence does not make those elements interchangeable or prove that the arrangement was consciously designed as a formal literary structure.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the revelation connect successful preaching with the ability to organize under civil law?
- What kinds of protection could lawful organization realistically provide, and what protections does the passage merely promise rather than explain?
- Why is relief for the poor commanded before all arrangements under the received law can be completed?
- Does “every bond” refer primarily to formal legal restraints, or is its scope intentionally broader?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare D&C 44:6 with D&C 38:35 and D&C 42:30–31, noting both their shared concern for the poor and their different wording and administrative details. D&C 42 may help clarify the previously received law, but D&C 44 does not identify that law by section.

Compare D&C 44:1 with Psalm 107:3 and Luke 13:29 as close four-direction formulas. Then compare 3 Nephi 20:13 as a looser “four quarters of the earth” gathering parallel. Acts 2:1–4 provides a broader comparison between an assembled body and a manifestation of the Spirit, without establishing direct quotation or identical events.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
