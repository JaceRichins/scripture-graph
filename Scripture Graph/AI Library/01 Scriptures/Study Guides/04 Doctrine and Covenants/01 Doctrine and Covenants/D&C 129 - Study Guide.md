# D&C 129 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 129]] · My notes: [[D&C 129 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 129 presents a compact taxonomy and a diagnostic procedure. Verses 1–3 distinguish resurrected [[Angels]] from unresurrected spirits of just people; verses 4–9 describe three possible responses when a claimed divine messenger is asked to shake hands. The argument connects bodily tangibility with [[Resurrection]], while treating honesty as a defining feature of a just spirit and attempted imitation as a feature of [[Satan and Temptation]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter has two closely connected movements:

1. **Classification (vv. 1–3):** resurrected angels have bodies of flesh and bones; just spirits are not yet resurrected.
2. **Discernment procedure (vv. 4–9):** a tangible handshake identifies an angel, an honest refusal identifies a just spirit, and an offered but unfelt hand exposes the devil.

Verse 9 summarizes these three outcomes as "three grand keys." The organization makes the bodily distinction in the first movement the rationale for the tactile results in the second.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter assumes that personal existence continues beyond death and distinguishes present spirit existence from embodied [[Resurrection]]. Its appeal to the risen Jesus aligns the teaching with [[Luke 24]], where Jesus presents bodily tangibility as evidence that he is not merely a spirit. The statement that unresurrected just spirits "inherit the same glory" should be read cautiously: the verse affirms shared glory but does not explain its timing, degree, or relationship to their future resurrection.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The word **angel** is used narrowly here for a resurrected personage, while **spirit of a just man made perfect** designates a righteous but unresurrected person. Readers should not assume without further study that every scriptural use of "angel" follows this chapter's narrow classification. The phrase **angel of light** describes deceptive appearance, not actual membership in the chapter's category of resurrected angels.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 4-9 are built on tight formulaic repetition: each of three messenger types is introduced, subjected to the same handshake request, and yields a distinct outcome — tangible contact (angel), honest refusal (just spirit), or an offered but unfelt hand (devil as an angel of light). This threefold, parallel design, capped by the summary phrase 'three grand keys' (v.9), gives the passage a memorable, catechetical quality.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does "the same glory" in verse 3 compare the glory of just spirits with that of resurrected angels, and if so, what kind of sameness is intended?
- Does "the only way he can appear" in verse 6 apply to every circumstance involving an unresurrected just spirit, or specifically to the kind of administration being discussed?
- How does this tactile procedure relate to the prayer-based discernment instructions in [[D&C 50]]?
- What limits should be placed on applying this chapter outside its stated setting of an appearing messenger?
