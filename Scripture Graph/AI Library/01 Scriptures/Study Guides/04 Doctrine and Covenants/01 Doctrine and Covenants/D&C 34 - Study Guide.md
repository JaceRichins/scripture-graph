# D&C 34 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 34]] · My notes: [[D&C 34 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 34 moves rapidly from identity to vocation and hope: Christ identifies himself as Redeemer (vv. 1–3), recognizes Orson's belief and calling (vv. 4–5), commissions public preaching (vv. 6, 10), explains its urgency through the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] (vv. 7–9), and closes with a conditional promise of divine presence (vv. 11–12).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A useful reading divides the chapter into five movements:

1. **The speaker and his saving work (vv. 1–3):** Jesus Christ is Redeemer, light, life, and the one who gave his life.
2. **Belief and calling (vv. 4–5):** Orson is blessed for believing and more blessed for being called to preach.
3. **The commission (v. 6):** He must proclaim [[Repentance]] and prepare the Lord's way.
4. **The reason for urgency (vv. 7–10):** Christ's coming, cosmic signs, and judgment motivate the renewed command to speak.
5. **Presence and return (vv. 11–12):** Faithfulness is joined to Christ's promise, “I am with you until I come.”
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter connects Christ’s self-giving, belief, service, repentance, and the Lord’s return. Christ says he gave his life so that believers might become sons of God (v. 3), while the immediate application of “my son” to Orson may reflect both this context and a conventional form of divine address. Orson is blessed for believing and “more blessed” because he is called to preach (vv. 4–5); this local comparison should not be turned into a universal ranking of people or callings. His [[Missionary Work]] centers on [[Repentance]] in preparation for the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] (v. 6). Verse 10 associates [[Prophecy]] with the power of the Holy Ghost, though the precise antecedent of “it shall be given” remains uncertain. Verse 11 adds a conditional promise of Christ’s presence during faithful service.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The revelation's vocabulary is densely biblical. Verses 2–3 draw together the light, life, darkness, belief, divine love, and sonship language of John 1 and John 3. Verses 6 and 10 recall Isaiah's trumpet-like prophetic cry. Verses 7–9 use imagery associated with Joel 2 and Matthew 24. These correspondences securely establish scriptural intertextuality; they do not, by themselves, decide whether the wording is best explained as revelation, conscious literary reuse, or internalized King James Bible idiom.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The repeated title “Redeemer” in verses 1 and 12 gives the revelation a modest framing effect, although ordinary repetition of devotional titles remains a sufficient explanation. The address also develops a practical sequence: Orson is told to hear, is recognized for believing, receives a call to preach, is commanded to lift his voice and prophesy, and is urged to remain faithful. This pattern helps connect the chapter’s Second Coming imagery with the conduct expected of a called disciple; it should be treated as a useful reading rather than a demonstrably intentional formal structure.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does the comparison between “blessed” and “more blessed” suggest about the relationship between receiving faith and serving others?
- Why does the revelation place the declaration of Christ's love and self-giving before its urgent warnings of judgment?
- In verse 10, does “it shall be given” refer to prophetic words, prophetic power, or the fulfillment of the command? The verse permits more than one precise grammatical reading.
- How does the promise “I am with you until I come” balance the chapter's alarming imagery of the last days?
