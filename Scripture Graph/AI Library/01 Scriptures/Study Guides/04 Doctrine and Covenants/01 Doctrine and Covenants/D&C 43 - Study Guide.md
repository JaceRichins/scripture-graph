# D&C 43 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 43]] · My notes: [[D&C 43 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 43 joins church order to an urgent outward mission. Verses 1–7 establish an appointed channel for commandments and revelations functioning as law for the church, with safeguards against deception; they do not expressly deny personal inspiration to others. Verses 8–16 emphasize mutual instruction, holiness, prayerful and material support for Joseph Smith, and preparation from on high. Verses 17–35 call the nations to [[Repentance]] in view of the day of the Lord, the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]], the [[Millennium]], and [[Final Judgment]]. The chapter closes by identifying the speaker as Jesus Christ and commanding sober obedience.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Authorized revelation (vv. 1–7):** an appointed recipient, ordered succession, and a test against rival commandments.

2. **An instructed and sanctified church (vv. 8–16):** mutual edification, obedience, purification, support for Joseph Smith Jr., and teaching empowered from on high.

3. **A universal warning (vv. 17–28):** missionaries, heaven, and creation all call the nations to repent.

4. **The promised consummation (vv. 29–35):** redemption, the [[Millennium]], Satan's defeat, transformation, judgment, and a concluding command to live soberly and obediently.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verses 2–7 concern commandments and revelations presented as binding direction to the church. They support ordered prophetic authority while leaving the precise boundaries of personal revelation unstated. Verses 8–11 connect instruction with action, sanctification, holiness, and the received [[Kingdom of God]]. Verses 12–13 command prayerful and material support for Joseph Smith without defining an exhaustive rule for obtaining spiritual blessings. Verses 24–26 place [[Justice and Mercy]] together within God's repeated efforts to gather and save. Verses 29–33 connect the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]], [[Millennium]], Satan's binding and release, transformation, and [[Final Judgment]], but the compressed account should not be treated as a complete chronology.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter is organized by commands to hear and speak: “hearken,” “give ear,” “instruct,” “teach,” “lift up your voices,” and “call upon the nations.” In verses 21–25, “voice” becomes the controlling word. A human call to repent expands into the voices of thunder, lightning, tempests, earthquakes, angels, judgment, mercy, and God. This repeated language presents the summons as universal while leaving open whether the natural phenomena are literal future events, prophetic imagery, or both.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
D&C 43:24 closely echoes the hen-and-chickens lament in [[Matthew 23]] and [[3 Nephi 10]]. In all three settings, protective gathering is desired by the divine speaker but resisted by the intended recipients. The image adds pathos to the judgment discourse: refusal, rather than divine unwillingness to gather, is emphasized. The verbal relationship is clear, but it does not determine literary direction or establish a larger claim about provenance.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How does the stated purpose “that you may not be deceived” define the function of verses 2–7? What distinction does the chapter make between receiving binding revelation, mutually instructing the church, and being taught from on high? Why are mercy and judgment included in the same catalogue of divine voices? How should “for the last time” be held together with “in mine own due time”? What practical response is implied by allowing “the solemnities of eternity” to rest upon one's mind?
