# D&C 20 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 20]] · My notes: [[D&C 20 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 20 serves several functions at once: founding narrative, doctrinal confession, ordinance guide, description of ministerial duties, and administrative rule. Verses 1–16 ground the Church's rise in claimed revelation, repentance, angelic ministry, the Book of Mormon, and witness. Verses 17–36 summarize [[Creation]], the [[Fall of Adam and Eve]], Christ's death and [[Resurrection]], salvation, [[Grace]], and [[Justification and Sanctification]]. Verses 37–79 turn from confession to practice by defining admission, offices, discipline, [[Baptism]], confirmation, blessing children, and the [[Sacrament]]. Verses 80–84 address discipline, records, and mobility between congregations.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
- **D&C 20:1–4:** Founding date and first and second elders.
- **D&C 20:5–16:** Repentance, angelic ministry, translation, and the Book of Mormon's witness.
- **D&C 20:17–28:** Creation, fall, Christ's saving work, and the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost.
- **D&C 20:29–36:** Repentance, endurance, justification, sanctification, and warnings against falling.
- **D&C 20:37:** Qualifications for baptismal admission.
- **D&C 20:38–67:** Offices, duties, limits, conferences, licenses, votes, and ordination.
- **D&C 20:68–74:** Instruction of members, blessing children, accountability, and baptism by immersion.
- **D&C 20:75–79:** Frequent sacramental worship and prescribed prayers.
- **D&C 20:80–84:** Discipline, membership records, and certificates of good standing.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's doctrinal center is a compact account of the [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]]. Humanity is created in God's image but becomes fallen through transgression; the Father sends his Son, who resists temptation, is crucified, rises, ascends, and reigns. Salvation is offered to believers before and after Christ's mortal ministry (D&C 20:17–27).

D&C 20:30–34 holds together divine initiative and continuing discipleship. Justification and sanctification come through [[Grace]], while disciples must love and serve God, pray, remain watchful, and endure. The warning that even sanctified people may fall prevents sanctification from being treated as automatic immunity from later apostasy.

Baptismal admission is both inward and public. D&C 20:37 requires humility, repentance, covenantal willingness, determination to endure, and observable works. D&C 20:72–74 then specifies authority, wording, and immersion. Children receive a blessing, but membership requires accountability and capacity for repentance (D&C 20:70–71), supporting the rejection of infant baptism without assigning an age in this chapter.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The recurring words **know**, **witness**, and **manifest** give the chapter a public and evidentiary register. Elders bear witness, candidates witness repentance and commitment, members manifest faith through conduct, and sacrament participants witness their remembrance before God. The language connects inward conviction with accountable action.

In D&C 20:69, "a godly walk and conversation" most naturally refers to one's manner of life. The surrounding phrases—works and faith, walking in holiness—make the sense broader than spoken conversation.

Repeated doxologies ending in "Amen" at D&C 20:4, 12, 16, 28, and 36 mark transitions within the founding and doctrinal material.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter moves from narrated history to corporate confession and then to direct regulation. D&C 20:1–16 tells how authority and scripture came; D&C 20:17–36 repeatedly declares what "we know"; D&C 20:37–84 prescribes what the Church, its officers, and its members are to do. This sequence makes practice flow from remembered events and confessed doctrine.

The sacrament prayers provide the clearest textual connections. D&C 20:77 closely parallels [[Moroni 4]], and D&C 20:79 closely parallels [[Moroni 5]]. The correspondence strongly supports a literary or common liturgical relationship. It cannot, without additional historical evidence, determine direction of dependence or establish the antiquity of either formulation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Intertextual
> The near word-for-word correspondence between D&C 20:77/79 and the Moroni 4-5 sacrament prayers is evidence of a direct literary/liturgical relationship between the Book of Mormon text and the earliest Restoration sacrament ordinance, but does not by itself establish the direction of dependence or resolve claims about the ancient origin of the underlying Nephite liturgy.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · relationship strongly supported; origin unresolved · [[Intertextual in D&C 20|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal Theological Argument
> Within the chapter's argument, the Book of Mormon functions as a corroborating witness for existing scripture and for the claim that God still inspires and calls people. This is strong evidence for the chapter's own theology of continuing revelation, but the document's assertions are not independent proof that every historical or supernatural claim is true.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 1.0 · internal argument clear; external force disputed · [[Internal Theological Argument in D&C 20|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual Correspondence
> The sacrament prayers in D&C 20:77 and 20:79 correspond nearly verbatim to Moroni 4:3 and 5:2. This is strong evidence of a direct literary or shared liturgical relationship between the texts, though verbal correspondence alone cannot determine the direction or historical origin of that relationship.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · strong verified correspondence; origin unresolved · [[Textual Correspondence in D&C 20|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual-Historical *(tentative)*
> D&C 20:66-67 refer to offices — "president of the high priesthood," "bishop," "high councilor," "high priest" — that, per broader Latter-day Saint historical accounts, were not yet organized in the Church in April 1830 (the high priesthood office is generally dated to June 1831 and high councils to 1834), suggesting these verses may reflect later expansion of the text rather than its original 1830 dictation. This is offered cautiously; the canonical text alone cannot establish which verses, if any, were added when.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.5 · plausible historical hypothesis requiring collation

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. Why does the founding narrative preserve the first elder's renewed entanglement and repentance rather than presenting uninterrupted success?
2. Which biographical episode, if any, best fits the sequence of repentance, angelic ministry, and receipt of translation power in D&C 20:5–8?
3. How do grace, works, vigilance, and endurance function together in D&C 20:29–34?
4. What protections and risks arise from combining claimed spiritual direction with church voting, certificates, licenses, and conferences?
5. How do home visits, family prayer, reconciliation, ordinance authority, and meeting leadership fit together in the chapter's account of ministerial duty?
6. Does the phrase "after this manner" present the sacrament prayers as exact verbal forms, prescribed models, or both?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare D&C 20:37 with [[Moroni 6]] on baptismal admission and continuing care. Compare D&C 20:77, 79 with [[Moroni 4]] and [[Moroni 5]], observing both the extensive wording shared and the limits of verbal correspondence for establishing direction or origin. Read D&C 20:73 alongside [[Matthew 28]] for the baptismal naming of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, and alongside [[2 Nephi 31]] for baptism, the Holy Ghost, and endurance. Compare D&C 20:70–71 with [[D&C 68]] on accountability and [[D&C 107]] on later priesthood and governance structures.

A separate textual-history study should collate the earliest surviving manuscript and early printed editions before deciding whether references to high priesthood, bishops, or high councils in D&C 20:66–67 were original, anticipatory, or later expansions.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
