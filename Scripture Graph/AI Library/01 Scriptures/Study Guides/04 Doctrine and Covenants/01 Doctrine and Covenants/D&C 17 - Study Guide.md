# D&C 17 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 17]] · My notes: [[D&C 17 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–4, By faith the Three Witnesses will see the plates and other sacred items; 5–9, Christ bears testimony of the divinity of the Book of Mormon.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Conditions and promised objects (vv. 1–2):** reliance on Christ's word, full purpose of heart, and faith precede the promised view.

2. **Commission and purpose (vv. 3–5):** seeing is followed by testimony; the stated purposes include Joseph Smith's preservation and the advancement of God's work.

3. **Attestation and concluding promises (vv. 6–9):** the text declares the book true, describes the recipients as receiving power, faith, and a gift like Joseph's, and promises sufficient grace and last-day deliverance conditional on obedience.

The repeated “righteous purposes” clause links verse 4 with the conclusion in verse 9. It is better understood as a refrain or as framing verses 4–9 than as an inclusio around the entire chapter.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
In this particular commission, [[Faith]] is the stated condition for sight, and sight precedes the duty to testify. The chapter therefore joins divine gift with accountability, although it does not define whether the experience was ordinary physical inspection, visionary perception, or a combination.

Verse 8 joins [[Obedience]] and [[Grace]] without making them identical: obedience is the stated condition, while Christ's grace supplies the promised sufficiency. Verse 7 does not clearly define the “same gift” received by the witnesses; sight and testimony fit the immediate commission, but the preceding reference to translation leaves a broader reading possible.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter combines sensory and theological descriptions. The recipients are to see “with your eyes,” yet the view is also obtained through faith and “by my power.” Both features belong to the text; neither should be used to erase the other or to settle the precise mode of perception.

The declaration in verse 6 follows the statement that Joseph translated “that part which I have commanded him.” The antecedent of “it is true” is not completely explicit: it may refer to the book, the translated portion, or the preceding statement as a whole.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verse 1 gathers objects associated with several earlier narratives: Laban's sword recalls [[1 Nephi 4]], Lehi's directors recall [[1 Nephi 16]], and the interpreters associated with the brother of Jared recall [[Ether 3]]. This permits a literary reading in which Jaredite and Lehite histories are presented as parts of one sacred-record tradition. It could also be a practical or rhetorical catalog, so deliberate authorial intent cannot be established from the list alone.

The chapter moves from reliance on Christ's word, through promised sight and commanded testimony, to the speaker's self-identification as Jesus Christ.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Theological Attestation
> The chapter contains an explicit first-person divine attestation that the translated book is true. This is central theological evidence within the revelation's own framework, but it is not independent corroboration for readers who do not already accept the revelation's divine authority.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 1.0 · accepted observation with explicit epistemic boundary · [[Internal Theological Attestation in D&C 17|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal Textual Evidence
> As a text, D&C 17 is strong primary evidence that a specific witness experience and subsequent public testimony were being prescribed; because its language is prospective, the chapter alone is not evidence that the recipients later saw the objects or fulfilled the command.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How should “seen them with your eyes” be understood alongside seeing by faith and divine power? What evidence outside this prospective revelation is needed to assess whether and how the promise was fulfilled? Why does the promised manifestation include several objects in addition to the plates? What might “the same power,” “the same faith,” and “the same gift” mean in verse 7? What kinds of danger could “destroyed” in verse 4 denote? To what does “it” most naturally refer in verse 6?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the witness commission in [[D&C 5]] with D&C 17 and with the three-witness passage in [[2 Nephi 27]]. Trace the objects in verse 1 through [[1 Nephi 4]], [[1 Nephi 16]], [[Alma 37]], and [[Ether 3]]. Compare the sufficient-grace language with [[Ether 12]] and [[2 Corinthians 12]]. The gates-of-hell wording may also be compared with [[Matthew 16]], while noting that the passages address different recipients and that shared biblical diction does not by itself establish an intentional allusion.
