# D&C 77 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 77]] · My notes: [[D&C 77 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Doctrine and Covenants 77 is a compact question-and-answer commentary on Revelation 4–11. It moves from heavenly imagery to the seven seals, angelic administration, the gathering of Israel, the seventh thousand years, John's commission, and the two witnesses. Its answers combine symbolic interpretation with concrete referents: an image may be figurative while still depicting an individual being or a future event. Major themes include [[Revelation]], [[Creation]], [[Angels]], [[Gathering of Israel]], [[Millennium]], and the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The sequence follows the order of John's Apocalypse:

- Verses 1–5 interpret the heavenly scene, living creatures, and elders associated with [[Revelation 4]].
- Verses 6–7 explain the sealed book and organize its seals into successive thousand-year periods.
- Verses 8–11 interpret the angels and sealing imagery of [[Revelation 7]], connecting them with worldwide gospel ministry and the [[Gathering of Israel]].
- Verses 12–13 place the trumpets and later judgments in relation to the seventh seal and Christ's coming.
- Verses 14–15 interpret John's little book and the two witnesses as prophetic commissions within the latter-day restoration.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter portrays salvation on a cosmic scale. The earth is destined for a sanctified, immortal state (verse 1), and human and nonhuman creatures appear within its imagery of eternal felicity (verses 2–3), although the text does not explain the mode of their future existence. Verse 12 associates the beginning of the seventh thousand years with sanctification, judgment, and redemption. Verse 11 joins Israel’s tribal imagery with high priests ordained from every nation to administer the gospel and bring willing people to the church of the Firstborn. The passage does not resolve whether “every nation” broadens the group beyond Israelite lineage or describes scattered Israel among the nations. These teachings connect [[Priesthood]], [[Missionary Work]], [[Gathering of Israel]], [[Final Judgment]], and [[Salvation]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
“Representation” and “likeness” govern the chapter's symbolic vocabulary. Eyes signify light and knowledge; wings signify power to move and act. The spiritual and temporal are said to be mutually “in the likeness” of one another, giving temporal forms genuine symbolic usefulness without stating that symbol and referent are identical. “Elias” in verses 9 and 14 is closely associated with gathering and restoring; it may describe a restorative commission or role, but the text does not fully explain whether both uses designate the same person.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
A useful reader’s lens is to notice how the answers move from a Revelation image to one or more kinds of explanation: a referent, a represented quality, an authorized function, or a place in sacred history. The text does not itself name this four-part scheme, and not every answer contains every element. Its clearest organizing analogy appears in verse 12, where six days of creation followed by a sanctified seventh day become the pattern for the beginning of the seventh thousand years. This supports a typological or schematic dimension to the chronology even though the section also assigns events to numbered millennia.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How does the chapter hold together its claim that the beasts are figurative expressions and its claim that they are four individual beings? Does “likeness” describe appearance, embodied form, functional correspondence, or some combination? Is the seven-thousand-year framework intended for exact date calculation, or primarily as creation-week typology? What is excluded by the phrase “except that which he hath not put into his power” in verse 12? How should the two uses of “this is Elias” be related without assuming more than the text states?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
