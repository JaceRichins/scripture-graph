# D&C 21 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 21]] · My notes: [[D&C 21 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 21 is a compact founding revelation. It commands that a record be kept, identifies the principal leader as seer, translator, prophet, apostle, and elder, and records the church's organization on April 6, 1830 (vv. 1–3). It then instructs the church concerning the reception of revealed words, promises divine aid, offers personal reassurance concerning [[Zion]], testifies of Christ's crucifixion and remission of sins, and gives ordination and preaching instructions (vv. 4–12). The chapter joins leadership with divine mediation, communal [[Faith]], patient obedience, and service rather than treating office merely as status.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Founding identity and record (vv. 1–3):** A record is commanded, leadership offices are named, and the church's organization is dated.
2. **Revelation and communal response (vv. 4–6):** The church must heed words received by revelation; patient, faithful obedience is followed by promises of divine protection.
3. **Zion, forgiveness, and witness of Christ (vv. 7–9):** Diligence, prayer, and grief for Zion give way to rejoicing, blessing, and testimony of Christ's crucifixion and remission of sins.
4. **Ordination and mission (vv. 10–12):** Oliver Cowdery receives an ordination responsibility and a preaching commission extending from the church to Gentiles and Jews.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents [[Revelation]] as mediated yet divinely authorized: the leader receives words and commandments, and the church is commanded to receive them 'as if from mine own mouth' with patience and [[Faith]] (vv. 4–5). This applies most directly to words received from God in the chapter's founding setting; it does not state that every opinion or administrative act of any leader is revelation. The closing phrase of verse 4 may charge either the leader or the receiving church to walk in holiness, so its precise grammatical application remains uncertain.

Verse 9 connects [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]] with [[Repentance and Forgiveness]]: Jesus was crucified for the sins of the world, and remission is extended to the contrite heart. Verses 10–12 connect office with [[Priesthood]], ordination, and outward service, although their dense pronouns make the exact sequence clearer with historical context than from this chapter alone.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The phrase 'as if from mine own mouth' (v. 5) expresses delegated revelatory authority. The comparison is strong, but its wording still distinguishes God, the messenger, and the receiving community. Verse 9 makes that mediation explicit: the words are given through God 'by the Comforter.'

The causal wording 'by doing these things' (v. 6) ties the ensuing promises to the instructions in verses 4–5. The text therefore does not present protection from darkness as an isolated or unconditional slogan.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter moves among institutional, personal, and proclamatory registers. It begins with a record, a list of offices, and a precise date (vv. 1–3); turns to commands, promises, prayer, weeping, and rejoicing (vv. 4–9); and closes with ordination and preaching instructions (vv. 10–12). This progression is a useful reading of the chapter's arrangement, though it does not by itself prove a deliberate literary design.

D&C 21:6 closely echoes Matthew 16:18 in declaring that 'the gates of hell shall not prevail.' Matthew's 'against it' becomes 'against you.' The verbal parallel is strong, but whether the verse deliberately transfers a specific ecclesiological promise, or simply employs familiar biblical language for obedient hearers, remains interpretive.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Verbal Intertextuality
> The near-verbatim 'gates of hell shall not prevail' wording is strong textual evidence that D&C 21:6 frames its promise in biblical language associated with Christ's declaration about his church in Matthew 16:18. It is only moderate evidence for a deliberate, specific allusion because the phrase was already memorable and widely available in the King James Bible.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does 'you' in verse 6 refer primarily to the founding members, the organized church collectively, or faithful hearers more generally?
- How do the qualifications in verses 4–5—receiving commandments, walking in holiness, patience, and faith—shape the meaning of [[Sustaining Church Leaders]]?
- Why does a revelation about church organization place Christ's crucifixion and remission of sins at the center of its promise to laborers (v. 9)?
- What does the movement from church to world to Gentiles and Jews in verse 12 suggest about the intended reach of the Restoration?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Read [[D&C 20]] alongside D&C 21, especially its descriptions of Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery as apostles and as first and second elders. Compare D&C 21:6 with Matthew 16:18, noting both the shared wording and the change from 'against it' to 'against you.' [[D&C 18]] may provide additional context for Oliver Cowdery's apostolic and missionary setting, but its precise relationship to D&C 21 should be assessed from the passages themselves rather than assumed from a general similarity.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
