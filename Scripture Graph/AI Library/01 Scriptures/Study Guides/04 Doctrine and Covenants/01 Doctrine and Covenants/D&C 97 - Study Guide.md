# D&C 97 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 97]] · My notes: [[D&C 97 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 97 was given through Joseph Smith at Kirtland, Ohio, on August 2, 1833, addressing conditions among the Saints gathering to [[Zion]] in Independence, Missouri. It moves through three linked concerns: the school in Zion under Parley P. Pratt (vv. 1-9), a commandment to build a temple in Zion funded by [[Tithing]] and [[Sacrifice]] (vv. 10-17), and Zion's future (vv. 18-28). Prosperity, divine presence, and protection are repeatedly made conditional on purity and obedience rather than guaranteed outright.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The revelation falls into three movements. (1) Vv. 1-9: the school in Zion — commendation of the humble, Parley P. Pratt's presidency (conditioned on his continuing to abide in the Lord), a warning that some must be chastened, and the ax/fruit-tree figure of judgment and blessing. (2) Vv. 10-17: the temple — command to build 'like unto the pattern,' its purposes (thanksgiving, ministerial instruction), and the condition that it remain undefiled if God's presence is to dwell there. (3) Vv. 18-28: Zion's destiny — glory and honor among the nations if she obeys (vv. 18-21), the definition of Zion as 'THE PURE IN HEART' (v. 21), warnings of judgment on the disobedient (vv. 22-26), and a closing word of mercy: 'if she sin no more none of these things shall come upon her' (vv. 27-28).
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter closely joins [[Education and Learning]], [[Temples]], [[Tithing]], [[Sacrifice]], [[Covenants]], [[Obedience]], and [[Zion]]. Divine acceptance in verse 8 is described through inward qualities—honesty, brokenness, and contrition—together with willingness to keep covenants through commanded sacrifice. The sacred house is both a place of worship and a place where ministers are instructed. Its promised glory and presence depend on holiness rather than construction alone.

The definition of [[Zion]] as “the pure in heart” adds a moral identity to the chapter's geographical references to Zion as a land and city. These senses coexist in the text; the moral definition should not simply erase the geographical and communal uses.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
In verses 11–12, “tithing” names the offering required to build the house and is paired with “sacrifice.” This local usage should be interpreted first within this command. The passage alone does not provide a complete definition of every later use of [[Tithing]].

The repeated conditional forms—“inasmuch as,” “if,” and “if she sin no more”—govern the promises concerning the house and Zion. They are central to the chapter's logic.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The ax/tree/fruit/fire figure of verse 7 closely — in places near word-for-word — parallels biblical judgment sayings: Matthew 3:10 and Luke 3:9 share the ax-at-the-root clause, while Matthew 7:19 and 3 Nephi 14:19 share the fruit/fire clause without the ax/root element. This may reflect deliberate reuse or simply the pervasive influence of King James phrasing on the revelation's language; either way it is strong verbal evidence, not proof of a unique source. D&C 97 reverses the destructive image into a blessing in verse 9, a fruitful tree 'planted in a goodly land, by a pure stream,' which plausibly but not certainly echoes [[Jacob 5]]'s tree-nourishing imagery. Verse 16's 'the pure in heart...shall see God' closely echoes the Beatitude 'Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God' (Matthew 5:8). The temple-purity language of verses 15 and 17 shares a thematic, though not verbatim, purity-of-access concern with 3 Nephi 27:19's 'no unclean thing can enter into his kingdom.'
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Verbal Intertextuality
> The wording about the ax, unfruitful trees, cutting down, and fire closely parallels biblical judgment sayings and is strong evidence that D&C 97 participates in the scriptural tree-fruit-fire tradition.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · strong, near-verbatim KJV overlap · [[Verbal Intertextuality in D&C 97|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual Conditionality
> The repeated conditional statements provide strong textual evidence that the chapter presents Zion's prosperity and escape from judgment as dependent on holiness, obedience, and refraining from further sin, rather than as unconditional guarantees.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · strong, well-caveated evidence claim · [[Textual Conditionality in D&C 97|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Historical-Context Correlation *(tentative)*
> D&C 97 is dated August 2, 1833, roughly two weeks after a Missouri mob destroyed the Church's Independence printing office and tarred and feathered Bishop Edward Partridge and Charles Allen (July 20, 1833), and about three months before Latter-day Saints were violently expelled from Jackson County (November 1833). Read against this timeline, the conditional warning of 'sore affliction...sword...devouring fire' in verse 26 sits close to events that in fact followed — though the text itself frames the outcome as contingent on Zion's own future conduct, not as an unconditional prediction.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · disputed historical correlation

> [!evidence] Textual-Historical Development *(tentative)*
> D&C 97:11-12 requires 'tithing' to fund the Zion temple in 1833, five years before the Lord's arithmetic definition of tithing as one-tenth of a person's interest annually in D&C 119 (1838); this gap invites the interpretation that 'tithing' in 1833 functioned more as a general term for consecrated offering under the still-operating law of consecration than as the later fixed percentage.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.5 · disputed interpretation of a real gap
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
What kind of experience is intended by the promise that the pure in heart will “see God”—a physical appearance, a revelatory encounter, an eschatological promise, or language capable of more than one application? The chapter does not specify.

What precisely is the “offering” accepted in verse 27? The nearby commands concerning tithing, sacrifice, and the house provide plausible context, but the verse does not explicitly define the offering's full scope.

How should the warning extending “until the Lord come” relate to the immediate commands given to Zion? The chapter combines local communal admonition with broader judgment language without supplying a detailed timetable.
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
For the Church's scriptural response to Missouri persecution, see [[D&C 98]], given four days later. Compare the sacred-house instructions with [[D&C 94]], a same-day companion revelation that also invokes a divine 'pattern,' and with the later formal definition of tithing in [[D&C 119]] — treating each revelation's own wording and context on its own terms rather than assuming one determines the other. Compare D&C 97:7 with Matthew 3:10, Luke 3:9, Matthew 7:19, and 3 Nephi 14:19 to distinguish clear verbal overlap from stronger claims about dependence.
