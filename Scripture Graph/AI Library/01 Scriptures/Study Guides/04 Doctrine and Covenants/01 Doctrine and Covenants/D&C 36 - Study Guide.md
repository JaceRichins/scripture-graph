# D&C 36 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 36]] · My notes: [[D&C 36 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 36 begins with a personal commission to a servant named Edward: he is declared forgiven, called to preach, and promised the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] through the Lord's action by Sidney Rigdon's hand (vv. 1–3). Verse 4 then broadens the instruction to others who embrace the calling, are ordained, and are sent to preach (vv. 4–7). The revelation closes with Jesus Christ's self-identification and a command to prepare for his sudden coming to his temple (v. 8). The chapter also uses conspicuous King James Bible language, especially from Acts 2:40, Jude 1:23, and Malachi 3:1. These verbal relationships do not by themselves determine how the wording was produced.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Personal commission (vv. 1–3):** Edward is blessed, forgiven, called to preach, promised the Comforter, and commanded to declare praise.
2. **Broadened commission (vv. 4–7):** the commandment concerns others who embrace the calling, come before designated servants, and may be ordained and sent to preach repentance.
3. **Divine identification and readiness (v. 8):** Jesus Christ identifies himself, commands the hearers to gird up their loins, and promises to come suddenly to his temple.

The movement from one named recipient to a wider body is explicit. It supports an institutional reading related to [[Missionary Work]], although the chapter is not a complete administrative policy and does not define every eligibility condition.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter places forgiveness, calling, reception of the Spirit, ordination, and missionary sending in close sequence without treating them as identical or explicitly making one the universal prerequisite for another.

Verse 2 identifies the promised Spirit as the Holy Ghost and the Comforter, who teaches the peaceable things of the kingdom. Its description of the Lord acting through Sidney Rigdon's hand supports an interpretation of authorized human ministry as an instrument of divine action, while not defining every ordinance or priesthood office.

Verses 5–7 hold inward willingness and outward commissioning together: a person embraces the calling with singleness of heart and is also ordained and sent. The expression “Save yourselves” appears within a call to [[Repentance and Forgiveness]], escape from corrupting danger, and rejection of defilement. It demands responsive action but does not, by itself, teach redemption independently of Jesus Christ.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
D&C 36:6 reproduces Acts 2:40's “Save yourselves from this untoward generation” verbatim. Its following images closely recall Jude 1:23, though Jude has “garment” rather than “garments” and “spotted by” rather than “spotted with.” D&C 36:8 likewise echoes Malachi 3:1 through the distinctive promise of a sudden coming to the temple, changing the biblical third-person formulation into Christ's first-person declaration.

These correspondences strongly establish scriptural and King James Bible-shaped diction. They do not, without further evidence, distinguish among conscious quotation, remembered biblical idiom, oral religious culture, revelatory expression, scribal transmission, or editing, nor do they establish a larger conclusion about authenticity or divine origin.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Two juxtapositions shape the chapter. First, “the peaceable things of the kingdom” appear beside forceful speech images: preaching “as with the voice of a trump” and declaring Hosanna “with a loud voice.” This may create a contrast between peaceful divine instruction and urgent public witness, although the text does not explicitly say that the Comforter's teaching is the precise content delivered loudly. Second, the address moves outward from one servant's forgiveness and calling to instructions concerning other willing candidates and elders, culminating in mission “among the nations.”
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does the text establish—and what does it leave unstated—about the relationship among forgiveness, calling, receiving the Spirit, ordination, and being sent?
- How do inward willingness and commissioning by others operate together in verses 5–7?
- How do Acts 2:40 and Jude 1:23 illuminate verse 6 without predetermining its theology or mode of composition?
- Does “I will suddenly come to my temple” refer to a particular historical event, a general expectation, or an eschatological fulfillment? What evidence outside this chapter would be needed to decide?
- Which features of verses 4–7 support an institutional pattern, and which details of implementation remain unspecified?
