# D&C 93 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 93]] · My notes: [[D&C 93 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 93 moves from a promise of direct knowledge of God to an account of Christ's identity and growth, then to teachings about human premortal existence, [[Truth]], [[Agency]], embodiment, and glory. Its final movement applies those teachings to [[Family]], [[Prayer]], missionary labor, and [[Education and Learning]]. The movement is significant: cosmic claims about light and intelligence culminate in ordinary duties to teach children, correct households, study, and obey.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Promise and divine identity (vv. 1-5):** A conditional promise of seeing the Lord introduces Christ as the true light, describes his unity with the Father, and recalls his incarnation.
2. **John's record (vv. 6-18):** The Lord introduces John's record; verses 11-17 contain repeated first-person witness formulas concerning Christ's glory, incarnation, baptism, fullness, and power. Verses 6 and 18 promise a future fullness of that record.
3. **Worship and promised fullness (vv. 19-22):** The sayings explain how and what to worship and extend grace-for-grace and fullness language to the faithful.
4. **Truth, agency, and embodiment (vv. 23-39):** The revelation treats existence with God in the beginning, truth, uncreated intelligence, agency, spirit and element, joy, and the reception or loss of light.
5. **Children and household application (vv. 40-50):** The command to teach children light and truth bridges the general doctrine to distinct corrections addressed to Frederick G. Williams, Sidney Rigdon, Joseph Smith Jr., and Newel K. Whitney.
6. **Mission and learning (vv. 51-53):** The chapter closes with travel, proclamation, united prayer, scriptural translation, and broad learning for [[Zion]].
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents salvation as participation in divine fullness. Christ is described as receiving grace for grace until he receives the Father's fullness; the faithful are likewise promised grace for grace and eventual glory in Christ (D&C 93:12-20). This correspondence does not erase distinction: Christ remains the Firstborn, Redeemer, creator, and the one through whom others are begotten (D&C 93:8-10, 21-22).

The chapter also joins [[Premortal Life]], [[Agency]], and embodiment. The audience was with the Father in the beginning, and man was in the beginning with God (D&C 93:23, 29). Intelligence, identified as the light of truth, is said to be uncreated, although the text does not fully define its relationship to individual human spirits. Verses 30-32 associate independence, agency, manifested light, and accountability, but the precise causal relation among these statements remains interpretive. Fullness of joy requires spirit and element inseparably connected (D&C 93:33-34), giving embodiment a positive theological role related to [[Resurrection]], though resurrection is not explicitly named.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Several repeated terms connect the chapter's teachings. **Fullness** moves from the Father's gift to Christ (D&C 93:4, 12-17) to the promise offered to the faithful (D&C 93:18-20, 27-28) and finally to embodied joy (D&C 93:33-34). **Light and truth** describe Christ, intelligence, divine glory, what people may receive, and what may be lost through disobedience and inherited tradition (D&C 93:2, 9, 23-29, 36-40).

The phrase **in the beginning** is applied to Christ, the audience, man, and the wicked one. This repetition provides thematic continuity without by itself settling every question about premortal identity or the nature of intelligence. Verse 24 defines truth through present, past, and future reality; verse 25 warns against what is more or less than that account.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter changes speakers and addressees several times. The Lord introduces John's record in verses 6-10; explicit first-person formulas such as 'I, John, bear record' appear in verses 11-17; the Lord resumes direct instruction in verse 18 and thereafter. The address then narrows from collective teaching to named household corrections in verses 41-50.

D&C 93:7-11 closely engages [[John 1]] through a dense sequence of shared language: beginning, Word, creation, life, light, glory, flesh, Only Begotten, grace, and truth. D&C 93 also adds the messenger of salvation, multiple worlds, and Christ's grace-to-grace progression. The later movement into household responsibility is linked to the preceding doctrine by verses 38-42, where inherited tradition removes light and truth, parents are commanded to teach children, and Frederick G. Williams is corrected for failing to do so. This supports a real ethical bridge while leaving the chapter's complete compositional history unresolved.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Textual/Authorship Ambiguity *(tentative)*
> The text does not unambiguously identify which 'John' is the source of the embedded testimony in verses 6-18. The traditional identification is the Apostle John (author of the Gospel of John, given the verbal echo of John 1:1,14), but the eyewitness description of the dove and heavenly voice at the baptism itself (v.15) is, in the biblical narrative, most naturally the report of an eyewitness present at that scene, which fits John the Baptist rather than the Apostle John, who was not depicted as present at the Jordan in the Gospels.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · Identity unresolved; historical characterization unsupported

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Who is the John speaking in verses 6-18? The passage names John but does not identify him more precisely within the chapter.
- What would constitute the promised 'fulness of the record of John' in verses 6 and 18, and does any extant text claim to fulfill that promise?
- Does 'intelligence' in verses 29-30 consistently mean the light of truth, an individual acting entity, or related concepts used with different emphasis?
- How should Christ's progression from grace to grace be related to his premortal status as Word and creator?
- What kinds of inherited tradition does verse 39 condemn, and how can harmful tradition be distinguished from faithful transmission?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare verses 7-11 closely with [[John 1]], marking both retained vocabulary and D&C 93's additions. Read D&C 88:6-13 alongside verses 2,9-10,28,36 to trace the relationship among Christ, creation, life, intelligence, and the [[Light of Christ]]. Compare verses 19-20 with D&C 84:36-38 for two accounts of receiving the Father and his fullness. Finally, compare the human-temple language of verses 33-35 with 1 Corinthians 3:16-17 while noting that D&C 93 explicitly situates that language within a teaching about spirit, element, and joy.
