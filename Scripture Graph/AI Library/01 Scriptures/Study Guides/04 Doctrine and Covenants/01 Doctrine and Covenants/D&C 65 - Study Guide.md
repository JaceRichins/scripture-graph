# D&C 65 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 65]] · My notes: [[D&C 65 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 65 is a short revelation built around a proclamation and a prayer. A 'voice...crying' issues a triple summons to prepare: for the Lord's way (v.1), for the [[Kingdom of God]]'s advance, likened to a stone cut without hands that fills the earth (v.2), and for the Bridegroom's supper (v.3). The Saints are then instructed to pray, call on God's name, and make his works known (v.4), and the section closes with language addressed directly to God, asking that the [[Kingdom of God]] go forth on earth so that the kingdom of heaven may come when the Son of Man descends in glory at the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] (vv.5-6).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The section moves in three connected steps: (1) vv.1-3, a proclaimed 'voice' announcing preparation through three biblical images — the prepared way, the expanding stone, and the wedding supper; (2) v.4, direct imperatives to pray and testify; (3) vv.5-6, language addressed directly to God ('thou, O God') culminating in a doxology and 'Amen.' The section shifts from third-person announcement to second-person instruction to direct address to God — though vv.4-5 remain grammatically continuous ('Call upon the Lord, that...') rather than three sharply separated genres.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter speaks of 'the keys of the kingdom of God' being 'committed unto man on the earth' (v.2), which Latter-day Saints commonly connect to [[Priesthood Restoration]], though the verse itself does not use that term. Verses 5-6 distinguish the kingdom of God already 'set up on the earth' from a kingdom of heaven yet to come when the Son of Man descends — language that anticipates the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] without spelling out how the two relate institutionally. The section ties this hope to human action: [[Missionary Work]] (the gospel rolling forth) and [[Prayer]] (calling on God's name, making his works known) are offered as ways the Saints participate in the kingdom's advance, while its ultimate purpose remains God's own glory, honor, and power (v.6).
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter is dense with earlier scriptural language: the 'voice...crying, prepare ye the way' formula (v.1) echoes [[Isaiah 40]]'s prophecy as quoted of John the Baptist in the Synoptic Gospels ([[Matthew 3]], [[Mark 1]], [[Luke 3]]); 'the stone...cut out of the mountain without hands' (v.2) closely tracks [[Daniel 2]]'s vision (2:34-35, 44-45) of a kingdom that fills the earth; 'the supper of the Lamb' (v.3) recalls 'the marriage supper of the Lamb' in [[Revelation 19]]:9, while 'the Bridegroom' evokes Gospel bridegroom imagery such as [[Matthew 25]]'s parable of the ten virgins; and the closing petition and doxology (v.6) parallel the Lord's Prayer's 'Thy kingdom come' and 'thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory' (Matthew 6:10, 13).
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The concentration of identifiable biblical language in six verses securely demonstrates biblical reuse: [[Isaiah 40]]'s preparation summons (via its Synoptic application), [[Daniel 2]]'s expanding stone, and [[Revelation 19]]/[[Matthew 25]]'s wedding imagery are combined into a single latter-day appeal. That the language is reused is clear; how to explain the reuse — conscious literary composition, revelatory adaptation, or Joseph Smith's KJV-saturated idiom — is a separate, unresolved question the text does not settle. Notably, D&C 65 borrows Daniel's stone-and-mountain image but drops its destructive element (smiting and grinding the prior kingdoms to dust), applying it instead solely to the gospel's peaceful spread.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Is the 'stone cut out of the mountain without hands' (v.2) a specific claim to fulfill Daniel's prophecy, or a generalized image of growth already current in the Saints' religious environment?
- What relationship does v.6's distinction between 'the kingdom of God' (going forth) and 'the kingdom of heaven' (coming) intend — two institutionally distinct kingdoms, or parallel expressions for the same reality at different stages?
- What does it mean for a revelation to instruct prayer (v.4) and then supply language that itself reads as the prayer (vv.5-6)?
- Which specific activities in vv.4-5 count as 'preparing the way of the Lord'?
