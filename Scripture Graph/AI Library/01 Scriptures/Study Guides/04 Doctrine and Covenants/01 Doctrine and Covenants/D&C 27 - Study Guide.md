# D&C 27 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 27]] · My notes: [[D&C 27 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 27 moves through three connected movements. Verses 1-4 give instruction on the [[Sacrament]], teaching that the specific food or drink matters less than approaching the ordinance 'with an eye single to my glory' and remembering Christ's body and blood, while also restricting the Saints from purchasing wine from 'enemies' and requiring wine 'made new among' the Saints. (Tradition associates this with an early dispute over sacramental wine, though the revelation's own text does not narrate that occasion.) Verses 5-14 expand Christ's promise to again drink 'the fruit of the vine' with his disciples into a roster of heavenly messengers and forebears — Moroni, Elias, John (son of Zacharias), Elijah, the patriarchs, Michael/Adam, and Peter, James, and John — each associated with a distinct role, promise, or commission rather than one undifferentiated 'restoration' moment. Verses 15-18 close with a call to take up spiritual armor, closely adapted from Ephesians 6, and to remain faithful 'until I come.' Together the chapter ties ordinance worthiness to [[Priesthood Restoration]] and to readiness for the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Sacramental instruction (vv. 1-4):** Proper remembrance and restrictions on obtaining wine.

2. **The promised gathering (vv. 5-14):** Christ names figures associated with records, restoration, priesthood, covenant promises, and apostolic keys.

3. **Preparation to stand (vv. 15-18):** The armor imagery of [[Ephesians 6]] is applied to truth, righteousness, peace, faith, salvation, the Spirit, revelation, unity, and endurance.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents the [[Sacrament]] as an act directed to the Father's glory and to remembrance of Christ's body and blood (v. 2). It also presents priesthood authority as conferred through ordination and transmitted by heavenly messengers (vv. 8, 12). The text assigns different keys or responsibilities to several figures rather than collapsing their ministries into one undifferentiated restoration event (vv. 5-13).

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 15-18 closely follow the wording and order of Paul's 'whole armour of God' passage in [[Ephesians 6]] (loins girt with truth, breastplate of righteousness, feet shod with the gospel of peace, shield of faith, helmet of salvation, sword of the Spirit) — one of the most sustained clause-by-clause verbal parallels between the Doctrine and Covenants and the King James Bible. Verse 13's 'dispensation... for the fulness of times, in the which I will gather together in one all things' likewise closely reuses Ephesians 1:10, and verse 9's description of Elijah's mission closely reuses Malachi 4:5-6. Verse 1's description of Christ's word as 'quick and powerful' echoes Hebrews 4:12, and verse 2's 'eye single to my glory' echoes both the Sermon on the Mount ('if therefore thine eye be single,' Matthew 6:22) and D&C 88:67. These are strong, checkable verbal parallels, but they demonstrate reuse of biblical language — they do not by themselves settle any particular theory of how revelation and scriptural familiarity interacted in producing the text.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Intertextual Parallel
> The millennial sacrament image in D&C 27:5 ('the hour cometh that I will drink of the fruit of the vine with you on the earth') closely echoes the Synoptic Last Supper saying in which Jesus tells the apostles he will not drink 'this fruit of the vine' again until he drinks it new with them 'in my Father's kingdom' (Matthew 26:29).
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.8 · well-recognized parallel · [[Intertextual Parallel in D&C 27|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Doctrinal Identification
> D&C 27:11 identifies 'Michael' with 'Adam' and titles him 'the ancient of days,' the same title given to the enthroned divine judge figure in Daniel 7. This reflects the doctrine, taught elsewhere in LDS scripture, that Adam and the archangel Michael are the same being.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.8 · textually explicit identification; Daniel link is disputed · [[Doctrinal Identification in D&C 27|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary Dependence Or Conscious Reuse
> The shared order and vocabulary provide strong evidence of a direct literary relationship between D&C 27:15-18 and the English biblical armor passage in Ephesians 6. This observation supports studying D&C 27 as an interpretive reuse of biblical language.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 0.9 · model of correct evidentiary layering · [[Literary Dependence Or Conscious Reuse in D&C 27|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Typological/Proof-Text Interpretation *(tentative)*
> LDS interpretive tradition reads Moroni's charge in D&C 27:5 over 'the keys of the record of the stick of Ephraim' against Ezekiel 37:15-19's prophecy of two inscribed sticks (one for Judah, one for Joseph/Ephraim) becoming one in the Lord's hand, taking the Book of Mormon to be the 'stick of Ephraim.'
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.5 · minority/typological reading, well-labeled

> [!evidence] Doctrinal/Intertextual Identification *(tentative)*
> D&C 27:6-7 identifies 'Elias' as the being who 'visited' Zacharias and promised him a son named John, matching the narrative role of the unnamed-in-D&C angel in Luke 1 who makes that same promise. This reflects a distinctively early-Restoration usage of 'Elias' as a title or function (distinct from Elijah, named separately in v. 9) rather than as one single fixed personal name.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.5 · disputed identification
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does verse 2 treat as essential to the sacrament even while treating the specific food or drink as secondary?
- What was the historical situation behind the wine-purchase restriction in vv. 3-4, and how does this section relate to the Church's later, separate revelation on diet, the [[Word of Wisdom]] (D&C 89)?
- Why does D&C 27:7 associate the title 'Elias' with the angel who visited Zacharias, when Luke 1:19 names that angel Gabriel?
- Which details in verses 16-18 adapt the armor imagery of [[Ephesians 6]] to this chapter's own setting?
- Does verse 13 describe the gathering of people, the unifying of divine work across eras, or both — and what does the verse state explicitly versus imply?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
For related content, compare D&C 20 (the sacrament prayers), D&C 13 (the 1829 restoration of the Aaronic Priesthood referenced in v. 8), and D&C 110 (the 1836 Kirtland Temple appearances, which record a later, more detailed set of key-restorations involving Moses, Elias, and Elijah — though D&C 27 itself does not mention Moses and describes these keys as already committed rather than yet to be given). D&C 84:6-17 lists a separate priesthood genealogy back to Adam; the two passages share subject matter but differ in structure and should not be treated as parallel in form. Malachi 4 is the source of the turning-hearts prophecy applied to Elijah in v. 9.
