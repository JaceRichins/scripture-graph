# D&C 5 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 5]] · My notes: [[D&C 5 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 5 answers Martin Harris's desire for a witness, but it does more than promise a future viewing. It defines Joseph's limited stewardship over the plates, prioritizes completion of translation, promises three additional witnesses, and places both Joseph and Martin under corrective commandments. The chapter holds material and spiritual testimony together: selected witnesses may view the objects, but artifacts alone are not portrayed as capable of compelling faith. Public reception centers on the Lord's words, the witnesses' testimony, and the manifestation of the Spirit.

The chapter is therefore especially relevant to [[Witnesses of the Book of Mormon]], [[Testimony]], [[Covenants]], and [[Obedience]]. Its claims should be evaluated at distinct levels: the text clearly promises witnesses; the later canonical development in [[D&C 17]] is coherent with that promise; whether either revelation is divine remains a larger conclusion not proved merely by literary or canonical coherence.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Verses 1–4 — Custody and assignment:** Martin's request is introduced; Joseph's control and display of the plates are bounded by covenant; translation is designated his first gift.
2. **Verses 5–10 — Words, objects, and unbelief:** the present generation is warned, and the text denies that displaying all the entrusted things would necessarily produce belief.
3. **Verses 11–18 — Three witnesses:** three servants are promised a divinely enabled view and public testimony; believers are promised manifestation of the Spirit and birth of water and Spirit.
4. **Verses 19–20 — Prophetic warning:** a recurring scourge and Christ's coming are compared with an earlier warning concerning Jerusalem.
5. **Verses 21–22 — Correction of Joseph:** Joseph is commanded to repent, resist human persuasion, and remain firm.
6. **Verses 23–29 — Conditions for Martin:** humility, prayer, faith, sincerity, acknowledgment of wrongdoing, covenant, and obedience govern whether he may see and what he may testify.
7. **Verses 30–35 — Conditional pause and preservation:** if Martin does not qualify, Joseph is to pause translation; stopping is then connected with threatened loss, danger to Joseph, promised future means, and final accountability.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents witnessing as a commissioned responsibility governed by covenant, not merely as access to private evidence. Martin's requested view is conditioned on [[Humility]], [[Prayer]], [[Faith]], [[Repentance]], and [[Obedience]] (D&C 5:24–28), while Joseph's stewardship is also bounded by commands and consequences (D&C 5:3–4, 21–22, 30–35).

D&C 5:16 joins belief, manifestation of the Spirit, and birth of water and Spirit, closely paralleling [[John 3]]. A baptismal reading is natural, though the verse does not itself use the words "baptism" or "ordinance."

The chapter places material viewing, transmitted words, commissioned testimony, and spiritual manifestation alongside one another. D&C 5:7 says that displaying the entrusted things would not necessarily produce belief. This can be read as teaching the limits of coercive evidence; a skeptical reading may also see it as insulating the plates claim from demands for unrestricted inspection. The text establishes the contrast but does not settle every interpretation of it.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
D&C 5 repeatedly uses legal and stewardship vocabulary: “witness,” “testimony,” “testified,” “covenant,” “entrusted,” “commanded,” and conditional “if” or “except” clauses. This language makes authority accountable and bounded. Joseph cannot show or control the plates independently (v. 3); Martin cannot dictate the conditions of seeing them (vv. 24–29); and neither man's role is presented as consequence-free.

The formula “I have seen” in verses 25–26 restricts Martin's authorized public claim to direct experience and its asserted divine source. The repetition gives the prospective testimony a formal, almost deposition-like quality, though calling it a legal deposition in a technical historical sense would go beyond the text.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
D&C 5:14 closely adapts [[Song of Solomon 6]]: "clear as the moon, and fair as the sun, and terrible as an army with banners." The KJV Song reads "fair as the moon, clear as the sun," so D&C 5 reverses the two adjectives. This securely demonstrates biblical reuse, while deliberate revision, remembered wording, dictation, transmission, and editing remain possible explanations for the reversal.

Applied to the church's coming forth, the borrowed image suggests beauty and formidable presence. Reading the entire passage as sustained bridal imagery is possible but not required, and the martial comparison does not itself prescribe violence.

Repeated contrasts help organize the chapter: seeing and believing, private desire and commissioned testimony, present gift and future ordination, human persuasion and divine command, urgent translation and commanded stillness. The latter half also repeatedly uses "if" and "except" to make promised outcomes conditional.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does the chapter authorize material witnesses while insisting that showing the objects would not necessarily overcome unbelief?
- What is gained—and what potential concerns arise—when a witness's authorized public wording is prescribed in advance?
- How do the corrections and threatened consequences directed at Joseph affect readings of authority in the chapter?
- Does the army-with-banners image in verse 14 primarily communicate majesty, order, protection, fear, or some combination?
- How should the broad warning of verses 19–20 be studied without prematurely assigning it to a particular later disaster?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the three-witness sequence across [[Ether 5]], [[2 Nephi 27]], D&C 5, and [[D&C 17]]. Track what each text separately claims: prediction of witnesses, promise of a view, conditions of participation, objects to be viewed, and duty to testify. This comparison establishes canonical continuity, but historical study of the witnesses' actual experiences requires sources independent of these passages.

Also compare D&C 5:16 with [[John 3]], and examine the modified quotation from [[Song of Solomon 6]]. For the chapter's theology of stewardship, trace the terms “gift,” “covenant,” “command,” “entrusted,” and “faithful” and relate them cautiously to [[Records and Sacred Writings]] and [[Revelation]].
