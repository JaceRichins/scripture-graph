## Overview

<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter forms five question-answer pairs: Stem (vv. 1–2), rod (vv. 3–4), root (vv. 5–6), Zion's strength (vv. 7–8), and Zion's bands (vv. 9–10). The first three pairs interpret personal symbols in [[Isaiah 11]]; the final two interpret corporate Zion imagery in [[Isaiah 52]]. The movement is from identifying agents to describing the latter-day restoration and [[Gathering of Israel]].

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter links latter-day gathering with priesthood authority and kingdom keys (D&C 113:6, 8). It also portrays the reversal of scattering as morally and revelatorily conditioned: the remnants are exhorted to return to the Lord, after which God promises to speak to them (D&C 113:10). The passage affirms a lineage-related right to priesthood power but does not explain how that corporate right relates to individual ordination or office.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The interpretation changes prophetic imagery into institutional and covenantal terms. “Strength” becomes priesthood authority (D&C 113:8); “bands” become divine curses manifested in Israel's scattering among the Gentiles (D&C 113:10); and the “root” becomes a priesthood-bearing, key-holding ensign for gathering (D&C 113:6). These are supplied interpretations, not definitions that can be derived from the isolated metaphors alone.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The catechism form — question, then 'thus saith the Lord,' then answer — gives the section a liturgical rhythm distinct from the sweeping narrative-vision style of sections like [[D&C 76]]. The interpretive method treats each Isaianic noun phrase ('Stem,' 'rod,' 'root') as bearing its own answer, though the text itself does not settle whether the rod (v. 4) and the root (v. 6) name one servant or two, nor whether this atomizing approach is meant to override the reading — common in Hebrew poetic parallelism — that 'Stem,' 'rod,' and 'root' restate a single figure. Only the distinction between Christ (v. 2) and the rod as 'a servant in the hands of Christ' (v. 4) is textually clear.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Are the rod and root two servants or two descriptions of one servant? The chapter does not say.
- What precise ancestry is intended by the phrase “Ephraim, or of the house of Joseph” in D&C 113:4?
- Does Zion's lineage-based “right” in D&C 113:8 describe a corporate covenant inheritance, individual authority, or both?
- How does the immediate literary context of [[Isaiah 11]] and [[Isaiah 52]] compare with the latter-day interpretation supplied here?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
