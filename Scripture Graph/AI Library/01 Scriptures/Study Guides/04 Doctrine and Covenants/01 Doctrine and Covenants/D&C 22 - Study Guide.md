# D&C 22 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 22]] · My notes: [[D&C 22 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1, Baptism is a new and everlasting covenant; 2–4, Authoritative baptism is required.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Covenant declaration (v. 1):** old covenants are displaced "in this thing," and a new, everlasting covenant is announced.

2. **Negative argument (v. 2):** even numerous baptisms are ineffective if they do not provide entry through the appointed gate; the [[Law of Moses]] and dead works are rejected as alternative routes.

3. **Institutional explanation (v. 3):** the last covenant and the church are presented as God's response to dead works and as a restoration of an earlier pattern.

4. **Concluding imperative (v. 4):** enter as commanded and do not attempt to counsel God.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter distinguishes an ordinance's outward repetition from its recognized efficacy: baptism must belong to the order God presently commands, not merely be performed. This links [[Baptism]], [[Covenants]], [[Ordinances]], and [[Obedience]]. The phrase 'all old covenants... in this thing' should not be read as a blanket repudiation of every earlier divine covenant; 'in this thing' qualifies its scope. 'Dead works' (vv.2-3) echoes New Testament covenant language (Hebrews 6:1's 'repentance from dead works'), but neither text defines the phrase precisely enough to say with confidence that it means specifically ordinances performed without priesthood authority — that reading follows the official chapter heading and later institutional interpretation more than the bare verses. Verse 4's rebuke against seeking to 'counsel your God' ties the passage to [[Obedience]], framing the ruling as something to submit to rather than negotiate — though the preceding verses do supply reasons (superseded covenants, dead works), so the command is not left wholly unexplained.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The combination "new," "everlasting," and "from the beginning" creates deliberate temporal tension. A natural reading is that the covenant is newly operative or newly revealed in this setting while ancient in divine origin. The chapter also alternates between "new and everlasting covenant" and "last covenant"; it does not explain whether these are technical titles or situational descriptions.

"Dead works" is rhetorically forceful but underdefined. The immediate context associates it with ineffective baptism and an inadequate route into the gate, yet readers should not assume a more precise definition than the chapter supplies.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter uses hyperbole ('although a man should be baptized an hundred times') to dramatize the futility of the ordinance without authority — an emphatic, almost absurd number chosen precisely because no reasonable amount of repetition can substitute for what is missing. The closing line ('Amen') marks this as a self-contained oracular unit despite its brevity.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Textual Evidence
> The chapter provides textual support for the official heading's doctrine of authoritative baptism, but that support is indirect: the revealed text invokes God's command, covenant, church, and an ineffective prior baptism without explicitly using the terms "authority" or "priesthood."
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · best-calibrated claim; separates text from heading · [[Internal Textual Evidence in D&C 22|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal/Circumstantial Textual Evidence *(tentative)*
> The chapter's insistence that repeated baptism 'availeth him nothing' and that even a hundred baptisms under the wrong authority are void suggests it was occasioned by a concrete controversy over whether persons previously baptized (in another denomination, or by informal covenant-making prior to the Church's 1830 organization) needed to be baptized again by proper authority.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does 'in this thing' (v.1) limit — baptism specifically, admission to the newly organized church, or a broader covenantal transition?
- Which features make a baptism conform to the command in v.4? The chapter assumes an answer (contrasted with mere repetition) but does not enumerate the features itself.
- Does 'dead works' (vv.2-3) refer to unauthorized ordinances, reliance on a superseded covenant, unrepented conduct, or several of these — the text does not say?
- What does 'even as in days of old' (v.3) refer to, and how does the gate imagery in [[2 Nephi 31]] (which explicitly names the gate as repentance and baptism) clarify D&C 22 without importing details D&C 22 itself leaves unstated?
- Does the 'new and everlasting covenant' language here (applied to baptism) relate to the same phrase's later use for eternal marriage, or are these two applications of a recurring covenant idiom?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
