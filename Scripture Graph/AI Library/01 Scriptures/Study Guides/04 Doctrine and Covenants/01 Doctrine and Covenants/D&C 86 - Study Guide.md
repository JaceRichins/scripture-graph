# D&C 86 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 86]] · My notes: [[D&C 86 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 86 divides into two joined movements. Verses 1–7 give the Lord's own interpretation of the [[Parables of Jesus|parable]] of the wheat and tares (compare [[Matthew 13]]): the field is the world, the apostles sow the good seed, and after their sleep (death) an apostate power described as 'the great persecutor of the church, the apostate, the whore, even Babylon' — driven by Satan — sows tares that choke the wheat and drive the church into the wilderness (v.3). Reaping angels are held back because the crop is still tender (v.4); wheat and tares grow together until a harvest 'fully ripe,' when the wheat is gathered out first, before the tares are bound and the field burned (v.7) — notably the reverse of Matthew 13:30, where the tares are gathered first. Verses 8–11 pivot, through a chain of 'Therefore' clauses (vv.7, 8, 10, 11), to a promise about [[Priesthood]]: the addressees are 'lawful heirs, according to the flesh' whose priesthood has continued through their lineage and will remain until 'the restoration of all things spoken by the mouths of all the holy prophets since the world began' (v.10) — closely echoing Acts 3:21. The concluding role as 'a light unto the Gentiles...a savior unto my people Israel' is explicitly conditioned on continuing 'in my goodness' (v.11), not granted by lineage alone.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Verses 1-3 — Sowing and opposition:** The field is identified as the world, the apostles sow, and Satan introduces tares after they fall asleep.
2. **Verses 4-7 — Delayed harvest:** The blade is tender, angels are ready, and reaping is postponed to avoid destroying wheat. When fully ripe, wheat is gathered before the tares are bound and the field burned.
3. **Verses 8-11 — Heirship and commission:** The revelation describes priesthood continuity through lineage and ends with a conditional vocation toward Gentiles and Israel.

The repeated word “Therefore” marks the transition from the parable's outcome to the identity and obligations of the hearers. The precise logical relationship between the harvest imagery and lineage declaration is suggested rather than explained.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's clearest teaching about judgment is restraint: removing evil prematurely could also harm what is still spiritually tender (vv.5–6), so separation waits for a harvest 'fully ripe' (v.7) — patience, not indifference to evil. Verses 8–11 join [[Priesthood]] to covenant lineage language reminiscent of the [[Abrahamic Covenant]] ('lawful heirs, according to the flesh'), but the promise is not unconditional: the concluding role — a light to the Gentiles and 'a savior' (lowercase, distinct from Christ) to Israel, tied to [[Gathering of Israel]] themes — is explicitly conditioned on 'if ye continue in my goodness' (v.11). The chapter does not itself specify whether this lineage is literal descent or an adoptive/covenantal identity; both readings have circulated in Restoration teaching, and neither is settled by the text alone.

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
D&C 86:1–7 closely tracks Matthew 13's wheat-and-tares discourse, but not as verbatim repetition. Two differences stand out: v.2 names 'the apostles' as sowers of the good seed where Matthew 13:37 names 'the Son of man,' and v.7 has the wheat gathered before the tares are bound, the reverse of Matthew 13:30's tares-first order — both differences are noted here rather than harmonized. V.3's 'the whore, even Babylon, that maketh all nations to drink of her cup' echoes Revelation's Babylon imagery (e.g., Revelation 14:8; 17:1–2) more than it quotes any single verse. V.10's closing clause closely parallels Acts 3:21 ('restitution of all things...spoken by the mouth of all his holy prophets since the world began'), though the phrase was common in early Restoration preaching and need not indicate a targeted citation. V.11's 'a light unto the Gentiles' echoes Isaiah 49:6's servant-song language, also echoed in Luke 2:32 and Acts 13:47.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Who is addressed as 'you'/'your fathers' in vv.8–11 — Joseph Smith and his immediate associates, a wider group, or a general priesthood-holding class? Is 'lawful heirs, according to the flesh' (v.9) meant as literal descent or covenantal/adoptive lineage? Why does v.2 name the apostles rather than Christ as sower, and why does v.7 reverse Matthew 13:30's harvest order? What is the substantive (not just rhetorical) relationship between the wheat-and-tares teaching and the priesthood declaration that follows it?
