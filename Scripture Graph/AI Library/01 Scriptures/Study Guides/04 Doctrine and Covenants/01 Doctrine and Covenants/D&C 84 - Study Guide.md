# D&C 84 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 84]] · My notes: [[D&C 84 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 84 brings together [[Zion]], [[Temples]], [[Priesthood]], [[Ordinances]], scripture, covenant, and [[Missionary Work]]. Its literary movement runs from gathering and promised sacred space, through priesthood and divine presence, to a worldwide commission and practical support for those who preach. The chapter also turns judgment inward: the whole church is condemned for treating received scripture lightly and is called to remember and obey, not merely speak.

These themes should not be flattened into one institutional formula. The text joins authority with sanctification and service, expansive promises with accountability, and reliance on God with organized care for families and the poor.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A useful outline is:

1. Verses 1–5: gathering, New Jerusalem, and the temple expectation.
2. Verses 6–30: priesthood transmission, greater and lesser priesthoods, ordinances, and offices.
3. Verses 31–42: resumption of the sons of Moses and Aaron, covenantal identity, and the oath and covenant.
4. Verses 43–59: God's word, truth, light, sin, and the church's condemnation.
5. Verses 60–95: worldwide commission, baptism, signs, divine provision, and reception or rejection of messengers.
6. Verses 96–102: judgment followed by the new song of Zion.
7. Verses 103–116: money, families, the poor, mutual strengthening, offices, and Newel K. Whitney's assignment.
8. Verses 117–120: final warning, cosmic shaking, and the Lord's coming and reign.

Verse 6 begins a statement about the sons of Moses that verse 31 explicitly resumes. This securely marks the intervening material as parenthetical in the present text, but it does not establish whether the structure arose through planning, dictation, or editing.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The greater priesthood administers the gospel and holds the key of the knowledge of God; its ordinances manifest the power of godliness (D&C 84:19–22). Moses sought to sanctify Israel so the people might behold God, but hardened hearts prevented them from enduring God's presence (D&C 84:23–25). The lesser priesthood remained with the ministering of angels and a preparatory gospel involving [[Repentance]], [[Baptism]], remission of sins, and carnal commandments (D&C 84:26–30).

Faithful recipients are said to become sons of Moses and Aaron and Abraham's seed, language that most naturally describes acquired covenantal or vocational identity rather than biological descent alone. The oath and covenant joins extraordinary inheritance promises with severe accountability. The chapter therefore presents covenant privilege as inseparable from faithfulness and service, while leaving later administrative questions outside its explicit scope.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verses 45–47 form a compact sequence: the Lord's word is truth, truth is light, and light is Spirit, identified as the Spirit of Jesus Christ. The Spirit gives light to everyone who enters the world, while further enlightenment is associated with hearkening. This resembles the language of [[D&C 93]] and [[John 1]], though resemblance does not determine literary direction or make every use of these terms strictly synonymous.

The reception formulas are related but not one perfectly linear chain. Receiving the priesthood and receiving Christ's servants are parallel ways the passage describes receiving Christ; receiving Christ then leads to receiving the Father, the Father's kingdom, and inheritance. Verse 85 likewise balances unpremeditated speech with continually treasuring up the words of life, supporting sustained preparation joined with reliance on inspiration.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
D&C 84 contains extensive biblical and Book of Mormon language. The teaching about lilies, food, clothing, and tomorrow closely parallels [[Matthew 6]], [[Luke 12]], and [[3 Nephi 13]]. The commission, baptism, and signs parallel [[Mark 16]] and [[Mormon 9]], while the body metaphor resembles [[1 Corinthians 12]]. These are strong observations about the received English wording, but they do not determine whether the mechanism was quotation, remembered scriptural idiom, deliberate adaptation, inspired restatement, or some combination. Historical discussion of [[Mark 16]] should also acknowledge the disputed manuscript status of its longer ending.

Verses 98–102 shift into a communal new song. Repeated declarations of what the Lord has done and paired images—Zion above and beneath, earth and heavens, mercy and justice—depict redemption as covenantal and cosmic. The song does not by itself provide a literal chronology of future events.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Predictive Statement
> Verses 4-5 express a concrete temporal expectation: a temple at the appointed place is said to be reared “in this generation,” and the generation is said not all to pass before a house is built. On the face of these verses, this is predictive language rather than merely a command to attempt construction.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · Prediction explicit; fulfillment disputed · [[Predictive Statement in D&C 84|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Time-Bounded Prophetic Language
> The chapter makes an apparently time-bounded prediction that a temple would be built at the appointed place within 'this generation.' The wording creates a genuine interpretive and historical question because the intended scope of 'generation,' the identity of the promised house, and the conditions attached to the prediction are not explained within these verses.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 1.0 · Observation accepted; fulfillment disputed · [[Time-Bounded Prophetic Language in D&C 84|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary Structure
> Verse 6 begins a statement about the sons of Moses, but the thought is interrupted by an extended discussion and explicitly resumed in verse 31 with “as I said concerning the sons of Moses.” This securely identifies a large parenthetical or digressive unit in the present text. It offers only weak evidence for an extemporaneous or dictated compositional texture because a deliberate written digression is also plausible.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 1.0 · Structure accepted; production inference weak · [[Literary Structure in D&C 84|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary Intertextuality *(tentative)*
> The chapter repeatedly reuses or closely echoes biblical language concerning divine rest, living by God's word, universal light, missionary signs, lilies and tomorrow, inspired speech, reception of messengers, cleansing feet, the ecclesial body, and Alpha and Omega. This is strong evidence of deliberate scriptural intertextuality and of an audience expected to recognize biblical idiom.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.8 · Dense biblical register accepted; intent disputed

> [!evidence] Textual Intertextuality *(tentative)*
> The instructions about purse, clothing, anxiety for food, lilies, and prepared speech reproduce recognizable language and themes from New Testament missionary and providence discourses, with especially close parallels to Matthew 6, Luke 12, and their Book of Mormon counterpart in 3 Nephi 13. This is strong evidence of scriptural intertextuality in the received English wording.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.8 · Strong overlap; directional wording disputed

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How should “this generation” in D&C 84:4–5 be defined, and what independent historical evidence supports proposed fulfillment or nonfulfillment?

What is the most likely antecedent of “this” in verse 22: ordinances, priesthood authority, manifested godliness, or the preceding complex as a whole?

Does “the new covenant” in verse 57 designate the Book of Mormon itself, the coordinated phrase “the Book of Mormon and the former commandments,” or a broader covenant embodied in them?

How should the universal light of verses 45–47 relate to the further enlightenment conditioned on hearkening?

Are the protection promises and miraculous signs individual guarantees, corporate commission language, or promises whose scope and conditions are unstated?

How do the financial directions in verses 103–105 qualify the earlier command to travel without purse or scrip?

What, if anything, can independent sources establish about the otherwise unexplained figures and transmissions in verses 6–16?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare D&C 84:81–85 with [[Matthew 6]], [[Luke 12]], and [[3 Nephi 13]], distinguishing exact verbal overlap from broader thematic resemblance and noting D&C 84's missionary setting. Compare D&C 84:64–73 with [[Mark 16]] and [[Mormon 9]], especially the prohibition on boasting about signs. Compare D&C 84:45–47 with [[D&C 93]] and [[John 1]] for the vocabulary of [[Truth]] and [[Light of Christ]], and compare D&C 84:109–111 with [[1 Corinthians 12]] for the many-membered body.

Historical evaluation of the Missouri temple expectation should begin with dated primary evidence about the appointed lot, attempted construction, later buildings, and contemporary meanings of “generation.” It should test conditional, delayed, and alternative-fulfillment readings rather than assuming them. Study of the priesthood lineage should likewise distinguish what the revelation asserts from what surviving independent sources can corroborate.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
