# D&C 4 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 4]] · My notes: [[D&C 4 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 4 is a compact call to [[Service]]. It announces an approaching work, invites those who desire to serve, describes the work as an urgent harvest, names the character expected of workers, and closes with an invitation to ask God. Its strongest emphases are wholehearted devotion and moral qualification, not technique or institutional position.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Announcement (v. 1):** a marvelous work is about to appear.

2. **Call and motive (vv. 2–4):** serve with the whole person; desire opens participation; the harvest is already ready.

3. **Character of the worker (vv. 5–6):** [[Faith]], [[Hope]], [[Charity]], humility, diligence, and related virtues qualify and shape service.

4. **Petition (v. 7):** asking and knocking provide the chapter's final response to needs left unspecified.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter joins [[Service]] with accountability before God: the worker is told to serve wholeheartedly so as to stand blameless at the last day. Verse 4 also associates vigorous labor with [[Salvation]] to the worker's soul. Neither statement supplies a complete doctrine of salvation, so neither should be isolated as teaching salvation by human effort alone.

Verse 3 makes desire spiritually consequential, but it does not define a formal calling procedure. Its safest application is that sincere willingness makes a person eligible to participate in God's work; questions of office and authority must be answered from additional texts.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The harvest language in verse 4 closely resembles [[John 4]], while the ask-and-knock conclusion adapts [[Matthew 7]]. The extended list in verse 6 is especially close to [[2 Peter 1]], though D&C 4 changes the order and adds humility. These correspondences show that the revelation speaks extensively in biblical diction; by themselves they do not settle how that diction entered the revealed text.

Verse 5 separately coordinates “charity and love.” The chapter does not explain whether they carry distinct meanings here, so a technical distinction should not be imposed without further evidence.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verse 6 is built as an asyndetic list (no conjunctions between terms: 'faith, virtue, knowledge, temperance...'), giving it a compact, almost catechetical rhythm distinct from the narrative sentences around it. The chapter also shares its opening 'marvelous work'/harvest-field formula, in verified text overlap, with [[D&C 6]], [[D&C 12]], and [[D&C 14]] — short, similarly patterned revelations given to different individuals in 1829. Whether this shared language reflects a deliberate, recognizable genre of call-to-labor revelations, or simply reusable phrasing suited to similar occasions, remains an open and tentative question.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does verse 3's desire-based standard for being 'called to the work' describe a general invitation to any kind of service, a formal ecclesiastical calling, or both, depending on context?
- Why does verse 6 modify the order of the shared 2 Peter 1 virtue chain (swapping 'brotherly kindness' and 'godliness') and add 'humility' and 'diligence'? Is the change meaningful or incidental?
- Does 'a marvelous work' in verse 1 refer most naturally to the Book of Mormon then being translated, the Restoration more broadly, or both — the text itself does not specify.
- How should the promise of salvation to the diligent worker in verse 4 relate to [[Grace]] and the [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]], without treating this brief chapter as a complete statement on salvation?
- What does having 'an eye single to the glory of God' (v.5) require when otherwise sincere motives are mixed with ambition or desire for recognition?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare directly with [[D&C 6]], [[D&C 12]], and [[D&C 14]] for the shared 'marvelous work'/harvest-field opening formula (verified text overlap), and with [[D&C 88]] for the reused 'eye single to the glory of God' phrase (v.5) and the compressed 'ask...knock' formula (v.7). Their differing recipients and settings can help distinguish genuinely reusable revelatory language from recipient-specific instruction.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
