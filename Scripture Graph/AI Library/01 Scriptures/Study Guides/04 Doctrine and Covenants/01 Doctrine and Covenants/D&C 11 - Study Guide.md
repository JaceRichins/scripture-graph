<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A useful textual outline is:

1. **Invitation to labor (vv. 1–5):** the marvelous work, powerful word, ripe field, and promise to those who ask.
2. **Desire governed by discipleship (vv. 6–14):** Zion, wisdom rather than riches, repentance, gifts, and tests associated with the Spirit.
3. **Preparation before proclamation (vv. 15–22):** wait for a call, keep the commandments, assist the translation, and obtain the word before declaring it.
4. **Personal and general application (vv. 23–27):** Hyrum is named, then the counsel expands to all with good desires.
5. **Christological conclusion (vv. 28–30):** Christ identifies himself and invokes the language of [[John 1]].
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter connects [[Revelation]] with righteous direction as well as enlightenment and joy. The Spirit leads toward goodness, justice, humility, and righteous judgment; it also enlightens the mind and fills the soul with joy (vv. 12–14). The promise of knowledge is expressly bounded by what pertains to righteousness and by faith in Christ.

The instructions also describe preparation for [[Missionary Work]]. Desire matters but does not by itself authorize public preaching: Hyrum is told to wait for a call, keep the commandments, study and obtain the word, and treasure it until the Lord's time (vv. 15–26). Verse 21 therefore places preparation before proclamation without defining one exhaustive model for every ministry.

Verse 7 contrasts material riches with wisdom and identifies [[Eternal Life]] as true wealth.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
D&C 11 uses dense biblical phraseology. Verse 2 closely echoes Hebrews 4:12; verse 5 uses the Gospel ask-and-knock formula; and verse 12 partially echoes Micah 6:8 through “do justly” and “walk humbly,” while adding “do good” and “judge righteously.” Verse 28 parallels [[3 Nephi 9]]:18 in calling Christ the life and light of the world, and verses 29–30 closely recast [[John 1]]:11–12 as first-person speech.

The Hebrews and John parallels are especially strong; the Micah connection is more limited. These observations establish scriptural reuse or saturation but do not by themselves determine whether the wording reflects divine adaptation, prophetic scriptural fluency, nineteenth-century composition, editorial practice, or a combination.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Repeated imperatives create the chapter's practical rhythm: “give heed,” “keep,” “seek,” “wait,” “hold,” “appeal,” “cleave,” “study,” and “treasure up.” A notable tension organizes these commands: Hyrum is invited into an urgent harvest, yet repeatedly told not to hurry into public preaching. The urgency concerns discipleship and preparation as much as immediate speech.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How does the moral test of the Spirit in verse 12 qualify the experiential signs in verses 13–14? What is the difference between being “called of God” to reap in verse 4 and being “called to preach” in verse 15? Why might an urgent harvest still require Hyrum to wait? What does it mean in practice to obtain the word rather than merely possess or repeat its language?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->

For thematic study, trace how [[Faith]], [[Obedience]], [[Revelation]], and [[Scripture Study]] interact rather than treating any one of them as sufficient by itself.
