# D&C 88 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 88]] · My notes: [[D&C 88 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 88, known as the 'Olive Leaf,' is among the most doctrinally dense sections of the Doctrine and Covenants. It opens by identifying the promised Comforter as the Holy Spirit of promise and describing the [[Light of Christ]] that fills and empowers the sun, moon, stars, and earth (vv.3-13), moves through teachings on [[Resurrection]] and the three [[Kingdoms of Glory]] (vv.14-32), and describes how law governs, sanctifies, and orders 'all kingdoms' of space (vv.34-50). A parable of a lord visiting twelve field-laborers illustrates that each kingdom receives its 'hour' and 'season' (vv.51-61), followed by an invitation to seek God (vv.62-69) and commandments to organize, study, and warn the world in preparation for the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] (vv.70-86, 117-126). Embedded within these organizational instructions is a vivid sequence of angels, trumpets, and signs describing Christ's return and the [[Millennium]] (vv.87-116) -- verse 117 resumes the solemn-assembly command first given in verse 70, suggesting this apocalyptic material sits inside a larger literary bracket rather than standing apart from it, though that bracket does not by itself settle how the material was composed. A later addition (received a few days after the rest) prescribes the order and ordinances of the School of the Prophets, including a ritual salutation and the washing of feet, patterned after [[John 13]] (vv.127-141).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious thematic outline is:

1. **Verses 1–13:** the Comforter, eternal life, and the universal light of Christ.
2. **Verses 14–41:** embodied resurrection, the sanctification of the earth, kingdoms of glory, and law.
3. **Verses 42–61:** ordered heavenly motions and the parable of the lord's successive visits to his servants.
4. **Verses 62–86:** seeking God, sanctification, study, preparation, and missionary warning.
5. **Verses 87–116:** signs, trumpets, resurrection, judgment, the Millennium, and the final defeat of Satan.
6. **Verses 117–126:** establishment and ethical order of the house of God.
7. **Verses 127–141:** detailed order for the school of the prophets, its covenantal salutation, and washing of feet.

This outline describes shifts in subject matter; it should not be treated as proof of a particular compositional history.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Central doctrines include: (1) the [[Light of Christ]], described as present in and giving power to the sun, moon, stars, and earth, and proceeding 'from the presence of God to fill the immensity of space' (vv.6-13) -- language that plainly attributes cosmic and life-giving functions to this light, though the text does not define it in modern physical terms. (2) [[Resurrection]] as the redemption of an embodied 'soul,' defined as spirit and body reunited (vv.14-17); resurrected celestial beings explicitly receive 'the same body which was a natural body' (v.28), though the parallel language for terrestrial and telestial glory (vv.30-31) does not repeat this specific continuity claim. (3) Three [[Kingdoms of Glory]] -- celestial, terrestrial, telestial -- each tied to a law a person must be able to 'abide' (vv.21-24); verses 32-35 also link a lesser portion of glory to unwillingness to receive what was offered, and law is described as what 'preserves,' 'perfects,' and 'sanctifies' (v.34). This capacity/willingness emphasis should not be read as excluding the mercy, justice, and judgment language that sits alongside it in the same discourse (vv.35, 40). (4) The [[Millennium]], including Satan bound for a thousand years (v.110) and a final battle led by the archangel Michael (vv.111-115) -- the chapter does not itself identify Michael with Adam; that identification comes from elsewhere in the Doctrine and Covenants. (5) [[Education and Learning]] as commanded preparation for ministry: a broad curriculum spanning doctrine, history, nations, and world events (vv.77-80), and the charge to 'seek learning, even by study and also by faith' (v.118), tied to establishing 'a house... of learning' (v.119).

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter draws heavily on KJV idiom: v.63 combines the ask/seek/knock teaching of Matthew 7:7-8 with the 'draw near' language of James 4:8; v.67 restates Matthew 6:22's 'single eye' teaching; and vv.87-116 rework phrases from Isaiah 13, Isaiah 24, Joel 2, and especially Revelation 6, 8, 10, 17, and 20 (e.g., 'silence in heaven... half an hour,' 'time no longer,' Satan 'bound... a thousand years,' a church that 'sitteth upon many waters').

Two verbs also thread through the whole chapter and reward close attention. **Quicken** links several subjects: the light quickens understanding (v.11), divine power quickens all things (v.17), the earth is quickened after its death (v.26), resurrected persons are quickened by differing portions of glory (vv.28-32), and living Saints are quickened at Christ's coming (v.96) -- across these uses it denotes imparted life or capacity rather than mere survival. **Comprehend** carries a related ambiguity: Christ comprehends all things (v.6); creation displays God even when he is not comprehended (vv.47-49); the quickened will eventually comprehend God (v.49); and a body filled with light 'comprehendeth all things' (v.67). Readers should not force every occurrence into a single modern sense of the word.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 51-61 form a self-contained parable of a lord and twelve field-laborers, structurally reminiscent of but substantively different from the Parable of the Laborers in the Vineyard (Matthew 20:1-16): there is no wage dispute or complaint about equal payment, only sequential visitation 'in his hour... according to the decree which God hath made' (v.61).

The chapter repeatedly moves from cosmic order to human order: heavenly bodies move in appointed times and seasons (vv.42-45); the servants in the parable receive ordered visits in their times and seasons (vv.51-61); divine manifestation comes in God's own 'time' and 'way' (v.68); the work is hastened 'in its time' (v.73); and participants in the school of the prophets must speak and listen in turn (v.122). This recurrence gives the chapter's demand to organize 'a house of order' (v.119) a literary as well as doctrinal basis.

Verse 117 ('call your solemn assembly, as I have commanded you') explicitly resumes the command first given in verse 70, so the intervening seven-trumpet apocalyptic material (vv.87-116) sits inside a bracket formed by the organizational instructions -- a plausible literary structure, though it does not by itself settle how or when the two kinds of material were composed or combined. The shift from human testimony to the testimony of earthquakes, storms, seas, and angels (vv.81-92) -- the text itself calls these events 'testimony,' vv.89-90 -- should be read as apocalyptic imagery rather than assumed to supply a literal timetable.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What is the relationship between the 'light of Christ' described in vv.6-13 and the Holy Ghost introduced as 'another Comforter' in vv.3-4 -- are these the same influence described two ways, or distinct?
- How does defining the 'soul' as spirit and body together (v.15) shape the meaning of resurrection and salvation in this chapter?
- How do law, capacity, willingness, mercy, justice, and judgment relate to one another in vv.21-40 -- is degree of glory best read as a lawful consequence of what one has become, a sovereign decree (v.61), or both held together?
- Is the twelve-hour structure of the parable in vv.51-61 meant to be mapped onto specific groups (dispensations, tribes, kingdoms), or does the text intentionally leave this open?
- Why does the commanded curriculum in vv.77-80 pair gospel doctrine with history, nations, wars, and geography, and what work does the repeated qualifier 'expedient for you' (vv.78-80) do in bounding that scope?
- Which features of the trumpet and sign sequence in vv.87-116 are best read as symbolic apocalyptic imagery, and which does the text present as a literal sequence of events?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the account of differentiated glory with [[D&C 76]], paying particular attention to D&C 88's emphasis on law, capacity, and willingness. Compare D&C 88:103–106 with [[Revelation 14]] to identify both the borrowed imagery and its new placement within a larger trumpet sequence. Compare D&C 88:118–119 with [[D&C 109]], where the language of study, faith, and the house of God recurs. Finally, read D&C 88:139–141 alongside [[John 13]] because the chapter itself names John's account as the pattern for washing feet.
