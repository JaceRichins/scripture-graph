# D&C 14 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 14]] · My notes: [[D&C 14 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 14 pairs a general invitation to labor in God's harvest with a specific commission to David Whitmer. Its promises are consistently conditional: the laborer must reap with his might (vv.3–4), the petitioner must ask and knock in faith (v.5), the disciple must keep the commandments and endure to the end (v.7), and David must act and remain faithful (v.11). The chapter closes with Christ identifying himself as Creator (v.9) and declaring that the fulness of the gospel will move from the Gentiles to the house of Israel (v.10). Key themes include [[Missionary Work]], [[Zion]], [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]], and [[Eternal Life]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Verses 1–2:** Announcement of the coming work and a command to heed God's living, penetrating word.
2. **Verses 3–5:** The harvest invitation, the divine calling of reapers, and the promises attached to asking and knocking.
3. **Verses 6–8:** Commands to establish [[Zion]] and obey, followed by the promises of [[Eternal Life]] and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]].
4. **Verses 9–10:** The speaker identifies himself as Jesus Christ, Creator and light, then announces the gospel's movement from the Gentiles to the house of Israel.
5. **Verse 11:** The commission narrows to a personal call to David to assist.

Verses 1–5 closely parallel the opening of [[D&C 6]] and [[D&C 12]] (verified 7- and 5-verse overlaps); [[D&C 11]] opens with similar language too, though the overlap index unexpectedly shows no verified match there.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The section names eternal life as 'the greatest of all the gifts of God' (v.7), conditioned on keeping the commandments and [[Endurance]] to the end. Verse 8 ties reception of the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] to faith-filled [[Prayer]] in Christ's name, with the stated purposes of utterance, standing as a witness, and declaring [[Repentance]]. Christ identifies himself as the one who created the heavens and the earth (v.9), touching [[Creation]]. Verse 10 states that the fulness of the gospel will move 'from the Gentiles unto the house of Israel' — a claim about [[Jews and Gentiles]] and the direction of gospel transmission, though the verse does not itself describe the mechanism or timing usually associated with the [[Gathering of Israel]]. These statements are brief and should not be expanded into a complete theology of salvation or spiritual gifts without comparison to other passages.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
'Quick' in verse 2 carries the older sense of living or alive; paired with 'powerful' and the two-edged-sword image, it closely echoes [[Hebrews 4:12]]'s description of the word of God as sharper than any two-edged sword, dividing joints and marrow. The harvest language of verse 3 ('white already to harvest') echoes John 4:35, and the ask-and-knock promise of verse 5 echoes Matthew 7:7. These correspondences show the section is framed in dense, recognizable King James biblical diction; they do not by themselves establish anything about the revelation's origin or process of composition.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
D&C 14 belongs to a group of short 1829 revelations sharing large blocks of nearly identical wording: [[D&C 6]] (to Oliver Cowdery) and [[D&C 12]] (to Joseph Knight Sr.) both open with the same 'great and marvelous work' announcement, the same two-edged-sword description of God's word, and the same harvest/sickle imagery found in D&C 14:1–5, confirmed by verified text overlap (7 shared verses with D&C 6, 5 with D&C 12). [[D&C 11]] (to Hyrum Smith) opens with closely similar language too, though the deterministic overlap index unexpectedly reports no matching verses there — an inconsistency worth rechecking rather than a settled result. Whether the repetition reflects a reusable revelatory template, adaptation to each recipient, or editorial standardization remains an open interpretive question; the textual overlap itself is well established.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> The first six verses of D&C 14 closely track the corresponding sequence in D&C 6: announcement of a marvelous work, the penetrating word, a harvest call, divine calling of reapers, asking and knocking, and establishing Zion. This is strong evidence of deliberate formulaic continuity within these revelations.

> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · verified against KJV text by critique · [[Literary Intertextuality in D&C 14|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal Thematic Anticipation *(tentative)*
> The wording of D&C 14:8 (standing as 'a witness of the things... hear and see') plausibly foreshadows David Whitmer's historically documented role as one of the Three Witnesses of the Book of Mormon, though the verse itself does not name that specific event.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does 'called of God' in verse 4 describe a general divine invitation to serve, a formal calling, or both? The verse does not specify an office.
- How are the 'greatest' gift of eternal life (v.7) and the promise of the Holy Ghost (v.8) related within the chapter's sequence?
- What process is envisioned by bringing the fulness of the gospel 'from the Gentiles unto the house of Israel' (v.10)? The verse gives a direction but not a mechanism.
- What specifically was David expected to 'hear and see' before standing as a witness (v.8)? The verse does not name it, though David Whitmer would later serve as one of the Three Witnesses to the Book of Mormon — a plausible but not textually stated connection.
- Why does the deterministic overlap index show no verified overlap between D&C 14 and D&C 11 despite their closely similar opening language? This is likely a matching artifact worth checking mechanically.
