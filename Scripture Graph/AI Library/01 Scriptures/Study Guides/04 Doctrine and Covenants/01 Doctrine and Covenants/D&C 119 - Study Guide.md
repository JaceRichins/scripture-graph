# D&C 119 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 119]] · My notes: [[D&C 119 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 119 presents [[Tithing]] in a clear sequence: surplus property is first placed in the hands of the bishop (vv.1-2), for building the Lord's house, laying [[Zion]]'s foundation, supporting the priesthood, and paying the Presidency's debts. Verse 3 calls this initial requirement only 'the beginning of the tithing'; verse 4 then institutes the enduring law itself — one-tenth of annual 'interest,' called 'a standing law unto them forever.' The chapter ties this law directly to Zion: those gathering there must observe it to be 'found worthy to abide' (v.5), and the land's very status as Zion depends on the Saints keeping the law and sanctifying the land (v.6). Verse 7 extends the arrangement as 'an ensample unto all the stakes of Zion.'
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Initial requirement and purposes (vv.1-3):** surplus property delivered to the bishop for the Lord's house, Zion's foundation, the priesthood, and Church Presidency debts; called 'the beginning of the tithing.'
2. **Continuing law (v.4):** those already tithed pay one-tenth of their interest annually, 'a standing law unto them forever.'
3. **Zion and accountability (vv.5-6):** observance tied to worthiness to abide in Zion and to sanctifying the land.
4. **Wider application (v.7):** the arrangement is declared 'an ensample unto all the stakes of Zion.'

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter links [[Tithing]] with [[Zion]], [[Priesthood]], and [[Obedience]]: material contributions serve named sacred and communal ends, not just private devotion. The strongest theological point in vv.5-6 is covenant accountability — a people cannot claim Zion merely by occupying a place while refusing the law meant to sanctify it. This does not mean payment alone produces holiness; the text speaks of observing the law and keeping it holy. The initial surplus-property donation is often read alongside the earlier, fuller law of [[Consecration]], though the chapter itself does not use that word or explicitly rank the two laws.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Two phrases require care. 'Surplus property' (vv.1,5) names the initial one-time-style requirement, while 'one-tenth of all their interest annually' (v.4) names the distinct ongoing requirement — treating the two as interchangeable erases the chapter's own sequence. The chapter does not define 'interest'; equating it with any specific modern accounting category (gross vs. net income) requires evidence outside this text, and it has historically been left to individual judgment. 'Ensample' (v.7), an older form of 'example,' signals the arrangement is meant as a pattern for other stakes, without specifying that every administrative detail must transfer unchanged.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The revelation uses a repeated 'verily' formula (vv.1,5,6) and repeats 'surplus propert(y/ies)' in vv.1 and 5. These repetitions are worth noting, but such diction is common throughout the Doctrine and Covenants, and no clear markers establish a deliberate three-part architecture or intentional bracketing — the observation is offered as a possible stylistic feature rather than an established literary structure.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
What distinction does the text draw between the initial surplus-property transfer and the ongoing one-tenth-of-interest payment? What would count as 'interest' today, and why might that be left undefined rather than specified? How does the one-time surplus donation relate to the fuller law of consecration taught elsewhere — substitute, preparatory step, or distinct law? In vv.5-6, is the emphasis individual worthiness, communal order, sanctification of place, or some combination? Why does the text tie Zion's very status as Zion (not just individual standing) to a financial law?
