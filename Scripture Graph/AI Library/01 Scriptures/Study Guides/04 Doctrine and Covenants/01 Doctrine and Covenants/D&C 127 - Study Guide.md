# D&C 127 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 127]] · My notes: [[D&C 127 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–4, Joseph Smith glories in persecution and tribulation; 5–12, Records must be kept relative to baptisms for the dead.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Temporary withdrawal and continuing administration (v. 1):** personal safety requires absence, but agents and clerks remain responsible for business affairs.

2. **Tribulation, deliverance, and exhortation (vv. 2–4):** the speaker interprets a lifetime of persecution through the pattern of [[Adversity]] and commands, with 'verily thus saith the Lord,' that [[Temples|temple work]] continue.

3. **Recording baptisms for the dead (vv. 5–9):** a formal revelatory block marked by repeated 'let...' imperatives and the refrain 'saith the Lord of Hosts' (vv. 4, 6, 8, 9), specifying that an eyewitness recorder document each baptism and that records be archived in the temple.

4. **Promise of further instruction and letter closing (vv. 10–12):** public teaching is not presently possible, so further instruction on baptism for the dead will follow by mail — a promise [[D&C 128]] fulfills. The letter ends with a blessing and signature.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Baptism for the dead calls for witnessed, permanent records.** Vv. 6–9 require an eyewitness recorder who personally hears the baptism, with records kept in order and archived in the temple 'from generation to generation' (v. 9). The chapter presents recordkeeping as consequential — tied to what is 'recorded in heaven' (v. 7) — without spelling out every condition under which that correspondence operates.
- **Binding and loosing.** V. 7's language ('whatsoever you bind on earth, may be bound in heaven') echoes the sealing terminology Jesus gives Peter in Matthew 16:19; here it is applied to ordinance recordkeeping. Whether this is a deliberate allusion or a recurring revelatory idiom is not settled by the text alone.
- **Ongoing priesthood restoration.** V. 8's promise to 'restore many things to the earth, pertaining to the priesthood' is forward-looking; the chapter does not specify what those things are, though [[D&C 128]] continues the subject.
- **Theology of adversity.** Vv. 1–3 present persecution as something Joseph Smith cannot fully explain ('for what cause it seems mysterious... Judge ye for yourselves') yet trusts is within God's purposes, paired with confidence in deliverance and eventual justice upon oppressors (v. 3).

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verse 2's statement about being ordained 'from before the foundation of the world' is grammatically hedged: the speaker offers it as one possible explanation, adds 'for some good end, or bad,' invites the audience to judge, and then defers to what 'God knoweth.' Reading this sentence as a straightforward, complete doctrinal claim about premortal identity would flatten that uncertainty.

'Recorded in heaven' and 'bind on earth' in verse 7 give recordkeeping theological weight, asserting correspondence between earthly and heavenly action without specifying whether correct documentation alone is sufficient or one element among authority, ordinance, witness, and divine acceptance.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The 'let X... saith the Lord of Hosts' refrain in vv. 4–9 gives the revelation portion a formal, proclamation-like cadence, distinct from the intimate first-person voice of vv. 1–3 and 10–12 — a shift the letter format accommodates without dramatic transition, since v. 4 and v. 5 both open with 'And again.'

Three biblical allusions anchor Joseph Smith's self-understanding to earlier scripture: he feels 'like Paul' in glorying in tribulation (v. 2), echoing Paul's own language in Romans 5:3; verse 4's promise of heavenly reward for persecution 'as persecuted they the prophets' parallels the beatitude of Matthew 5:12; and verse 11 explicitly attributes 'the prince of this world cometh, but he hath nothing in me' to 'the Savior,' closely reproducing (with minor wording variants) John 14:30. Only the last of these is flagged by the text itself as a citation; the others are unattributed echoes.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> [!evidence] Textual/Form Inference *(tentative)*

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What specific circumstance, if any, prompted the eyewitness-recorder requirement in vv. 6–7 — was there a known dispute over the validity of earlier baptisms for the dead, or is this proactive institution-building?
- Is the 'bind on earth... bound in heaven' language in v. 7 best read as an intentional echo of Matthew 16:19, or as a recurring idiom in Joseph Smith's revelations more broadly?
- What did Joseph Smith mean by the 'many things... pertaining to the priesthood' still to be restored (v. 8), and how much of that promise is addressed in [[D&C 128]] versus later Nauvoo-era developments?
- Why does the recorder need to both see and hear each baptism (v. 6) rather than one or the other?
- Does verse 2's language about premortal ordination function as doctrine, rhetorical humility, or some combination — and how should readers hold that uncertainty?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
