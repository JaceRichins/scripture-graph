# D&C 32 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 32]] · My notes: [[D&C 32 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 32 adds Parley P. Pratt and Ziba Peterson to the mission already assigned to Oliver Cowdery and Peter Whitmer, Jun.: together they are sent 'into the wilderness among the Lamanites' (vv. 1–3). Parley's charge joins [[Missionary Work]] with personal discipleship — he is to declare the gospel, learn of the Lord, and be meek and lowly of heart, cultivating [[Humility]] (v. 1). The Lord promises to go with the missionaries and be 'their advocate with the Father,' adding that 'nothing shall prevail against them' (v. 3). He then instructs them to give heed to what is written, pretend to no other revelation, pray always for understanding, and not trifle with these words (vv. 4–5).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter moves through three compact stages:

1. **Personal preparation (verse 1):** Parley is called to preach, learn, and become meek and lowly.
2. **Collective assignment and assurance (verses 2–3):** Parley and Ziba join Oliver and Peter for the mission among the Lamanites; divine presence, advocacy, and protection are promised.
3. **Discipline in receiving instruction (verses 4–5):** the missionaries must heed what is written, avoid unauthorized claims to revelation, pray for understanding, and not trifle with the words given.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents [[Missionary Work]] as dependent on more than public speech. The messenger must learn and develop [[Humility]] (verse 1), labor with companions (verses 2–3), and combine [[Scripture Study]] with [[Prayer]] (verse 4). The assurance that the speaker is an advocate with the Father also connects the mission to the mediating role of [[Jesus Christ (Gospel Topic)]], although the verse does not explain that doctrine in detail.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The pronouns in verse 4 require caution. “That which is written” and “the same” clearly identify a written object whose meaning is to be unfolded, but the excerpt does not name that object explicitly. The official chapter heading understands the command as referring to the scriptures. “Pretend to no other revelation” most naturally warns against claiming revelation beyond what was authorized, but its precise scope may be limited to this mission.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The revelation follows a compact commissioning pattern: naming those called, stating the assignment, promising divine presence, and closing with conditioned instruction and blessing. Its brevity is consistent with the concise, direct style found in many of the shorter early sections of the Doctrine and Covenants.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How does the pairing of 'declare my gospel' and 'learn of me' (v. 1) frame the relationship between teaching others and one's own ongoing discipleship?
- What is the most likely referent of 'that which is written' and 'the same' in verse 4, and what does that imply about the scope of 'pretend to no other revelation'?
- Is that instruction a mission-specific safeguard against unauthorized claims, or a broader principle about revelatory authority in the early Restoration?
- Does 'nothing shall prevail against them' (v. 3) promise freedom from opposition, or ultimate divine support despite it?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare with [[D&C 28]] (Oliver Cowdery's earlier call to preach to the Lamanites) and [[D&C 30]] (Peter Whitmer, Jun.'s companion call), which together with D&C 32 show how this mission's membership was assembled across several revelations. The description of the Lord as advocate with the Father (v. 3) can also be compared with D&C 45, which develops that same title more fully.
