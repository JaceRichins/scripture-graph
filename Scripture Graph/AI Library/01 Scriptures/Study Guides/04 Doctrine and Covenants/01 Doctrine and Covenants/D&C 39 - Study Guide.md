# D&C 39 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 39]] · My notes: [[D&C 39 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 39 is a January 1831 revelation given at Fayette, New York, first addressed broadly to all who will hear (vv.1-6) and then personally to a man named James, generally identified with James Covill/Covel (vv.7-24). It opens with Jesus Christ identifying himself as "the Great I AM" (v.1) and defines "my gospel" as repentance, baptism by water, and baptism of fire and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] (v.6). Christ acknowledges James's heart is right "at this time" (v.8) despite past rejection (v.9), and conditionally calls him to repent, be baptized, and preach — directing him not east but to "the Ohio" (v.14), where the Saints were gathering. The promises in this chapter are consistently conditional ("if," "inasmuch as"), and [[D&C 40]] records that James did not, in fact, fulfill this call. See [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]], [[Missionary Work]], [[Gathering of Israel]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The revelation falls into four movements: (1) vv.1-6 — Christ identifies himself and defines his gospel, in language closely echoing John 1's prologue; (2) vv.7-13 — a conditional call to James, acknowledging his present readiness and past rejection, and inviting baptism and future ministry; (3) vv.14-18 — the specific call to Ohio, a promised blessing on those who gather there, and a conditional prophecy that judgment will be stayed "inasmuch as" the people repent; (4) vv.19-24 — general missionary instruction (proclamation, baptism, laying on of hands) framed by the certain but undated nearness of Christ's coming. The repeated phrase "receiveth me" in verses 5 and 22 loosely brackets this material, though this is a suggestive echo rather than a demonstrated formal inclusio.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verse 6 gives one of the more compact scriptural definitions of the [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]]: [[Repentance]], [[Baptism]] by water, and then "the baptism of fire and the Holy Ghost, even the Comforter." Verse 23 pairs water baptism with the laying on of hands for the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] as sequential [[Ordinances]]; the text does not itself explain how this relates to the "baptism of fire" language of verse 6. Receiving the gospel is framed relationally, as receiving Christ himself (vv.5, 22), not merely accepting information. Many of the chapter's promises are conditional rather than unconditional: James's heart is right "at this time" (v.8); his greater work follows "if" he acts (v.11); and national judgment is stayed only "inasmuch as" people repent, receive the gospel, and become sanctified (v.18). Verses 19-21 tie the missionary cry to the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]], affirming its certainty while explicitly denying knowledge of its timing.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verses 2-4 closely follow the sequence and diction of John 1:5, 11-12 — light shining in darkness, rejection by Christ's own, and power given to those who receive him — recast as "power to become my sons" rather than John's "sons of God." This is best read as evidence of deliberate reuse of familiar biblical language rather than proof of any particular mode of composition. Verse 6's gospel-defining clause overlaps with wording used in [[D&C 33]] and other sections from this period (confirmed at the verse-overlap level), consistent with a recurring formulaic self-description of the gospel in these early revelations, though whether that reflects fixed revealed wording or reused phrasing across dictation sessions cannot be settled from the text alone.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter's repeated "receiveth me" formula (vv.5, 22) and the pairing of Christ's opening eternal self-description (v.1) with his closing "I come quickly" (v.24) are suggestive framing devices, but should be read as thematic echoes rather than a rigorously demonstrated inclusio or structural frame — the intervening material broadens well beyond a tight bracket, addressing James individually and then a wider missionary audience.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
What work is done by the qualification "at this time" in the assessment of James's heart (v.8)? How should "baptism of fire and the Holy Ghost" (v.6) relate to the laying on of hands for the gift of the Holy Ghost (v.23)? Does "stay mine hand in judgment" (v.18) mean cancellation, delay, or mitigation of judgment? How does the sequel in [[D&C 40]] — where James does not fulfill this call — affect how conditional promises like this one should be read, without treating them as insincere? Is the recurring "this is my gospel" wording across nearby sections fixed revealed language or a phrase Joseph Smith reused across closely spaced dictations?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
[[D&C 40]] records the Lord's response after James apparently did not fulfill this call, and the two sections read together illustrate agency alongside conditional, unfulfilled prophecy. Compare the gospel-defining formula here with [[D&C 33]] and [[2 Nephi 31]] for further treatment of the [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]].
