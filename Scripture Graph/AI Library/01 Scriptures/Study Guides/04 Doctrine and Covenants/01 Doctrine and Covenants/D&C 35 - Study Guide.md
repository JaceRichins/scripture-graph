# D&C 35 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 35]] · My notes: [[D&C 35 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 35 is a revelation given through Joseph Smith to Joseph and Sidney Rigdon, traditionally dated to December 1830 in New York, shortly after Rigdon and Edward Partridge arrived from Ohio. It opens with Christ's identity and saving work (vv1-2), turns to Sidney's preparation and the relationship between [[Baptism]] and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] (vv3-6), then joins conditional promises of [[Faith]]-based [[Miracles]], judgment on Babylon, and the weak and despised called to missionary labor with the fig-tree sign of Christ's coming (vv7-16). The final section assigns Sidney a supporting role alongside Joseph — writing, preaching, and corroborating revealed words — and closes with covenantal promises to Israel and Zion and expectation of the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] (vv17-27).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious outline is:

- Verses 1–2: the speaker's identity, crucifixion, belief, and unity.
- Verses 3–6: Sidney's preparation and the relationship between [[Baptism]] and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]].
- Verses 7–16: the coming work, faith-dependent signs, judgment on Babylon, weak instruments, and the fig-tree image.
- Verses 17–23: Joseph's conditional commission and Sidney's duties as supporter, writer, companion, and preacher.
- Verses 24–27: covenant faithfulness, Zion and Israel, approaching redemption, and the coming of Christ.

This outline describes shifts in subject matter; it does not imply a formally marked literary division.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter associates believing in Christ with becoming sons of God and entering a unity patterned after the unity of the Son and Father (verse 2). It treats baptism by water and reception of the Holy Ghost by laying on of hands as distinguishable but related acts (verses 5–6). Its promises of [[Miracles]] are framed by belief, asking in Christ's name, and [[Faith]] rather than presented as unconditional guarantees (verses 8–11). Prophetic service is likewise expressed conditionally: Joseph must abide in Christ, and Sidney is told to watch over him so that his faith does not fail (verses 18–19).
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter makes extensive use of biblical language. Babylon and the wine of her fornication recall [[Revelation 14]]; the fig tree and approaching summer recall [[Matthew 24]]; and 'Fear not, little flock' recalls [[Luke 12]]. The call to 'the weak things of the world, those who are unlearned and despised' (v13) also echoes Paul's language in 1 Corinthians 1:27-28 about God choosing the weak and despised to confound the strong. Sidney is compared to 'John' as one sent to 'prepare the way' before Christ and before Elijah (v4) — commonly read as John the Baptist, though the verse itself does not name him. These parallels establish scriptural reuse or resonance; they do not by themselves determine whether the language arose through direct quotation, remembered biblical idiom, or revelatory adaptation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Textual Evidence
> Within the chapter, Joseph's commission concerning revealed mysteries is explicitly conditional upon his abiding in Christ, with the stated possibility that another could be planted in his stead.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · accepted within LDS exegesis; not a broader academic consensus · [[Internal Textual Evidence in D&C 35|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual/Theological Evidence *(tentative)*
> The conditional clause 'if he abide in me, and if not, another will I plant in his stead' (D&C 35:18) is evidence that this early revelation frames Joseph Smith's prophetic standing as contingent on his personal faithfulness rather than unconditionally fixed, which is relevant to broader discussions of conditionality and human agency in Restoration prophetic claims.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · redundant with a stronger B claim

> [!evidence] Internal Procedural Description *(tentative)*
> Verses 20 and 23 provide internal evidence for differentiated roles: Joseph is associated with receiving or prophesying words, while Sidney is assigned writing, preaching, and scriptural corroboration.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · overstates stability of the division

> [!evidence] Historical Background *(tentative)*
> D&C 35:5's description of Sidney Rigdon baptizing 'unto repentance' without conferring the Holy Ghost corresponds to his documented pre-1830 ministry within the Reformed Baptist / 'Disciples of Christ' restorationist movement associated with Alexander Campbell, which practiced baptism by immersion for remission of sins but did not teach a separate priesthood ordinance for receiving the Holy Ghost.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · plausible historical background, citation underspecified

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Who is the 'John' of verse 4, and what precise relationship between Sidney, John, and Elijah is intended?
- Is the reference to 'Elijah which should come' (v4) a specific prediction later understood as fulfilled in [[D&C 110]], or a general expectation drawn from Malachi's promise, read back into the text after 1836?
- What work is designated by 'the scriptures' in verse 20? The chapter itself does not name a particular document or project.
- Are the military images in verses 13-14 metaphorical descriptions of spiritual conflict, predictions of physical protection, or a mixture of both?
- How should the severe statement in verse 12 be read in relation to the chapter's invitation to the poor, meek, weak, and despised?
- What does it mean in practice for earlier prophets to 'prove' newly given words in verse 23?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
