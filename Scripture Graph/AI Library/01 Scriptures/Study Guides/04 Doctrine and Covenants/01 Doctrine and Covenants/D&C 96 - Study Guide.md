# D&C 96 — Study Guide

## Overview

<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter falls into two compact units:

1. **The place and its purposes (vv. 1–5):** strengthen the stake, assign stewardship to Newel K. Whitney, divide the land in council, benefit the order, and send forth the word.
2. **John Johnson and his duties (vv. 6–9):** acknowledge his offering and prayers, give a conditional promise, identify his lineage, admit him to the order, and assign him work on the house.

Both units end with “Even so. Amen.” More importantly, both culminate in the purpose of bringing forth the word to the children of men (vv. 4–5, 8).

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Stewardship and order:** Verses 2–4 connect property, inheritances, council deliberation, a holy house, and benefit to the Lord's order. These features fit a [[Consecration]] context, although the chapter does not itself define the order or identify it by a formal historical name.
- **Conditional promise:** John Johnson's offering is accepted and his prayers are heard, yet the promise of [[Eternal Life]] remains expressly conditioned on keeping the commandments from that point forward (v. 6). Assurance and continuing [[Obedience]] appear together.
- **Lineage and responsibility:** Verse 7 identifies Johnson as a descendant of Joseph and a participant in promises made to his fathers. This plausibly evokes ancestral covenant identity, but the chapter does not explicitly name the [[Abrahamic Covenant]] or establish lineage as a prerequisite for his appointment. Verses 8–9 then associate his blessing with present [[Service]].

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The recurring words “wisdom” and “expedient” frame administrative instructions as matters of divinely directed practical judgment (vv. 1, 3, 5–6, 8). The phrase “bringing forth my word unto the children of men” links the use of property in verses 4–5 with Johnson's membership in the order in verse 8.

“Subduing the hearts” (v. 5) should be read cautiously. The immediate agent is the word going forth, so inward persuasion or transformation fits the context better than physical coercion; the verse itself, however, does not explain the precise psychological or spiritual process.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The parallel endings at verses 5 and 9 mark two units, while repeated purpose language ties them together. The movement in each unit is from authorization to administration to mission: the place is managed so the word may go forth, and Johnson joins the order so he may assist that same work. This is a strong thematic pattern, though it need not imply a formal poetic structure.
<!-- SG:END literary -->
