# D&C 62 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 62]] · My notes: [[D&C 62 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–3, Testimonies are recorded in heaven; 4–9, The elders are to travel and preach according to judgment and as directed by the Spirit.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter has three compact movements:

1. **Assurance and unfinished responsibility (vv. 1–3):** Christ identifies himself as advocate, recognizes human weakness, and blesses the elders while declaring that their mission is not complete.
2. **Gathering, worship, and renewed witness (vv. 4–6):** They are to assemble in Zion, rejoice, offer a sacrament, and return to proclaim glad tidings.
3. **Discretion and promise (vv. 7–9):** Choices about transportation and other practical matters are left to thankful judgment and the Spirit, while faithfulness remains required.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter joins divine help with continuing duty. Christ's knowledge of weakness and ability to succor the tempted provide a basis for trust in [[Jesus Christ (Gospel Topic)]], but that reassurance does not cancel the elders' unfinished assignment. Verse 3 associates [[Testimony]], angelic rejoicing, and forgiveness without explaining a causal relationship among them. Verses 5 and 8 present a restrained model of [[Agency]]: some means may be chosen by human judgment under spiritual direction, while fidelity to the mission remains fixed.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The phrase "it mattereth not unto me" is narrowly embedded in the choice between returning together or two by two. It should not be generalized into a claim that God is indifferent to all choices. Similarly, "according to judgment and the directions of the Spirit" holds practical reasoning and spiritual guidance together rather than explicitly opposing them.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
D&C 62:1 describes Christ as one who 'knoweth the weakness of man and how to succor them who are tempted' — language that echoes Hebrews 2:18's teaching about Christ's ability to help those who are tempted, and parallels Alma 7:12, where Christ is said to know 'according to the flesh how to succor his people according to their infirmities.' All three passages tie Christ's own experience of weakness or suffering to his capacity to help those who are tempted or afflicted, though whether D&C 62 deliberately alludes to either passage or simply shares a common scriptural idiom cannot be determined from the text alone.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does it mean for a testimony to be 'recorded in heaven' (v. 3) — a literal record, legal imagery, or an assurance that faithful witness is known to God?
- Why might the Lord leave some decisions (traveling together or two by two, v. 5; riding horses or mules, v. 7) to the elders' own judgment rather than specifying them, and what does this suggest about the scope of [[Agency]] within obedience?
- How does naming Christ as 'your advocate' who 'knoweth the weakness of man' (v. 1) shape the tone of the instructions that follow?
- How narrowly should 'these things' in verse 8 be read — the immediately preceding travel decisions, or the whole remaining mission?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
