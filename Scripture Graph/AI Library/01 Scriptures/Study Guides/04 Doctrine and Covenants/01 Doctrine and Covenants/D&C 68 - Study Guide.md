# D&C 68 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 68]] · My notes: [[D&C 68 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
[[D&C 68]] contains four connected instruction blocks: missionary proclamation and Spirit-moved speech (vv. 1–12), appointment and accountability of bishops (vv. 13–24), parental obligations toward children (vv. 25–28), and communal correction in [[Zion]] (vv. 29–35). The opening commission expands from named elders to a broader missionary ensample and includes witness of Christ, baptism, signs accompanying belief, signs of the Son of Man's coming, and a qualified promise of sealing power. Across the chapter, authority is repeatedly joined to conditions: speakers must be moved upon by the Holy Ghost, bishops must be worthy and properly ordained, condemnation requires unimpeachable testimony, and parents and laborers are accountable for neglected duties.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Mission and inspired speech (vv. 1–12):** a commission initially addressed to named elders becomes an ensample for other ordained missionaries.

2. **Bishops, lineage, and adjudication (vv. 13–24):** hereditary right, delegated authority, institutional appointment, evidentiary procedure, repentance, and forgiveness are distinguished.

3. **Formation of children (vv. 25–28):** parents must teach the first principles and cultivate prayer and upright conduct; baptism and laying on of hands occur at age eight.

4. **Covenant life in Zion (vv. 29–35):** Sabbath observance, faithful labor, opposition to idleness and greed, regular prayer, and transmission of the commands receive communal emphasis.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
**Spirit-moved speech.** Verses 3–4 call qualifying speech scripture and the will, mind, word, voice, and power of God. The repeated condition—being moved upon by the Holy Ghost—supports confidence in genuine inspiration without making every statement by an ordained speaker scripture or providing an objective test for identifying every qualifying utterance.

**Priesthood and the bishopric.** Verses 14–21 combine hereditary claim with institutional authorization. A firstborn descendant of Aaron has a stated legal right, but must still be designated, found worthy, anointed, and ordained. When no qualified descendant can be found, a properly appointed high priest may officiate. Lineage is therefore not self-executing authority.

**Parents and children.** Verses 25–28 require parents in Zion or its organized stakes to teach repentance, faith in Christ, [[Baptism]], and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]], as well as [[Prayer]] and upright living. Children are to be baptized and receive laying on of hands at age eight. The chapter states this jurisdiction but does not by itself settle how broadly every provision was intended to apply beyond it.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The phrase “as they are moved upon by the Holy Ghost” appears in consecutive verses and controls the meaning of the declaration that follows. Verse 4 then uses a six-part crescendo: Spirit-moved speech is called scripture, the Lord's will, mind, word, voice, and power unto salvation. The rhetoric maximizes the authority of genuinely inspired speech while the repeated condition limits the category.

The words “legal right,” “keys or authority,” “designated,” “found worthy,” and “legally authorized” give verses 15–21 a juridical register. Verses 22–24 continue that register through trial, testimony, guilt, condemnation, repentance, and forgiveness.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The missionary commission draws conspicuously on [[Matthew 28]] and [[Mark 16]]. D&C 68:8 combines worldwide preaching language with Matthew's baptismal formula, while verse 9 closely follows Mark's sequence of belief, baptism, salvation, and condemnation. Verse 10 is better described as an explicit allusion—“even as it is written”—to the signs tradition than as another near quotation. These correspondences strongly support biblical reuse or restatement, but they do not determine whether that reuse arose through revelation, ordinary scriptural idiom, or both.

Verse 4 uses cumulative parallelism: Spirit-moved speech is described as scripture, the Lord's will, mind, word, voice, and power unto salvation. The chapter then shifts from missionary commission to juridical and communal instruction. Its closing language, “Alpha and Omega” and “I come quickly,” parallels [[Revelation 22]], although these expressions are recurring Doctrine and Covenants idiom and therefore have limited chapter-specific significance.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary Intertextuality
> The dense verbal and thematic correspondence between D&C 68:8–10 and the New Testament Great Commission strongly supports conscious reuse or restatement of biblical commissioning language.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · Strong evidence of biblical reuse; mechanism unresolved · [[Literary Intertextuality in D&C 68|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual Qualification
> The chapter makes being moved upon by the Holy Ghost the stated condition under which an elder's speech is called scripture and the word or voice of the Lord. This is strong internal evidence against reading the passage as granting automatic scriptural status to every statement by an ordained speaker.
> — strength 1.0 · confidence 1.0 · Strong textual evidence with interpretation properly bounded · [[Textual Qualification in D&C 68|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual Scope *(tentative)*
> The age-eight baptism law is textually addressed to 'the inhabitants of Zion, or in any of her stakes which are organized' (v26) rather than framed as a universal commandment to the whole Church, which complicates treating this passage alone as establishing a universal requirement without reference to other texts or later practice.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.7 · Textual scope observed; implications disputed

> [!evidence] Doctrinal-Authority Discourse *(tentative)*
> D&C 68:4 is frequently invoked in later LDS discourse on when a leader's statements should be treated as authoritative doctrine, but the verse's own condition ('when moved upon by the Holy Ghost') leaves the actual test internal/discernment-based rather than specifying an objective marker, so it does not by itself resolve which specific statements qualify.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · Textual limitation sound; reception-history claim unverified

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How can a community responsibly discern whether speech satisfies the condition “moved upon by the Holy Ghost” without treating subjective confidence as proof?

Why does the text distinguish a hereditary claim to anointing from present authorization to officiate?

What safeguards and limitations are created by requiring unimpeachable testimony before condemning a bishop?

Why are prayer, upright conduct, Sabbath observance, labor, and resistance to greed presented alongside ordinances as essential features of Zion?

Does the jurisdictional wording about Zion and its organized stakes limit verses 25–33, or does it state a local form of principles intended more broadly?
