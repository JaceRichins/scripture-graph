# D&C 83 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 83]] · My notes: [[D&C 83 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 83 supplements existing church law concerning women and children who have lost husbands or fathers. It recognizes a wife's claim on her husband for maintenance, a child's claim on parents for maintenance until coming of age, and a later claim on the church storehouse when parents cannot provide an inheritance. The storehouse is maintained through church consecrations and provides for widows, orphans, and the poor. Repeated use of “claim” presents this care as an acknowledged obligation within the revealed order, although the chapter does not define its civil enforceability or complete administration.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Verse 1** — Introduces additional church law concerning women and children who have lost husbands or fathers.
2. **Verses 2–3** — Addresses women's maintenance claims, church fellowship, and the right to remain upon inheritances under the laws of the land.
3. **Verses 4–5** — Gives children a claim on parents for maintenance until they are of age, followed by a conditional claim on the church storehouse when parents cannot provide inheritances.
4. **Verse 6** — Identifies consecrations as the means of maintaining the storehouse and names widows, orphans, and the poor as beneficiaries.

The progression is a straightforward legal-administrative sequence; no elaborate literary pattern is necessary to explain it.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter connects [[Family]] responsibility with organized care through [[Consecration]]. Husbands and parents have stated support obligations, while the church storehouse also has defined responsibilities toward vulnerable people. For children, recourse to the storehouse is expressly conditioned on parents lacking means to provide inheritances; verse 6 does not separately state that condition for every widow, orphan, or poor person. Verse 3 also distinguishes loss of church fellowship from the specific ability to remain upon an inheritance according to the laws of the land. It should not be expanded into a complete theory of church discipline or civil property rights.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The word "claim" occurs in verses 2, 4, and 5. Its repetition is significant because the beneficiaries are not merely portrayed as possible recipients of charity; the text recognizes duties owed to them. "Maintenance" describes immediate support, while "inheritances" suggests a more durable provision. The exact economic scope of either term is not defined in this section.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The word “claim” appears in verses 2, 4, and 5, giving the passage a legal-administrative register and emphasizing obligations owed to the named recipients. The chapter moves from particular household relationships to the storehouse's wider provision for widows, orphans, and the poor. This progression is plausible as deliberate organization, but the text does not require a technical rhetorical formula or chiastic design. [[D&C 42]] supplies close storehouse context; [[James 1]] and [[1 Timothy 5]] provide thematic and structural biblical comparisons without establishing dependence or identical policy.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare D&C 83:5–6 with D&C 42:30–34 for the relationship among consecrated property, the storehouse, and care for the poor. D&C 104:15–18 offers broader teaching about abundance and responsibility for those in need, though it is less specific to storehouse administration. James 1:27 parallels the concern for widows and the fatherless. First Timothy 5:4–16 offers a particularly useful comparison of family and church responsibility, while placing greater emphasis on preventing unnecessary burdens on the church.
