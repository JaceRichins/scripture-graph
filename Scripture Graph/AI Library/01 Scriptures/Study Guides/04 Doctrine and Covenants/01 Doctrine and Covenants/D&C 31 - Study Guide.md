# D&C 31 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 31]] · My notes: [[D&C 31 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–6, Thomas B. Marsh is called to preach the gospel and is assured of his family’s well-being; 7–13, He is counseled to be patient, pray always, and follow the Comforter.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter moves through four loosely bounded units:

- **vv.1-2** — Address and blessing; acknowledges family affliction, promises eventual family unity in the church.
- **vv.3-8** — Missionary commission: tongue loosed, sins forgiven, charge to "thrust in your sickle," brief separation from family, and the promise that a congregation will be raised up and strengthened.
- **vv.9-12** — Personal counsel: patience under affliction, meekness at home, non-retaliation, guidance by the Comforter, constant prayer.
- **v.13** — Closing charge to faithfulness and a first-person divine authentication.

The official heading splits at v.6/7; the content shows a gradual shift toward personal counsel around v.9 rather than a sharp public/private division, since vv.7-8 (congregational strengthening) and v.11 (itinerant guidance) still concern the ministry.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **[[Missionary Work]]** — the "field... white already to be burned" and "thrust in your sickle" language (vv.4-5) frames preaching as urgent labor with its own reward ("the laborer is worthy of his hire"); the same verse ties wholehearted labor to forgiveness of sins, though the text does not offer a full theory of how forgiveness is obtained.
- **[[Family]]** — family stability and eventual family unity are treated alongside the missionary call rather than opposed to it; the departure is explicitly temporary ("go from them only for a little time," v.6), with promises of provision. This promise is grammatically directed at Marsh's family specifically, not a universal guarantee for every missionary's household.
- **[[Prayer]]** — v.12 ties constant prayer directly to avoiding temptation and "losing" one's reward.
- **[[Satan and Temptation]]** — v.12 warns explicitly against entering into temptation.
- **[[Gift of the Holy Ghost]]** — v.11 promises direction "by the Comforter" rather than a fixed itinerary.
- **[[Repentance and Forgiveness]]** — v.5 declares "your sins are forgiven you" in connection with missionary labor.
- **[[Adversity]]** and **[[Endurance]]** — patience under affliction (v.9) and faithfulness "unto the end" (v.13) bookend the personal counsel.

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Several verses echo recognizable New Testament phrasing, repurposed as personal counsel — though given the era's KJV-saturated religious idiom, these may reflect common devotional vocabulary rather than deliberate allusion to a specific passage:
- v.5, "the laborer is worthy of his hire" — close to Luke 10:7.
- v.6, "I will prepare a place for them" — echoes John 14:2-3, here applied to Marsh's family rather than the apostles.
- v.9, "revile not against those that revile" — echoes 1 Peter 2:23's description of Christ, though the text does not itself invoke Christ's passion as the model.
- v.12, "lest you enter into temptation" — echoes the Gethsemane counsel of Matthew 26:41/Luke 22:40.

The chapter's harvest cluster (vv.4-5: field, sickle, laborer, sheaves) turns missionary preaching into urgent, productive labor. Its most distinctive feature is v.4's "the field which is white already to be burned," a variant of the more familiar D&C/NT phrase "white already to harvest" (cf. John 4:35; D&C 6:3). Whether the substitution of "burned" for "harvest" deliberately merges harvest and wheat-and-tares/judgment imagery (Matthew 13) or is simply distinctive phrasing is not settled by the text alone.

"Physician unto the church, but not unto the world" (v.10) is a metaphor the text does not define beyond context; vv.7-8 suggest a strengthening/restorative sense, but it should not be read as excluding the outward preaching that vv.3-7 explicitly command.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How do the missionary charge (vv.3-8) and the "physician unto the church, but not unto the world" restriction (v.10) fit together — sequential roles, simultaneous responsibilities, or a real tension?
- What might "the field which is white already to be burned" (v.4) suggest that the more common "white already to harvest" does not?
- The text promises Marsh's family will "be one with you in my church" (v.2) without specifying when or how — what does the promise commit to, and what does it leave open?
- Which of this chapter's promises and commands are personal to Marsh, and which principles can responsibly be generalized to other missionaries or families?
- Does "your tongue shall be loosed" (v.3) describe boldness, inspired speech, a formal spiritual gift, or some combination?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
