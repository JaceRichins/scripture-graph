# D&C 18 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 18]] · My notes: [[D&C 18 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 18 moves from reliable written foundations (vv. 1–5) to a universal call to [[Repentance]] (vv. 6–16), then grounds the worth of souls in the death and [[Resurrection]] of [[Jesus Christ (Gospel Topic)]] (vv. 10–13). It joins salvation with Christ's name, repentance, [[Baptism]], and [[Endurance]] (vv. 17–25), commissions the Twelve (vv. 26–39), and closes with worldwide preaching and a strong demand for [[Obedience]] (vv. 40–47). Its repeated imperatives make it less a detached theological essay than a commissioning revelation.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious outline is:

1. **Written foundation and conditional promise (1–5):** Oliver is told to rely upon existing written material.
2. **Need for repentance and worth of souls (6–16):** worldwide iniquity frames the commission; Christ's redemption grounds the value of each soul.
3. **Qualifications and saving name (17–25):** prayer, the Holy Ghost, faith, hope, charity, Christlike speech, repentance, baptism, and endurance.
4. **Prospective commission of the Twelve (26–39):** their desires, conduct, ordinances, and preaching duties are described before Oliver and David are told to find them.
5. **Public message and final warning (40–47):** repentance, baptism, accountability, commandment keeping, and promised divine work.

This outline follows changes in addressee and subject; it should not be mistaken for a formally marked structure supplied by the text.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's doctrine of human worth is explicitly Christ-centered: souls are precious in God's sight, and the explanation immediately invokes the Redeemer's suffering, death, resurrection, and joy over repentance (vv. 10–13). Missionary labor in vv. 14–16 follows from that premise.

Its account of [[Salvation]] combines divine initiative and human response. Christ suffers, rises, draws, supplies grace, and gives the Spirit (vv. 11–12, 18, 31), while people are summoned to repent, receive baptism, endure, and keep the commandments (vv. 22, 41–46). Isolating either side would flatten the chapter's presentation.

Taking Christ's name is likewise connected with conduct and covenantal response: sober truth-speaking, repentance, baptism, endurance, uprightness, and wholehearted desire (vv. 21–31).
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated words **name**, **repent**, **written**, **called**, and **my gospel** hold the chapter together. “Name” moves across several related uses: baptism in Christ's name (v. 22), the uniquely saving name (v. 23), the name taken upon disciples (vv. 24, 27–28), and authority exercised in that name (vv. 29, 40–41).

Verses 34–36 also create a notable relation between text and voice. The revelation says its words are Christ's, are given by the Spirit, can be read aloud, and can consequently be testified of as his voice. This supports a mediated sense of divine speech but does not resolve every question about how revelation was received.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 10–16 form a compact progression: the worth of souls, Christ's suffering and resurrection, divine joy over repentance, the call to preach, and escalating joy from one soul to many converts — the missionary appeal is grounded theologically before its promised reward is described.

The revelation also uses two similar first-person divine formulas — "I, Jesus Christ, your Lord and your God, have spoken it" (v33) and "I, Jesus Christ, your Lord and your God, and your Redeemer... have spoken it" (v47). These read most defensibly as repeated self-authenticating declarations rather than a deliberate literary "bracket" around verses 26–36: v33 falls near the end of that unit rather than opening it, and the text returns to addressing Oliver Cowdery and David Whitmer directly in vv37–39 before reaching v47.

The address to "the Twelve" in vv26–36, followed only afterward by the instruction to Oliver and David to search them out (vv37–39), reads naturally as prospective — words prepared for a group not yet identified to the human recipients. Confirming how this relates to the 1835 organization of the Quorum of the Twelve requires evidence outside the chapter itself.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Historical Fulfillment Claim *(tentative)*
> Church history records that the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles was formally organized in Kirtland, Ohio, in February 1835 — nearly six years after this revelation — with Oliver Cowdery, David Whitmer, and Martin Harris (the Three Witnesses) participating in identifying and blessing the original Twelve. This is presented in Restoration historiography as a fulfillment of the charge in D&C 18:37-39.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.6 · plausible history, needs primary sourcing

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. What identifiable written material did Oliver Cowdery understand by "the things which are written" (vv2-4), and what evidence outside this chapter supports identifying it with the Book of Mormon manuscript?
2. Does D&C 18:9's comparison to Paul claim apostolic office for Oliver Cowdery and David Whitmer, or a shared missionary calling more generally — especially since neither man was later ordained to the Quorum of the Twelve?
3. How does grounding the worth of souls in Christ's redemptive work (vv10-13) shape the missionary commission that follows?
4. What does "contend" mean in verse 20, and how does the command to "speak the truth in soberness" (v21) constrain its application?
5. Which qualifications for the Twelve are inward desires and which are observable works (vv27-38), and how do the two relate as a test in v38?
6. Since v42 does not specify an age of accountability, what would readers in 1829 have understood "years of accountability" to mean absent the later clarification in D&C 68:27?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare [[D&C 20]] on the church's foundational ordinances and the roles of priests and teachers, and [[2 Nephi 31]] on the doctrine of Christ (repentance, baptism, enduring to the end). Matthew 16:18 and Acts 4:12 supply the closest biblical parallels to vv5 and 23. For the faith-hope-charity triad, compare 1 Corinthians 13:13 rather than an isolated earlier verse from that chapter.

Historical study — outside this chapter — can address when and how "the things which are written" were identified, and the relationship between this June 1829 revelation's description of an as-yet-unidentified "Twelve" and the February 1835 organization of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles in Kirtland, Ohio, in which Oliver Cowdery and David Whitmer participated as [[Witnesses of the Book of Mormon]]. Note that the 1835 implementation was carried out by the same movement that produced the text, so it demonstrates institutional follow-through rather than independent confirmation of predictive revelation.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
