<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The section develops through successive assignments:

1. D&C 55:1–3 gives baptismal, priesthood, and preaching instructions.
2. D&C 55:4 adds printing and educational work.
3. D&C 55:5 locates that work in the land of inheritance and commands a journey.
4. D&C 55:6 adds Joseph Coe to the travelers and leaves further details for later disclosure.

The repeated “And again” in verses 4–6 gives the short revelation an additive structure. Each new instruction enlarges the calling rather than replacing what came before.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter coordinates outward actions with inward dispositions. William's baptism is to be undertaken "with an eye single to my glory" (D&C 55:1), while those upon whom he lays hands are described as contrite (D&C 55:3). This is relevant to [[Baptism]], [[Repentance]], [[Priesthood]], and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]]. It supports attention to sincere participation but does not state whether an ordinance is invalid or ineffective when the named dispositions are absent.

The chapter also assigns religious significance to [[Education and Learning]]. William is to assist with printing and with selecting and writing books so little children may receive instruction pleasing to God (D&C 55:4). This establishes a specific educational commission, not a comprehensive philosophy of education or proof that the proposed materials were completed.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
“Ordained” appears in two settings. In D&C 55:2 William is ordained an elder; in D&C 55:4 he is ordained to assist with printing and educational books. The first clearly names an ecclesiastical office. The second may use the word more broadly for appointment or designation. Readers should therefore avoid assuming, without further historical evidence, that both uses describe exactly the same kind of act.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
[[D&C 53]] and [[D&C 55]] contain closely related commissioning elements, including calling, baptism and the Holy Spirit, ordination and preaching, travel, and deferred instruction. The supplied overlap index independently confirms shared wording. This makes the comparison useful, but the textual resemblance alone cannot determine whether it reflects recurring revelatory diction, a genre convention, dictation or scribal practice, editorial transmission, or deliberate template reuse.

[[Moroni 3]] also offers a narrower verbal parallel by connecting ordination with preaching repentance and remission of sins through Jesus Christ. The offices and surrounding instructions are not identical.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How does “an eye single to my glory” qualify the promise in D&C 55:1?
- Why does D&C 55:3 explicitly mention contrition when describing the laying on of hands?
- Does “ordained” in D&C 55:4 denote a formal ritual, an appointment, or a broader sense of divine commissioning?
- What does the pairing of preaching with printing suggest about the chapter's conception of ministry?
- How does the promise of later information in D&C 55:6 shape expectations about [[Revelation]] and obedience?
