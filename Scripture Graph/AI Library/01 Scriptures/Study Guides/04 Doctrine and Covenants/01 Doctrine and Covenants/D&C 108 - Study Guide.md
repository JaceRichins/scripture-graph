# D&C 108 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 108]] · My notes: [[D&C 108 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–3, Lyman Sherman is forgiven of his sins; 4–5, He is to be numbered with the leading elders of the Church; 6–8, He is called to preach the gospel and strengthen his brethren.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A compact three-part movement is visible:

1. **Assurance and correction (verses 1–3):** forgiveness and spiritual rest are paired with renewed obedience and care for vows.
2. **Waiting and promised commission (verses 4–6):** a solemn assembly, ordination, and authority to preach are anticipated, conditional upon continued faithfulness.
3. **Service and divine presence (verses 7–8):** Lyman is charged to strengthen others and promised blessing and deliverance.

Temporal expressions—"this morning," "henceforth," "until," "in that day," "from that time," and "forever"—give the revelation a progression from an immediate encounter toward enduring service.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter places [[Repentance and Forgiveness]] beside continuing [[Obedience]]. Its initial assurance does not cancel the need for correction, vigilance concerning [[Covenants]], or faithful endurance. The promised "right by ordination" and "right to preach" connect [[Ordinances]] and [[Priesthood]] with authorized [[Missionary Work]]. Verse 7 broadens ministry beyond formal preaching: [[Service]] includes conversation, [[Prayer]], exhortation, and conduct.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated phrase "in all your" in verse 7 creates a fourfold cadence and emphasizes the comprehensive character of the charge. Verse 6 carefully qualifies the broad word "wheresoever" with "I shall send you," balancing geographical openness with dependence upon divine commission. The expression "right by ordination" is important but not self-defining; the chapter does not specify the exact office or give a complete institutional explanation of that right.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verse 7 has a close verbal parallel in [[Luke 22]], where Peter is told to "strengthen thy brethren" (Luke 22:32). In both settings, personal spiritual experience is followed by responsibility toward fellow believers. The parallel is useful for comparison, though resemblance in wording alone cannot prove that D&C 108 intentionally quotes Luke.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Textual Condition
> The explicit condition "if you continue faithful" is strong textual evidence that the promise described in the surrounding verses is conditional upon continuing faithfulness; it is not evidence within the chapter that the promise was historically fulfilled.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · model claim: separates text from unfulfilled claim · [[Internal Textual Condition in D&C 108|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intertextual/Literary Echo *(tentative)*
> The charge in verse 7 to 'strengthen your brethren in all your conversation, in all your prayers, in all your exhortations, and in all your doings' echoes Jesus's charge to Peter in Luke 22:32, 'when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren,' applying a New Testament apostolic commission to a personal 1830s revelation.
