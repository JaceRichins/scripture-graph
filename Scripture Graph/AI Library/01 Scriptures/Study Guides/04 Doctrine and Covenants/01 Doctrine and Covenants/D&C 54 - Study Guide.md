# D&C 54 — Study Guide

## Overview

<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The speaker's identity (v. 1):** the eternal and crucified Christ speaks.
2. **Covenant crisis (vv. 2–6):** Newel Knight must stand fast; the brethren are called to repent and become humble; the covenant is declared void; the offender receives a warning; covenant keepers are promised mercy.
3. **Relocation (vv. 7–9):** the group must flee, appoint someone as leader and to handle payments, travel toward Missouri, and seek a living while awaiting a prepared place. Verse 7 does not clearly establish whether leadership and payment are separate offices.
4. **Endurance and promise (v. 10):** the group must remain patient in tribulation, with Christ's coming, reward, and promised rest as the concluding horizon.

Verses 1 and 10 plausibly form a Christ-centered frame, although conventional revelatory formulas could also account for the placement.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Within this chapter, covenant accountability and mercy appear together. The covenant has been broken and made void, severe woe is pronounced upon the person through whom the offense came, and those who kept the covenant and observed the commandment are promised mercy (vv. 4–6). This local contrast should not be treated as a complete account of every condition of divine mercy.

Repentance includes becoming “truly humble” and contrite (v. 3). Faithful waiting is also active: the group must travel, organize its leadership and payments, and “seek ye a living like unto men” while God prepares a place (vv. 7–9). This reasonably connects the passage with [[Repentance]], [[Humility]], [[Justice and Mercy]], and, more cautiously, [[Work and Self-Reliance]].

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
D&C 54 uses several conspicuous biblical expressions:

- The opening titles “Alpha and Omega” and “the beginning and the end” closely parallel Revelation 22:13.
- The closing declaration “I come quickly, and my reward is with me” closely parallels Revelation 22:12.
- Verse 5 combines Matthew 18:6's drowning image with Matthew 18:7's warning about the person through whom offense comes.
- In verse 10, “sought me early” resembles Proverbs 8:17, while “find rest to their souls” more closely resembles Matthew 11:29.

These correspondences establish a strong KJV biblical register. They do not by themselves determine whether each expression is a conscious quotation, remembered scriptural diction, a conventional religious idiom, or part of another mode of revelatory composition.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter develops several contrasts: broken covenant and kept covenant, woe and blessing, enemies and tribulation on one side and mercy and rest on the other. It also moves strikingly between cosmic claims about Christ and practical instructions concerning travel, leadership, money, and livelihood.

The placement of Revelation 22 language in the opening and closing verses creates a plausible literary frame around the immediate crisis. That frame is a defensible interpretation, not proof of deliberate structural design.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What can be learned from the distinction between the unidentified plural group who made the covenant and the singular person “by whom this offense cometh”?
- What covenant does the chapter presuppose, and which details must be supplied from historical or surrounding-scripture context?
- Does verse 3 promise physical escape, or does it primarily prescribe the spiritual posture required amid danger?
- How does verse 9 relate temporary livelihood to dependence on God's future preparation without turning a situational command into a universal economic doctrine?
- What literary effect results from the Revelation 22 wording at both boundaries, regardless of whether the framing was consciously designed?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare D&C 54:1, 10 with [[Revelation 22]], and D&C 54:5 with [[Matthew 18]]. For the final promise in verse 10, compare both [[Proverbs 8]] and [[Matthew 11]]: the former resembles “sought me early,” while the latter closely resembles “find rest to their souls.”

For historical context, study [[D&C 51]] alongside the Joseph Smith Papers historical introduction to D&C 54, carefully distinguishing the documented Thompson crisis from the chapter's unspecified covenant language. [[D&C 52]] may provide contemporaneous context for the movement toward Missouri. Further thematic study may include [[Covenants]], [[Repentance]], [[Adversity]], and [[Endurance]].
