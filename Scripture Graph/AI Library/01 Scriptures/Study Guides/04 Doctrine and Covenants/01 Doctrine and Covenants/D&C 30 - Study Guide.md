# D&C 30 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 30]] · My notes: [[D&C 30 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–4, David Whitmer is chastened for failure to serve diligently; 5–8, Peter Whitmer Jr. is to accompany Oliver Cowdery on a mission to the Lamanites; 9–11, John Whitmer is called to preach the gospel.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **David (verses 1–4):** diagnosis of misplaced fear and attention, followed by inquiry, pondering, residence, and ministry instructions.

2. **Peter (verses 5–8):** journey and proclamation, solidarity with Oliver, prayer for deliverance, clarification of authority, and a promise tied to diligence.

3. **John (verses 9–11):** immediate proclamation, a provisional local assignment, total devotion to Zion, and assurance of divine presence.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
David's address joins correction with a continuing but restricted opportunity for [[Service]]. His rebuke concerns fear of man, insufficient reliance on God, earthly preoccupation, neglect of the Spirit, and failure to heed appointed leaders. Verse 3 places personal inquiry after those failures, but it may describe a corrective consequence rather than a complete doctrine harmonizing personal and institutional guidance.

Peter's instructions connect shared adversity with [[Prayer]], [[Faith]], and [[Endurance]]. Verse 8 relates diligent [[Obedience]] to [[Eternal Life]] in a promise addressed specifically to Peter; it should not be treated as a complete general formula for salvation. Verses 5–7 describe relationships relevant to this assignment without establishing a universal or comprehensive church hierarchy.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The contrast between fearing people and relying on God governs the chapter. David's fear is named as a failure (verse 1); Peter receives the direct counter-command to fear not (verse 5); and John is told not to fear because God is with him (verse 11). “Open your mouth” similarly turns inward trust into outward proclamation in verses 5 and 11. The trumpet comparison in verse 9 depicts public, audible, urgent announcement.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Although the three messages address different recipients, recurring vocabulary gives the compilation unity: fear and courage, heeding and commandment keeping, opening the mouth, ministry, and geographically bounded labor. This is meaningful thematic patterning, though it is not enough by itself to prove a formally designed composition. Compare the proclamation language in [[D&C 24]] and [[D&C 36]].
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How does verse 3's call for personal inquiry relate to verse 2's criticism for disregarding the Spirit and appointed leaders?

- What does the carefully limited phrase “concerning church matters” contribute to the authority statement in verse 7?

- Is “Zion” in verse 11 primarily a local field of labor, a covenant community, or both? The chapter itself does not settle the question.

- How does the promise of God's presence in verse 11 answer the fear-of-man problem introduced in verse 1?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
