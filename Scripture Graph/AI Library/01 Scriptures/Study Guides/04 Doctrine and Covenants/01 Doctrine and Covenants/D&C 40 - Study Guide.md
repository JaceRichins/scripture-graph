# D&C 40 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 40]] · My notes: [[D&C 40 - My Notes]]

## Overview

> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–3, Fear of persecution and cares of the world cause rejection of the gospel.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
Three brief verses trace a complete arc:

1. **Covenant made (v.1):** Covel's heart is called right, and he covenants to obey.
2. **Covenant broken (v.2):** glad reception gives way to Satan's temptation, fear of persecution, and worldly cares.
3. **Outcome reserved (v.3):** the Lord names the breach - "he broke my covenant" - but states no specific consequence, leaving the matter "to do with him as seemeth me good."

At three verses centered on one named individual, this is an unusually brief and personal section, though its exact rank among all Doctrine and Covenants sections for brevity hasn't been checked here.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
D&C 40 sets [[Covenants|covenant]]-making next to covenant-breaking: Covel's sincere start (v.1) does not by itself secure follow-through. [[Satan and Temptation|Satan]]'s temptation, fear of persecution, and worldly cares are all named as contributing causes (v.2), yet the text still holds Covel responsible - it is Covel, not Satan, who "broke my covenant" (v.3), consistent with [[Agency|agency]] operating even under real temptation. The chapter distinguishes a promising beginning from durable [[Endurance|endurance]] in a covenant. Verse 3 states only that the outcome remains with the Lord; it doesn't specify whether this points toward mercy, further opportunity, or judgment, and the text shouldn't be read as filling in that gap either way.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verse 2's vocabulary closely tracks the Synoptic Parable of the Sower, though it compresses elements from more than one type of hearer rather than mirroring a single one: "received... with gladness" and falling away under "persecution" match the stony-ground hearer (Mark 4:16-17; Matthew 13:20-21); "the cares of the world" matches the thorny-ground hearer (Mark 4:19; Matthew 13:22; Luke 8:14); and Satan's role in v.2 echoes the wayside hearer, from whom Satan "catcheth away" the word (Mark 4:15; Matthew 13:19; Luke 8:12). D&C 40 draws on this shared vocabulary without stating that it is quoting or interpreting the parable directly.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter's compact three-part shape - covenant, reception-then-rejection, reserved outcome - reads as a condensed personal narrative rather than a doctrinal discourse, a form distinct from most Doctrine and Covenants sections. A looser, non-verbal parallel exists in Peter's vow of loyalty ("though I should die with thee, yet will I not deny thee," Matthew 26:35) followed by his denial of Christ under pressure; unlike the sower echo, this comparison rests on shared theme rather than shared wording, and the Gospels don't explicitly name fear as Peter's motive, so it should be treated as suggestive rather than established.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What is the difference between receiving a word "with gladness" (v.2) and receiving it with lasting conviction, and what does that gap suggest about testing a testimony over time?
- The chapter names Satan's temptation, fear of persecution, and cares of the world together as causes - why might the Lord list all three rather than assigning primary blame to one?
- Why does the revelation leave God's future action toward Covel unspecified in v.3, rather than pronouncing a consequence as some other sections do?
- Does calling Covel's heart "right before me" (v.1) describe a lasting character trait, a sincere intent at one moment, or something else - and how does that shape how we read his later failure?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
