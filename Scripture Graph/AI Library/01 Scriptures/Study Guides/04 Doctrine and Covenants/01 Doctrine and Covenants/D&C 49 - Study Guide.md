# D&C 49 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 49]] · My notes: [[D&C 49 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 49 is a revelation directing three named men — Sidney, Parley, and Leman (traditionally identified as Sidney Rigdon, Parley P. Pratt, and Leman Copley) — to preach the received gospel to the Shakers (United Society of Believers), a religious community explicitly named in the text (v.1). The Shakers are described as desiring 'the truth in part, but not all' and in need of repentance (v.2). Framed by an opening commission (vv.1-4) and a closing charge (vv.26-27), the chapter restates the [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]] and moves through teachings on Christ's coming, [[Marriage]], the use of created things, and the manner of the Second Coming. Historical tradition connects several of these teachings (marriage, diet, the 'form of a woman') to known Shaker beliefs and practices, but that correspondence rests on outside historical knowledge rather than anything the chapter itself states, and should be read as a plausible interpretive lens rather than settled fact.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Commission and preparation (vv.1-4):** Sidney, Parley, and Leman receive differentiated assignments; Leman is told to rely on what Sidney and Parley teach him, not on what he previously received from the Shakers.
2. **Christ, repentance, and the gospel proclamation (vv.5-14):** Christ's redemption, present reign, and return of unknown hour, followed by a mission message given 'like unto mine apostle of old, whose name was Peter' (v.11) — belief, repentance, [[Baptism]], and the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] (vv.12-14).
3. **Marriage and the use of creation (vv.15-21):** [[Marriage]] affirmed as ordained of God; food and clothing from created things permitted, but inequality, bloodshed, and needless waste condemned.
4. **The coming of the Son of Man (vv.22-25):** deceptive claims about Christ's form rejected; cosmic and geographic signs foretold; Israel and [[Zion]] promised to flourish and gather.
5. **Renewed charge and assurance (vv.26-28):** repeated command to go, repent, ask, and knock, closing with Christ's promise of His presence and self-identification (v.28).
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's clearest doctrinal core is a compact statement of the [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]]: belief in Christ, repentance, [[Baptism]] for the remission of sins, and reception of the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] by the laying on of hands (vv.12-14), explicitly likened to the apostle Peter's preaching (v.11; compare Acts 2:38). [[Marriage]] is affirmed as ordained of God, with the one-flesh union tied to the earth 'answering the end of its creation' (vv.15-17); verse 17's reference to a 'creation before the world was made' is ambiguous on its own and shouldn't be over-read as a settled statement about premortal existence. Food and clothing from created things are permitted for human use (v.19), but the text just as pointedly condemns one person possessing 'that which is above another' (v.20) and wasting flesh 'and hath no need' (v.21) — the chapter neither forbids meat nor treats it as unrestricted; this stewardship principle anticipates, without being identical to, dietary counsel later given in the [[Word of Wisdom]] (D&C 89), given roughly two years afterward. On the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]], the chapter is confident that Christ will come and reign but withholds the specific day or hour, known 'neither [to] the angels in heaven' (v.7).

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verse 11's 'like unto mine apostle of old, whose name was Peter' signals that the following gospel formula (vv.12-14) is patterned on Peter's preaching (compare Acts 2:38: repent, be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and receive the gift of the Holy Ghost), though 'like unto' indicates a stated resemblance rather than a verbatim quotation. Verses 15 and 18 — forbidding to marry, and forbidding to abstain from meats — closely echo 1 Timothy 4:1-3's warning against those who, in the latter times, would be 'forbidding to marry, and commanding to abstain from meats.' Read against that backdrop, verse 18 most plausibly rejects an imposed command not to eat meat (consistent with v.19's permission) rather than commanding meat consumption, though the verse's own syntax remains somewhat difficult on its own. Verse 23's imagery of a shaken heaven, a reeling earth, exalted valleys, and lowered mountains draws on Isaiah's day-of-the-Lord vocabulary (compare Isaiah 24:20; 40:4), and verse 24's 'blossom as the rose' echoes Isaiah 35:1's wilderness imagery, applied here to the Lamanites.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Mission language brackets the chapter: 'go and preach' opens it (v.1), while 'go forth as I have commanded you' and the promise of Christ's accompanying presence close it (vv.26-27). Within that frame, the address moves from gospel proclamation to specific corrective teaching and then to eschatological steadfastness. Historical tradition — not the text itself — connects several of the intervening teachings (marriage, diet, the 'form of a woman') to distinctive Shaker beliefs and practices, which would make the mission frame more than incidental; but the chapter does not name Shaker doctrine directly, so that reading should be held with appropriate caution.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Literary And Contextual Evidence
> The opening and closing mission commands bracket teachings on gospel ordinances, marriage, use of creation, and the Second Coming. This is strong internal evidence that the chapter presents these teachings as relevant to the Shaker mission, but it does not prove that every intervening statement answers a separately documented Shaker belief.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation · [[Internal Literary And Contextual Evidence in D&C 49|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] General Religious-History Background *(tentative)*
> Background on the addressed group: the Shakers (United Society of Believers in Christ's Second Appearing) are broadly documented in religious-history sources as having practiced celibacy, communal property, and, in some communities, dietary restriction, and as regarding their founder Ann Lee as the female embodiment of Christ's promised second appearing. This external historical profile — not stated within D&C 49 itself — is what gives verses 15–22 their apparent point-by-point specificity.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.6 · uncited general historical background

> [!evidence] Historical Background (Believing/Church-History Scholarship) *(tentative)*
> Historically, Leman Copley is generally identified as a former member of the Shaker (United Society of Believers) community near Kirtland/North Union, Ohio, prior to his baptism into the Church — a background that would explain why verse 4 specifically warns him against reasoning with the Shakers "according to that which he has received of them." This is extra-textual historical context, not stated in the chapter itself.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.6 · plausible historical tradition, uncited

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does the sequence of teachings in vv.15-22 reflect a deliberate, point-by-point response to specific Shaker beliefs (marriage/celibacy, diet, claims about Christ's form), or more general doctrinal instruction that happens to overlap with Shaker practice?
- What does verse 4's caution to Leman suggest about his personal history with the Shaker community, and how does that shape the tone of the whole revelation?
- How does verse 20's teaching against unequal possession relate to the Shakers' own communal economic practice — correction, or common ground?
- Why does the text specify that 'the hour and the day no man knoweth, neither the angels in heaven' (v.7) even while describing detailed signs of the Lord's coming (v.23)?
