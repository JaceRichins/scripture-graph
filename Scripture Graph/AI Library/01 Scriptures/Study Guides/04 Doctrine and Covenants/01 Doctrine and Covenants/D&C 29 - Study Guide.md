# D&C 29 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 29]] · My notes: [[D&C 29 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 29 is presented as the voice of Jesus Christ addressed to early Church members in September 1830. It moves through three broad movements: (1) a call to gather as the [[Gathering of Israel]] and heed Christ's voice, paired with a commission to proclaim the gospel (vv.1-9); (2) an eschatological survey of the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]], [[Signs of the Times]], the [[Millennium]], and the [[Final Judgment]] (vv.9-30); and (3) an excursus on [[Creation]], [[Agency]], the premortal fall of Satan (see [[Satan and Temptation]], [[Premortal Life]]), and the [[Fall of Adam and Eve]] (vv.31-50), closing with the redemption of little children and a distinction between those with knowledge and those without understanding. The shift from apocalyptic prophecy to cosmology-and-agency instruction partway through is one of the chapter's most distinctive literary features.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter can be outlined as follows:

- **vv.1-9** — Call to hear Christ's voice; sins forgiven conditionally ('sin no more'); the elect are gathered as with a hen and her chickens (v.2); the elect 'hear my voice and harden not their hearts' (v.7).
- **vv.9-21** — Signs and judgments before the Second Coming: the earth's wickedness ripens like stubble to be burned (v.9); apostolic fulfillment (v.10); Christ's coming with the Twelve as judges (vv.11-13); cosmic signs — sun darkened, moon to blood, stars fall (v.14); plagues of flies and maggots (vv.18-19); destruction of 'the great and abominable church' (v.21).
- **vv.22-29** — The Millennium ends, wickedness resumes 'but for a little season' (v.22); the earth and heavens pass away and are renewed (vv.23-25); Michael's trump and universal resurrection (v.26); final separation of righteous and wicked (vv.27-28).
- **vv.30-39** — Digression on the order of Creation (spiritual/temporal, v.32) and the premortal fall of Satan and a third of the hosts of heaven (vv.36-38), framed around agency (v.39).
- **vv.40-50** — The Fall of Adam, the distinction between spiritual and temporal death (vv.41-44), and the unconditional redemption of little children until the age of accountability (vv.46-48).
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter holds mercy and accountability together: Christ appears as Redeemer and advocate, forgiveness is granted but paired with a warning against returning to sin, and election is described in responsive terms — those who hear his voice and do not harden their hearts (v.7). Verses 31-35 present two orderings of creation ('first spiritual, secondly temporal' and the reverse), explicitly framed as accommodated to human understanding, since to God 'my works have no end, neither beginning' (v.33); this is best read as a qualified statement about how creation is described, not a resolved harmony of the Genesis accounts. Verses 36-39 tie the devil's opposition to a premortal rebellion and to [[Agency]] — a third of the 'hosts of heaven' were thrust down because of their agency. Verses 41-43 distinguish spiritual death (immediate separation from God's presence at the Fall) from temporal death, deferred so mortals could have a probationary period in which repentance is preached; the text does not use the word 'covenant' for this arrangement. Verses 46-48 teach that little children are redeemed through the Only Begotten and cannot sin before accountability — relevant to, though not explicitly about, [[Infant Baptism (rejected doctrine)]], since baptism itself is never mentioned here. Verses 49-50 close by distinguishing those with knowledge, who are commanded to repent, from those without understanding, left to God's judgment.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter uses the doubled 'verily, verily, I say unto you' formula at vv.3, 12, and 22, alongside single 'verily' declarations (e.g., v.4) — a register recalling the Gospel of John's rendering of Jesus's speech. The narrative formula 'and it came to pass' appears twice (vv.36, 40), briefly narrating Adam's temptation in the third person within an otherwise first-person discourse — a notable shift in register, though not necessarily evidence of a change in speaker or source. Verse 32 exhibits a tight ABBA inversion: 'First spiritual, secondly temporal... first temporal, and secondly spiritual,' reversing the same two terms across its two clauses. The chapter also draws repeatedly on recognizable biblical imagery — protective gathering under a hen's wings, trumpet blasts, Sinai, darkened heavenly bodies, stubble burned by fire, right-and-left separation, and a new heaven and earth.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
D&C 29 draws heavily on recognizable biblical and Book of Mormon idiom. The 'hen gathereth her chickens' image of v.2 recurs in nearly identical wording in Matthew 23:37 and in [[3 Nephi 10]]'s account of Christ's post-resurrection lament. Verse 9's description of the wicked as 'stubble' to be 'burned' by 'the Lord of Hosts' closely tracks Malachi 4:1's wording. Verse 13's trump sounding 'even as upon Mount Sinai' invokes the Exodus theophany. Verses 27-28's separation of the righteous to the right hand and the wicked to the left, followed by 'Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels,' closely parallels Matthew 25:31-33, 41 — among the closest verbal parallels in the chapter. These are strong evidence of biblical intertextuality; whether the mechanism is direct revelation, conscious reuse of memorized KJV language, or shared idiom cannot be settled from the text alone, and that question is best left open.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does 'spiritual' mean in vv.31-35 — a mode of existence, or something like 'governed by higher law regardless of physical form'? The chapter does not address how this relates to the later teaching that spirit is itself a form of matter.
- Is 'Michael, mine archangel' (v.26) to be identified with Adam, as taught elsewhere? D&C 29 itself makes no such identification.
- Does 'according as it is spoken by the mouth of Ezekiel the prophet' (v.21) point to a specific oracle, or to Ezekiel's broader harlot-city theme (Ezekiel 16, 23), given no verbatim match exists?
- How should verses 27-30 be read together — the statement that the condemned 'cannot come' followed immediately by the admission that 'all my judgments are not given unto men'?
- Is 'a third part' of the hosts of heaven (v.37) a precise quantity or a symbolic expression?
- How should the shift from apocalyptic prophecy (vv.9-30) to cosmology and agency (vv.31-50) within one revelation be understood — two joined oracles, or one discourse moving from 'what will happen' to 'why it can happen at all'?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
