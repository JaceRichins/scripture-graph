# D&C 70 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 70]] · My notes: [[D&C 70 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 70, received November 12, 1831 at Hiram, Ohio, appoints six men — Joseph Smith, Martin Harris, Oliver Cowdery, John Whitmer, Sidney Rigdon, and W. W. Phelps — as stewards over the Lord's revelations and commandments, both those already given and those yet to come (vv. 1–5), tying management of these sacred texts ([[Records and Sacred Writings]], [[Revelation]]) to material benefit. An account of this stewardship will be required in the day of judgment (v. 4). The stewards may retain what is needful for their necessities and wants, but any surplus is to go into the Lord's storehouse for the benefit of the inhabitants of Zion and their generations (vv. 6–8), enacting the law of [[Consecration]]. The revelation then generalizes this principle: no church member is exempt from the law of accountable stewardship, and those who administer spiritual things are worthy of support just as those over temporal things are (vv. 9–13). The Saints are commanded to be equal in temporal things, and not grudgingly, since withholding equality risks withholding the abundance of the Spirit's manifestations (v. 14). The chapter closes by naming the purposes of the arrangement — blessing, reward, security, and material provision — and promises mercy and joy to servants who have not sinned (vv. 15–18).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter moves through four linked stages:

1. **Appointment and accountability (vv. 1–5):** six servants are placed over the revelations, with an account required at the [[Final Judgment]].
2. **Needs and surplus (vv. 6–8):** necessities and wants may be supplied, but excess benefits belong in the storehouse for Zion.
3. **A general law of stewardship (vv. 9–14):** no church officer is exempt; spiritual labor is worthy of support; temporal equality must be willing rather than grudging.
4. **Purpose and promise (vv. 15–18):** the arrangement is for benefit, reward, security, and concrete provision, ending with a qualified commendation and promise of joy.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents stewardship as delegated responsibility rather than unrestricted ownership: an assignment comes from the Lord, must be administered, and will be accounted for. Its treatment of [[Consecration]] preserves both provision for real human needs and an obligation to redirect excess toward the covenant community.

Verse 12 also rejects a rigid separation between spiritual ministry and material provision. Those assigned to spiritual work are "worthy of" support, just as temporal stewards are. The passage does not, however, specify a universal salary system or settle every question about compensation for religious service.

Temporal equality in verse 14 should be read with the chapter's references to necessities, wants, differing circumstances, houses, lands, and inheritances. That context suggests equitable covenantal provision, but it does not fully define the required degree or mechanism of equality.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Several terms require restraint. **"These things"** in verse 6 probably points back to the revelations, commandments, concerns, and benefits of verses 3 and 5, but its precise scope is not explicit. **"Benefits"** may include material proceeds, advantages, or rights connected with the stewardship; the chapter itself does not provide an accounting definition. **"More abundantly"** in verse 13 and **"abundance"** in verse 14 connect spiritual manifestations with temporal conduct, but they do not identify particular gifts or provide a measurable formula.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What exactly is included in "these things" and "the benefits thereof" in verses 5–7?
- How do the differing necessities, wants, and circumstances in verses 7 and 16 qualify the command to be equal in verse 14?
- Why does the text connect willingness in temporal equality with manifestations of the Spirit?
- What changes when the revelation moves from six named stewards in verses 1–8 to "every man in his stewardship" in verse 9?
