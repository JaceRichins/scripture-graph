# D&C 105 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 105]] · My notes: [[D&C 105 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
[[D&C 105]] explains why Zion's immediate redemption is deferred and what the text says must precede it. Its condemnation is corporate but qualified: disobedience, neglect of the poor, and disunity are attributed to the church, while not every individual or first elder is placed under the condemnation. The resulting program includes instruction, experience, [[Obedience]], [[Consecration]], [[Humility]], sanctification, temple endowment, lawful land purchase, legal redress, and peace. These are the chapter's stated explanations and directives; the chapter alone does not establish that communal failings were the only historical cause of the delay.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious outline is: (1) diagnosis of the delay and the requirements of Zion (vv. 1–10); (2) promised endowment and evaluation of the assembled company (vv. 11–19); (3) interim instructions concerning residence, discretion, gathering, and legal redress (vv. 20–27); (4) land purchase and conditional future possession (vv. 28–32); and (5) Kirtland endowment, choosing and sanctification, followed by an emphatic peace commission (vv. 33–41). This outline describes the progression without claiming a formal literary pattern.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter links [[Zion]] with [[Consecration]], [[Obedience]], unity, care for the poor and afflicted, and sanctification. Geographic possession remains important, but land, gathering, and organized strength are presented as insufficient without the required communal practices. Chastening and delay are described here as means of learning and preparation, without implying that every participant is individually condemned or that all suffering has the same explanation. Promised power is associated with humility, worthiness, following counsel, and the endowment to be received in the Kirtland house, connecting [[Temples]] with preparation for Zion.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter links a cluster of expressions concerning waiting, power, blessing, and endowment: the elders must “wait for a little season,” be “endowed with power from on high,” receive a promised “great endowment,” and later receive their “endowment from on high” in the Kirtland house. These are related expressions, not one phrase repeated verbatim throughout. The text also juxtaposes martial terms—army, warriors, towers, watchmen, vengeance, and banners—with civic and conciliatory terms—law, justice, redress, favor, and peace. That contrast should not be reduced either to uncomplicated militarism or to absolute pacifism.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The most important tension is between martial and peaceful language. Verses 30–32 imagine an enlarged, sanctified army and forceful possession, whereas verses 25 and 38–40 prescribe legal redress, an ensign of peace, and proposals for peace. The sequence matters: purchase, growth, and sanctification precede the future action, while peace is commanded in the immediate setting. That sequencing mitigates but does not eliminate the ethical and interpretive tension.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How should the corporate condemnation in verses 2–6 be distinguished from individual accountability, especially given verse 7?
- What do care for the poor, unity, and celestial law contribute to the chapter's understanding of [[Zion]]?
- Which martial statements describe conditional future action, and which instructions govern the immediate setting?
- Does the command to seek peace function as a temporary strategy, a durable ethic, or both?
- How do the Kirtland endowment and the movement from calling to choosing reshape the chapter's interpretation of the expedition's outcome?
- Does chronological sequencing resolve the tension between peace and coercion, or merely postpone it?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
