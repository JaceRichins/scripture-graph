# D&C 57 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 57]] · My notes: [[D&C 57 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–3, Independence, Missouri, is the place for the City of Zion and the temple; 4–7, The Saints are to purchase lands and receive inheritances in that area; 8–16, Sidney Gilbert is to establish a store, William W. Phelps is to be a printer, and Oliver Cowdery is to edit material for publication.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Sacred location (vv. 1–3):** Missouri, Zion, Independence, and the temple site are identified.

2. **Land and inheritances (vv. 4–7):** The Saints are to purchase land; the agent handles funds and purchases, while the bishop divides inheritances.

3. **Commerce and mission (vv. 8–10):** A store is to supply settlers and help finance land acquisition, contributing to the material support of [[Missionary Work]].

4. **Printing and editing (vv. 11–13):** Phelps and Cowdery receive complementary responsibilities for producing reliable publications.

5. **Settlement and deferred direction (vv. 14–16):** Named participants and their families are to relocate promptly; instructions for others will follow.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter depicts [[Zion]] as both sacred and practical. Its religious purposes do not eliminate ordinary means: land must be bought, money received, inheritances administered, goods sold without fraud, publications corrected, and families prepared. This makes integrity in temporal stewardship part of the Zion project rather than a merely incidental concern.

The text also distinguishes promise from implementation. The land is called a land of promise and an everlasting inheritance, yet acquisition remains limited by ability, righteousness, and wisdom (vv. 2, 5–6). The chapter therefore does not present divine designation as permission to disregard lawful or ethical means.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Forms of **plant** recur in connection with Gilbert, Phelps, the named workers and their families, and arriving families (vv. 8, 11, 14–15). This repetition can suggest establishment and rootedness, though it may also reflect ordinary settlement vocabulary rather than a deliberately constructed literary motif.

The phrases **here is wisdom** and **in righteousness** recur around practical directions (vv. 3, 6, 9, 12). They emphasize prudent and morally constrained action; the text does not clearly establish that such directions were optional. Verse 10's language about those who sit in darkness and the shadow of death also echoes familiar biblical diction associated with Isaiah 9:2, Matthew 4:16, and Luke 1:79, without identifying one passage as its certain source.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter narrows from region to city to temple lot in vv. 1–3, then expands outward into the network required to sustain that sacred center: lands, finances, inheritances, commerce, preaching, printing, editing, and families. This geographic narrowing followed by administrative expansion gives the short revelation a coherent movement from vision to implementation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
What historical or symbolic boundary is meant by the “line running directly between Jew and Gentile” in verse 4? The chapter itself does not define either group in this phrase.

How should “everlasting inheritance” in verse 5 be read alongside the practical qualifications placed on acquiring the land? Does it describe covenant purpose, an anticipated permanent community, literal tenure, or some combination?

What does verse 13 mean operationally when it says editorial work will be proved by the Spirit? The verse identifies a spiritual standard but does not describe a reproducible method for resolving editorial disagreement.
<!-- SG:END questions -->
