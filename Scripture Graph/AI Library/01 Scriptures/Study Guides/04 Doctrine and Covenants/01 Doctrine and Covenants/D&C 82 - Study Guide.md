# D&C 82 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 82]] · My notes: [[D&C 82 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 82 moves from forgiveness and heightened accountability (vv. 1–10) to the founding of a covenant binding nine named Church leaders — Edward Partridge, Newel K. Whitney, A. Sidney Gilbert, Sidney Rigdon, Joseph Smith, John Whitmer, Oliver Cowdery, W. W. Phelps, and Martin Harris — to jointly manage stewardships for the poor in both Kirtland and [[Zion]] (vv. 11–19). The chapter closes with the covenant's terms of permanence and penalty (vv. 20–21) and closing counsel on wealth, judgment, and steadfastness (vv. 22–24). Historians commonly associate this organization with what later sources call the 'United Firm,' though that name never appears in the text itself.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. Forgiveness, universal sinfulness, and a warning to watch (vv. 1–7). 2. A 'new commandment' framed as directions for salvation, with an explicit conditional promise — obedience carries the Lord's promise, disobedience has none (vv. 8–10). 3. Naming of nine stewards and their charge over the poor and bishopric affairs in Kirtland and [[Zion]] (vv. 11–16). 4. Equal claims according to just wants and needs, improvement of talents into the common storehouse, and the order's conditional permanence and breach penalty (vv. 17–21). 5. Closing counsel on wealth, deferred judgment, and steadfastness (vv. 22–24).
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter joins [[Repentance and Forgiveness]] with ongoing accountability: forgiveness in v. 1 does not cancel the warning to cease sin in vv. 2 and 7, and 'of him unto whom much is given much is required' (v. 3) ties access to revelation to heightened responsibility. Verse 10 states a conditional divine obligation — the Lord's promise holds only when his word is kept — a principle later expressed at greater length in D&C 130:20-21, though that later text should be read as a conceptual echo rather than a demonstrated development of this verse. The [[Consecration]] material (vv. 11-19) combines a named covenant, care for the poor, stewardships with equal claims apportioned by just wants and needs, productive use of talents, and a common storehouse; no one element should be read as the whole system. Verse 19 supplies its ethical center: seeking the neighbor's interest while acting with an eye single to God's glory. The order's permanence is explicitly conditional ('inasmuch as you sin not,' v. 20), and covenant-breaking carries church discipline and the 'buffetings of Satan' (v. 21). The closing counsel to 'leave judgment alone with me' (v. 23) tempers, but does not eliminate, the church-administered discipline authorized in v. 21.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Several phrases closely echo New Testament language. The greater-gifts/greater-requirement maxim in D&C 82:3 parallels [[Luke 12]], while the unusual phrase about making friends with the mammon of unrighteousness in D&C 82:22 echoes [[Luke 16]]. D&C 82:6 also resembles [[Romans 3]] in its description of all going out of the way and none doing good. These parallels establish scriptural reuse or shared wording, but by themselves do not determine why that wording was selected or settle broader authorship questions.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verse 22's 'make unto yourselves friends with the mammon of unrighteousness' reuses the distinctive phrase of the unjust-steward parable (Luke 16:9), though the surrounding clause differs: Luke promises 'everlasting habitations,' while D&C 82:22 promises only that 'they will not destroy you.' Verse 19's 'eye single to the glory of God' draws on the same single-eye idiom found in the Sermon at the Temple (3 Nephi 13:22, paralleling Matthew 6:22) to describe undivided intent, though shared KJV idiom does not by itself prove deliberate allusion. Verse 6's 'none doeth good... all have gone out of the way' closely parallels Romans 3:12's language on universal sinfulness.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. What does it mean that God is only 'bound' when commandments are kept (v. 10) — a metaphysical law, or God's own chosen consistency? 2. Why does v. 11 bind specific named leaders rather than the Church generally? 3. Does the warning that former sins 'return' (v. 7) describe a defined mechanism, or intensify the warning against relapse after forgiveness? 4. What practical distinction did the recipients understand between 'equal claims' and equal possessions (v. 17)? 5. Who are the 'friends' contemplated in v. 22, and what conduct did making them entail? 6. How does 'leave judgment alone with me' (v. 23) relate to the church discipline explicitly authorized in v. 21 — complementary or in tension?
