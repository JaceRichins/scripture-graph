# D&C 25 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 25]] · My notes: [[D&C 25 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
D&C 25 combines personal address with general discipleship. Emma Smith is assured of forgiveness and divine calling, then entrusted with work involving consolation, scribal service, scripture exposition, exhortation, writing, learning, and sacred hymns. The closing declaration that the revelation is the Lord's voice "unto all" invites broader application, but it does not erase the personal and marital setting of several commands.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A useful reading divides the chapter into five movements:

1. **Identity, promise, and reassurance (vv. 1–4):** daughterhood in God's kingdom, conditional promises, forgiveness, calling, and counsel about what has been withheld.
2. **Relational and ecclesial callings (vv. 5–9):** consolation, travel, scribal work, ordination, scripture exposition, exhortation, the Holy Ghost, writing, and learning.
3. **Reordered desire and sacred song (vv. 10–12):** seeking better things, selecting hymns, and understanding righteous song as prayer.
4. **Concluding exhortations and promise (vv. 13–15):** joy, covenant loyalty, meekness, rejection of pride, commandment keeping, and a crown of righteousness.
5. **Universal scope (v. 16):** the personally addressed revelation closes as a divine voice unto all.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter joins grace and covenant responsibility without reducing either one to the other. Emma's forgiveness is announced in verse 3, yet she is still commanded to remain faithful, cleave to her [[Covenants]], and practice continual [[Obedience]]. Verses 11–12 give sacred music a theological role: heartfelt righteous song functions as [[Prayer]] and is associated with divine blessing. The chapter also connects spiritual empowerment with [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]], [[Scripture Study]], writing, and [[Education and Learning]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The expression "elect lady" in verse 3 closely parallels 2 John 1:1. This may provide scriptural coloring for Emma's designation, but the shared phrase alone cannot show whether it is a formal office or prove deliberate dependence on 2 John. The repeated qualification "as it shall be given thee" in verses 7 and 11 emphasizes received guidance in both teaching and hymn selection.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter is framed by divine speech: it opens with a command to hear the Lord's voice and closes by declaring that voice to be unto all. Repetition links its parts. Meekness appears in both the instruction to console Joseph and the final warning against [[Pride]], making [[Humility]] a governing disposition for Emma's varied work. Verses 11–12 also form a compact commission-and-rationale unit: Emma is to select hymns because righteous song is pleasing to God and can function as prayer.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Which instructions arise from Emma's unique circumstances, and which principles does verse 16 extend to all disciples?
- What can verse 7 establish about Emma's authority to teach, and what questions about the meaning of "ordained" remain unanswered by the chapter?
- How do the assurance of forgiveness in verse 3 and the conditional language of verses 2 and 15 illuminate the relationship between grace and continuing discipleship?
- What changes when congregational song is approached as prayer rather than performance?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the phrase "elect lady" with 2 John 1:1, the theology of hymns and heartfelt song with Colossians 3:16 and Ephesians 5:19, and the "crown of righteousness" with 2 Timothy 4:8. These parallels clarify the chapter's biblical vocabulary, although verbal similarity by itself does not establish a direct quotation or a single intended source.
