# D&C 10 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[D&C 10]] · My notes: [[D&C 10 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> 1–26, Satan stirs up wicked men to oppose the Lord’s work; 27–33, He seeks to destroy the souls of men; 34–52, The gospel is to go to the Lamanites and all nations through the Book of Mormon; 53–63, The Lord will establish His Church and His gospel among men; 64–70, He will gather the repentant into His Church and will save the obedient.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Loss and recommissioning (vv.1-5)** — the gift is restored; the work must continue with both diligence and recognition of human limits.
2. **The alleged plot (vv.6-33)** — the chapter describes altered writings and warns that deceptive retaliation remains morally culpable regardless of the other side's dishonesty.
3. **Protective instructions (vv.34-37)** — disclosure is delayed for two related but distinct reasons: protecting the translator until the work is finished (vv.34-35), and the general difficulty of judging character (v.37).
4. **The replacement record (vv.38-45)** — translation shifts to the plates of Nephi rather than repeating the lost wording.
5. **The record's intended audience (vv.46-52)** — earlier prayers extend its purpose from the Lamanites to every nation and people.
6. **Gospel, gathering, and church (vv.53-70)** — Christ identifies himself (v.57), explains the "other sheep," invites all to come, and defines his church by repentance and endurance.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
D&C 10 joins [[Prayer]] with disciplined effort rather than treating prayer as a substitute for work (vv.4-5). Its sharpest ethical statement is v.28: suspecting that someone else is lying does not excuse answering with one's own deception, "for such are not exempt from the justice of God."

The closing definition of Christ's church is notably Christ-centered and behavioral: "whosoever repenteth and cometh unto me, the same is my church" (v.67), and "whosoever declareth more or less than this... is not of my church" (v.68). Read alongside v.56's criticism of churches "built up... to get gain," this frames [[Repentance]] and coming to Christ — not doctrinal elaboration or institutional claims — as the boundary of "my church" named in this passage. This need not erase the more institutional sense of "church" used earlier in the same chapter (vv.53-56); the two senses sit side by side without the text fully reconciling them. Those who endure in Christ's church "to the end" are promised to be established "upon my rock," against which "the gates of hell shall not prevail" (v.69) — language that echoes, without citing, Matthew 16:18.

The invitation is universal in scope but conditional in practice: the gospel is meant to be "free unto all... whatsoever nation, kindred, tongue, or people" (v.51), yet gathering and establishment are repeatedly conditioned on people not hardening their hearts and being willing to come (vv.53, 65-66).

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The anonymous pronouns in the opposition narrative require care. The text repeatedly says what “they” intend and what Satan places in their hearts, but it does not name most participants or present their altered manuscript. Historical claims about particular conspirators therefore require evidence beyond this chapter.

“Build up” functions as a recurring counterword to “destroy.” Opponents seek to destroy the work; Christ says the new record will build up what people have already received and build up his church. This repetition links the manuscript crisis to the chapter's larger restorative purpose.

D&C 10:57-59 deliberately invokes the language of [[John 1]] and [[John 10]]. The verbal relationship is clear; whether it reflects revelatory quotation, biblical idiom in composition, or both is a separate interpretive question.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verses 20-27 build Satan's activity through a chain of escalating verbs — stirreth, flattereth, draggeth, causeth to catch themselves, goeth up and down — culminating in conspirators caught in their "own snare" (v.26). The if-then logic of vv.15-19 dramatizes one specific scenario: reproducing the same translated words would, given an already-altered copy in the conspirators' hands, be twisted into an accusation of fraud — a device illustrating why the text treats retranslation as a trap in the case described, rather than a formally exhaustive no-win argument covering every conceivable outcome.

More broadly, the chapter's structure is expansive rather than merely defensive: lost pages lead to a replacement record, which leads to remembered prayers, which leads to a worldwide audience, which leads to a definition of Christ's church. The relationship between D&C 10:38-45 and the Book of Mormon passages describing the small plates as a distinct record kept for an undisclosed "wise purpose" ([[1 Nephi 9]]; [[Words of Mormon 1]]) is a striking internal interlock — though it should be read with the caveat that the small-plates account was translated after this revelation was received, so the coherence is at least partly an expected consequence of composition order rather than solely independent, self-authenticating evidence.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Scriptural Intertextuality
> D&C 10:57-59 closely reproduces or adapts the language of John 1:5, John 1:11, and John 10:16. The verbal proximity is strong evidence of deliberate Johannine intertextuality in the English text.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · strong, well-hedged evidentiary claim · [[Scriptural Intertextuality in D&C 10|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Historical Dating / Internal Consistency *(tentative)*
> Traditional chapter dating places this revelation in summer 1828, before the Church's formal organization in April 1830; the conditional future-tense wording of verse 53 ("I will establish my church") is consistent with, though it does not by itself independently prove, that pre-organization timeframe.
> — strength 0.2 · confidence 0.3 · dating disputed, needs sourcing

> [!evidence] Internal Literary Coherence *(tentative)*
> D&C 10's replacement-record instructions cohere with Book of Mormon passages that describe a distinct record made for a wise purpose not initially known to its keeper and later included for a purpose known to God. This is meaningful evidence of internal literary integration, although its explanation is disputed.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.8 · real coherence, evidentiary framing overstated

> [!evidence] Internal Textual/Rhetorical Analysis *(tentative)*
> The if-then scenario in verses 15-19, in which the text asserts Satan pre-planned the exact objection critics could raise from comparing a fresh translation to an altered copy, is sometimes cited as evidence the revelation shows foreknowledge of the situation; a skeptical reading holds this instead reflects reasoned, after-the-fact justification for why the same portion could not simply be retranslated.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · disputed, well-hedged

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does it mean that the addressee "lost" the gift of translation along with the writings (v.2), and how does that shape the counsel to be diligent "unto the end" (v.4)?
- Why does the Lord respond to a lost, alterable manuscript by directing a different, "more particular" record (vv.40-45) rather than simply forbidding retranslation without an alternative?
- Verses 34-37 give two distinct reasons for temporary secrecy — protecting the translator and the difficulty of judging character. How do these relate, and does either still apply once the work is finished?
- Does "more or less than this" in v.68 condemn doctrinal alteration, needless contention, claims beyond the immediate formula of v.67, or some combination?
- How do the more institutional uses of "church" in vv.53-56 relate to the discipleship-based definition in vv.67-69?
- What is gained — and what remains unproved — by reading the replacement-record instructions (vv.38-45) alongside [[1 Nephi 9]] and [[Words of Mormon 1]], given that the small plates were translated after this revelation?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare D&C 10:38-45 with [[1 Nephi 9]] (the stated "wise purpose" for a second record) and [[Words of Mormon 1]] (Mormon's inclusion of that record for a purpose known to the Lord) — bearing in mind the small plates were translated after this revelation was received. Compare D&C 10:57-60 with [[John 1]], [[John 10]], and [[3 Nephi 15]] for the Johannine sayings and their explicit application to the Nephites. Compare D&C 10:65 with [[3 Nephi 10]] for the hen-gathering image and its emphasis on human willingness, and see [[3 Nephi 21]] for the "remnant"/"branch of Jacob" language that the verified cross-reference index ties most closely to this chapter. [[D&C 11]], received in the same period, shares diligence, other-nations, and gathering language worth reading alongside this chapter.
