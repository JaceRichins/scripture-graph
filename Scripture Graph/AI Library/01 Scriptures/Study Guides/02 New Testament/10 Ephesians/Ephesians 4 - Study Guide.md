# Ephesians 4 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Ephesians 4]] · My notes: [[Ephesians 4 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> There is one Lord, one faith, and one baptism—Apostles and prophets are essential to the Church—The Saints are exhorted to live righteously—They are sealed unto the day of redemption.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **A worthy walk and the basis of unity (4:1–6):** character and peace lead into the sevenfold confession of one body, Spirit, hope, Lord, [[Faith]], [[Baptism]], and God and Father.

2. **Different gifts serving one body (4:7–16):** Christ gives grace and ministries so that the saints may serve, mature, resist deception, speak truth in love, and build up the body.

3. **A changed way of life (4:17–24):** the former walk is rejected through the imagery of putting off the old man, inward renewal, and putting on the new man.

4. **Concrete communal practices (4:25–32):** falsehood, theft, destructive speech, bitterness, and malice give way to [[Truth]], useful labor, generous sharing, edification, kindness, and [[Repentance and Forgiveness]].
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter holds unity and diversity together. The repeated word “one” in verses 4–6 gives the theological basis for unity, while verses 7–16 insist that grace, roles, and contributions differ. [[Apostles]], [[Prophets]], and the other named ministries are means toward service and corporate maturity, not ends in themselves.

Maturity is measured by relation to Christ and by the health of the whole body. Doctrinal stability matters, but it is joined to speaking [[Truth]] in love. The closing commands similarly make holiness relational: truthful speech protects fellow members, labor enables generosity, constructive speech ministers [[Grace]], and forgiveness imitates God's action through Christ.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
In the KJV, “vocation” in verse 1 means calling, as the repeated language of being called in verse 4 confirms. “Conversation” in verse 22 refers contextually to a former manner of life, not merely to spoken words.

The “old man” and “new man” are contrasting identity-and-conduct metaphors. The surrounding plurals and the body imagery suggest a corporate dimension, although individual moral renewal is also plainly involved.

“Perfect man” in verse 13 should not automatically be read as an isolated, sinless individual. In context it describes movement from corporate childhood toward mature humanity measured by Christ's fulness.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter is framed by two contrasting walks: walking worthily of the calling (verse 1) and no longer walking as other Gentiles walk (verse 17). Repetition gives verses 4–6 a compact confessional rhythm, while the extended body metaphor in verses 12–16 depicts both dependence on Christ the head and interdependence among members.

Ephesians 4:8 adapts [[Psalm 68]], changing the KJV Psalm's receiving of gifts into Christ's giving of gifts in order to serve the chapter's argument. Verse 26 reuses [[Psalm 4]] in wording closer to the ancient Greek form of that Psalm. Verse 25 closely parallels [[Zechariah 8]]. The old/new-person and virtue-list material also strongly parallels [[Colossians 3]], while the body imagery invites comparison with [[1 Corinthians 12]].
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary/Source Comparison *(tentative)*
> The ethical list in Ephesians 4:22-32 (put off old man/put on new man, put away lying, anger instructions) closely parallels Colossians 3:8-10, which shares the old-man/new-man language and several of the same vices (anger, wrath, malice, lying), suggesting a shared Pauline paraenetic (ethical exhortation) tradition or common source material between the two letters.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · parallel established; source relationship disputed

> [!evidence] Textual/Exegetical Comparison *(tentative)*
> Ephesians 4:8 renders Psalm 68:18 as Christ having 'gave gifts unto men,' whereas the Hebrew/KJV Psalm text has the ascending figure 'received gifts for men' (or 'from among men'). This shift from receiving to giving is a deliberate exegetical adaptation rather than a straightforward citation, showing the author reading the psalm christologically.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.6 · disputed interpretation of a real textual crux

> [!evidence] Theological Language Pattern *(tentative)*
> The coordinated sequence of one Spirit, one Lord, and one God and Father is evidence for a triadic pattern in the chapter's account of Christian unity. It is consistent with later Christian reflection on the Godhead but does not by itself articulate the detailed metaphysical claims of later creeds.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.6 · interpretive extraction, not a foregrounded textual feature
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does “the lower parts of the earth” in verse 9 mean the earth itself, Christ's earthly descent, the grave, or the realm of the dead? The grammar and immediate context permit more than one reading.

- Does “till we all come” in verse 13 specify how long each ministry in verse 11 continues, or only state the ministries' goal?

- How does the command to speak truth “in love” constrain both dishonest avoidance and loveless confrontation?

- Why does verse 28 make generosity to the needy, rather than merely private respectability, the goal of honest labor?

- How should the urgent limit placed on anger in verses 26–27 shape practices of reconciliation?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the ethical list of verses 22-32 with [[Colossians 3]]:8-10 for the closely related old-man/new-man and vice-list material. Compare the body-with-many-members metaphor (vv. 15-16) with [[1 Corinthians 12]] for a fuller treatment of differentiated spiritual gifts within one body.
