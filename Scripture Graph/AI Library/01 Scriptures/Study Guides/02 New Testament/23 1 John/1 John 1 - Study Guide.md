## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Manifestation and witness (vv. 1–2):** sensory testimony concerning the Word of life.

2. **Declaration and purpose (vv. 3–4):** fellowship and fullness of joy.

3. **The governing message (v. 5):** God is light, without darkness.

4. **Tests of claimed fellowship (vv. 6–7):** walking in darkness contradicts fellowship; walking in light accompanies fellowship and cleansing.

5. **Denial and confession of sin (vv. 8–10):** two denials surround the positive alternative of confession.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter holds moral accountability and divine cleansing together. Human beings must not deny their sin, but forgiveness is not represented as self-produced: God forgives and cleanses, and the blood of Jesus Christ is explicitly named as the means of cleansing (vv. 7, 9). This makes the chapter especially relevant to [[Sin]], [[Repentance and Forgiveness]], and the [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]].

Fellowship also has both vertical and communal dimensions. The recipients are invited into fellowship with the witnesses, while that fellowship is said to be with the Father and his Son (v. 3). Verse 7's “one with another” may refer primarily to believers' mutual fellowship, though fellowship between God and believers remains possible in the immediate context.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The verbs “say” and “walk” create a sustained contrast between profession and practice. “Walk” is best read as a manner of life: the issue is not merely what a person claims but whether that claim is enacted. The expressions “have no sin” (v. 8) and “have not sinned” (v. 10) overlap but are not identical. The first can deny a present condition of sinfulness; the second denies sinful acts. The chapter rejects both forms of denial.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The prologue's repeated sensory verbs—heard, seen, looked upon, and handled—give the declaration a testimonial form. Its vocabulary also closely resembles [[John 1]], especially beginning, Word, life, light, and darkness. That literary relationship is clear, but this chapter alone cannot determine whether common authorship, shared community tradition, or deliberate imitation best explains it.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What precise relationship does the text envision among proclamation, fellowship, and joy in verses 3–4?

- In verse 7, does “fellowship one with another” primarily mean fellowship among believers, fellowship between God and believers, or both?

- How does the immediate context help distinguish “have no sin” in verse 8 from “have not sinned” in verse 10?

- Why is God described as both “faithful” and “just” when forgiving confessed sins?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the Word, life, light, and darkness vocabulary with [[John 1]], and compare the ethical connection between light and truth with John 3:19–21. Read [[1 John 2]] immediately afterward: its opening warning against sin and teaching about Jesus Christ as advocate guards against two opposite misreadings of this chapter—despair over the reality of sin and complacency toward continued sin.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
