# 1 John 5 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 John 5]] · My notes: [[1 John 5 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Saints are born of God through belief in Christ—Water, blood, and the Spirit testify of Christ—Belief in Christ is required in order to gain eternal life.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Belief, love, and victory (vv. 1–5):** New birth is associated with belief in Jesus Christ; love of God and his children is tied to keeping commandments; faith overcomes the world.
2. **Witness concerning the Son (vv. 6–12):** Water, blood, and Spirit bear witness, followed by God's record that life is in his Son.
3. **Assurance and prayer (vv. 13–17):** The stated purpose is assurance of eternal life. Confidence in [[Prayer]] is qualified by God's will and applied to intercession for a sinning brother.
4. **Concluding knowledge and warning (vv. 18–21):** Three statements beginning with 'we know' contrast God's children with the wicked world and culminate in knowledge of the true God, followed by a warning against idols.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Regeneration and love**: being 'born of God' (vv.1, 4, 18) is bound to loving God's children and keeping his commandments (vv.1-3).
- **Faith as victory**: vv.4-5 name faith in Jesus as the Son of God as what 'overcometh the world.'
- **Witness/testimony**: the Spirit, water, and blood 'agree in one' testimony (v.8), and receiving 'the witness of God' is greater than human testimony (v.9). The 'heavenly witnesses' wording of v.7 (Father, Word, Holy Ghost) reflects a later textual addition (the Comma Johanneum) absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts; it should not be relied on alone to establish [[The Godhead]].
- **[[Eternal Life]] in the Son**: 'he that hath the Son hath life' (v.12); the letter's stated purpose (v.13) is that believers may know they have this life.
- **[[Prayer]] bounded by God's will**: confidence in answered prayer is explicitly qualified by asking 'according to his will' (v.14), applied to intercession for a sinning brother short of 'a sin unto death' (vv.16-17, undefined in the text).
- **'Sinneth not' in context**: v.18's claim that one born of God 'sinneth not' should be read alongside 1 John 1:8 and 2:1, where the same author acknowledges believers may sin — the chapter is unlikely to teach absolute, unqualified sinlessness.
- **Avoiding idols**: the abrupt closing charge (v.21) follows the chapter's insistence on the true God and true [[Testimony]], though its precise occasion is not stated in the text.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter repeatedly uses a family vocabulary: born of God, him that begat, him that is begotten, children, and brother. This language binds belief, love, and ethical conduct together. It also repeats legal or testimonial terms—witness, record, testified—so that faith in the Son is presented as a response to divine testimony rather than as an unsupported feeling.

The phrase 'sin unto death' is not defined here. Any precise identification should therefore be labeled as an interpretation rather than as the chapter's explicit teaching.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The repeated 'we know' in verses 18–20 creates a compact conclusion: believers know the implications of divine birth, know their allegiance amid a wicked world, and know that the Son has given understanding. The final command concerning idols may sound abrupt, but it coheres with the preceding contrast between the true God and false allegiances.

Readers should also distinguish literary analysis from textual criticism. The heavenly-witness wording in verse 7 belongs to the supplied canonical text, but its absence from the earliest Greek manuscript tradition makes it unsafe to use as though its originality were undisputed.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual Criticism
> The supplied canonical wording of verse 7 explicitly names the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost as heavenly witnesses. Text-critically, this expanded wording—commonly called the Comma Johanneum—is absent from the earliest Greek manuscript tradition and is omitted from the main text of modern critical Greek editions. It is therefore weak evidence for the wording of the earliest recoverable text, even though it has a substantial history in the Latin and later received-text traditions.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · broadly accepted textual criticism

> [!evidence] Textual Criticism
> The Trinitarian formula in verse 7 ('there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one') is widely regarded by New Testament textual critics as a later addition (the 'Johannine Comma'), absent from all early Greek manuscripts and from the earliest versions, entering the Latin tradition first and only appearing in a handful of very late Greek manuscripts before being included in the Textus Receptus underlying the KJV.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 0.9 · broadly accepted textual criticism · [[Textual Criticism in 1 John 5|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Exegetical Interpretation *(tentative)*
> Verses 16-17's distinction between 'a sin which is not unto death' and 'a sin unto death' is one of the most exegetically disputed passages in the epistle; proposed identifications of the 'sin unto death' include apostasy/willful renunciation of Christ, sin against the Holy Ghost, and (in some readings) a sin resulting in literal physical death, with no scholarly consensus on which is meant.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.8 · disputed, but 'no consensus' claim unsourced

> [!evidence] Exegetical Interpretation *(tentative)*
> Verse 20's closing clause 'This is the true God, and eternal life' has a grammatically ambiguous antecedent for 'This' (houtos): it may refer back to the nearest noun, 'his Son Jesus Christ,' or to 'him that is true' (understood as the Father) mentioned earlier in the same verse; commentators are divided.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · grammatically disputed, no sources cited
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What does verse 16's 'sin unto death' most plausibly refer to, and how should that shape how believers pray for others?
- Does the antecedent of 'This is the true God' in verse 20 point to the Father or to Jesus Christ, and how does that affect the chapter's Christology?
- How does the manuscript history of verse 7 change (or not change) how the chapter's teaching on [[The Godhead]] should be read?
- How do [[1 John 1]] and [[1 John 2]]'s acknowledgment of sin constrain an absolute-sinlessness reading of verse 18?
- How does the qualification 'according to his will' shape the promises about [[Prayer]]?
- Why does the letter end so abruptly with a warning about idols, and how does that charge relate to everything argued in vv.1-20?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
