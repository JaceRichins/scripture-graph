# Matthew 7 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Matthew 7]] · My notes: [[Matthew 7 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Jesus concludes the Sermon on the Mount—He commands, Judge not; ask of God; beware of false prophets—He promises salvation to those who do the will of the Father.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Judgment, self-examination, and discernment (vv. 1–6):** reciprocal judgment; the mote and beam; care with holy things.
2. **Asking and acting toward others (vv. 7–12):** ask, seek, and knock; the good Father; the rule summarizing the law and the prophets.
3. **Contrasting ways and tests (vv. 13–23):** narrow and broad ways; true and false prophets; profession contrasted with doing the Father's will.
4. **Two builders (vv. 24–27):** both hear, but only the doer builds on rock.
5. **Narrative conclusion (vv. 28–29):** the hearers respond with astonishment at Jesus's authority.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter connects [[Prayer]] with confidence in the Father's goodness, but describes the promised gifts as "good things" rather than guaranteeing every desired outcome. It links [[Salvation]] and [[Obedience]] by distinguishing verbal profession from doing the Father's will and hearing from doing Jesus's sayings. The warning about false prophets also makes discernment necessary: outward appearance and impressive claims must be tested by fruit.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
'Strait' in verses 13–14 is an older English word meaning narrow or constricted; it is distinct from 'straight,' meaning not crooked. This matches the passage's contrast between the strait or narrow way and the wide or broad way. In verse 23, 'I never knew you' plainly functions as rejection, but Matthew 7 alone does not establish whether the expression is a specifically legal formula.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The argument gains force through memorable contrasts and repetitions. Ask/seek/knock forms a threefold appeal. Bread/stone and fish/serpent contrast nourishing gifts with harmful substitutes. The final movement repeatedly divides hearers between two destinations: broad and narrow ways, good and corrupt trees, accepted and rejected claimants, rock and sand. The storms strike both houses; the foundation, not freedom from adversity, distinguishes their outcomes.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Explicit Narrative Contrast
> The rejected speakers claim prophecy, exorcism, and wonderful works in Jesus's name, yet are identified as workers of iniquity. This is strong textual evidence that spectacular religious claims or works are not presented as sufficient proof of faithful discipleship.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · strong explicit contrast; unanimous · [[Explicit Narrative Contrast in Matthew 7|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intratextual Contextual Evidence *(tentative)*
> Within the chapter, the commands to recognize what is holy and to beware of false prophets provide strong contextual evidence that "Judge not" is not a blanket prohibition on every act of discernment. The chapter instead opposes hypocritical or presumptuous judgment while still requiring careful evaluation.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.8 · strong contextual reading; compositional intent disputed

> [!evidence] Textual/Translation Dependency *(tentative)*
> The Book of Mormon's 3 Nephi 14 reproduces Matthew 7 in English wording that verified indexing shows overlapping with 31 verses' worth of text, i.e., essentially the King James Version's own phrasing (including its distinctive archaisms like 'strait gate').
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.7 · English dependence plausible; metric and implications disputed
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How do verses 5, 6, and 15–20 qualify a reading of "Judge not" as a prohibition on all discernment?
- Why does verse 11 promise "good things" rather than simply repeating whatever was asked for?
- What kinds of "fruits" offer reliable evidence about a teacher, and how long might such fruit take to become visible?
- Why are prophecy, exorcism, and wonderful works insufficient in verses 21–23?
- In the builders' comparison, what changes when hearing becomes doing?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
