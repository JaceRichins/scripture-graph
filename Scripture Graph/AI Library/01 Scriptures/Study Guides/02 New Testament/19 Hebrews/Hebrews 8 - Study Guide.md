## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The heavenly high priest and sanctuary (8:1-2):** the argument's stated “sum.”
2. **Priestly offering and the earthly pattern (8:3-5):** earthly priests serve an example and shadow patterned after heavenly things.
3. **The better covenant thesis (8:6-7):** the superior ministry corresponds to better promises.
4. **Jeremiah's new-covenant oracle (8:8-12):** covenant failure is answered by internalized law, covenant belonging, knowledge of God, and forgiveness.
5. **The author's inference (8:13):** calling the covenant “new” renders the first old.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The new covenant is not portrayed as lawlessness: God promises to place his laws in minds and write them in hearts (8:10). The oracle joins this inward appropriation to covenant belonging, knowledge of God, mercy, and forgiveness (8:10-12).

The promise is expressly addressed to Israel and Judah (8:8), so the passage should not casually be converted into a claim that God rejected Israel. At the same time, Hebrews draws a strong discontinuity conclusion: calling the covenant “new” makes the first old and ready to vanish (8:13). The chapter does not independently enumerate which commandments, ordinances, or institutions that conclusion includes. These tensions are central to studying [[Covenants]], the [[Law of Moses]], and [[Repentance and Forgiveness]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Hebrews 8:9 reads “I regarded them not,” while the KJV form of Jeremiah 31:32 reads “although I was an husband unto them.” Hebrews corresponds in sense to the ancient Greek form of Jeremiah. This is strong evidence that the citation reflects a Greek scriptural textual tradition, but it does not identify a particular manuscript or prove direct copying from one surviving form.

In 8:5, “example,” “shadow,” and “pattern” relate earthly priestly service and the Mosaic tabernacle to heavenly things. “Shadow” indicates a derivative and incomplete correspondence in this argument; it does not describe the earthly sanctuary as fraudulent or purposeless.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter moves from a compressed thesis to extended scriptural support. Verses 1-6 summarize the heavenly-priesthood and better-covenant claims; verses 8-12 present Jeremiah's oracle; verse 13 adds the author's deduction from the designation “new.” Distinguishing the quoted oracle from that editorial deduction helps prevent Hebrews' application from being attributed directly to Jeremiah.

The oracle progresses from covenant failure (8:9) to inward law and belonging (8:10), knowledge of God (8:11), and forgiveness (8:12). That final promise becomes one important component of the later sacrificial argument in [[Hebrews 10]], alongside the book's other priesthood, sanctuary, and offering themes.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual-Version Correspondence
> At the end of the covenant-breaking clause, Hebrews 8:9 says that God "regarded them not," whereas the KJV rendering of the Hebrew form of Jeremiah 31:32 says that God was "an husband unto them." The wording in Hebrews corresponds to the ancient Greek form of Jeremiah, supplying strong evidence that this portion of the citation derives from a Greek scriptural textual tradition.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 0.9 · Well-established textual-version correspondence · [[Textual-Version Correspondence in Hebrews 8|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Explicit Prophetic Intertext
> Jeremiah's pre-Christian promise of a new covenant provides strong textual evidence for the narrower claim made in Hebrews: Israel's own prophetic scriptures anticipated a covenantal development beyond the exodus covenant. It does not by itself settle every question about which Mosaic practices cease, continue, or are transformed.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · Strong evidence for the narrow stated conclusion · [[Explicit Prophetic Intertext in Hebrews 8|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Textual-Critical *(tentative)*
> The wording of the quotation in Hebrews 8:9 ('because they continued not in my covenant, and I regarded them not') is commonly noted by textual scholars to follow the Greek Septuagint reading of Jeremiah 31:32 rather than the Hebrew Masoretic Text's idiom (traditionally rendered 'although I was an husband unto them').
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.8 · Known textual difference, but this formulation was not source-checked

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
How does “finding fault with them” in 8:8 qualify the statement about the first covenant in 8:7? Does the passage locate the deficiency in the covenant, in human covenant-breaking, or in both?

What exactly is said to be “ready to vanish away” in 8:13: the covenant as an arrangement, the earthly priestly administration, particular ordinances, or some combination of these?

How does the promise that all will know the Lord (8:11) relate to the continuing presence of teaching within Christian communities?
