## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Grace and calling**: salvation and holy calling come 'not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace' (v.9), grace given in Christ 'before the world began' and now 'made manifest' through Christ's appearing (vv.9-10). See [[Grace]]. The 'before the world began' language most directly describes the timelessness of God's purpose; whether it also implies anything about human premortal existence is a matter of interpretation, not settled by the verse itself. See [[Premortal Life]].
- **Death overcome, immortality disclosed**: Christ 'hath abolished death, and hath brought life and immortality to light through the gospel' (v.10). Since physical death continued after Christ's ministry, 'abolished' is best read as death's decisive defeat rather than its literal cessation. See [[Eternal Life]].
- **Gifts stirred by the laying on of hands**: Timothy is told to 'stir up the gift of God' that is in him 'by the putting on of my hands' (v.6); the verse does not name the specific gift or occasion. A related instance appears in 1 Timothy 4:14, which attributes the act to 'the presbytery' rather than to Paul alone — the two texts are not fully harmonized within the chapter itself. See [[Spiritual Gifts]].
- **Testimony under adversity**: 'not ashamed... partaker of the afflictions of the gospel' (v.8) and 'I know whom I have believed' (v.12) pair bold testimony with confidence in God's ability to keep what has been entrusted, without claiming exemption from suffering. See [[Testimony]], [[Adversity]].

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Two threads run through the chapter. The first is repeated 'ashamed / not ashamed' language: Timothy must not be ashamed of the testimony or of Paul's imprisonment (v.8); Paul himself is 'not ashamed' amid suffering (v.12); and Onesiphorus 'was not ashamed of my chain' (v.16). This turns the closing anecdote (vv.15-18) into a concrete illustration of the abstract charge given earlier, contrasting those in Asia who 'turned away' with Onesiphorus who 'sought me out very diligently.' The second is a thread of something committed or entrusted: Paul trusts God to keep 'that which I have committed unto him' (v.12), and Timothy is charged to keep 'that good thing which was committed unto thee' (v.14) — though the exact object of Paul's own commitment in v.12 is ambiguous in the English text.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual/Doctrinal Interpretation *(tentative)*
> The phrase 'grace ... given us in Christ Jesus before the world began' (1:9) is sometimes read as consonant with a doctrine of premortal foreordination, but the grammar most directly describes God's purpose/plan predating creation rather than asserting the individual premortal existence of human spirits.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · disputed

> [!evidence] Historical-Critical / Authorship *(tentative)*
> Critical scholarship is divided on whether Paul personally authored the Pastoral Epistles, citing vocabulary and style differing from the undisputed Pauline letters; however, the density of specific, seemingly incidental personal names and details in this chapter (Lois, Eunice, Phygellus, Hermogenes, Onesiphorus, a specific imprisonment in Rome, prior ministry at Ephesus) is often cited — including by some scholars who doubt direct Pauline authorship of the Pastorals as a whole — as material that is harder to explain as later invented pseudonymous filler than more generic doctrinal passages.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.3 · disputed, uncited
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- In v.9, does 'before the world began' refer only to God's eternal purpose and grace, or does it also suggest something about [[Premortal Life]]? What would each reading require of the rest of the verse?
- What gift is meant in v.6, and what does the chapter leave unspecified about the occasion and manner of 'the putting on of my hands' — compare 1 Timothy 4:14, which credits 'the presbytery'?
- 'That which I have committed unto him' (v.12) — is this Paul's own life entrusted to God, the gospel entrusted to Paul, or something else? The English text does not resolve the direction of the commitment.
- Why might Paul name both those who abandoned him (Phygellus, Hermogenes) and the one who sought him out (Onesiphorus) rather than speaking only in general terms — and is 'all they which are in Asia' (v.15) literal or a generalization?
- Does the prayer for Onesiphorus 'that he may find mercy of the Lord in that day' (v.18) imply he had died? The chapter does not say so explicitly.
