# John 17 — Study Guide

## Overview

<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A useful three-part outline is:

- **17:1-5 — Jesus' work and glory:** Jesus announces that the hour has come, speaks of finishing the work given him, and asks for the glory he had with the Father before the world existed.
- **17:6-19 — Prayer for the existing disciples:** Jesus recounts their reception of the Father's words and asks that they be kept, filled with joy, protected from evil, and sanctified in truth as they are sent into the world.
- **17:20-26 — Prayer for future believers:** The prayer expands to those who will believe through the disciples' word. Jesus asks for their unity, participation in divine love, and presence with him.

This is a helpful editorial outline, not the only possible division. Giving, glory, name, word, knowing, sending, love, and oneness overlap across the movements.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
John 17:3 presents [[Eternal Life]] as knowing the Father and Jesus Christ. This gives eternal life a strong relational emphasis without excluding its other scriptural dimensions.

The chapter is important for study of [[The Godhead]]. The Father and Son are portrayed as relationally distinguishable: the Father sends, gives, loves, and glorifies the Son. They are also described through shared possessions, prior glory, love, oneness, and mutual indwelling. Later theological traditions explain this combination differently; the chapter supplies important evidence but does not settle every metaphysical dispute.

John 17:5 and 17:24 strongly portray Christ as sharing glory with the Father and being loved by him before the world's foundation. This supports Christ's preexistence but does not by itself establish a complete doctrine of [[Premortal Life]].

Sanctification is joined to [[Truth]], the Father's word, Jesus' self-consecration, and the disciples' sending. In this setting it includes consecration for faithful mission, without necessarily excluding moral transformation.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated verb **give** traces movement from the Father, through the Son, to the disciples. Authority, work, people, words, and glory are given to Jesus; eternal life, words, and glory are given onward. This pattern presents ministry as received and transmitted rather than self-originated.

The word **world** functions in several ways: it is the creation before which the Son shared glory with the Father, the sphere of Jesus' and the disciples' missions, and a resistant human order that does not know the Father and hates the disciples. Yet the world remains an intended audience of their witness. These uses do not make physical creation or every person intrinsically evil.

To manifest or declare the Father's **name** plausibly means making the Father—his identity, character, or authority—known, rather than only teaching a form of address. The connection with divine love in verse 26 supports this reading, but the chapter does not formally define 'name.'
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The prayer moves repeatedly from past accomplishment to present petition and future result: Jesus has completed the work, asks the Father to act, and anticipates future believers and their witness. Its temporal horizon reaches from before the world's foundation to those who will believe through the disciples' word.

The sending language forms a clear parallel: the Father sent Jesus into the world, and Jesus sends the disciples into the world. Likewise, the unity, glory, and love associated with Father and Son become the source and pattern of what Jesus requests for believers. [[John 20]] restates the sending pattern, while [[3 Nephi 19]] offers a thematic parallel involving prayer, indwelling, and unity; no verse-level textual overlap has been verified here.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. How do the immediate focus of “I pray not for the world” in verse 9 and the desired belief of the world in verses 21 and 23 qualify one another?
2. What aspects of the Father-Son relationship become the model for believers' oneness—shared purpose, love, presence, glory, or all of these?
3. Does “sanctify” in verses 17-19 chiefly mean moral purification, consecration for mission, or both?
4. How does being protected from evil differ from being removed from the world?
5. What does the chain of received and transmitted gifts imply about authority and stewardship in discipleship?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
