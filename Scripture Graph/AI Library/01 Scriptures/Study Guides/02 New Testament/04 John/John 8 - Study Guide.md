# John 8 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[John 8]] · My notes: [[John 8 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The woman taken in adultery is brought before Christ—Christ is the Light of the world—He again proclaims that He is the Messiah—The true children of Abraham believe in Christ—Jesus says, Before Abraham was I, Jehovah.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **8:1-11** — The woman taken in adultery brought as a legal trap (textually disputed passage; see overview)
2. **8:12-20** — 'I am the light of the world'; dispute over Jesus' self-testimony and the Father as second witness
3. **8:21-30** — 'Ye shall die in your sins'; Jesus' origin 'from above'
4. **8:31-36** — Truth and freedom through continuing in Christ's word; servant vs. Son
5. **8:37-47** — Whose children? Abraham's, God's, or the devil's
6. **8:48-59** — Abraham rejoiced to see my day; 'Before Abraham was, I am'; attempted stoning

The unit opens with Jesus entering the temple to teach (8:2) and closes with him departing after an attempted stoning (8:59), giving the chapter a loose temple-based frame — though the disputed textual status of 8:1-11 means this frame shouldn't be pressed as deliberate authorial design.

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
John 8 joins [[Justice and Mercy]] rather than opposing them: Jesus refuses to condemn the woman (8:11) yet immediately calls her to 'sin no more.' In 8:31-36, [[Freedom and Liberty]] is not political independence — bondage is defined as slavery to [[Sin]] (8:34), and freedom is granted by the Son to those who continue in his word (8:31-32, 36).

The testimony dispute (8:13-18) contributes to [[The Godhead]]: Jesus distinguishes himself from the Father while insisting their witness and mission are united. The dialogue about Abraham (8:33-58) distinguishes physical descent from covenant-shaped conduct, relevant to the [[Abrahamic Covenant]] — 'I know that ye are Abraham's seed' (8:37) is granted, but doing 'the works of Abraham' (8:39) is treated as a separate, moral criterion.

Jesus' declaration 'Before Abraham was, I am' (8:58) makes the chapter's strongest claim to existence prior to Abraham; a link to the divine self-identification of [[Exodus 3]] is plausible and influential among scholars but not an uncontested certainty.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
John 8:44 is directed at the specific hostile interlocutors the narrative portrays as seeking Jesus' death — it is not a sound basis for characterizing Jews collectively; the controversy is intra-Jewish, with Jesus, his disciples, and his opponents all inhabiting a shared Jewish scriptural world.

The repeated absolute 'I am' sayings (8:24, 8:28, 8:58) use a bare 'ego eimi' without a stated predicate — an unusual construction, and in 8:58 paired with a present-tense verb describing a time 'before Abraham.' Many scholars read this as evoking biblical divine self-identification (Exodus 3:14; the 'I am he' formulas of Isaiah), but the phrase is not intrinsically a divine title in every context, so the strength of the allusion is debated rather than certain.

Separately, Jesus stooping to 'write on the ground' (8:6, 8:8) is left textually unexplained — the content of the writing is never stated.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The chapter is organized around escalating direct address ('Verily, verily, I say unto you' at 8:34, 8:51, 8:58) and a running motif of misunderstanding: the crowd repeatedly takes Jesus' words literally or evasively (e.g., 'Will he kill himself?', 8:22; 'Thou art not yet fifty years old', 8:57) while John frames the reader to see a deeper meaning. The three-way contest over fatherhood (Abraham / God / the devil, 8:37-44) functions as an extended rhetorical structure building toward the climactic self-identification in 8:58.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual Criticism
> The account of the woman taken in adultery in John 8:1-11 is absent from several of the earliest and most important Greek witnesses, and manuscripts that include it sometimes place it elsewhere. Textual critics therefore broadly regard the passage as secondary to the earliest recoverable form of John, while its historical origin remains a separate question.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 0.9 · broadly accepted in textual scholarship · [[Textual Criticism in John 8|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary And Intertextual Evidence
> Jesus' declaration "Before Abraham was, I am" clearly asserts priority to Abraham within the narrative. Its unusual wording, the absolute "I am," and the ensuing attempt to stone Jesus strongly support a divine-identity reading and a possible allusion to biblical divine self-identification. The exact dependence on Exodus 3:14, however, is debated.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · preexistence claim widely accepted; divine-name allusion disputed · [[Literary And Intertextual Evidence in John 8|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary/Theological Interpretation *(tentative)*
> Jesus' repeated absolute declarations 'I am he' (8:24, 8:28) and, climactically, 'Before Abraham was, I am' (8:58) use a construction that many Johannine scholars read as an echo of the divine self-designation of Exodus 3:14 and the 'I am he' formula of Isaiah (e.g., Isaiah 43:10-13); the shift to present tense ('I am') to describe a time before Abraham is a notable grammatical feature that supports a claim to timeless existence rather than mere temporal priority over Abraham.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. Why does the narrative emphasize that the legal question was intended as a trap (8:6)?
2. The accusers describe an act with two participants but bring only the woman (8:3-4) — the text is silent on why; what possibilities (narrative economy, an unreliable accusation, the man's absence) does that silence leave open?
3. What did Jesus write on the ground (8:6, 8:8)? The text never says, and any specific answer goes beyond what is written.
4. What kind of 'continuing' belief does 8:31 require, and does it clarify the tense exchange beginning in 8:33 — are the same people believing in 8:30-31 and hostile from 8:37 on, or does the audience shift?
5. In what sense are Jesus' hearers Abraham's seed (8:37) but not Abraham's children (8:39)?
6. Does 'Before Abraham was, I am' (8:58) primarily assert preexistence, invoke the divine name of Exodus 3:14, or both — and what evidence would distinguish these readings?
7. How should the disputed manuscript history of 7:53-8:11 inform how the passage is taught or cited?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare [[John 9]] for the second 'light of the world' declaration (verified text-overlap), [[John 7]] for the shared temple/hostility setting (verified overlap), and [[John 12]] for the 'lifted up' motif applied to Jesus' death. The testimony dispute in 8:13-18 echoes themes in John 5's discussion of witnesses, though the index shows no verified verse-level overlap between John 5 and John 8, so treat that connection as thematic rather than textual. For Old Testament background: [[Exodus 3]] for divine self-naming, and Deuteronomy 17:6-7, Deuteronomy 19:15, Deuteronomy 22:22-24, and Leviticus 20:10 for the witness and adultery laws behind 8:1-11 and 8:17.
