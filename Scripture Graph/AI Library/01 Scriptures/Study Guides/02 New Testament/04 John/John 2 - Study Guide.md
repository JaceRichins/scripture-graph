# John 2 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[John 2]] · My notes: [[John 2 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Jesus turns water into wine in Cana—He attends the Passover, cleanses the temple, foretells His death and resurrection, and performs miracles.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter has four parts: (1) the sign at Cana (vv.1-11), explicitly called the 'beginning of miracles'; (2) a brief travel notice to Capernaum (v.12); (3) the Passover journey to Jerusalem and the temple confrontation, including Jesus's veiled prophecy of his death and resurrection (vv.13-22); and (4) a closing comment on the quality of belief his Jerusalem miracles produced (vv.23-25). John later confirms that the Cana sign is the first of a deliberately numbered pair: [[John 4]] explicitly calls the healing there, after Jesus returns to Cana, the 'second miracle' — though beyond this explicit pair, claims of a larger numbered 'signs' scheme for the whole Gospel are a scholarly reconstruction, not something John states directly.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter presents Jesus exercising creative power over the physical world ('manifested forth his glory,' v.11) and comprehensive knowledge of human hearts ('he knew what was in man,' v.25) — suggestive of, though not by itself proof of, the doctrine of [[The Godhead]]. His zeal for his Father's house (v.16) and the disciples' recollection of [[Psalm 69]] (v.17) tie his temple action to a scriptural pattern of righteous suffering. Most significantly, his saying about the temple (v.19) is explicitly identified by the narrator as pointing to his own [[Resurrection]] (v.21), understood by the disciples only after it was fulfilled (v.22) — [[Prophecy]] given cryptically and clarified in hindsight.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The KJV's 'governor of the feast' (vv.8-9) translates the official overseeing a banquet's food and drink. John's word rendered 'miracle' throughout this chapter (e.g., v.11, v.23) is elsewhere translated 'sign,' reflecting how John structures much of his Gospel's first half around such signs, of which Cana is explicitly called the first (v.11) and the Capernaum healing in [[John 4]] the second. Separately, in verses 23-24 the Greek text uses forms of the same verb (pisteuō) for both the crowd's 'believing' in Jesus and Jesus's not 'committing himself' to them — a wordplay the KJV's translation obscures, suggesting a contrast between entrusting oneself to Jesus and Jesus reciprocally entrusting himself to observers.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Knowledge is unevenly distributed throughout the chapter: the governor tastes the wine without knowing its source while the servants know (v.9); the temple interlocutors hear a saying about a building while the narrator identifies it with Jesus's body (vv.19-21); the disciples understand the saying only after the resurrection (v.22); and Jesus knows what is in every person (v.25). Remembering also matters: the disciples remember [[Psalm 69]] during the temple action (v.17) and remember Jesus's word after he rises (v.22). More speculatively, some readers see the accumulated day-count from [[John 1]] into 'the third day' (2:1) as evoking a creation-week structure; this is a live but minority literary reading, not something the text states outright. Also worth noting with appropriate caution: Jesus addresses his mother as 'Woman' both here (v.4) and, by tradition, at the cross — a possible bookend that cannot be established with certainty from this chapter alone.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Explicit Narrative Interpretation
> John 2:21-22 is strong internal evidence that the Gospel's narrator intends the temple saying to be read as a prediction of Jesus's death and resurrection, understood retrospectively by the disciples.

> [!evidence] Scriptural Quotation / Typology
> The disciples' recollection in verse 17, 'The zeal of thine house hath eaten me up,' is a direct quotation of Psalm 69:9, applying a psalm of the suffering righteous one to Jesus's temple action; the same psalm is echoed elsewhere in the Passion narratives (e.g., Psalm 69:21 and the vinegar detail), suggesting early Christian reading of Psalm 69 as prefiguring Jesus.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Why does Jesus's mother still expect action after his statement that his hour has not yet come? What narrative significance, if any, attaches to using vessels associated with Jewish purification? Why are the servants said to know the wine's source while no response from them is recorded? How does the disciples' belief in verse 11 compare with their post-resurrection belief in verse 22 and the crowd's miracle-based belief in verse 23? What exactly prevents Jesus from entrusting himself to the Jerusalem believers?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the temple account with [[Matthew 21]] (12-13), noting both the shared actions and the different narrative placement. Read [[Psalm 69]] for the context of the zeal quotation. Compare [[John 4]], which explicitly numbers the Capernaum healing as the second Cana-connected miracle. For the contrast between sign-prompted belief and Jesus's reserve, see the closing verses of this chapter alongside John's later treatment of belief and testimony.
