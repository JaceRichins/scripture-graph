## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Jesus speaks of many mansions—He says that He is the way, the truth, and the life and that to see Him is to see the Father—He promises the first and second Comforters.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Departure, destination, and return (vv. 1-4):** Jesus answers troubled hearts with trust and the promise of a prepared place.
2. **Thomas: How can we know the way? (vv. 5-7):** Jesus identifies himself as the way, truth, and life.
3. **Philip: Show us the Father (vv. 8-14):** Jesus explains his relation to the Father, points to his works, and promises continuing works and answered [[Prayer]].
4. **Love, obedience, and the Comforter (vv. 15-21):** The Spirit of truth will abide with the disciples, and Jesus will not leave them comfortless.
5. **Judas: Why not manifest yourself to the world? (vv. 22-24):** Jesus answers in terms of love, keeping his words, and divine indwelling.
6. **Teaching, peace, and departure (vv. 25-31):** The Holy Ghost will teach and remind; Jesus gives peace and explains why he has spoken beforehand.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
**Divine unity and distinction.** The Father is in the Son and works through him (vv. 9-11), while the Son also prays to, is sent by, returns to, and obeys the Father (vv. 16, 24, 28, 31). The Holy Ghost is “another Comforter,” sent by the Father in Jesus's name to abide, teach, and remind (vv. 16-17, 26). These statements support intimate unity while preserving distinguishable speakers and actions; they do not by themselves settle later metaphysical debates about [[The Godhead]].

**Love expressed through obedience.** Keeping Jesus's commandments and words repeatedly functions as the visible expression of love (vv. 15, 21, 23-24). Jesus models the pattern by doing as the Father commanded because he loves the Father (v. 31). See [[Obedience]].

**Prayer in Jesus's name.** The promise in verses 13-14 is oriented toward the Father's being glorified in the Son. This purpose cautions against treating the verses as an unconditional guarantee of every requested outcome.

**The Comforter and Christ's manifestation.** John explicitly identifies the Comforter as the Holy Ghost (v. 26). It also promises that Christ will manifest himself and that the Father and Son will make their abode with the faithful (vv. 21, 23). Calling this latter promise a formally named “Second Comforter” belongs to later LDS interpretation rather than the chapter's express wording.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The KJV uses different English words where the Greek repeats one noun: “mansions” in verse 2 and “abode” in verse 23 translate plural and singular forms of *mone*, a dwelling or staying place. The recurrence supports a literary connection between dwelling in the Father's house and the Father and Son dwelling with the faithful person, though it does not prove that the promises describe an identical event or location.

The Greek noun rendered “Comforter” in John 14:16 is *parakletos*. The same noun describes Jesus in [[1 John 2]] 2:1, where the KJV renders it “advocate.” The lexical connection permits an analogy of aid, representation, or advocacy, but it does not make Jesus and the Holy Ghost identical or establish that every function is shared.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The repeated command “Let not your heart be troubled” in verses 1 and 27 frames the chapter's principal consolation material, though verses 28-31 continue after the second occurrence. Three disciples' questions move the discourse forward: Thomas asks about the way, Philip about seeing the Father, and Judas about manifestation. Their questions shift the discussion from destination and visible display toward knowing, believing, loving, keeping, abiding, and indwelling.

The chapter also repeatedly contrasts the disciples with “the world.” The world cannot receive the Spirit and will cease to see Jesus, yet Jesus intends the world to know of his love for the Father through his obedience. The contrast is therefore genuine without making the world irrelevant to Jesus's revelatory purpose.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does 'I will come again' in verse 3 refer exclusively to the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]], or does the chapter deliberately place that hope beside resurrection appearances and spiritual indwelling?

- In what sense are the disciples' works 'greater' because Jesus goes to the Father: geographic reach, number of believers, effects of the Spirit, miraculous character, or some combination?

- How do verses 9-11 and 28-31 jointly inform a reading of the Father's and Son's unity and distinction?

- How does the shared dwelling vocabulary in verses 2 and 23 affect the interpretation of 'many mansions'?
