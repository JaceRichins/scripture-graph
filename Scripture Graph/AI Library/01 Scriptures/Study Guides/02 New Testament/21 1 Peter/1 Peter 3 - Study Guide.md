# 1 Peter 3 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Peter 3]] · My notes: [[1 Peter 3 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Husbands and wives should honor each other—Saints should live by gospel standards—Christ preached to the spirits in prison.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Marriage and witness (vv. 1–7):** wives are counseled about conduct and inward character; husbands must live knowledgeably with their wives, honor them as joint heirs, and recognize that marital wrongdoing affects [[Prayer]].
2. **A community that blesses (vv. 8–12):** all believers are called to unity, compassion, love, courtesy, nonretaliation, and peace. Verses 10–12 draw on [[Psalm 34]].
3. **Hope amid unjust suffering (vv. 13–17):** righteous conduct may not prevent suffering, but believers should answer questions about their [[Hope]] with meekness, fear, and a good conscience.
4. **Christ's suffering and triumph (vv. 18–22):** Christ suffers for sins, preaching to spirits and the Noah narrative are introduced, Noah's deliverance is related figuratively to baptism, and the section concludes with Christ exalted over every power.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verses 18-20 state that Christ, having been "put to death in the flesh, but quickened by the Spirit," "went and preached unto the spirits in prison... which sometime were disobedient... in the days of Noah." Latter-day Saints read this, together with [[D&C 138]] — which explicitly draws on this passage to describe an organized missionary effort among the spirits of the dead — as a scriptural anchor for the teaching that the gospel continues to be preached to the dead (see [[Death and the Spirit World]]).

This is a genuinely disputed passage, and honesty requires naming the alternatives: a long exegetical tradition holds Christ preached *through* Noah to Noah's living generation, so the "spirits in prison" are simply people who died long before Christ but heard the message while alive; other scholars identify the "spirits" as the disobedient angelic "sons of God" of Genesis 6, to whom the risen Christ proclaims triumph and judgment rather than offering repentance. The text alone does not resolve which reading is correct, nor does it by itself teach vicarious ordinance work — the [[Baptism for the Dead]] connection depends on additional revelation, not on 1 Peter 3 in isolation.

Verse 21 denies that baptism's saving power lies in physical washing ("not the putting away of the filth of the flesh") and locates it instead in "the answer of a good conscience toward God... by the resurrection of Jesus Christ." The underlying phrase is translation-sensitive (rendered elsewhere as "pledge" or "appeal"), so this is best read as [[Baptism]] tied to covenant and conscience rather than a fully settled technical formula.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
In this KJV text, "conversation" in verses 1, 2, and 16 denotes observable conduct rather than merely speech. That usage links the wife's quiet witness in the opening verses to the good Christian conduct that answers false accusation later in the chapter. The phrases "with meekness and fear" and "having a good conscience" also qualify how believers give an answer for their hope; verse 15 is not a warrant for combative argument.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Several verbal and thematic contrasts organize the chapter: outward adornment versus the hidden heart; evil for evil versus blessing; evil speech versus peace; suffering for evil doing versus suffering for well doing; the just versus the unjust; death in the flesh versus quickening by the Spirit; and subjection to earthly hostility versus Christ's final authority over angels and powers. The Noah-baptism correspondence is explicitly called a "like figure," but the text carefully identifies the relevant baptismal meaning as more than removal of bodily dirt.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

> The meaning of 'spirits in prison' and Christ's 'preaching' in 1 Peter 3:19-20 is one of the most disputed passages in the New Testament, with major alternative readings competing against the postmortem-evangelization interpretation.

> [!evidence] Textual Evidence
> Verses 18–20 explicitly connect Christ, the Spirit, preaching, imprisoned spirits, and disobedience in Noah's day. This is strong textual evidence that the author envisages a proclamation involving spirits associated with Noah's generation, but the compressed syntax leaves the timing, audience, and nature of the proclamation disputed.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · disputed interpretation; strong observation · [[Textual Evidence in 1 Peter 3|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary Dependence / Scriptural Citation
> 1 Peter 3:10-12 closely reproduces Psalm 34:12-16 in wording and order (refraining the tongue from evil, seeking peace, the Lord's eyes on the righteous and face against evildoers), constituting a direct literary quotation of the Psalm rather than a coincidental echo.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- How should the command to wives in verses 1–6 be interpreted alongside the command that husbands honor wives as joint heirs in verse 7?
- Does "weaker vessel" refer primarily to physical vulnerability, social disadvantage, or something else? The verse does not specify.
- Who precisely are the "spirits in prison," when did Christ preach to them, and was the preaching an offer of salvation or a proclamation of victory?
- What does "the answer of a good conscience toward God" mean, and how does that qualification shape the statement that baptism saves?
- How does the context of suffering change the way verse 15 should be applied to testimony and apologetics?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
