# 1 Corinthians 13 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Corinthians 13]] · My notes: [[1 Corinthians 13 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Paul discusses the high status of charity—Charity, a pure love, excels and exceeds almost all else.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
- **vv1-3 — Necessity:** three parallel 'though I...' clauses (tongues/angels; prophecy/mysteries/knowledge/faith; total self-sacrifice) each declared worthless without charity, escalating from spectacular speech to total self-sacrifice.
- **vv4-7 — Character:** a chain of mostly negative verbs describes what charity does and refuses to do, rather than defining it abstractly.
- **vv8-13 — Permanence:** charity is contrasted with [[Prophecy]], [[Gift of Tongues|tongues]], and knowledge, which 'fail,' 'cease,' and 'vanish away' (13:8); the child/man (13:11) and mirror (13:12) images illustrate the shift from partial to complete knowledge; the chapter closes on the [[Faith]]-[[Hope]]-charity triad (13:13).
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
1 Corinthians 13 teaches that [[Charity]] is not one [[Spiritual Gifts|spiritual gift]] among others but the necessary condition for any gift or sacrifice to have value (13:1-3), and the one thing that outlasts them: [[Prophecy]], [[Gift of Tongues|tongues]], and knowledge are explicitly called partial and temporary (13:8-9), while charity 'never faileth' (13:8). Read alongside Moroni 7:45,47, charity emerges as a love defined by sustained action ('suffereth,' 'beareth,' 'endureth') rather than sentiment, and is ranked above even [[Faith]] and [[Hope]] (13:13).
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The verbs of vv4-7 are mostly negative — charity 'envieth not,' 'vaunteth not,' 'seeketh not her own,' 'is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil' — defining the virtue largely by what it refuses to do, which suits a congregation Paul elsewhere describes as riven by envy and self-seeking factions. The image in v12, 'we see through a glass, darkly,' plausibly evokes ancient metal mirrors (not clear glass), which gave a dim, distorted reflection — an apt figure for 'we know in part' (13:9,12) rather than a claim about glass technology as such.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
vv1-3 use anaphora ('Though I...though I...and though I...') building toward the repeated refrain 'and have not charity' — each clause names a genuinely valuable gift or sacrifice (eloquence, prophetic insight, total knowledge, mountain-moving [[Faith]], radical generosity, martyrdom) only to void it. vv4-7 shift to an unbroken chain of short verb clauses with no connecting conjunctions, giving the passage a rhythmic, almost liturgical quality that has made it a favorite for readings independent of its original polemical context — correcting a congregation quarreling over spiritual gifts. v8's 'Charity never faileth' functions as a hinge, immediately followed by a triad of gifts that explicitly do fail — prophecies, tongues, knowledge — underscored by the child/man (13:11) and mirror (13:12) images.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual Parallel *(tentative)*
> The near-verbatim wording shared between 1 Corinthians 13:4-7 and Moroni 7:45 is sometimes cited in faithful scholarship as evidence of a shared, ancient teaching on charity known to both Paul and Mormon; it is equally cited by critics as evidence that Book of Mormon language was shaped by direct dependence on the King James New Testament during translation. The verbal overlap itself is well established; what it establishes about origins is not.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does 'that which is perfect' (13:10) refer to the Second Coming/resurrected, face-to-face state, or (as cessationist traditions argue) to the completion of the New Testament canon?
- Is the near-verbatim overlap between 13:4-7 and Moroni 7:45 evidence of a shared ancient source, or does it reflect the King James Bible's influence on Book of Mormon translation language?
- Why does Paul rank charity above [[Faith]] and [[Hope]] (13:13) when faith is elsewhere central to his gospel? Is this a claim about intrinsic worth, or about which virtue survives into eternity?
