# 1 Corinthians 1 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[1 Corinthians 1]] · My notes: [[1 Corinthians 1 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Paul addresses a congregation richly endowed by divine [[Grace]] yet fractured by human allegiances. His answer is not merely better group management: baptism belongs to Christ's name, Christ alone was crucified for believers, and the cross overturns conventional measures of wisdom and status. The argument culminates by locating wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, and redemption in Christ so that boasting is redirected to the Lord.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Calling, grace, and future confirmation (vv. 1-9):** Paul greets people sanctified and called in Christ, gives thanks for their gifts, and looks toward Christ's coming.

2. **The reported factions and Paul's appeal (vv. 10-17):** Chloe's household reports contention; Paul exposes the incongruity of allegiance to individual ministers and explains the priority of his preaching commission.

3. **The cross overturns rival wisdom (vv. 18-25):** the proclamation judged foolish by some is God's power to those who believe; both Jewish sign-seeking and Greek wisdom-seeking are confronted by Christ crucified.

4. **The congregation as an enacted reversal (vv. 26-31):** God's choice of socially weak and disregarded people prevents human boasting and directs glory to the Lord.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter grounds unity in the undivided Christ rather than in loyalty to a favored teacher. Its discussion of [[Baptism]] emphasizes Christ's name and saving work, not the prestige of the person administering the ordinance. Verse 17 therefore need not be read as depreciating baptism; verses 14-16 confirm that Paul did baptize, while the surrounding concern is factional appropriation of his ministry.

Verses 18-25 place the [[Crucifixion of Jesus Christ]] at the center of the gospel's reversal of ordinary expectations. Verse 30 then gathers several effects of union with Christ—wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, and redemption—making the passage especially relevant to [[Jesus Christ (Gospel Topic)]], [[Salvation]], and [[Justification and Sanctification]]. Verses 7-9 connect present [[Spiritual Gifts]] and divine faithfulness with hope for the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]].
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Calling language recurs in verses 1, 2, 9, 24, and 26. It links Paul's apostleship, the saints' identity, fellowship with Christ, recognition of Christ as God's power and wisdom, and the congregation's social composition.

The argument also depends on contrasts: divisions/joined, foolishness/power, wisdom/foolishness, weakness/strength, perishing/saved, and human boasting/glorying in the Lord. The phrase "not many" in verse 26 is an essential qualification. Paul characterizes the congregation's general makeup but does not claim that every learned, powerful, or noble person is excluded.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Verse 13's three rhetorical questions dismantle factional logic by moving from Christ's indivisibility to crucifixion and baptism. Verses 18-29 expand the same critique into a sustained reversal: what the world calls foolish or weak becomes the instrument by which God confounds conventional wisdom and power. The sequence reaches its stated purpose in verse 29—excluding human boasting—and its positive conclusion in verse 31—glorying in the Lord.

Two scriptural appeals frame this reversal. Verse 19 invokes [[Isaiah 29]], while verse 31 invokes [[Jeremiah 9]]. The latter is especially apt because Jeremiah also juxtaposes wisdom, might, and boasting. The factional names in verse 12 are not an isolated list: [[1 Corinthians 3]] resumes Paul and Apollos and identifies both as servants through whom people believed.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does "the same mind and in the same judgment" require uniformity on every question, or agreement sufficient to prevent rival identities from displacing allegiance to Christ?

- How do verses 13-17 distinguish the importance of an ordinance from the particular commission and public prominence of one minister?

- Which kinds of wisdom does Paul oppose, and how do verses 24 and 30—where Christ is explicitly called God's wisdom—prevent an indiscriminate rejection of [[Education and Learning]]?

- How can verses 26-29 challenge status-based [[Pride]] without treating weakness, poverty, or lack of education as virtues in themselves?

- Why does Paul begin with gratitude for the congregation's gifts before confronting its divisions?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
- Read [[Isaiah 29]] beside verse 19 and [[Jeremiah 9]] beside verses 26-31. Both are strong scriptural sources, although Paul's Greek wording or adaptation need not match the KJV Old Testament verbatim.
- Follow the Paul-and-Apollos argument into [[1 Corinthians 3]], where both ministers are subordinated to God's work.
- Compare Acts 18 for narrative context concerning Crispus, Apollos, and the Corinthian mission. These correspondences are highly plausible, though names alone do not independently prove identity.
- Cephas in verse 12 is Simon Peter; John 1:42 connects the names, and Galatians 2 uses Cephas and Peter for the same apostle.
- Romans 6 further develops the Christ-centered meaning of [[Baptism]] assumed by Paul's questions in verse 13.
