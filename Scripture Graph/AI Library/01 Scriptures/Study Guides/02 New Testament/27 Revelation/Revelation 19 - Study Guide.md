## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
A cautious outline is:

1. **Heaven celebrates judgment (19:1–6).** A fourfold "Alleluia" (vv.1,3,4,6) frames declarations of God's righteous judgment against "the great whore" (v.2), answering the fall of Babylon narrated in [[Revelation 18]].
2. **The bride and the blessed supper (19:7–10).** The Lamb's wife is ready, guests at the marriage supper are pronounced blessed, and John's attempt to worship the revealing angel is refused (v.10).
3. **The royal rider (19:11–16).** Heaven opens on a rider named Faithful and True, accumulating titles that culminate in "KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS" (v.16).
4. **The judgment supper (19:17–21).** Birds are summoned to a different kind of supper before the beast, false prophet, and armies are defeated.

The marriage supper (v.9) and the birds' supper (v.17) share the same underlying banquet term and land on opposite outcomes — a suggestive contrast, though calling it a deliberately engineered literary structure goes beyond what the text alone proves. John's refused worship of the angel in verse 10 is echoed almost verbatim later, in [[Revelation 22]].

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
**Worship belongs to God alone.** When John falls at the angel's feet, he is stopped: "See thou do it not... worship God" (v.10). Even an angel who identifies himself as a fellow servant sharing the testimony of Jesus declines the honor — one of the New Testament's clearest statements that heavenly messengers, however exalted their role, are not objects of worship.

**Testimony and [[Prophecy]].** Verse 10 closely associates the two: "the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy." The phrase "testimony of Jesus" is genuinely ambiguous — it can mean testimony about Jesus, testimony borne by Jesus, or both — so the verse is best read as tying authentic prophecy closely to Jesus without over-specifying the grammar.

**Righteousness as both gift and readiness.** The bride's fine linen is "granted" to her and is explicitly glossed as "the righteousness of saints" (v.8), while verse 7 notes "his wife hath made herself ready." The text places divine gift and the saints' own preparedness side by side without resolving their full theological relationship.

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
"Alleluia" (Hebrew hallelujah, "praise the LORD") appears four times here (vv.1,3,4,6) and nowhere else in the New Testament, giving the chapter's opening a distinctly liturgical character before the narrative turns to battle.

The rider of verses 11-16 accumulates titles rather than a single name — Faithful and True, an unrevealed hidden name, The Word of God, and King of Kings and Lord of Lords — even as "a name written, that no man knew, but he himself" (v.12) marks part of his identity as undisclosed.

The chapter draws on earlier scriptural imagery: the promise to "rule them with a rod of iron" (v.15) echoes Psalm 2:9's royal language, already reused earlier in Revelation (2:27; 12:5). The summons for birds to "eat the flesh of kings... and of all men" (vv.17-18) closely parallels Ezekiel 39:17-20's sacrificial feast over a defeated army. The bride imagery develops further in [[Revelation 21]], where the New Jerusalem is called the Lamb's wife. The false prophet's defeat (v.20) resolves the second beast's activity from [[Revelation 13]], though that chapter never itself uses the title "false prophet" — Revelation assigns that name to the figure only later.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- In what sense does the bride both make herself ready and receive her clean linen as something "granted" (vv.7-8)?
- Does "the testimony of Jesus" mean testimony about Jesus, testimony given by Jesus, or both (v.10)?
- Who makes up "the armies which were in heaven... upon white horses" (v.14) — resurrected saints, angels, or both?
- Whose blood stains the rider's garment in verse 13: his own, his enemies', or deliberately multivalent imagery?
- How does picturing the rider's sword as proceeding "out of his mouth" (vv.15, 21) shape the meaning of his warfare?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
