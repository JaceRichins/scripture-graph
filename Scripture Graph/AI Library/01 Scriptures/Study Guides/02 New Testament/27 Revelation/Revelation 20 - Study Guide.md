# Revelation 20 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Revelation 20]] · My notes: [[Revelation 20 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Satan is bound during the Millennium—The Saints will then live and reign with Christ—The dead stand before God and are judged out of the books according to their works.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Satan restrained (vv. 1–3):** An angel binds, confines, and seals Satan so that he cannot deceive the nations during the thousand years.

2. **The faithful live and reign (vv. 4–6):** John sees thrones, faithful witnesses, the first resurrection, priestly service, and reign with Christ.

3. **The final rebellion and Satan's punishment (vv. 7–10):** Satan is released, gathers Gog and Magog against the saints and beloved city, and is cast into the lake of fire.

4. **The dead judged (vv. 11–15):** Before the great white throne, books and the book of life are opened; the dead are judged according to their works; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.

The recurring declaration “And I saw” marks important visual transitions in verses 1, 4, 11, and 12.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **[[Millennium]]:** Satan is restrained from deceiving the nations while the faithful live and reign with Christ for a thousand years. The chapter does not by itself settle whether this duration is literal, symbolic, or both.

- **[[Resurrection]]:** The first resurrection is associated with blessedness, holiness, priestly service, reign with Christ, and immunity from the second death. The exact scope and nature of this resurrection remain disputed.

- **[[Final Judgment]]:** The dead are judged according to their works from opened books, while a separate book of life also has a decisive role. Revelation 20 does not fully explain how these images relate and should not alone be used to settle later debates about works and [[Grace]].

- **[[Satan and Temptation]]:** Satan's confinement limits his deception temporarily; after his release and final rebellion, he is assigned unending torment in the lake of fire.

- **[[Death and the Spirit World]]:** The sea, death, and hell surrender their dead, after which death and hell are cast into the lake of fire. This depicts their defeat, but further doctrinal conclusions about intermediate states require evidence beyond this chapter.

[[D&C 128]] applies the books imagery to earthly and heavenly records. That is an explicit Restoration interpretation and should remain distinct from what Revelation itself explains.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The repeated formula “And I saw” organizes the chapter into successive tableaux (vv. 1, 4, 11–12). “A thousand years” or “the thousand years” occurs six times in verses 2–7, coordinating Satan's confinement, the saints' reign, the remaining dead, and Satan's release. The chapter also develops clear reversals and contrasts: Satan is bound and then loosed; faithful witnesses are killed and then live and reign; the first resurrection is contrasted with the second death.

Revelation 20:2 closely recalls [[Revelation 12]], where the dragon is likewise identified as the old serpent, Devil, and Satan. The judgment scene draws strongly on [[Daniel 7]] through its thrones, judgment, and opened books. The Gog and Magog episode adapts [[Ezekiel 38]] and [[Ezekiel 39]] by transforming their invasion imagery into a worldwide gathering. These intertexts illuminate Revelation's imagery but do not identify a single modern geopolitical fulfillment or settle the vision's chronology.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Textual-Dependency
> D&C 128 (Joseph Smith's 1842 letter on baptism for the dead) draws directly on Revelation 20:12's language of "books" and "the book of life" being opened and the dead judged "according to their works" to argue for the necessity of earthly ordinance records; the verified index shows a genuine 2-verse text overlap between the two documents, indicating direct literary dependence rather than mere topical similarity.
> — strength 0.9 · confidence 1.0 · Direct citation broadly established · [[Textual-Dependency in Revelation 20|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Scholarly-Consensus
> Christian interpreters have long divided over how to read the "thousand years" of vv.4-7 — premillennial (a literal future earthly reign beginning at Christ's return), postmillennial (a golden age achieved through gospel progress before Christ's return), and amillennial (a symbolic description of the present church age or the intermediate state) — and the text of Revelation 20 itself does not settle which framework is correct.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.9 · Longstanding interpretive dispute accurately represented · [[Scholarly-Consensus in Revelation 20|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary Intertext
> The clustered correspondences with Daniel 7 are strong evidence that Revelation 20 presents final judgment through Danielic courtroom imagery.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · Strong qualitative evidence; precise mechanism uncertain · [[Literary Intertext in Revelation 20|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Narrative Sequence *(tentative)*
> On a surface-sequential reading, the chapter places the saints' reign before Satan's release, final rebellion, and great-white-throne judgment. This is meaningful textual support for a future millennial sequence, but the literary order of visions does not conclusively prove chronological order.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.9 · Narrative order clear; chronological inference disputed

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does “the first resurrection” refer narrowly to the faithful described in verse 4, or to a larger group? Is it bodily resurrection, heavenly vindication, or both?

- Is the thousand-year period an exact duration, a symbolic period, or a symbol corresponding to an actual future era? What does the chapter establish without deciding that question?

- How do the books associated with works relate to the book of life? Which proposed answers arise from Revelation itself, and which come from later interpretation?

- Why “must” Satan be released after the thousand years when the chapter does not explain that necessity?

- Does the beloved city denote Jerusalem, the whole community of the saints, or another symbolic reality?

- How freely does Revelation adapt the Gog and Magog imagery of [[Ezekiel 38]] and [[Ezekiel 39]]?

- How does the casting away of death prepare for the renewed creation and absence of death in [[Revelation 21]]?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare the dragon's identification and deceptive activity in [[Revelation 12]], the punishment of the beast and false prophet in [[Revelation 19]], and the removal of death in [[Revelation 21]]. For major scriptural backgrounds, compare the heavenly courtroom of [[Daniel 7]] and the Gog oracle in [[Ezekiel 38]]–[[Ezekiel 39]]. Within Restoration scripture, [[D&C 88]] parallels Satan's binding, later release, and defeat, while [[D&C 128]] explicitly invokes Revelation 20:12 and applies its books imagery to earthly and heavenly records. That application should not be confused with a demonstration of John's original intended referent.
