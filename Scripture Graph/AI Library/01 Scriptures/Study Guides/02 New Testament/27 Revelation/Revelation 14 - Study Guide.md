# Revelation 14 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Revelation 14]] · My notes: [[Revelation 14 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> The Lamb will stand upon Mount Zion—The gospel will be restored in the last days by angelic ministry—The Son of Man will harvest the earth.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **The Lamb and the 144,000 (14:1–5):** The redeemed stand with the Lamb on Mount Sion, bear the Father's name, sing a new song, follow the Lamb, and are called firstfruits.
2. **Three proclamations and the saints' response (14:6–13):** The angels announce the everlasting gospel and judgment, Babylon's fall, and the consequences of worshiping the beast. The passage then commends the saints' endurance and blesses those who die in the Lord.
3. **Two harvest scenes (14:14–20):** A crowned figure like the Son of man reaps the earth; afterward, a sickle-bearing angel gathers grapes into the winepress of God's wrath.

The Father's name and the beast's mark create a strong local contrast of identity and allegiance, but this motif should not be made the sole organizing structure for the entire chapter.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Worship and judgment:** The everlasting gospel calls every nation, kindred, tongue, and people to fear God, give him glory, and worship the Creator because the hour of judgment has come (14:6–7; see [[Final Judgment]]).
- **Restoration interpretation:** Latter-day Saint study associates the angelic proclamation with the [[Restoration of the Gospel]] through [[Angels]], especially when Revelation 14 is read with [[Doctrine and Covenants 133]]. Revelation 14 alone does not name the angel or specify a modern event.
- **Enduring allegiance:** Revelation 14:12 describes the saints through patience, keeping God's commandments, and the faith of Jesus (see [[Endurance]] and [[Obedience]]).
- **The faithful dead:** Those who die in the Lord are blessed, rest from their labors, and have their works follow them (14:13). The verse places rest and works together but does not say that works cause the promised rest.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The chapter repeatedly uses bodily marks and consumption as images of allegiance. The Father's name and the beast's mark identify rival communities (14:1, 9–11). Babylon makes nations drink her wine (14:8), while her adherents drink the wine of God's wrath (14:10). These reversals depict judgment as participation in the consequences of one's chosen loyalty.

The word **rest** also forms a sharp contrast. Beast worshipers have “no rest day nor night” (14:11), whereas those who die in the Lord “rest from their labours” (14:13).
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Several earlier scriptural images converge here. The cloud-borne figure like the Son of man recalls [[Daniel 7]]. The sickle, ripe harvest, and full winepress closely resemble [[Joel 3]]. The 144,000 connect backward to [[Revelation 7]], while Babylon's brief fall announcement anticipates [[Revelation 18]]. These links make the chapter a hinge between the conflict with the beasts and the later visions of judgment.

The two harvests should not be flattened into certainty. The grape harvest is explicitly punitive because it ends in the winepress of wrath. The first harvest is not explicitly labeled either salvation or punishment; its distinction from the second may suggest a gathering of the righteous, but both scenes can also be read as complementary images of judgment.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Interpretive Framework Applied To Ambiguous Text
> A common reading treats the grain harvest (14:14-16) as the gathering of the righteous and the grape/winepress (14:17-20) as judgment on the wicked, paralleling the wheat/tares separation elsewhere in the Gospels. However, Revelation 14 itself never states that the grain harvest is positive or that it gathers only the righteous; some scholars read both the harvest and the vintage as parallel images of the same climactic judgment on 'the earth,' with the wheat/tares contrast imported from Matthew 13 rather than stated here.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.9 · Affirmed as a genuine interpretive dispute · [[Interpretive Framework Applied To Ambiguous Text in Revelation 14|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Literary Numerology
> The number 144,000 equals twelve times twelve times one thousand and recurs in Revelation 7. That patterned form supports reading the number as a symbolically ordered covenant community, but it does not exclude an intended literal enumeration.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.9 · Pattern accepted; referential meaning disputed · [[Literary Numerology in Revelation 14|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Interpretive Scriptural Support
> Within Latter-day Saint interpretation, the angel bearing the everlasting gospel provides scriptural support for associating latter-day gospel proclamation with angelic ministry. The verse itself, however, neither identifies a particular angel nor names a specific historical restoration event.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.9 · Accepted within stated interpretive limits · [[Interpretive Scriptural Support in Revelation 14|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal Textual Ambiguity *(tentative)*
> The description of the 144,000 as 'not defiled with women; for they are virgins' (14:4) is genuinely ambiguous in scope: it can be read literally (a class of celibate men) or symbolically (undefiled from the 'fornication' associated with Babylon/idolatry mentioned two verses later in 14:8, echoing OT usage of adultery/harlotry as apostasy). The chapter itself does not disambiguate, and both readings appear in serious commentary.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.7 · Interpretation disputed
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does the Father's name signify ownership, character, covenant allegiance, or all three?
- Is the first harvest in verses 14–16 a saving gathering, a judgment, or a deliberately ambiguous image?
- How should the symbolic associations of 144,000 be balanced against readings that treat it as an exact number?
- What does it mean in practice to keep “the faith of Jesus” while resisting the beast's economic and social pressure?
- How does the promise that works follow the faithful relate to [[Grace]], [[Obedience]], and [[Final Judgment]]?
