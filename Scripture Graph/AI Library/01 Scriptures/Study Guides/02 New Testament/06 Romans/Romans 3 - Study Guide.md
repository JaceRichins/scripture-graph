# Romans 3 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Romans 3]] · My notes: [[Romans 3 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Man is not justified by the law of Moses—He is justified through righteousness, which comes through faith in Christ, made possible through Christ’s atoning sacrifice.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
- **Verses 1-8:** Questions and responses concerning Jewish privilege, human unbelief, God's justice, and the slanderous claim that evil may be done for good.
- **Verses 9-18:** Jews and Gentiles placed under sin, followed by a cumulative chain of scriptural indictments.
- **Verses 19-20:** The law stops every mouth, brings accountability, and gives knowledge of sin; deeds of the law do not justify.
- **Verses 21-26:** “But now” introduces God's manifested righteousness, justification by grace, redemption in Christ, and the declaration of divine justice.
- **Verses 27-31:** Boasting is excluded; the one God justifies circumcision and uncircumcision through faith; faith establishes rather than voids the law.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter's logic joins several teachings that should not be isolated from one another: universal [[Sin]] (Romans 3:9, 23), the law's power to expose rather than justify (3:19–20), righteousness received through [[Faith]] (3:21–22, 28), free justification by [[Grace]] (3:24), and redemption through Jesus Christ (3:24–26). Romans 3:25–26 is especially important for [[Justice and Mercy]]: remission does not mean that God disregards evil, for the saving work of Christ is presented as declaring God both just and the justifier.

Paul also blocks two distortions. Grace is not permission to do evil, since he condemns that inference in verse 8. Faith is not the simple abolition of moral or scriptural law, since verse 31 says that it establishes the law.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
“Law” must be interpreted contextually. Romans 3 speaks of what the law says, deeds of the law, the law and the prophets, the law of faith, and the law established through faith. These expressions are related, but their exact referents should not be flattened into one undifferentiated meaning.

The KJV phrase “faith of Jesus Christ” in verse 22 leaves open a disputed question: whether the phrase emphasizes faith directed toward Christ, Christ's own faithfulness, or a richer relationship involving both. Human believing is explicit in verses 22 and 26, but that does not by itself settle the debated phrase.

The word translated “propitiation” in verse 25 has been understood as emphasizing the averting of wrath, the removal of sin, or a possible mercy-seat allusion. The verse securely joins Christ, blood, faith, remission, and God's righteousness, but the translated word alone does not establish one exhaustive theory of the [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]].
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Romans 3:10-18 is a scriptural catena—a cumulative chain of excerpts used to support the claim that Jews and Gentiles alike are under sin. The text moves through understanding, desire, conduct, speech, violence, life's paths, and the fear of God, portraying sin as pervasive without requiring that every individual commit every offense listed.

Clear English-text connections include [[Psalm 14]] and the “open sepulchre” wording of Psalm 5:9, while [[Psalm 53]] closely parallels Psalm 14. Additional traditional source identifications include Psalms 10 and 140 and [[Isaiah 59]], but their precise verbal relationships are more evident in the Greek scriptural tradition than in the supplied English-overlap data and should be studied rather than treated here as mechanically proven.

The chapter also has a thematic bookend: it begins with Jewish advantage and ends with the one God's justification of both circumcision and uncircumcision. This is a clear argumentative correspondence, though not necessarily a formal inclusio.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Literary Composition *(tentative)*
> The coordinated scriptural chain in Romans 3:10–18 is strong evidence of deliberate literary arrangement serving Paul's universal indictment. Its progression through dispositions, speech, conduct, and orientation toward God is unlikely to be an accidental juxtaposition.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.8 · deliberate cumulative use likely; assembler and scheme uncertain

> [!evidence] Linguistic/Grammatical *(tentative)*
> The phrase in 3:22, 'the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ,' preserves a Greek genitive construction (pistis Christou) that is grammatically ambiguous: it can be read as an objective genitive ('faith in Christ,' the traditional/majority reading, which the KJV's phrasing suggests) or as a subjective genitive ('the faithfulness of Christ himself,' a minority reading defended by some modern scholars).
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.8 · genuine disputed grammatical interpretation

> [!evidence] Textual/Translation-History *(tentative)*
> The KJV text of 3:28 reads 'a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law' -- it does not contain the word 'alone.' The word 'alone' (allein) was famously inserted by Martin Luther in his 1522 German New Testament translation of this verse, a rendering that became controversial precisely because it is an addition not present in the Greek.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.8 · historically plausible but externally unsourced in proposal

> [!evidence] Linguistic/Translation *(tentative)*
> The word rendered 'propitiation' in 3:25 (Greek hilasterion) is translated differently across scholarly traditions: as 'propitiation' (an offering that turns away God's wrath), as 'expiation' (an offering that removes/cleanses sin without implying appeasement of wrath), or as an allusion to the 'mercy seat' (the same Greek word used in the Septuagint for the atonement cover on the ark of the covenant).
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.8 · lexical range accepted; preferred interpretation disputed

> [!evidence] Textual/Translation *(tentative)*
> Romans 3:4b ('That thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest overcome when thou art judged') is drawn from Psalm 51:4, but its wording differs noticeably from the KJV's own translation of that verse ('that thou mightest be justified when thou speakest, and be clear when thou judgest'). Both render the same underlying source, but Paul's Greek text follows the Septuagint while the KJV Old Testament follows the Hebrew, producing two non-identical English renderings of what is presented as 'one' quotation.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.8 · quotation relationship clear; original-language explanation unverified here
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Why does Paul preserve a real Jewish advantage in verses 1–2 while denying Jewish moral superiority in verse 9?

How do verses 19–21 distinguish the law's ability to reveal sin from its inability to justify?

What does the phrase “witnessed by the law and the prophets” contribute to the claim that righteousness is manifested “without the law”?

How do verses 24–26 hold together divine grace, Christ's redemption, remission of sins, and God's justice?

Why does Paul end the chapter by insisting that faith establishes the law?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Romans 3:10-18 with [[Psalm 14]] and [[Psalm 53]] to examine the catena's scriptural language. Psalm 5:9, Psalm 10:7, Psalm 36:1, Psalm 140:3, and [[Isaiah 59]] may then be examined as proposed sources for other portions of the chain, with attention to differences between English translations and the Greek scriptural tradition.

Read [[Romans 4]] for Paul's further treatment of faith, works, boasting, circumcision, and Abraham, and [[Galatians 3]] for a related argument about faith and law. Comparing careful translations of Romans 3:22 and 3:25 can also reveal interpretive questions concealed by reliance on a single English rendering.
