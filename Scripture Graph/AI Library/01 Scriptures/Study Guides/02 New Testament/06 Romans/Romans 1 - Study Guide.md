# Romans 1 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Romans 1]] · My notes: [[Romans 1 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Romans 1 opens with an unusually elaborate greeting (vv. 1-7) that summarizes the [[Gospel of Jesus Christ]] itself: Jesus is of Davidic descent 'according to the flesh' and 'declared to be the Son of God with power ... by the resurrection from the dead' (vv. 3-4). Paul then expresses his desire for mutual encouragement with the Roman believers (vv. 8-15) before stating the letter's programmatic thesis: the gospel is 'the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth,' and in it the righteousness of God is revealed 'from [[Faith]] to faith' (vv. 16-17), a claim tied to Habakkuk 2:4. The chapter then describes humanity's culpable rejection of the knowledge of God available through creation (vv. 18-20), tracing ingratitude and idolatry (vv. 21-23, 25) into an extended account of consequence — marked by a threefold refrain, 'God gave them up/over' (vv. 24, 26, 28) — and a broad catalogue of wrongdoing (vv. 29-32) that is not limited to sexual sin. Romans 2:1 immediately turns this indictment back on anyone who judges, cautioning against reading vv. 18-32 as aimed solely at an external group.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Greeting and gospel summary (1:1-7):** Christ's Davidic descent and resurrection-declared sonship.
2. **Thanksgiving and desired visit (1:8-15):** Paul hopes for mutual encouragement, not one-directional ministry.
3. **Programmatic thesis (1:16-17):** gospel, salvation, righteousness, and [[Faith]].
4. **Revelation of wrath through rejected creation-knowledge (1:18-23):** ingratitude and idolatry.
5. **Exchange and divine handover (1:24-28):** a threefold 'gave up/over' refrain marks consequence following idolatry.
6. **Catalogue of wrongdoing (1:29-32):** a broad list of vices, not confined to sexual sin, closing with approval of wrongdoing as its own offense.

The chapter break should not obscure the turn in [[Romans 2]] toward the one who judges.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Verses 3-4 hold together two descriptions of Christ — descended 'of the seed of David according to the flesh' and 'declared to be the Son of God with power ... by the resurrection' — without resolving every question of how the two relate. Verses 16-17 join [[Grace]], apostleship, [[Faith]], and [[Salvation]] as a compressed thesis rather than a full explanation; 'the righteousness of God' is contested language (God's own character, God's saving action, or a granted status) that this chapter alone does not settle. The chapter grounds accountability in creation's disclosure of God's 'eternal power and Godhead' (vv. 19-20) — the text asserts divine disclosure ('God hath shewed it unto them,' v. 19) rather than unaided human discovery, and does not claim creation supplies the gospel's full content. The threefold 'gave them up/over' refrain (vv. 24, 26, 28) is best read as consequence following prior idolatry, though whether it depicts active judgment, withdrawn restraint, or both remains debated.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
Verse 17 quotes Habakkuk 2:4, 'The just shall live by faith' — Paul's wording drops the possessive found in the KJV Habakkuk text ('by his faith'), a small but noted textual variance. Paul uses the same citation in [[Galatians 3]] to found his argument that righteousness comes by faith rather than law-works. The chapter's closing section is tied together by two repeated word-patterns: people 'changed' God's glory, truth, and natural use (vv. 23, 25, 26), and God 'gave them up' or 'gave them over' in response (vv. 24, 26, 28) — a structured refrain rather than a loose list. In vv. 26-27, the male same-sex conduct is stated explicitly ('men with men'); the parallel female conduct is not given an explicit object, and applying either verse to modern categories of sexual orientation goes beyond the vocabulary the text itself uses.

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
Why does Paul revise his opening promise to 'impart ... some spiritual gift' (v. 11) into language of mutual comfort 'both of you and me' (v. 12)? What is the relationship between the threefold 'gave them up/over' refrain (vv. 24, 26, 28) and the idolatry described in vv. 21-23 — consequence, active judgment, or both? Does 'the righteousness of God' (v. 17) describe God's character, God's saving action, a believer's granted status, or some combination? Who is 'they' throughout vv. 18-32 — Gentiles generally, a specific group, or a rhetorical stand-in — and how does the turn to the judging reader in [[Romans 2]] change how this section should be used?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Romans 1:17 with [[Habakkuk 2]], noting the small wording difference. Compare Romans 1:19-25 with [[Acts 17]], where Paul likewise reasons from the Creator and creation while rejecting idol-images — though this parallel rests on thematic judgment rather than verified textual overlap. Read Romans 1:18-32 together with [[Romans 2]] and [[Romans 3]] before drawing conclusions about the scope of Paul's indictment.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
