# Romans 14 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Romans 14]] · My notes: [[Romans 14 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Avoid quarreling about opinions and making unrighteous judgment of each other—Every knee will bow to Christ—The kingdom of God embraces righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter moves through a clear argumentative sequence:
- **vv.1-6** — Receive the weak without disputing opinions; two cases (food, days) are introduced, each requiring a person to be 'fully persuaded in his own mind.'
- **vv.7-12** — Theological basis: none of us belongs to ourselves; we live and die 'unto the Lord,' and all will stand before the judgment seat of Christ and give account to God (reinforced by a citation of Isaiah 45 in v.11).
- **vv.13-21** — Practical application: stop judging, and instead take responsibility not to place a stumblingblock before a brother, even at the cost of forgoing meat or wine.
- **vv.22-23** — Closing maxim: keep disputable convictions between yourself and God, and do not act against your own conviction, 'for whatsoever is not of faith is sin.'
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Conviction under accountability**: individual persuasion (v.5) operates within, not instead of, accountability to God — conduct must be done 'unto the Lord' (v.6) and every person will give account of himself to God (v.12).
- **[[Final Judgment]]**: all believers stand before the judgment seat of Christ, which is the stated reason judging a brother is out of bounds (vv.10-12).
- **[[Kingdom of God]]** defined by inward qualities — righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost — rather than external observance (v.17).
- **[[Charity]]** as the deciding principle when liberty and a weaker conscience come into conflict (vv.15,19-21).
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated expressions “to the Lord” and “giveth God thanks” in verse 6 shift attention from the outward practice alone to its Godward intention. Yet intention is not the chapter's only test: verses 13–21 also examine the practice's effect on another believer.

“Weak” is Paul's description of a believer whose faith or conscience does not permit the disputed food. It should not be silently expanded into a judgment about that person's total discipleship. Likewise, “damned” in the KJV wording of verse 23 need not be read as an explicit declaration of eternal damnation; the immediate statement concerns condemnation incurred by eating against doubt rather than from faith.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
A notable reversal occurs in v.13: after prohibiting judgment of one another, Paul says to 'judge this rather' — turning scrutiny away from another's standing before God and toward one's own potentially harmful conduct. The chapter is also loosely framed by the language of faith, opening with one 'weak in the faith' (v.1) and closing with 'whatsoever is not of faith is sin' (v.23); this inclusio is a plausible literary observation rather than a certainty. Paul inserts a formal scriptural citation (v.11, drawing on Isaiah 45:23's oath about every knee bowing and every tongue confessing), though the wording is adapted rather than verbatim — Isaiah has 'swear,' not 'confess' — an adaptation that resembles the confession language later applied explicitly to Christ in [[Philippians 2]]:10-11. Some manuscripts read 'judgment seat of God' rather than 'of Christ' in v.10, a variant worth noting before drawing conclusions from Paul's alternation between 'Christ' and 'God' in vv.10-12.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Explicit Scriptural Citation
> Romans 14:11 explicitly introduces a scriptural citation and closely corresponds to Isaiah 45:23 in its declaration that every knee will bow and every tongue will acknowledge God. The explicit formula and distinctive paired images make the citation relationship very strong.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.8 · citation real but adapted, not verbatim · [[Explicit Scriptural Citation in Romans 14|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Intertextual/Citation Analysis *(tentative)*
> Romans 14:11's introductory oath formula ('As I live, saith the Lord') and its content ('every knee shall bow... every tongue shall confess') closely track the wording of Isaiah 45:23, an oracle originally spoken by YHWH about universal acknowledgment of himself alone as God (cf. Isaiah 45:22-23). Paul applies this Isaiah oracle within an argument about standing before 'the judgment seat of Christ' (v.10), while the confession itself is stated as being made 'to God' (v.11) and the final account is given 'to God' (v.12) rather than explicitly to Christ.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · disputed identity-claim reading

> [!evidence] Intertextual Parallel To A Saying Of Jesus *(tentative)*
> Paul's claim that 'nothing [is] unclean of itself' (Romans 14:14), attributed to teaching he received 'by the Lord Jesus,' parallels the substance of Jesus' own teaching recorded in Mark 7:15-23, where Jesus declares that nothing entering a person from outside can defile them, only what comes from within the heart (Mark's narrator adds the comment 'This he said, making all meats clean,' Mark 7:19).
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.4 · speculative intertextual parallel
<!-- SG:END evidence -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Who were the 'weak in the faith' at Rome — Jewish Christians retaining kosher and Sabbath practice, Gentile ascetics, or a mixed group? The chapter never names Jews, Gentiles, the Law of Moses, or the Sabbath, so any identification is an inference, not a claim the text makes directly.
- What kinds of disagreement fit Paul's category of disputable practice, and what kinds involve matters that cannot be treated as optional?
- How do vv.5-6 affirm conscientious conviction while vv.13-21 still restrict its public exercise?
- Does 'one day above another' (vv.5-6) refer to the [[Sabbath Day]], Jewish festivals, fasting days, or something else? The chapter does not say.
- Some manuscripts read 'judgment seat of God' rather than 'of Christ' in v.10 — how does that variant affect the relationship between Christ's judgment seat (v.10) and giving account to God (v.12)?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
