# 2 Corinthians 1 — Study Guide

## Overview

<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Greeting (verses 1–2):** Paul and Timothy address Corinth and the saints in Achaia.

2. **Comfort, suffering, and deliverance (verses 3–11):** God's comfort equips sufferers to comfort others; Paul recounts a death-threatening crisis and acknowledges the Corinthians' prayers.

3. **Integrity and mutual rejoicing (verses 12–14):** Paul appeals to conscience, sincerity, grace, and transparent writing.

4. **The disputed itinerary and God's reliable promises (verses 15–22):** Paul explains his intended route, rejects the charge of vacillation, and moves from human 'yea and nay' to God's 'yea' in Christ and the sealing gift of the Spirit.

5. **Reason for postponement and qualified authority (verses 23–24):** Paul says he delayed to spare Corinth and defines his role as helping their joy.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
**Comfort through shared suffering.** Verses 3-7 present affliction as purposeful: God's comfort to Paul is meant to overflow into comfort for others, making suffering and consolation communal rather than purely private experiences. See [[Adversity]].

**Reliance on God amid crisis.** Paul's near-death experience in Asia (1:8-10) teaches him to trust 'God which raiseth the dead' rather than himself — a pointed appeal to God's power over death, not a developed treatment of [[Resurrection]] doctrine. Verse 11 makes [[Prayer]] part of the community's response to danger and deliverance.

**Christ as God's 'yea.'** Paul answers a challenge to his own reliability by pointing to Christ: God's promises are 'yea' and 'Amen' in him (1:19-20). The immediate point concerns the reliability of the gospel message and God's promises, not a claim that every personal plan is guaranteed to succeed.

**The Spirit as seal and pledge.** Verses 21-22 describe believers as established in Christ, anointed, and sealed, with the Spirit given as an 'earnest' — a partial, present pledge pointing toward a fuller future reality. See [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]]. The verses do not specify a ritual or institutional form for this sealing and should not be read as a full account of later ordinance theology.

**Authority as service.** Verse 24 rejects a coercive model of leadership ('dominion over your faith') in favor of one that helps others stand by their own faith. See [[Apostles]] and [[Faith]].

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The root behind 'comfort' and 'consolation' occurs nine times in just verses 3-7 (comforteth, comfort, comforted, consolation), tightly binding divine comfort, human affliction, and ministry to others through repeated vocabulary and first/second-person plurals ('we,' 'us,' 'you,' 'ye'). In verses 17-20, 'yea' and 'nay' concern reliability and consistency: Paul denies vacillating, and God's promises are 'yea' in Christ — a claim about the trustworthiness of God's promises and the gospel message, not a guarantee that every human plan will succeed. 'Earnest' (v22) is an older term for a pledge or initial installment guaranteeing more to come, giving the Spirit's presence in believers' hearts a concrete image of promise without spelling out a full timeline of future blessings.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The letter opens with a Jewish-style blessing (berakah)—'Blessed be God...'—rather than the more common Hellenistic thanksgiving opening, aligning it with similar openings elsewhere in the Pauline corpus (compare [[Ephesians 1]]). Paul's insistence in verse 17 that his word is not 'yea yea, and nay nay' verbally recalls Jesus' teaching in [[Matthew 5]]:37 that one's speech should simply be 'Yea, yea; Nay, nay'; whether this is a deliberate allusion or shared idiom is not certain, but the verbal echo invites comparison of the two passages' differing points (Paul denies inconsistency, whereas Jesus commends simplicity).
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What was the 'trouble... in Asia' (1:8) that brought Paul to despair of life? The text gives no details, and any specific identification remains speculative.
- Why does Paul spend so much space defending his change in travel plans (1:15-17, 23)? Does this suggest the Corinthians had already questioned his reliability, or is this simply Paul's characteristic thoroughness?
- Why does Paul move from defending a travel decision to speaking of God's promises in Christ (1:17-20)? What does that move suggest about the relationship between a messenger's integrity and the message proclaimed?
- How does receiving comfort create responsibility toward other sufferers rather than merely private reassurance (1:4-7)?
- What safeguards for religious leadership appear in the distinction between having 'dominion' over faith and being a 'helper' of joy (1:24)?
