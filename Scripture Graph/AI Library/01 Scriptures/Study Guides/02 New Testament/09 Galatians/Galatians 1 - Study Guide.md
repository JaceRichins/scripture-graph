# Galatians 1 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Galatians 1]] · My notes: [[Galatians 1 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Preachers of false gospels are accursed—Paul received the gospel by revelation—He believed, was taught, and preached to the Gentiles.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Greeting and theological summary (1:1–5):** apostleship, [[Resurrection]], grace, peace, and Christ's self-gift for sins.

2. **The gospel controversy (1:6–10):** the Galatians are turning to a purported alternative that Paul calls a perversion; the warning is repeated and applies regardless of the messenger's status.

3. **Claim of revelatory origin (1:11–12):** Paul states the controlling thesis of his defense.

4. **Former life and divine call (1:13–16):** persecuting zeal is contrasted with God's gracious call and a mission among the heathen.

5. **Travel and limited apostolic contact (1:17–21):** Arabia, Damascus, a later fifteen-day visit with Peter, contact with James, and travel into Syria and Cilicia.

6. **Report among Judean churches (1:22–24):** the persecutor has become a preacher, leading the churches to glorify God.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter connects several teachings without developing each into a complete doctrinal system. Christ "gave himself" for sins and deliverance (1:4), directly supporting study of the [[Atonement of Jesus Christ]]. God raised Christ from the dead (1:1), affirming the [[Resurrection]]. Both the Galatians' calling and Paul's commission are associated with [[Grace]] (1:6, 15). Paul's appeal to [[Revelation]] concerns the claimed source of his gospel and commission; the chapter does not turn that claim into a general rule that apostolic teaching requires no community, instruction, or accountability.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
- **'Accursed' (anathema, 1:8-9)** — a formal curse/ban formula, not a casual insult; Paul repeats it in successive verses for maximum rhetorical weight.
- **'Another gospel... which is not another' (1:6-7)** — a deliberate correction: the rival teaching may present itself as an alternative gospel, but Paul denies it deserves that name.
- **'Conferred not with flesh and blood' (1:16)** — an idiom for declining human consultation, reinforcing Paul's claim to unmediated revelation.
- **'Conversation' (1:13, KJV)** — refers to Paul's former conduct or way of life, immediately glossed by the verse itself as persecution.
- **'To see Peter' (1:18)** — the KJV's plain rendering; some commentators think the underlying verb can carry a sense of visiting to gather information, though this reading is debated.
- **'Before God, I lie not' (1:20)** — a solemn oath common to ancient personal testimony, here certifying the sensitive claims about his limited Jerusalem contact that immediately precede it.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Galatians 1's autobiography is argumentative rather than merely biographical: each selected detail — Paul's prior hostility to the church, his movements before ever reaching Jerusalem, the brevity of his eventual visit — serves his claim that his gospel and commission did not originate with human authorities. The chapter also skips the thanksgiving conventional to Hellenistic and other Pauline letters, moving directly from blessing to rebuke (1:5-6), a feature many commentators read as a sign of urgency (see Betz, *Galatians*, Hermeneia, 1979), though the omission alone does not prove a consciously designed rhetorical strategy. The prophetic-call echoes in 1:15-16 (compare [[Jeremiah 1]] and [[Isaiah 49]]) are worth noting cautiously as a resemblance in pattern and vocabulary, not a demonstrated quotation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Internal Autobiographical Detail
> The named places, elapsed period, length of visit, and limited named contacts provide concrete internal support for the claim Paul is arguing: that his commission and gospel did not originate with the Jerusalem apostles. These details are historically useful but remain Paul's own advocacy rather than independent corroboration.
> — strength 0.6 · confidence 0.9 · exemplary epistemic labeling · [[Internal Autobiographical Detail in Galatians 1|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Historical-Chronological *(tentative)*
> Galatians 1:17-18 places an undefined stay in Arabia and a full three years between Paul's call and his first meeting with the Jerusalem apostles; this timeline is often discussed alongside Acts 9:19-30, which narrates Paul's early Damascus preaching and escape without mentioning an Arabian sojourn, raising a longstanding harmonization question rather than a flat contradiction.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.6 · disputed, genuinely open chronological question

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What is the significance of the double repetition of the curse in 1:8-9 rather than a single statement?
- Does 'Arabia' (1:17) refer to the Nabatean kingdom near Damascus or a longer desert retreat, and how does this affect the timeline against [[Acts 9]] — particularly Acts' account of Ananias restoring Paul's sight and baptizing him (Acts 9:10-19), which reads as immediate human contact right after Paul's Damascus-road experience?
- Why does Paul specify he saw only Peter and James on his first Jerusalem visit (1:18-19)?
- What information about the rival teachers or their message is supplied in this chapter, and what must wait for later chapters?
- What limits should apply to extending Paul's curse against 'another gospel' (1:8-9) to later religious disagreements?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare Paul's first-person account with [[Acts 9]], noting both the shared elements (revelation near Damascus, a persecutor turned preacher) and a real point of tension: Acts has the disciple Ananias restore Paul's sight and baptize him within days of his experience (Acts 9:10-19), while Galatians emphasizes that Paul 'conferred not with flesh and blood' and delayed his Jerusalem visit for three years — a difference worth weighing rather than smoothing over. [[1 Timothy 1]] offers another account, traditionally attributed to Paul, of a former persecutor called to ministry, though many scholars regard the Pastoral Epistles (1-2 Timothy, Titus) as written in Paul's name by a later hand rather than by Paul himself; the same caveat applies to the shared closing doxology with [[2 Timothy 4]] ('to whom be glory for ever and ever. Amen,' also in Galatians 1:5), which may reflect a common liturgical formula rather than direct dependence. [[2 Corinthians 11]] uses similar language about accepting 'another Jesus' or 'another gospel.' For the possible echo of a prophetic call-from-the-womb pattern, compare [[Jeremiah 1]] and [[Isaiah 49]], without assuming Galatians directly quotes either. [[Hebrews 13]]'s closing benediction shares only superficial resurrection/blessing language with Galatians 1 and is a weaker connection.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
