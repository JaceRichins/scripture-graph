# Acts 18 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Acts 18]] · My notes: [[Acts 18 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Acts 18 closes Paul's second missionary journey and opens his third. Paul settles in Corinth, partners with the newly arrived Jewish couple Aquila and Priscilla (who had come from Rome after Claudius's expulsion order against Jews there, [[Acts 18|18:2]]), and supports himself as a tentmaker while reasoning in the synagogue every Sabbath. Synagogue opposition provokes Paul's declaration that he will turn to the Gentiles (18:6) — but this is best read as a local, strategic response rather than a permanent policy: within the same chapter Paul continues reasoning with Jews at Ephesus (18:19), and Apollos likewise argues from the scriptures that Jesus is the Christ before Jewish audiences (18:28). A night vision promises Paul protection and 'much people' in Corinth (18:9-10), after which he remains eighteen months. A hearing before the proconsul Gallio (18:12-17), datable via the Delphi inscription to roughly AD 51-52, gives this chapter one of the firmer anchor points for Pauline chronology, though it constrains a window rather than fixing an exact date. The second half traces Paul's travels through Ephesus, Cæsarea, and Antioch before he sets out again through Galatia and Phrygia, while a parallel story introduces Apollos, an eloquent Alexandrian teacher whose understanding — limited at first to the baptism of John — is privately completed by Aquila and Priscilla.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Corinthian ministry and manual work (Acts 18:1–4):** Paul meets Aquila and Priscilla, works in their shared trade, and reasons every Sabbath.
2. **Opposition, conversion, and reassurance (Acts 18:5–11):** Paul testifies that Jesus is the Christ, announces a turn to Gentiles, sees converts baptized, and receives a night vision.
3. **The hearing before Gallio (Acts 18:12–17):** Gallio refuses to adjudicate the religious dispute; Sosthenes is then beaten.
4. **Paul's travel and strengthening of disciples (Acts 18:18–23):** Paul leaves Corinth, briefly teaches at Ephesus, returns to Antioch, and travels through Galatia and Phrygia.
5. **Apollos instructed and commissioned (Acts 18:24–28):** Aquila and Priscilla supplement his knowledge, the brethren commend him to disciples in Achaia, and he argues publicly from scripture.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter connects belief with hearing, testimony, and [[Baptism]] (Acts 18:5, 8, 28), but it does not reduce conversion to a single uniform sequence. Divine aid also appears without eliminating human effort: the Lord reassures Paul through [[Revelation]] (Acts 18:9–10), while Paul continues teaching for eighteen months (Acts 18:11). Acts 18:27 explicitly describes disciples as having believed through [[Grace]], though the verse alone does not define a complete theology of grace. Apollos's story emphasizes that sincere zeal and considerable scriptural knowledge can coexist with incomplete understanding (Acts 18:24–26).

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Acts 18:25-26 develops 'the way' language for the Christian message — Apollos knows 'the way of the Lord' but is taught 'the way of God more perfectly' by Aquila and Priscilla — consistent with Luke's broader use of 'the Way' as an early designation for the movement, though whether it functions here as a fixed title or a more general idiom for right teaching is not fully certain. Paul's testimony that 'Jesus was Christ' (18:5) and Apollos's public demonstration of the same claim from the scriptures (18:28) use closely parallel language, but this is best read as Luke's recurring formula for apostolic preaching elsewhere in Acts (e.g., 9:22; 17:3) rather than a literary device unique to this chapter's two ministers. The chapter does juxtapose two teaching scenes in different registers — Paul's public, extended synagogue ministry in Corinth and Aquila and Priscilla's private correction of Apollos in Ephesus — though Acts never frames this explicitly as a deliberate contrast, so it is best treated as a useful reading lens rather than a confirmed authorial structure.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Direct Narrative Evidence
> Paul's residence and labor with Aquila and Priscilla provide direct textual evidence that his teaching ministry in Corinth coexisted, at least for a time, with work in a shared trade.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · broadly accepted

> [!evidence] Direct Narrative Evidence
> The narrative is direct textual evidence that Priscilla participated with Aquila in further instruction of Apollos: both are named as hearing him, taking him to themselves, and expounding the way of God more perfectly. The plural wording does not specify each spouse's individual contribution.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · best-supported treatment of Priscilla's role · [[Direct Narrative Evidence in Acts 18|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] External Corroboration / Epigraphic
> Luke's note that Paul was brought before Gallio, proconsul ('deputy') of Achaia, ties this episode to a datable Roman official; an inscription discovered at Delphi recording a rescript of the emperor Claudius mentioning Gallio as proconsul of Achaia is used by scholars to anchor Gallio's term to approximately AD 51-52, giving one of the firmest fixed points in Pauline chronology.
> — strength 0.8 · confidence 0.9 · broadly accepted in scholarship · [[External Corroboration / Epigraphic in Acts 18|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Cross-Document Convergence / Apologetic Argument *(tentative)*
> Acts records that Crispus, the ruler of the Corinthian synagogue, believed and was baptized; Paul's own letter to the Corinthians separately and without narrative context states he personally baptized Crispus, which is sometimes cited as an 'undesigned coincidence' suggesting independent, non-collusive access to genuine tradition on the part of Luke and Paul.
> — strength 0.3 · confidence 0.5 · minority apologetic argument; mildly overstates the text

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- What was the content of Paul's vow at Cenchrea (18:18), and does the underlying grammar clearly identify Paul (rather than Aquila) as its subject?
- Is Paul's declaration about going to the Gentiles (18:6) best read as a local change of audience, given his return to synagogue teaching at Ephesus (18:19) and Apollos's continued preaching to Jews (18:28)?
- What specifically did Priscilla and Aquila add to Apollos's understanding (18:26), given that he already taught accurately as far as he knew?
- Is the Sosthenes beaten before Gallio (18:17) the same person named as Paul's co-sender in 1 Corinthians 1:1? The text gives no basis beyond the shared name.
- Does Gallio's refusal to judge represent principled restraint, indifference to the Jews' complaint, or both — and how should his disregard for the beating of Sosthenes affect that assessment (18:14-17)?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Useful further study would compare the sequence of Paul's journeys with the surrounding chapters, examine independent historical evidence for Claudius's expulsion order and Gallio's term in Achaia, and investigate textual and grammatical questions in Acts 18:17–21. Those external inquiries may clarify chronology and wording, but they should be kept distinct from what Acts 18 itself explicitly states.
