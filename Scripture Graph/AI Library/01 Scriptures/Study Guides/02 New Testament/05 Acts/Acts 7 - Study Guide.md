# Acts 7 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Acts 7]] · My notes: [[Acts 7 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Stephen recounts the history of Israel and names Moses as a prototype of Christ—He testifies of the apostasy in Israel—He sees Jesus on the right hand of God—Stephen’s testimony is rejected, and he is stoned to death.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Abraham and the unfulfilled promise (vv2-8):** God calls Abraham out of Mesopotamia before he possesses land or has a child; the covenant of circumcision follows, and the line continues through Isaac and Jacob to the twelve patriarchs.
2. **Joseph rejected and raised up (vv9-16):** Envious brothers sell Joseph into Egypt, but God is with him and exalts him to save the family from famine; the family's burial in the land as Stephen summarizes it compresses several distinct burial traditions from Genesis (see Further Study).
3. **Moses rejected, then sent, then resisted (vv17-43):** Moses is refused as "a ruler and a judge" by his own people at forty, then commissioned by God at the burning bush and sent as "a ruler and a deliverer" at eighty; after the Exodus, Israel turns to the golden calf even after receiving "the lively oracles." This is by far the longest section of the speech.
4. **Tabernacle, temple, and the Creator (vv44-50):** Stephen acknowledges the divinely patterned tabernacle and Solomon's temple, then quotes Isaiah to insist the Most High is not confined to "temples made with hands."
5. **Indictment and martyrdom (vv51-60):** The historical survey becomes a direct, second-person accusation; Stephen sees the glorified Christ, is cast out of the city, and is stoned while praying for his killers.

A rejection-then-recognition pattern runs through the history — both Joseph and Moses are first rejected by their own people before being recognized as God's chosen deliverers — which the speech implicitly applies to the council's rejection of Jesus.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
- **Moses as a type of Christ.** Acts 7:37 quotes Deuteronomy 18:15's prophecy of "a prophet... like unto me," the same text Peter applies to Jesus in Acts 3:22. Moses is twice rejected by his own people (7:27, 35) before being sent as "ruler and deliverer" — a pattern the speech implicitly extends to Jesus, rejected by this council and called "the Just One" (7:52).
- **Revelation without obedience.** Israel received the covenant of circumcision, the "lively oracles" through Moses, and the law itself "by the disposition of angels" (7:53), yet Stephen's climax charges that his hearers, like their fathers, resist the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost|Holy Ghost]]. Possessing revelation is not the same as heeding it.
- **God is not confined to a house.** Stephen affirms the wilderness tabernacle followed a pattern God revealed (7:44) and that Solomon built a temple (7:47) — he is not simply anti-temple. He then quotes Isaiah to insist "the most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands" (7:48-50): no building, even one built at God's own direction, can contain the Creator.
- **A vision touching the Godhead.** Acts 7:55-56 narratively distinguishes the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost|Holy Ghost]], the glory of God, and Jesus "standing on the right hand of God" within a single vision — three referents named together, though the text itself does not develop this into a formal doctrinal statement about their nature.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Stephen's dying words — "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit" and "Lord, lay not this sin to their charge" (7:59-60) — closely echo Jesus' words from the cross in Luke's Gospel ("Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit," Luke 23:46; "Father, forgive them," Luke 23:34, though this verse is absent from some early manuscripts). Since the same author wrote Luke and Acts, this plausibly reflects deliberate shaping of Stephen's death as Christlike, though it could also reflect a shared idiom of martyr prayer.

The speech also has a tight internal design: it moves from "our father" and "our fathers" (7:2, 11, 19, 39) to "your fathers" at the moment of accusation (7:51-52), turning the hearers from co-heirs of the story into its guilty party. "Hands" recur three times with pointed contrast — Israel "rejoiced in the works of their own hands" (the idol, 7:41), God "dwelleth not in temples made with hands" (7:48), yet "my hand made all these things" (7:50). And Jesus is seen "standing" rather than seated at God's right hand (7:55-56) — a detail that stands out against other New Testament pictures of Christ seated (e.g., Hebrews 1:3) and has invited several explanations (welcome, advocacy, simple visionary vividness) without settled consensus.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Evidence & Easter Eggs
<!-- SG:BEGIN evidence -->
> [!evidence] Narrative And Thematic Convergence
> God appears or acts in Mesopotamia, Egypt, Sinai, and the wilderness before Stephen states that the Most High does not dwell in human-made temples. This geographical pattern strongly supports reading the speech as opposing the confinement of God's presence to one building.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.7 · true pattern, overstated strength · [[Narrative And Thematic Convergence in Acts 7|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Internal Literary Pattern
> The repeated movement from rejection to divine authorization provides moderate internal evidence that Stephen presents Moses as a precursor to the rejected Just One. This supports a Moses-Christ typological reading, although the speech does not explicitly map every feature of Moses' life onto Jesus.
> — strength 0.7 · confidence 0.8 · accepted observation / disputed interpretation, well-hedged · [[Internal Literary Pattern in Acts 7|full dossier]]

> [!evidence] Early Christology / Titles Of Jesus *(tentative)*
> Stephen calls Jesus "the Just One" (Acts 7:52) rather than using his name; the same title recurs in Peter's speech (Acts 3:14) and in Paul's own later account of his conversion (Acts 22:14), suggesting "the Just/Righteous One" functioned as an early primitive christological title in the Jerusalem church's earliest preaching.
> — strength 0.4 · confidence 0.6 · Lukan literary pattern, not confirmed as a fixed historical title

> [!evidence] Textual Criticism *(tentative)*
> The 75-souls figure in Acts 7:14 is best explained as Stephen's speech (as recorded by Luke) reflecting the Greek Septuagint textual tradition of Genesis rather than the Hebrew Masoretic Text, consistent with a Hellenistic-Jewish/Greek-speaking milieu for this material in Acts.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · plausible but unconfirmed causal explanation

> [!evidence] Textual/Historical Discrepancy *(tentative)*
> The apparent conflation in Acts 7:16 of two distinct burial traditions (Abraham's Machpelah purchase and Jacob's Shechem purchase) is a long-noted textual/historical difficulty in Stephen's speech.
> — strength 0.5 · confidence 0.6 · disputed; no explanation should be presented as resolving it

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Why does Stephen answer the charge against him with a historical survey rather than a direct legal defense?
- Does Acts 7:48-50 reject the temple itself, or only the claim that God can be confined to it? How do 7:44 and 7:47 shape the answer?
- What might the rejection-then-recognition pattern in Joseph's and Moses' stories suggest about how Stephen wants his hearers to see their rejection of Jesus?
- Why might Jesus be seen "standing" rather than seated at God's right hand in Stephen's vision (7:55-56)?
- How should Stephen's severe accusation against his hearers be distinguished from a judgment against Jewish people generally?
- What is the significance of Saul's silent presence at Stephen's death (7:58), given who he becomes?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
- Compare Acts 7:37 with Acts 3:22 (and, for a Restoration-scripture parallel, 3 Nephi 20:23) on the recurring "prophet like unto Moses" prophecy.
- Compare Acts 7:59-60 with Luke 23:34 and 23:46 for the Stephen/Jesus parallel; note that some early manuscripts omit Luke 23:34.
- Acts 7:14's "threescore and fifteen souls" matches the Greek Septuagint tradition of Genesis 46:27/Exodus 1:5 rather than the Hebrew Masoretic count of seventy — a well-known text-critical detail, not an error unique to Stephen's speech.
- Acts 7:16 compresses distinct Genesis traditions: Abraham's purchase of the Machpelah cave near Hebron from Ephron (Genesis 23), and Jacob's purchase of land at Shechem from the sons of Hamor (Genesis 33:19), where Joseph — not Abraham — was later buried (Joshua 24:32). A genuine textual difficulty worth sitting with rather than explaining away.
- Acts 7:42-43 quotes Amos 5:25-27 but changes "beyond Damascus" (both the Hebrew and the standard Greek text of Amos) to "beyond Babylon" — likely an adaptation to the exile Stephen's audience actually knew, not a simple manuscript variant.
<!-- SG:END further-study -->
