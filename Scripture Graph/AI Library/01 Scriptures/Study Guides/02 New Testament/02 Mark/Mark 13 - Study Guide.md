# Mark 13 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Mark 13]] · My notes: [[Mark 13 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
> [!info] Chapter heading (official)
> Jesus tells of the calamities and signs preceding the Second Coming—There will be false Christs and false prophets—He gives the parable of the fig tree.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
1. **Temple prediction and private question (vv. 1–4):** Jesus foretells the temple buildings' destruction; four disciples ask, privately, when this will happen and what sign will precede it.
2. **Preliminary signs and persecution (vv. 5–13):** Wars, earthquakes, and famines are called "the beginnings of sorrows," not the end (vv. 7-8); disciples are warned of councils, beatings, and betrayal, but promised the Holy Ghost will speak through them, and told that enduring to the end brings salvation.
3. **Urgent flight and renewed warning (vv. 14–23):** The "abomination of desolation" (citing Daniel) triggers concrete flight instructions for those in Judæa, followed by a repeated warning that false Christs and false prophets will show signs and wonders convincing enough to threaten even the elect.
4. **Cosmic signs and the Son of Man's coming (vv. 24–27):** After the tribulation, the sun and moon darken, the stars fall, and the Son of Man is seen coming in the clouds with power and glory, sending angels to gather the elect "from the four winds."
5. **The fig tree and the unknown hour (vv. 28–32):** Recognizable signs mean the end is near, and "this generation shall not pass" before it happens — yet no one, not the angels, not even the Son, knows the day or hour, only the Father.
6. **The command to watch (vv. 33–37):** A parable of an absent householder's servants closes the discourse, framed by four repeated commands to watch, ending with "what I say unto you I say unto all, Watch."
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
Several threads run through the chapter: [[Signs of the Times]] such as wars and earthquakes are explicitly *not* meant to signal the immediate end (vv. 7-8); [[Endurance]] under persecution is tied to salvation (v. 13); testimony under hostile examination is aided by the [[Gift of the Holy Ghost]] rather than premeditated defense (v. 11); the [[Missionary Work|gospel's proclamation]] to all nations is named as a necessary precondition (v. 10); the coming tribulation is called unmatched "from the beginning of the creation," yet God shortens it "for the elect's sake" (vv. 19-20); even "the elect" can be at risk of deception from false Christs who show signs and wonders (vv. 21-22); and the [[Second Coming of Jesus Christ]] is tied to a visible coming of the Son of Man and an angelic gathering (vv. 24-27). Verse 32 states that the Son does not know the day or hour, distinguishing his knowledge from the Father's — the verse itself does not explain that distinction further, and it should not be pressed into a settled Christological conclusion.
<!-- SG:END doctrines -->

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
The repeated imperatives “take heed” and “watch” supply the discourse's clearest refrain. “These things” and “that day and that hour” may not have identical scope, but the text never explicitly defines their relationship. “This generation” in verse 30 and “abomination of desolation” in verse 14 are therefore interpretive pressure points rather than self-explanatory chronological labels.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
The discourse alternates prediction with pastoral command: each warning of danger is met with a directive — do not be deceived, do not be troubled, testify, flee, disbelieve false claimants, watch. Two parables work the same way, using an ordinary, observable process (a budding fig tree; a servant's watch through the night) to teach readiness without supplying exact timing. The closing section (vv. 33-37) is itself framed by a fourfold repetition of "watch" (vv. 33, 34, 35, 37), turning that unit into an exhortation rather than a timetable.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
- Does "this generation" (v. 30) mean the generation of Jesus's own hearers, or — as the Joseph Smith Translation of the parallel account in Joseph Smith—Matthew 1 suggests — whichever generation witnesses the signs described?
- Does "all these things" (v. 30) include the coming of the Son of Man (vv. 26-27), or only the preceding signs?
- Where, if anywhere, does the discourse shift from describing the temple's fall and flight from Judæa to a still-future Second Coming — and does it mark that shift at all?
- What does it mean that "the Son" does not know the day or hour (v. 32) — a statement about the incarnate Christ's mortal condition, or something more?
<!-- SG:END questions -->

## Further Study
<!-- SG:BEGIN further-study -->
Compare directly with [[Matthew 24]] and Luke 21 (the other two synoptic Olivet Discourses, though Luke's wording diverges more than Matthew's) and with Joseph Smith—Matthew 1, whose revisions bear on the "this generation" question. [[Matthew 10]] parallels the persecution and Spirit-assisted-testimony material. [[D&C 45]] offers a latter-day expansion of the same discourse. For the "abomination of desolation," compare Daniel 9, 11, and 12; for the "Son of man coming in the clouds," compare Daniel 7:13.
