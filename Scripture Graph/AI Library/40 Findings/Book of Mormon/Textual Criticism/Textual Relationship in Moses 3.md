# Textual Relationship in Moses 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Moses 3 and Genesis 2 share close wording across the formation of man, the planting of Eden, the garden vocation, the tree command, and the creation of woman, strongly supporting a literary or transmission relationship. This fits ordinary revision of an inherited biblical text, revelatory expansion expressed in familiar idiom, or shared tradition; the wording cannot decide among them. Evidence_strength is 0.84 (strong), aligned with the registry's other Genesis/Exodus dependence issues, since no attestation independent of Genesis itself is available.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Moses 3 and Genesis 2 share the same extended sequence and largely the same English wording for the formation of man, the planting of Eden, the garden vocation, the tree-of-knowledge command, and the creation of the woman, while Moses 3 also contains expansions absent from Genesis 2 (e.g., the v.7 'spiritually were they created' gloss).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The sustained sequence and close wording strongly indicate a literary or transmission relationship. They do not by themselves show whether Moses 3 directly revises Genesis, whether biblical wording entered through a revelatory or translation process, or whether both descend from an older tradition.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Establishes a literary/textual relationship compatible with several different accounts of Moses 3's origin (inspired restoration expressed in KJV idiom, inspired revision of the open KJV text, or theological expansion), but does not by itself decide among them.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
The pattern matches a familiar scriptural-rewriting shape: an authoritative base text retained in sequence and wording, with theological expansions layered onto it. On that reconstruction, Genesis 2 supplies the narrative spine and Moses 3 supplies the interpretive additions; the wording alone does not decide whether those additions are ancient, revealed, or editorial.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The overlap strongly supports textual dependence on the Genesis 2 tradition -- the same evidentiary class and ceiling used elsewhere in the registry for comparable Genesis/Exodus/Matthew dependence issues (pgp-abraham5-genesis2-literary-dependence 0.84, pgp-moses2-genesis1-abraham4-dependence 0.84, bom-mosiah13-exodus20-dependence 0.84). It gives no independent traction on whether the added or altered material is ancient, revealed, or nineteenth-century theological expansion.

**Inspiration:** Textual dependence on Genesis says nothing on its own about whether Moses 3's distinctive material is divinely revealed.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the direction or mechanism of the relationship, an ancient source language behind the wording, the historicity of the Eden narrative, or that any specific addition is revealed restoration rather than editorial expansion.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Inspired restoration of authentic ancient content expressed in KJV idiom** — predicts: Extensive shared wording with theologically meaningful additions. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Revision/expansion of the open KJV Genesis text during the JST project** — predicts: Near-verbatim retained wording interspersed with expansions, matching the documented physical process of the JST manuscripts. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent 19th-century composition unrelated to Genesis** — predicts: No sustained verbatim overlap. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
- **Shared ancient oral tradition independent of the biblical text** — predicts: Thematic but not verbatim-English overlap. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Moses 3 directly revises and expands the received Genesis 2 text — *plausible*: Economically explains both the preserved order and the close English wording without requiring an additional unattested source.
- An ancient source was rendered into King James idiom through translation or revelation — *possible*: Compatible with the result but not independently verifiable from the wording alone.
- Both accounts independently preserve a shared ancient tradition — *possible*: Could explain narrative agreement but does not by itself explain sustained near-verbatim English.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
