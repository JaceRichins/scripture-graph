# Textual Parallel in 3 Nephi 25

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
All six verses of 3 Nephi 25 correspond in sequence and close, though not uniform, wording to Malachi 4, strongly supporting dependence on or mediation through that textual tradition. The Son/Sun difference in verse 2 is a genuine transmission datum but does not identify the mechanism. This chapter belongs to the same continuous quotation event as 3 Nephi 24 and should not be aggregated as independent evidence.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
All six verses of 3 Nephi 25 correspond in order and substance to all six verses of Malachi 4. The wording is close but not uniform: notably, 3 Nephi 25:2 reads 'Son of Righteousness' where Malachi 4:2 reads 'Sun of righteousness.'
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The complete ordered correspondence strongly indicates quotation, adaptation, or translation-level mediation of Malachi 4. The Son/Sun difference is relevant to competing transmission explanations but does not materially weaken the chapter-level relationship.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compatible with ancient quotation of Malachi rendered into KJV-influenced English, revelatory harmonization, or direct modern reuse of Malachi 4.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
This chapter pairs with 3 Nephi 24/Malachi 3 as one continuous quotation event and fits the corpus's broader Malachi/KJV dependence pattern rather than constituting a second, independent instance of it.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports a textual relationship with Malachi 4. This shares one underlying quotation event with the adjacent 3 Nephi 24/Malachi 3 dependence (0.83) and should carry the same weight rather than being scored as a separate, additional data point.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: matched wording with a known English Bible chapter says nothing about whether the underlying prophecy or its translation was divinely inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish ancient authorship, translation mechanism, or the Book of Mormon's antiquity; not independent additional evidence beyond the already-registered Malachi-dependence pattern.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient citation rendered via KJV-influenced translation** — predicts: Close but not necessarily verbatim wording with translator-supplied idiom. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Direct modern reuse of Malachi 4** — predicts: Near-verbatim six-of-six correspondence. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Independent composition unconnected to Malachi's text** — predicts: Substantial independent wording. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jesus historically transmitted Malachi's prophecy to the Nephites, with the later translation using familiar biblical English. — *possible*: This is the narrative presentation, but the event and ancient record are not independently attested by the parallel.
- An ancient quotation was rendered in KJV-shaped English. — *plausible*
- A revelatory, translational, or editorial process harmonized corresponding material with Malachi 4. — *possible*: It fits the final text, but this mechanism is not independently recoverable from the correspondence.
- A nineteenth-century composer directly adapted Malachi 4. — *plausible*
- Shared apocalyptic imagery independently produced the complete sequence. — *ad hoc*: Common imagery cannot explain the six-of-six ordered verbal correspondence.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
This receives the same treatment as other chapter-length KJV quotations in the corpus (Isaiah chapters, the Sermon on the Mount): the parallel establishes a textual relationship, not authenticity or fraud, by itself.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->
