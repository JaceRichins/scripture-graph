# Intra-Textual Ethical Contrast in Alma 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 4 contrasts prideful attachment to riches and neglect of the needy with humility and sharing, directing its criticism at what possessions become socially and spiritually rather than at wealth or productive labor as such.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 4 associates church members' riches and costly display with pride, setting their hearts on wealth, scorn, persecution, inequality, and neglect of the needy, then contrasts that with people who humble themselves, impart their substance, feed the hungry, and succor the afflicted.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter's ethical contrast centers on pride, attachment to riches, inequality, and treatment of other people; productive labor is not itself identified as the offense, though riches remain a recurring condition in which pride and neglect can develop.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage illuminates the text's economic ethic by evaluating possessions through their social and spiritual effects: status display and refusal of aid mark apostasy, while voluntary sharing marks humility and fidelity.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read as a single ethical arc, the chapter moves from material abundance to status competition and neglect of the vulnerable, then supplies its own counterpicture in v.13, where substance is redirected toward the needy and suffering for Christ's sake replaces using prosperity to elevate oneself over others.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
