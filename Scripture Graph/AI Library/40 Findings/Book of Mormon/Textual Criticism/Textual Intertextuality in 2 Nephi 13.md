# Textual Intertextuality in 2 Nephi 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 13's chapter-length correspondence with Isaiah 3 strongly supports deliberate incorporation of the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition, consistent with every other Book of Mormon Isaiah block. This note reuses the registry's canonical weight (0.84) for the shared issue rather than scoring it independently, since it is additional evidence of the same phenomenon, not a new one. It does not determine the date, provenance, or translation mechanism of the underlying record. Scripture: [[2 Nephi 13#^2ne-13-1|2 Nephi 13:1-26]], [[Isaiah 3#^isa-3-1|Isaiah 3:1-26]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 13 reproduces the wording of Isaiah 3 almost verbatim across its full length, including archaic KJV phrasing ("the stay and the staff, the whole stay of bread, and the whole stay of water").
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The sustained, chapter-length verbal match is best read as a direct textual relationship between 2 Nephi 13 and the King James rendering of Isaiah 3, not independent translation of a Hebrew Vorlage that happens to converge on KJV English.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This is one instance of the corpus-wide Isaiah/KJV correspondence already registered at `bom-isaiah-textual-dependence` (strong, 0.84); per the canonical-assessment rule, this note reuses that weight rather than being scored independently, since it is one more example of the same underlying phenomenon rather than a new data point.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Whichever compositional model is correct, the chapter functions in its final form as a direct citation of the Isaiah 3 tradition in King-James-shaped English; distinguishing genuine ancient quotation-in-translation from KJV-mediated dictation or direct copying requires evidence external to the wording match itself.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance

Strongly supports textual dependence on the Isaiah/KJV tradition for this chapter, consistent with the registry's overall assessment. It gives no separate traction on translation mechanism, provenance, or antiquity beyond what the general Isaiah-dependence issue already establishes.

**Inspiration:** Nothing — textual mediation through KJV English says nothing by itself about whether the underlying content or process was inspired.

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **ancient record containing an Isaiah quotation, translated with KJV-influenced English** — predicts: close but not necessarily verbatim correspondence, since a genuine translation act intervenes. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **translation mediated directly through an open King James Bible** — predicts: near-verbatim correspondence including archaic KJV-specific phrasing, exactly as observed. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **nineteenth-century composition copying the King James Isaiah text** — predicts: the same near-verbatim correspondence. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Translation of a genuine ancient record containing Isaiah, rendered in period-appropriate biblical English — *plausible*: internally consistent with the text's own narrative claim, though it does not independently explain the near-verbatim KJV-specific wording
- KJV mediation during a dictation-based translation process — *plausible*: widely proposed and consistent with the pattern across all Isaiah blocks, though not independently verified apart from the pattern itself
- Direct nineteenth-century copying/composition from the King James Bible — *plausible*: requires no additional unattested mechanism
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
