# TextualLiterary Parallel in 3 Nephi 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sustained sequence and close wording of [[3 Nephi 22#^3ne-22-1|3 Nephi 22:1]], [[3 Nephi 22#^3ne-22-9|3 Nephi 22:9]], [[3 Nephi 22#^3ne-22-17|3 Nephi 22:17]], and the intervening material strongly support dependence on or mediation through Isaiah 54 in its KJV textual tradition. An ancient quotation rendered in biblical English, revelatory harmonization, and direct nineteenth-century use of the KJV all fit that relationship. The chapter is one strong instance of the broader Isaiah-dependence pattern and should not be counted again verse by verse.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 22 follows Isaiah 54 across essentially the whole chapter in the same sequence and with extensive agreement with KJV wording. The cited opening, Noah-covenant, and concluding inheritance sayings are representative parts of that sustained correspondence.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter is a quotation or close adaptation of the Isaiah 54 textual tradition in KJV-shaped English, rather than an independent convergence of themes or vocabulary.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The correspondence establishes a literary or transmission relationship with Isaiah 54. It is compatible with an ancient community preserving Isaiah, an ancient quotation translated through the KJV, revelatory harmonization, or direct use of the KJV during nineteenth-century composition.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Isaiah 54 is a restoration oracle addressed to a formerly desolate covenant community, using images of renewed family, enduring covenant, enlarged habitation, and protection. In 3 Nephi, its placement after the destruction and the risen Christ's covenant teaching makes those images a fitting scriptural account of Israel's restoration. On an ancient-record reconstruction it is inherited scripture applied to a new audience; on translation or modern-composition reconstructions it is a deliberate KJV-mediated quotation serving the same literary purpose.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The strongest supportive reading observes that an Israelite-derived Nephite record would be expected to preserve and interpret Isaiah, while a KJV-shaped translation could account for the English wording. The principal limitation is that direct modern reuse predicts the chapter-length English agreement equally well or better, so the finding strongly establishes textual dependence but does not independently support ancient authorship.

**Inspiration:** Textual dependence on Isaiah 54 implies nothing by itself about whether 3 Nephi 22 or the Book of Mormon is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The correspondence does not establish the age or provenance of the proposed underlying record, the direction or mechanism of mediation, the historical delivery of the chapter in the narrated setting, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient Nephite quotation with KJV-shaped translation** — predicts: A preserved Isaiah passage appears in its inherited sequence while the English translation follows familiar KJV diction.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Ancient quotation without KJV mediation** — predicts: Broad agreement with Isaiah is expected, but the extensive specifically English correspondence requires further explanation.. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
- **Revelatory or editorial KJV harmonization** — predicts: The final English text intentionally uses the familiar Isaiah 54 form regardless of the underlying wording.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Nineteenth-century composition using the KJV** — predicts: Isaiah 54 is copied or closely adapted as the base text for the chapter.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient Nephite text quoted Isaiah from an inherited Israelite textual tradition and was translated in KJV language — *plausible*: The narrative provides a setting in which Isaiah is valued and quoted, and KJV-shaped translation can account for the English form.
- The KJV was used directly as a base text during nineteenth-century composition — *plausible*: This straightforwardly predicts both the sustained sequence and close English wording.
- The revealed English was deliberately harmonized to Isaiah 54 — *possible*: This fits a revelatory translation model but cannot be distinguished from direct textual reuse by the correspondence itself.
- Independent composition produced the chapter-length match without textual or translation mediation — *ad hoc*: Independent convergence is not a credible explanation for sustained sequence and extensive verbal agreement.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

The sustained sequence and close English wording strongly support textual dependence on the Isaiah 54/KJV tradition, while leaving ancient quotation, translation harmonization, and direct modern reuse unresolved.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Scriptural authors often quote or adapt authoritative earlier texts, but near chapter-length agreement in both order and wording has a very low chance of arising independently. The chapter should count as one instance of the broader Book of Mormon Isaiah/KJV dependence pattern, not as numerous independent verse-level pieces of evidence.

What would move this: Evidence for specifically KJV-level mediation would rise with systematic agreement in distinctive KJV readings against earlier Hebrew or ancient-version evidence and fall if early textual forms distinctive from the KJV were shown to underlie the Book of Mormon variants. No plausible finding would reduce the chapter-level relationship with Isaiah 54 itself below strong.
