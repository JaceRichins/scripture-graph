# Textual Correspondence in 2 Nephi 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter-length sequence and extensive wording shared by 2 Nephi 7 and Isaiah 50 are strong evidence for transmission or deliberate reuse of the Isaiah textual tradition. The final-form relationship is clear, while the mainstream exilic dating of Isaiah 50 creates a difficulty for the brass-plates model and KJV-shaped translation, harmonization, and modern adaptation remain viable.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 7:1-11 reproduces Isaiah 50:1-11 in full sequence with extensive verbatim English correspondence.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter stands in a direct literary relationship to the Isaiah 50/KJV tradition; the relationship's existence is essentially uncontested, while its historical mechanism is the genuinely disputed question.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The chapter is compatible with KJV-shaped translation, editorial harmonization, or nineteenth-century reuse. Ancient transmission from the brass plates remains possible, but mainstream critical scholarship commonly dates Isaiah 50 within the exilic Isaiah 40–55 corpus, later than the narrated acquisition and departure of the plates; earlier or unified authorship remains a contested response.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On an ancient-record reconstruction accepting an early form of Isaiah 50, Jacob transmits the unit and it is rendered into familiar biblical English. Translation-stage harmonization can produce the same final form without requiring verbatim ancient wording, while nineteenth-century composition can obtain it through direct adaptation. The chapter-length match securely identifies the textual tradition, whereas the conventional dating of Isaiah 50 bears separately on the brass-plates reconstruction.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
This is strong support for the proposition that 2 Nephi 7's English form textually depends on the Isaiah/KJV tradition, but per the registered issue it is capped at strong (0.84): tight-control ancient translation, harmonizing revelation, and direct modern use of the KJV all predict essentially the same full-chapter correspondence, so the observation's near-certainty should not be read into the mechanism weight.

**Inspiration:** The textual relationship says nothing by itself about whether the underlying translation or revelation is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish ancient Book of Mormon authorship, the direction of dependence, the antiquity of specific variant readings, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- The passage translates Isaiah 50 from an ancient record possessed within the narrative. — *possible*: It fits the narrative framework, but the record is not externally attested and mainstream dating of Isaiah 50 creates a chronological difficulty.
- Ancient material was rendered or harmonized into KJV language. — *plausible*: This explains the close English agreement, while any claim that Isaiah 50 was already on the plates retains the source-dating problem.
- The chapter was composed or expanded using the KJV of Isaiah 50. — *plausible*: KJV availability and the wider Isaiah-mediation pattern make this a serious economical mechanism, though they do not independently establish its exclusive use here.
- Both texts independently reproduce an unidentified common tradition without KJV-level mediation. — *possible*: Common ancestry could explain sequence, but an unidentified witness alone explains the close English wording less economically than a model including English biblical mediation.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
