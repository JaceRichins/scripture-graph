# Intertextual New Testament Citation in 3 Nephi 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[Matthew 11#^matt-11-10|Matthew 11:10]], [[Mark 1#^mark-1-2|Mark 1:2]], and [[Luke 7#^luke-7-27|Luke 7:27]] explicitly apply Malachi's preparing messenger to John the Baptist. In [[3 Nephi 24#^3ne-24-1|3 Nephi 24:1]], the risen Jesus supplies and expounds Malachi's oracle, inviting a Christian reading of the passage, although the recorded text does not preserve his identification of either messenger.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Matthew 11:10, Mark 1:2, and Luke 7:27 quote the statement about a messenger preparing the way and apply it to John the Baptist. In 3 Nephi 24:1, the risen Jesus directs that Malachi's words be written and then expounds them, but the surviving cited text does not record an explicit identification of either "my messenger" or "the messenger of the covenant."
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
A canonical Christian reading naturally identifies the preparatory messenger with John the Baptist and often reads the covenant messenger as Christ. The first identification is explicit in the cited Gospels; the second is a contextual Christian interpretation rather than an identification explicitly preserved in 3 Nephi 24:1.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passages illustrate how Malachi 3:1 was received and reapplied within early Christian texts and later Restoration scripture. They also preserve the possibility that Malachi's language could function typologically rather than as a prediction restricted to one narrowly defined referent.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
In the Gospel reception of Malachi, the preparing messenger becomes John the Baptist, who precedes Jesus. On a common Christian reconstruction, the distinct "messenger of the covenant" who comes to the temple is Christ. Third Nephi places Malachi's oracle on the lips of the risen Jesus and says that he expounded it, so that two-stage Christian reading fits the narrative setting; the text as preserved leaves the exposition unstated and therefore does not itself settle the second messenger's referent.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
