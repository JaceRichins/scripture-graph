# Internal-Textual-Honesty in Moroni 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter's explicit claim that Nephite wartime atrocities "exceed" those of the Lamanites (v.9), and that Nephite "wickedness doth exceed that of the Lamanites" (v.20), shows the Book of Mormon narrator willing to depict his own covenant people as morally worse than their traditional enemies rather than presenting a one-sided, self-flattering account.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish precise historical facts about relative atrocity rates; it establishes only that the text, as we have it, records Mormon's own verdict against his people.

**Alternative explanations:**
- This is rhetorical hyperbole from a grieving, exhausted father-general rather than a calibrated moral ledger.
- War-lament literature in many traditions uses maximalist 'worse than the enemy' language to shock audiences into repentance, independent of precise historicity.
- Mormon's harsh self-assessment of his own people may reflect a specific theological purpose (warning future readers) more than an evenhanded historical judgment.
