# Internal-Textual-Honesty in Moroni 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Moroni 9 twice presents Mormon judging Nephite conduct as worse than Lamanite conduct. The comparison functions as an anguished in-group indictment, showing that covenant identity does not shield the Nephites from the text's harshest moral judgment. **Scripture:** [[Moroni 9#^moro-9-9|Moroni 9:9]], [[Moroni 9#^moro-9-20|Moroni 9:20]]
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
In the letter preserved in Moroni 9, Mormon says that Nephite atrocities at Moriantum exceeded the described Lamanite abomination and later repeats that his people's wickedness exceeded that of the Lamanites.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The letter's speaker condemns his own covenant community more severely than its traditional enemy. This is self-accusatory wartime lament and prophetic indictment, although its comparative language may also be rhetorically maximalist.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage complicates any description of the Book of Mormon as uniformly flattering toward the Nephites. Its moral division is not simply Nephite versus Lamanite: near the narrative's end, covenant identity offers the Nephites no exemption from severe condemnation.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Within the proposed rhetorical setting of a father and commander writing amid social collapse, comparison with the traditional enemy intensifies the lament: the people who possessed greater covenant knowledge have fallen beneath those they formerly opposed. The language therefore serves both as wartime grief and as a warning that inherited religious identity cannot substitute for moral conduct.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
