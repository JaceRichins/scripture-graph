# Textual And Literary Dependence in 3 Nephi 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 14 follows Matthew 7 through nearly the entire discourse in the same sequence and with extensive distinctive KJV wording ([[3 Nephi 14#^3ne-14-1|3 Nephi 14:1–27]], [[Matthew 7#^matt-7-1|Matthew 7:1–27]]). This strongly supports Matthew/KJV mediation in the final English text and is more specific than a resemblance in general doctrine. Historical repetition rendered through biblical English, revelatory or editorial harmonization, and direct nineteenth-century adaptation remain viable mechanisms, so the parallel identifies the textual relationship without settling provenance or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
After a brief narrative introduction, 3 Nephi 14 follows Matthew 7:1–27 through almost the entire chapter, preserving the same broad sequence of judgment, the mote and beam, holy things and pearls, asking and receiving, the golden rule, two ways, false prophets and fruits, profession without obedience, and the houses built on rock and sand. Numerous clauses reproduce distinctive KJV English closely.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The density, preserved order, and verbal specificity strongly indicate that the final English form of 3 Nephi 14 is related to the Matthew 7/KJV textual tradition, not merely to general teachings independently attributed to Jesus.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The chapter belongs textually to the received Matthean Sermon tradition in its English form. Comparison can identify where 3 Nephi reproduces, omits, introduces, or reframes Matthean material and can test proposed translation or compositional mechanisms.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Matthew 7 provides the recognizable English and sequential framework through which 3 Nephi 14 is presented. On a repeated-sermon reconstruction, Jesus teaches closely related material and the English translation deliberately uses the familiar Matthean form; on harmonization or accommodation reconstructions, the received biblical text shapes the revelation's wording for its audience; on a nineteenth-century compositional reconstruction, Matthew 7 is adapted directly into the Nephite setting. Each explains the final-form relationship, while a model of wholly independent English wording does not.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The strongest supportive reading is that a historically repeated sermon could contain closely related teachings and be translated through familiar scriptural English. That makes the correspondence compatible with an ancient sermon under a loose or harmonizing translation model. Nevertheless, the sustained KJV wording is directly expected under nineteenth-century reuse as well, so the parallel strongly supports Matthew/KJV mediation but does not independently favor ancient delivery over modern adaptation; it creates greater tension for a strict model requiring the English to preserve wording independent of the New Testament tradition.

**Inspiration:** The textual relationship itself implies nothing about inspiration because inspired translation, revelatory harmonization, and ordinary adaptation all predict recognizable biblical mediation.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The relationship does not establish direct nineteenth-century copying rather than translation-level harmonization, identify the language or exact content of any underlying Nephite sermon, prove that Jesus historically repeated the sermon, or determine whether the text is ancient, fabricated, or inspired.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Historically repeated sermon with KJV-shaped translation** — predicts: Jesus may repeat substantially similar teaching, while a translator renders it using the familiar English wording and sequence of Matthew 7.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Revelatory or editorial harmonization** — predicts: A revealed or edited English text intentionally aligns a related discourse with Matthew 7 so readers recognize the connection.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Nineteenth-century adaptation of Matthew 7** — predicts: The available KJV chapter supplies the wording, order, and discourse structure, with a short narrative frame adapting it to the Nephite setting.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Strictly independent ancient wording preserved without New Testament mediation** — predicts: Related doctrine is possible, but extensive agreement with the later English KJV in sequence and distinctive expressions is unexpected.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
- **Independent preservation of common Jesus tradition** — predicts: Some sayings and themes overlap, but near-chapter-length KJV agreement requires additional textual or translation-level mediation.. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jesus historically delivered substantially the same sermon to another audience. — *possible*: Teachers can repeat material, and the narrative explicitly presents a second audience, but historical repetition alone does not explain the precise KJV English form.
- An ancient parallel sermon was translated through familiar KJV wording. — *plausible*: Loose translation can deliberately use an established biblical register and explains both shared content and English wording, though the underlying sermon cannot be independently recovered from this parallel.
- The English wording reflects revelatory accommodation or harmonization. — *possible*: This coheres with a theological model of revelation, but textual comparison alone cannot independently verify divine accommodation as the mechanism.
- The final text directly adapts Matthew 7 in the nineteenth century. — *plausible*: The KJV was available and the nearly continuous agreement in wording and order is straightforwardly predicted by literary reuse.
- Both accounts independently preserve common Jesus tradition without textual mediation. — *possible*: Common tradition can explain individual teachings, but the sustained KJV sequence and phrasing make it insufficient by itself.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *The final English text of the Sermon material in 3 Nephi is textually mediated through or deliberately dependent on the Matthew/KJV tradition.*

The sustained sequence and close KJV English strongly support Matthew/KJV mediation in 3 Nephi 14's final form, while leaving historical repetition with translation harmonization, revelatory accommodation, and nineteenth-century adaptation viable.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Individual ethical sayings, paired contrasts, and building metaphors recur widely, but a near-complete chapter preserving numerous units in the same order and close KJV English has a much lower accidental base rate. The note compares one obviously parallel chapter rather than reporting a blinded corpus search, and 3 Nephi contains extensive Sermon-on-the-Mount material elsewhere; this instance is therefore highly specific but dependent on the broader Matthew/KJV mediation phenomenon rather than an additional independent item.

What would move this: Assessment dated 2026-09-04.
