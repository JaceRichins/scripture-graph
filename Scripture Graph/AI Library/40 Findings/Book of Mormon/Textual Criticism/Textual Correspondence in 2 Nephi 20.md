# Textual Correspondence in 2 Nephi 20

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The continuous correspondence between [[2 Nephi 20#^2ne-20-1|2 Nephi 20:1–34]] and [[Isaiah 10#^isa-10-1|Isaiah 10:1–34]] is strong evidence of dependence on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition. Its chapter-wide sequence is far more specific than shared prophetic themes, but it does not distinguish an ancient Isaianic source rendered in biblical English from translation-level harmonization or direct nineteenth-century reuse.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 20 corresponds closely and continuously to Isaiah 10 across the full chapter, including matched wording, clause order, and idiosyncratic KJV phrasing (e.g., v.1's 'decree unrighteous decrees... write grievousness which they have prescribed').
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The density and continuity of the match indicate a direct textual relationship to the KJV rendering of Isaiah specifically, not merely a shared topic or independently translated Hebrew source.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This establishes a literary relationship to the KJV textual tradition far more securely than it establishes any particular mechanism (ancient quotation rendered through Bible English, translation-level harmonization, or direct modern reuse) or the chapter's date or antiquity.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
This fits the well-documented pattern, established across Book of Mormon textual criticism, that its ~433 quoted Isaiah verses closely track the KJV wording, including KJV's own italicized insertions in places. Under the leading reconstructions, this reflects either a translation process saturated in period Bible-English idiom or direct consultation of a physical KJV for the Isaiah blocks specifically; the chapter-wide match to Isaiah 10 is one instance of that broader, already-registered pattern rather than a separate data point.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports KJV-mediated dependence as a textual fact about the final English text. It does not independently support (or count as additional evidence beyond) the general Isaiah-dependence pattern already established across the Book of Mormon's other Isaiah chapters -- this instance should not be added on top of that pattern as separate corroboration.

**Inspiration:** Nothing -- a close textual match to KJV Isaiah says nothing by itself about whether the Book of Mormon is divinely inspired; that question turns on the mechanism of translation, which this evidence does not resolve.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the direction or mechanism of dependence, the date of the underlying material, the precise underlying-language text, or the Book of Mormon's antiquity.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Direct copying from, or heavy reliance on, a physically present KJV Bible** — predicts: Near word-for-word correspondence including KJV's specific translation choices and italicized-word insertions. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Tight ancient-source translation rendered in period Bible-English idiom** — predicts: Close but not necessarily identical correspondence, with some independent variants. Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
- **Independent ancient transmission with no KJV contact** — predicts: Agreement with Isaiah's content but no reason to match KJV's specific English translation choices. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient Isaiah text on the brass plates was translated using period Bible English, with the Isaiah portions especially prone to conscious or unconscious KJV phrasing since the KJV was the only English Bible available — *plausible*: Consistent with the translation's known reliance on King James idiom throughout the Book of Mormon, not only in quoted Isaiah
- The translator or author directly consulted, or incorporated, a physical KJV Bible for these chapters specifically — *independently supported*: Textual criticism of the Book of Mormon (e.g., Skousen's work) has documented KJV italicized words carried into the Book of Mormon's Isaiah blocks, evidence of direct textual contact with a printed KJV
- Coincidental convergence without any contact between the two texts — *ad hoc*: The length and precision of the chapter-wide match make independent coincidence implausible without added unsupported assumptions
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
Extensive verbal correspondence between two texts is treated identically across corpora as strong evidence of a literary relationship without settling direction or mechanism (e.g., Kings/Chronicles parallels, or Synoptic Gospel overlaps); this note applies the same standard rather than a more credulous or more skeptical one.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *The Book of Mormon's Isaiah chapters depend on or descend from the Isaiah textual tradition represented in the King James Bible.*

The chapter-wide correspondence between 2 Nephi 20 and Isaiah 10 strongly supports KJV-mediated textual dependence in the final English text, but this instance is the same evidence class already registered under bom-isaiah-textual-dependence and does not add independent weight beyond it.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Not a look-elsewhere pattern claim; this is a full-chapter, continuous correspondence rather than an isolated phrase, which is why the underlying observation is essentially certain even though the registry caps its evidentiary weight for the contested mechanism question.

What would move this: A documented pre-1830 manuscript tradition of Isaiah 10 diverging from KJV in ways the Book of Mormon nonetheless follows would strengthen an independent-ancient-source reading; further documented KJV-specific idiosyncrasies (italics, translator errors) reproduced in this chapter would strengthen direct-KJV-contact readings.
