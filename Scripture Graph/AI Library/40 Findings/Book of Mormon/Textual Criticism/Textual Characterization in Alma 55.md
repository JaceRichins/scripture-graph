# Textual Characterization in Alma 55

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 55:18-19's explicit counterfactual and stated motive, followed by the captured-not-killed outcome in v.24, strongly support that the final text deliberately frames Moroni's choice as an ethical ideal privileging restraint over bloodshed. This is a claim about literary design, not independent evidence of Moroni's historical motives, and it sits in tension with the same passage's use of enemy intoxication as the enabling tactic. The registered strong-band weight (0.78) is retained; the note's earlier 0.86 score overstated it relative to the canonical assessment for this issue.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 55:18 states that the Nephites could have killed the intoxicated Lamanites. Verse 19 explicitly attributes Moroni's refusal to his aversion to bloodshed, desire to save his people, and concern about injustice. Verse 24 reports the capture of the city, liberation of the Nephite prisoners, and taking of opposing soldiers as prisoners.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The narrator characterizes this particular decision as purposeful restraint: when Moroni could accomplish deliverance without killing incapacitated enemies, capture was preferable to bloodshed.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The episode distinguishes killing during resistance from sparing enemies who can be subdued without combat and portrays military conduct as subject to ethical evaluation. Its approval is specific to the choice not to kill and need not extend to the operation's deception, exploitation of intoxication, or later treatment of prisoners.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a historical-narrative reconstruction, Moroni combines restraint with the practical objectives of liberating captives, occupying the city, and acquiring prisoners. On a literary or editorial reconstruction, the forgone opportunity to kill is narrated precisely to make Moroni's restraint visible. Both reconstructions illuminate how the episode joins strategic success to an explicit preference for deliverance over unnecessary bloodshed.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
