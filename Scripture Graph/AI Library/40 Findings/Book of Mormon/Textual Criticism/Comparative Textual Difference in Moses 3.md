# Comparative Textual Difference in Moses 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Moses 3:5 and 3:17 contain substantive additions to the corresponding wording of Genesis 2:5 and 2:17. The additions make spiritual creation and Adam's capacity to choose explicit, showing how Moses 3 develops the familiar Genesis account for Latter-day Saint theological instruction.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Relative to the corresponding King James wording of Genesis 2, Moses 3:5 adds an explanation that creation occurred spiritually before naturally, and Moses 3:17 adds that Adam may choose for himself.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Moses 3 presents Genesis-related material in an expanded form, using the additions to articulate spiritual creation and human moral agency more explicitly than the corresponding Genesis verses do.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The comparison documents how the Latter-day Saint revision of the creation account develops doctrines that became important within the movement's scriptural and theological tradition.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->
## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a restoration reconstruction, the additions express creation and agency teachings understood to belong to a fuller account than the received Genesis text preserves. On an inspired-revision reconstruction, they clarify the theological meaning of Genesis without requiring verbatim recovery of an earlier manuscript. As final-form scripture, both clauses make the creation narrative serve a broader account of spiritual existence and accountable human choice.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
