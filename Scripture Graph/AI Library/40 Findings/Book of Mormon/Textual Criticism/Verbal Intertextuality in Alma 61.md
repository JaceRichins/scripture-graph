# Verbal Intertextuality in Alma 61

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 61:21 closely reproduces Galatians 5:1's ordered stand-fast, liberty, and made-free language, while Alma 61:12 supplies the reinforcing phrase "the yoke of bondage" and verse 9 restates part of the same theme. This strongly supports deliberate Galatians-related or KJV-shaped mediation in the final English text, while leaving loose translation, revelatory or editorial harmonization, and nineteenth-century adaptation unresolved.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 61:21 closely follows Galatians 5:1 by combining standing fast, liberty, and being made free in the same order and similar syntax. Alma 61:12 adds the exact phrase "the yoke of bondage," while Alma 61:9 repeats the stand-fast and liberty language as dependent thematic reinforcement rather than an independent third correspondence.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The near-verbatim clause in verse 21, reinforced elsewhere in the chapter by "the yoke of bondage," is most plausibly read as deliberate Galatians-related or KJV-shaped mediation in the final English text.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The relationship places Alma 61's English wording within the reception history of Pauline liberty diction. It does not identify when or by whom that wording entered the text or determine whether an ancient discourse underlies it.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On an ancient-source reconstruction, an underlying liberty discourse was rendered or harmonized through the familiar language of Galatians 5:1. On a nineteenth-century-composition reconstruction, Galatians supplied memorable wording for Pahoran's political letter. Both fit the final English relationship well, whereas a lexically transparent pre-Pauline reconstruction requires an independently unattested close convergence.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The correspondence strongly supports specific Galatians/KJV mediation and creates difficulty for a lexically transparent pre-Pauline translation. It provides little discrimination among loose translation, revelatory or editorial harmonization, and nineteenth-century composition, all of which readily predict the final-English relationship.

**Inspiration:** The verbal relationship provides no independent discrimination concerning inspiration because inspired translation, editorial harmonization, and ordinary literary composition can all reuse earlier scripture.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The parallel does not determine the date, direction, human author, or mechanism of mediation; establish that the narrated Pahoran knew Pauline wording; resolve whether an ancient epistle underlies the passage; or independently establish or challenge historicity.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
**What the text itself requires:** Alma 61 requires a political and theological contrast between liberty and bondage, but it does not require wording derived from Galatians. The unusually close Galatians-like wording is therefore a feature of the final English form rather than a narrative necessity.

- **Ancient source with loose, KJV-shaped translation** (*plausible*) — predicts: Ancient liberty content may be expressed through familiar biblical English, including wording unavailable to the narrated speaker.. Fit with this finding: *fits well*. Support for the model: The text does not define its translation mechanics, and extensive KJV-shaped diction is independently documented across the English Book of Mormon.
- **Ancient source with revelatory or editorial harmonization** (*possible*) — predicts: A later voice may intentionally connect an ancient liberty theme with Galatians.. Fit with this finding: *fits well*. Support for the model: The mechanism explains both chronology and wording, but this passage supplies no independent marker identifying such a harmonizer.
- **Tight, lexically transparent translation of a pre-Pauline source** (*possible*) — predicts: A pre-Pauline expression would need to correspond closely both to Galatians and to its KJV rendering.. Fit with this finding: *substantial difficulty*. Support for the model: No independently attested ancient counterpart for the combined wording has been supplied.
- **Nineteenth-century composition using the KJV** (*plausible*) — predicts: A KJV-literate composer could adapt Galatians 5:1 with context-sensitive substitutions.. Fit with this finding: *fits well*. Support for the model: The KJV was available in the production environment, but the broader KJV-mediation pattern does not independently establish this particular mechanism.
- **Independent ancient liberty-and-bondage tradition** (*plausible*) — predicts: Independent ancient discourse can readily supply the underlying freedom-versus-bondage theme but does not by itself predict the close final-English clause and phrase.. Fit with this finding: *fits the content but not the wording without later mediation*. Support for the model: Exodus and covenant traditions make the theme historically ordinary; no proposed ancient source reproduces the specific English correspondence.

**Considered and set aside:**
- Direct access by the narrated Pahoran to Paul's epistle — It conflicts with the narrative chronology and adds an unsupported transmission mechanism.
- A demonstrated common pre-Pauline written source for the complete wording — Such a source would matter, but none has been identified.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- A translator deliberately rendered ancient liberty language with Galatians 5:1. — *plausible*: This explains the English match without attributing Pauline language to the narrated pre-Pauline speaker.
- A revelatory or editorial voice harmonized the discourse with Galatians. — *possible*: It predicts the wording, although this passage does not independently identify such an intervention.
- A nineteenth-century composer directly adapted Galatians 5:1. — *plausible*: The source was available and the substitutions suit Pahoran's political context.
- Shared religious vocabulary produced the overlap without a specific relationship to Galatians. — *possible*: It explains generic liberty and bondage language less well than the ordered verse-21 clause and the reinforcing phrase in verse 12.
- The complete correspondence arose accidentally. — *possible*: An unquantified search denominator prevents excluding coincidence, but the concentrated ordered wording makes it a comparatively poor explanation.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
