# Verbal Intertextuality in Alma 61

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The English wording of Alma 61 closely echoes Galatians 5:1: both combine standing fast, liberty, being made free, and the yoke of bondage. The concentration of shared language makes literary or translational dependence on Pauline/KJV diction substantially more plausible than accidental resemblance.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not by itself establish the date, direction, author, or mechanism of dependence, nor does it prove either ancient or modern authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A translator could deliberately render an ancient liberty motif in familiar Pauline and King James diction.
- The wording could be an intentional scriptural allusion by the English narrator or translator.
- A nineteenth-century author could imitate the King James Bible directly.
- Some overlap might arise from broadly shared Christian religious vocabulary, although the clustered verbal correspondences make this less sufficient by itself.
