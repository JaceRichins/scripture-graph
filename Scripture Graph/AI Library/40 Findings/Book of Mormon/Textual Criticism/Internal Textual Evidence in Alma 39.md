# Internal Textual Evidence in Alma 39

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 39 mounts an explicit internal defense of pre-Christian knowledge of Christ's atonement, but that defense is textual self-justification, not external corroboration; ancient revelation, translation-level Christianizing, and nineteenth-century composition all predict the same passage about equally well, placing this in the none band (0.12), consistent with the registry's bom-2nephi6-predictive-prophecy.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
In Alma 39:15-19, Alma tells Corianton that Christ will come to take away the sins of the world, describes proclaiming that coming as part of Corianton's charge, and asks rhetorically why the plan of redemption should not be known to the present generation, reasoning that a soul now is as precious to God as a soul will be at Christ's coming and that sending an angel to declare these things is no harder for God now than later.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage is an explicit in-text theological defense of detailed pre-advent Christian content: it does not merely assume such knowledge but argues for its plausibility from divine impartiality across generations and God's capacity to reveal truth by angelic ministry in any era.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This illuminates the internal theological logic the text uses to justify pre-Christian revelation about Christ; it is a doctrinal explanation offered by a character, not an externally verifiable historical claim.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read as a piece of internally consistent Nephite theology, the argument addresses a real theological question that any pre-advent religious community proclaiming a future messiah would face: how could that knowledge be considered valid in advance? Alma's answer — that God's capacity to reveal truth by angelic ministry is not bound to a single moment in history — fits a broader pattern in the text's theology (and later, Restoration theology, e.g. D&C 20:26) that revelation is not confined to one dispensation.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The text anticipates and answers a modern anachronism objection (why would ancient people already possess detailed Christian doctrine?) rather than being silent about it; that internal self-awareness is interesting but is not independent evidence resolving the objection.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish that Alma existed, that any angel appeared, that the discourse dates to the sixth century BCE, or that the doctrine taught is true.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->
