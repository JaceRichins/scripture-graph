# Textual Variants in 2 Nephi 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 7 departs from KJV Isaiah 50 at several points, firmly establishing a distinct English form. Because no ancient witness corroborates any of these specific variants, and the corpus's own established KJV-mediation pattern makes modern or translation-level adaptation the better-supported default, this falls in the none band (0.1) for the ancient-Vorlage proposition specifically — a real asymmetry with cases like Amos 5, which do have independent ancient attestation.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 7 adds direct address to 'O house of Israel' in vv.2 and 4 where the KJV lacks it, and adds 'and I will smite him with the strength of my mouth' in v.8, not present in KJV Isaiah 50:8. No ancient witness matching these specific readings is identified.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The differences are real and establish that 2 Nephi 7 is not a bare KJV transcription at these verses, but nothing here ties any specific variant to an attested ancient reading.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Demonstrates that the Book of Mormon's Isaiah text is not a mechanical KJV transcription at these verses; compatible with several very different explanations for why it differs.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Ancient Isaiah copies did diverge (as the Dead Sea Scrolls show), so a genuinely divergent Vorlage remains a live logical possibility for any given variant. But this note identifies no external match, so these particular differences are at least as well explained by ordinary translation, dictation, or editorial expansion as by a lost ancient text.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Without an identified ancient witness matching these specific variants, translator/dictation creativity, editorial clarification, and access to a genuinely divergent ancient text all predict this same pattern of KJV departure about equally well. Per the discrimination rule, that puts this in the none/weak boundary for the ancient-Vorlage proposition specifically, even though the fact of a distinct English form is itself firmly established.

**Inspiration:** Says nothing about inspiration; a distinct English wording is equally predicted whether the process was inspired translation of an ancient text or uninspired paraphrase.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish access to a non-Masoretic ancient manuscript, textual superiority over the KJV, a specific underlying Hebrew reading, or Book of Mormon historicity generally.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Translation of a genuinely divergent ancient Hebrew Vorlage** — predicts: Departures from KJV, ideally matching a known ancient variant (DSS, LXX, etc.). Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
- **Loose/interpretive translation or dictation-level paraphrase** — predicts: The same kind of expansive, audience-directed departures without requiring any distinct source text. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Editorial clarification for a Nephite/Book of Mormon audience** — predicts: Added vocatives and clarifying clauses of exactly this kind. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century adaptation of KJV language** — predicts: Same surface pattern of KJV departure without any ancient-text claim. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Translation from a genuinely different ancient source text — *possible*: Cannot be ruled out from the wording alone, but no independent ancient witness is cited to support it.
- Interpretive expansion during translation or dictation — *plausible*: A widely attested general feature of the Book of Mormon's translation process, requiring no additional unattested source.
- Editorial clarification within the narrative — *plausible*: The added vocatives fit the surrounding sermon's address to 'the house of Israel.'
- Modern adaptation of King James language — *plausible*: Consistent with, and not independent of, the corpus's own established broader KJV-mediation pattern (bom-isaiah-textual-dependence, 0.84), which makes this at least as expected as an ancient-Vorlage reading.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
