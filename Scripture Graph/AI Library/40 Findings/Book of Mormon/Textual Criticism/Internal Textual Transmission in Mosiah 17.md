# Internal Textual Transmission in Mosiah 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mosiah 17:4 supplies the narrative's transmission mechanism for Abinadi's teachings: Alma escaped, remained concealed, and wrote what he had heard. The wording portrays Alma's record as comprehensive while leaving room for remembered formulation rather than stenographic transcription. **Scripture:** [[Mosiah 17#^mosiah-17-4|Mosiah 17:4]]
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 17:4 states that Alma escaped, remained concealed for many days, and wrote "all the words which Abinadi had spoken."
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The narrative explains how Abinadi's teachings could survive his execution: a sympathetic hearer remembered and committed them to writing. "All the words" presents the record as comprehensive, but need not describe a verbatim transcript.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The verse portrays a recognizable form of textual transmission in which an eyewitness or participant writes remembered teaching after the event. It also makes Alma's record an intermediate source within the story rather than presenting the later account as an unexplained transcript.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the narrative's proposed reconstruction, Alma heard Abinadi directly, preserved the teachings in writing while concealed, and thereby created a source from which those teachings could enter the community's later record. The detail connects oral delivery, eyewitness memory, writing, and subsequent preservation in one intelligible transmission sequence.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
