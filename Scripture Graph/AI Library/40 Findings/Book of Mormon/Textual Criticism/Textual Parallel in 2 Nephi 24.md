# Textual Parallel in 2 Nephi 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The 32-verse ordered correspondence between 2 Nephi 24 and Isaiah 14 strongly supports dependence on or mediation through the Isaiah textual tradition. Minor wording differences do not materially weaken that relationship, while ancient quotation rendered in biblical English, translation-level harmonization, and direct nineteenth-century KJV adaptation remain viable routes.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 24 follows Isaiah 14 across all 32 verses in the same sequence and with extensive verbal agreement. The supplied samples also preserve minor differences, including 'did weaken' versus 'didst weaken' in verse 12 and the omission of 'one' plus plural 'nations' versus singular 'nation' in verse 32.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter is best read as a quotation, adaptation, or translation-mediated presentation of Isaiah 14 rather than an independently composed passage that merely resembles it; the correspondence does not identify the direction or mechanism.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compatible with several models: an ancient Nephite copy of Isaiah later rendered into KJV-influenced English during translation, revelatory harmonization to a known English Bible, or direct nineteenth-century use of an open KJV text.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
This chapter is one instance of the broader, already-registered Isaiah/KJV dependence pattern that recurs across roughly two dozen Isaiah chapters in the Book of Mormon; it fits that pattern precisely rather than constituting independent new evidence.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports a textual/literary relationship with the Isaiah/KJV tradition. This is the same evidence class already registered corpus-wide at 0.84 and this chapter does not independently discriminate a translation mechanism beyond that.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: a textual-dependence pattern says nothing about whether the underlying revelation or translation process was divinely inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish ancient Nephite authorship, a particular translation method, or the text's date. Should not be counted as independent weight beyond the general, already-registered Isaiah-dependence pattern.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient record, KJV-mediated translation** — predicts: Close correspondence with occasional archaic-English variants supplied by the translation process. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **19th-century composition from an open KJV** — predicts: Near-verbatim correspondence with minor copying variants. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Independent ancient-Hebrew-only rendering with no KJV contact** — predicts: Substantial independent wording differing from the KJV. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient Isaiah quotation was rendered in KJV-shaped English during translation. — *plausible*
- A translation-level, revelatory, or editorial process harmonized Isaiah-related material to the received English text. — *plausible*
- A nineteenth-century composer directly adapted Isaiah 14 from the KJV. — *plausible*
- The chapter-long resemblance arose without textual or traditional dependence. — *ad hoc*: Source independence does not credibly explain the 32-verse agreement in sequence and wording.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A comparably extensive verbatim match between two biblical books (e.g. 2 Kings/Isaiah or Kings/Chronicles parallels) would be described in the same value-neutral literary-dependence terms without inferring fraud or authenticity from the parallel alone; the same standard is applied here.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->
