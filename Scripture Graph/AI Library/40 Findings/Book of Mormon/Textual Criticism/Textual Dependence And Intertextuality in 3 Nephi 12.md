# Textual Dependence And Intertextuality in 3 Nephi 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sustained sequence and close English wording shared by 3 Nephi 12 and Matthew 5 are strong evidence for deliberate Matthew/KJV mediation in the final text. Matthew-specific wording such as 'poor in spirit' challenges unmediated independent repetition, while historical repetition followed by translation harmonization, revelatory restatement, and nineteenth-century adaptation remain viable mechanisms; the relationship does not independently determine historicity or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 12 follows Matthew 5 in sequence, imagery, and structure with sustained close-to-verbatim English wording across many verses (e.g. vv.3, 13, 17, 21, 27).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter stands in direct literary relationship with the Matthew 5/KJV tradition; that a relationship exists is not seriously disputed, while its historical mechanism is.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The correspondence is compatible with a substantially parallel historical sermon subsequently rendered or harmonized into Matthean English, with revelatory restatement, or with nineteenth-century adaptation. Under common Synoptic source-critical readings, Matthew's 'poor in spirit' differs from Luke's simpler 'poor' and is often treated as Matthean redaction; its appearance in 3 Nephi therefore creates tension for an unmediated claim that the received wording independently preserves Jesus's original delivery, while translation or harmonization directly accommodates it.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a historical-repetition reconstruction, Jesus gives related teachings to a new audience and the English production process renders shared material through Matthew's familiar wording, including Matthean formulations such as 'poor in spirit.' Revelatory harmonization predicts the same final form. On a nineteenth-century-composition reconstruction, Matthew 5 supplies the framework and language while new clauses adapt it to the Nephite setting. These reconstructions all support Matthew/KJV mediation; an unmediated independent repetition model fits less well.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strong support for the proposition that 3 Nephi 12's final English form textually depends on Matthew 5/KJV. Per the registered bom-3nephi-matthew-kjv-dependence issue this is capped at strong (0.84), not near-certain, because tight-control translation of a genuinely repeated discourse and direct modern use of Matthew both predict the same sustained verbal overlap.

**Inspiration:** The textual relationship says nothing by itself about whether the discourse or its rendering is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The English correspondence does not establish the date, original language, authorship, or historical delivery of the underlying discourse. It provides little independent discrimination between a repeated sermon rendered through Matthean English, revelatory harmonization, and nineteenth-century adaptation, and it does not determine inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Historical repeated sermon, KJV-mediated translation** — predicts: Substantial thematic and structural overlap with some verbatim phrasing from shared translation register.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Revelatory/loose translation drawing on memorized KJV Matthew** — predicts: High overlap with occasional divergence.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century composition adapting Matthew 5** — predicts: Sustained near-verbatim overlap across many consecutive verses, including English-specific idiom.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent parallel sermon tradition** — predicts: Thematic similarity without close English-level verbal matching.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jesus historically delivered a substantially parallel sermon that was later rendered into Matthean/KJV English. — *possible*: Teachers may repeat material, and translation-level mediation explains Matthew-specific wording, but the historical event is not independently attested.
- An ancient parallel discourse was translated or harmonized into familiar KJV language. — *plausible*: This explains both contextual adaptations and close English agreement, including wording commonly regarded as distinctive to Matthew's redaction.
- Revelatory restatement deliberately aligned the discourse with Matthew 5. — *plausible*: This directly predicts the shared sequence and Matthean wording without requiring verbatim repetition in the original historical setting.
- A nineteenth-century producer adapted Matthew 5 to the Nephite setting. — *plausible*: The KJV was available and the mechanism economically explains both the sequence and English wording, though availability and corpus-wide mediation do not independently establish this exclusive provenance.
- Jesus independently repeated Matthew-specific English-equivalent wording without later textual mediation. — *ad hoc*: It does not economically explain extended order and wording associated specifically with Matthew, including 'poor in spirit,' without adding an unsupported coincidence.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *The Sermon material in 3 Nephi depends on or is mediated through the Matthew/KJV textual tradition.*

The sustained sequence and close wording strongly support a direct literary relationship between 3 Nephi 12 and Matthew 5/KJV, without indicating whether that relationship arose through translation, revelation, or nineteenth-century composition.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: One instance of the broader, already-registered Matthew/KJV mediation pattern running through 3 Nephi 12-14; not independent evidence beyond bom-3nephi-matthew-kjv-dependence, and should not be added to it as a separate score.

What would move this: Assessment dated 2026-09-04. For provenance, independently attested ancient non-Matthean readings in the earliest Book of Mormon text would favor an underlying parallel discourse, while systematic agreement with demonstrably late KJV peculiarities would increase pressure on strict ancient-verbatim models.
