# Biblical English Intertextuality in Moroni 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Moroni 6:4's description of Christ as "the author and finisher of their faith" closely follows Hebrews 12:2 and strongly supports biblical or KJV-shaped mediation in the final English text. Loose translation, revelatory or editorial harmonization, indirect devotional transmission, and nineteenth-century reuse remain viable, so the wording does not select an authorship model and should not be counted independently from the broader KJV-mediation pattern.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Moroni 6:4, in full, describes the newly baptized as 'relying alone upon the merits of Christ, who was the author and finisher of their faith,' closely matching the distinctive KJV phrase 'the author and finisher of our faith' in Hebrews 12:2. The note's own cited excerpt truncates before this clause and should be corrected to include it, since as quoted it does not display the claimed parallel.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
This is not generic shared biblical vocabulary but a rare compound title essentially unique to Hebrews 12:2 in the KJV, strongly suggesting the Moroni 6:4 wording was directly shaped by that verse.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The phrase's rarity makes independent coincidental phrasing unlikely; some English-textual relationship is well supported, consistent with the corpus's already-registered broader KJV-mediation pattern.

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Within the corpus's broader, documented pattern of KJV New Testament phrasing throughout the Book of Mormon's English text, this match fits a reconstruction in which a doctrinal formula was rendered, by whatever mechanism, into the closest available King James idiom for the concept of Christ as faith's originator and completer.

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
This strongly supports that the Book of Mormon's final English text draws on Hebrews 12:2's specific wording, which some critics read as evidence against a strictly literalistic, word-for-word ancient-language translation (since Hebrews postdates any purported Nephite Vorlage by centuries). Defenders typically respond that translation rendered ancient concepts into the receptor language's available religious idiom, making this a translation phenomenon rather than evidence against an ancient underlying text.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: a shared distinctive English phrase is equally compatible with inspired translation into available religious idiom and with uninspired literary borrowing.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->
