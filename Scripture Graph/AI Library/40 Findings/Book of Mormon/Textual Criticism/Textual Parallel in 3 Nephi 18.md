# Textual Parallel in 3 Nephi 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sequence in [[3 Nephi 18#^3ne-18-3|3 Nephi 18:3]], [[3 Nephi 18#^3ne-18-7|3 Nephi 18:7]], [[3 Nephi 18#^3ne-18-8|3 Nephi 18:8]], and [[3 Nephi 18#^3ne-18-11|3 Nephi 18:11]] strongly connects the passage with the Lord's Supper traditions represented by [[Matthew 26#^matt-26-26|Matthew 26:26–28]], [[Luke 22#^luke-22-19|Luke 22:19–20]], and [[1 Corinthians 11#^1cor-11-23|1 Corinthians 11:23]]. Repetition of the same ordinance, stable liturgical tradition, biblical-English translation, and modern synthesis can all explain much of the cluster. The relationship is strong, but its direction and mechanism remain unresolved.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 18 presents Jesus taking bread, breaking and blessing it, giving it to disciples, commanding participants to eat and drink, and connecting the acts with remembrance of his body and blood. This sequence and vocabulary overlap the Lord's Supper accounts in Matthew 26 and Luke 22 and Paul's tradition in 1 Corinthians 11.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage is deliberately connected to the Christian Lord's Supper tradition, although the combination of shared ritual actions and wording does not by itself establish direct copying from any single New Testament account.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage places Nephite worship within the same ritual world as New Testament and later Christian eucharistic practice. Its distinctive emphasis on witnessing to the Father and retaining the Spirit represents adaptation or expansion rather than simple duplication.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the narrative's historical reconstruction, Jesus instituted the same commemorative ordinance in another community, naturally repeating its central actions while adapting its application. On a liturgical or translation reconstruction, an ancient rite was expressed through familiar New Testament language. On a modern-composition reconstruction, the passage combines the Synoptic and Pauline supper traditions with distinct Book of Mormon covenant language. All three explain the recognizable ordinance; the latter two more directly predict the close English vocabulary.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The clustered sequence and remembrance language strongly support participation in the New Testament Lord's Supper tradition and challenge complete source independence. Because ritual repetition, liturgical transmission, translation harmonization, and modern synthesis all predict much of the pattern, the evidence provides little discrimination among ancient and modern provenance models.

**Inspiration:** Participation in an inherited Christian ritual tradition says nothing by itself about whether the passage is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
It does not establish direct dependence on a particular Gospel, the direction of transmission, the antiquity of the English wording, the historical occurrence of the narrated ordinance, or whether the shared language entered through event, tradition, translation, revelation, or composition.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Historical repetition of the ordinance** — predicts: The same teacher and commemorated event should produce the same bread-and-wine actions and body-and-blood meanings, with contextual adaptations.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Ancient liturgical tradition with biblical-English translation** — predicts: Stable ritual actions and formulas should recur, while the English translator supplies wording familiar from the New Testament.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Translation-level or revelatory harmonization** — predicts: The passage should combine wording from several canonical supper accounts rather than reproduce only one source.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Nineteenth-century scriptural synthesis** — predicts: Accessible Gospel and Pauline formulas should be recombined with new doctrinal and narrative material.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent ritual narrative unrelated to Christian supper tradition** — predicts: Generic communal eating might occur, but not the full ordered cluster of Jesus, bread, wine, body, blood, and remembrance.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jesus instituted the same ordinance in both the Old World and the New World. — *possible*: A repeated ordinance naturally preserves core actions and meanings, though translation is still needed to explain exact English affinities.
- Shared early Christian liturgy preserved stable actions and remembrance formulas. — *independently supported*: The New Testament itself preserves multiple related versions of the tradition, showing that common ritual language circulated, although this does not independently document a Nephite form.
- The translator or revelatory process rendered corresponding material with biblical phrases drawn from several New Testament accounts. — *plausible*: The distributed rather than single-source resemblance fits harmonization or biblical-English translation.
- A nineteenth-century author synthesized familiar New Testament supper accounts. — *plausible*: The sources were accessible and their combination economically explains the English sequence.
- The entire cluster is an accidental consequence of generic meal language. — *ad hoc*: Generic meals do not predict the combined body, blood, remembrance, and ordered ritual actions.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.68) for the proposition: *The final form of 3 Nephi 18 deliberately participates in or is mediated through the New Testament Lord's Supper textual and liturgical tradition.*

The ordered ritual actions and body, blood, and remembrance language strongly support a relationship with the New Testament Lord's Supper tradition, while their formulaic and repeatable character prevents identifying a particular source or historical mechanism.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Taking, blessing, breaking, distributing, eating, and drinking are conventional elements of meal and eucharistic narratives, and remembrance formulas became stable Christian liturgical language. Their clustering around Jesus's body and blood is nevertheless specific to the Lord's Supper tradition. No matched liturgical corpus, phrase-frequency study, or reported denominator establishes how rare the complete cluster is, and the several New Testament comparison candidates impose a look-elsewhere cost.

What would move this: Assessment dated 2026-09-04. The weight would rise if a complete alignment identified rare, same-order wording or KJV-specific variants traceable to one source beyond ordinary eucharistic formulae; it would fall if matched early Christian liturgies and independently composed communion texts routinely produced an equally specific cluster without literary dependence.
