# Internal Textual Evidence in 1 Nephi 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
First Nephi 11:22 and 25 interpret the tree, waters, and iron rod through the love and word of God, while verses 35–36 associate the great building with the world, its wisdom, and its pride. These explicit glosses organize the vision into a moral and theological contrast between movement toward divine love and identification with a proud world destined to fall.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
First Nephi 11 associates the tree and living waters with the love of God, identifies the iron rod as the word of God, and associates the great and spacious building with the world and its wisdom in verse 35 and with the pride of the world in verse 36.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
These statements function as authorized intratextual glosses, guiding readers from the vision's images to their principal theological and moral functions while preserving the related wording used for the building.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The chapter illustrates an interpretive mode in which symbolic vision is explained within the narrative. Movement toward or away from the tree is thereby expressed through responses to divine love, divine teaching, worldly wisdom, and pride.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the chapter's interpretive reconstruction, the tree and waters form a complex of divine love and life, the rod supplies guiding divine speech, and the building represents the proud world opposed to them. The symbols create a spatial moral map in which divine guidance leads toward love while worldly wisdom and pride culminate in collapse.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
