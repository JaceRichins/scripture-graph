# Textual Intertextuality in Moroni 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close verbal correspondence with Mark 2:17 and 1 John 4:18 is strong evidence that the English text of Moroni 8 participates in recognizable New Testament intertextuality. It does not, by itself, identify whether that relationship arose through ancient transmission, revealed restatement, translation vocabulary, or modern composition.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not independently establish the date, authorship, translation mechanism, or historical provenance of Moroni 8.

**Alternative explanations:**
- An ancient writer or source could have preserved sayings also found in the New Testament.
- A revealed message could restate sayings of Christ known from the New Testament.
- A translator could render distinct source-language expressions in familiar biblical language.
- A modern author could deliberately reuse King James Bible phrasing.
