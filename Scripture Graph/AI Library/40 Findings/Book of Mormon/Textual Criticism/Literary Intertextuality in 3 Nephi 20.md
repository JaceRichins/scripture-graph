# Literary Intertextuality in 3 Nephi 20

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 20 explicitly names Isaiah and then weaves Isaiah 52 wording into a sustained covenant-fulfillment discourse, which strongly supports reading the passage as deliberate, KJV-mediated interpretive integration rather than incidental echo. This is a dependent instance of the corpus's broader Isaiah/KJV-dependence finding and should not be added to it as separate evidence, and neither reading decides ancient versus modern origin or bears on the prophecies' actual fulfillment.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
After explicitly instructing its audience to search Isaiah, 3 Nephi 20 connects Isaiah's fulfillment with the Father's covenant and incorporates an extended sequence from Isaiah 52 into its discussion of gathering, Jerusalem, redemption, proclamation, and divine self-disclosure.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter openly interprets and reapplies Isaiah 52 rather than presenting an isolated verbal echo. Its introductory instruction, covenant frame, sustained quotation, and surrounding exposition make that scriptural reuse part of the chapter's overt literary activity.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage exemplifies interpretation through reuse: an inherited prophetic text is quoted within a new covenantal and eschatological discourse and applied to a later community's expectations concerning Israel, Zion, and redemption.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a final-form literary reconstruction, 3 Nephi 20 follows a practice familiar from later Jewish and Christian scriptural interpretation: Isaiah 52:1-3, 6-7, and 9-11 supplies the language of awakening, release, proclamation, purification, and divine salvation through which the chapter explains Israel's anticipated restoration.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
