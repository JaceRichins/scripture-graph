# Doctrinal Textual Inference in Alma 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 13:3-5 permits, but does not favor, a premortal-foreordination reading: phrases like 'from the foundation of the world,' 'foreknowledge of God,' and 'in the first place' fit that interpretation, while a mortal-response reading (eternal preparation of the calling, mortal exercise of faith and choice) fits the same words about equally well. Because the leading rival readings predict this wording with about equal ease, the passage gives only none-band support for the premortal-occurrence proposition specifically — it is a genuinely open, textually live question, not evidence that favors either side.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 13:3-5 states that certain men were 'called and prepared from the foundation of the world according to the foreknowledge of God, on account of their exceeding faith and good works,' that 'in the first place' they were 'left to choose good or evil,' and that having 'chosen good' they were called to a 'holy calling.' Verse 5 repeats that 'in the first place they were on the same standing with their brethren' and that the calling was 'prepared from the foundation of the world.'
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage can be read as locating the qualifying faith, works, and moral choice in a premortal state (a foreordination reading), or as describing an eternally prepared calling that is activated by faith and choices made in mortal life, with 'in the first place' functioning rhetorically rather than temporally.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The verses are compatible with both a premortal-existence cosmology and a purely mortal-merit theology; the syntax alone does not fix which the author intended.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Second Temple and rabbinic Jewish literature contains real precedent for reading divine foreknowledge and pre-appointment language as compatible with either a strong predestinarian sense or a softer 'foreknown but freely chosen' sense (compare later Christian debates over Romans 8-9 and Ephesians 1). On the reconstruction where Alma 13 draws on that same interpretive ambiguity, 'from the foundation of the world' modifies the timeless preparation of the priesthood order and the atonement (v.7 makes this explicit: the order 'was from the foundation of the world... without beginning of days or end of years'), while the faith, works, and choice described in vv.3-5 belong to the ordinary mortal lives of the men being described, mirrored later by their non-elect 'brethren' who had 'as great privilege.' Read this way, the passage teaches meritocratic calling within mortality using eternity language for the office, not the person's history. An equally available reconstruction treats 'in the first place' as marking a real premortal moment of choice, paralleling later LDS canon (Abraham 3, D&C 138) that was not yet articulated when Alma 13 was written down. Both reconstructions are available from the verses alone.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
For readers who already hold a premortal-existence doctrine (as later articulated in LDS scripture, e.g. Abraham 3), this passage offers a textual anchor that is compatible with that reading. But because the mortal-response reading (eternal preparation of the calling; mortal exercise of faith and choice) accounts for the same phrases about equally well, the passage gives only none-band discrimination toward the premortal-occurrence proposition specifically. It shows the premortal reading is textually permissible, not that it is favored.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
It does not establish that the faith, good works, or rejection described in these verses necessarily occurred before mortal birth, that premortal foreordination is doctrinally required by this passage, or that foreknowledge removes meaningful agency.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Premortal-choice reading** — predicts: Explicit temporal markers ('in the first place,' 'from the foundation of the world') locate faith, works, and the good/evil choice before birth.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Mortal-response reading** — predicts: The eternal/timeless language attaches only to the calling's preparation and the atonement, while faith and choice remain ordinary mortal terms elsewhere in Alma's usage.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Blended/ambiguous reading** — predicts: No sharp grammatical marker separates the two registers, so the passage supports both readings without deciding between them.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- The calling was prepared eternally while the qualifying faith and works occurred in mortality. — *plausible*: This is the more common reading among LDS commentators and requires no premortal cosmology beyond what is stated.
- The passage moves between divine preparation and mortal response without marking the temporal transitions explicitly. — *plausible*: Consistent with the text's own repeated but unresolved 'in the first place' phrasing.
- The language emphasizes foreknowledge rather than prior ordination of particular individuals. — *possible*: Foreknowledge and foreordination are conceptually distinct, and the text foregrounds 'foreknowledge of God' explicitly.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
