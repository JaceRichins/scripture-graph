# English-Language Intertextuality in Moroni 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Moroni 7:45-46 closely tracks 1 Corinthians 13:4-8 in wording and order across roughly eight to nine clauses, strongly supporting a direct literary or translation-level relationship between the two texts at a weight comparable to the corpus's other tight multi-verse KJV/NT dependence issues. The evidence does not determine whether this arose through King James-mediated translation of an ancient discourse, revelatory harmonization, or nineteenth-century reuse, and says nothing about whether the underlying teaching is inspired.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Moroni 7:45-46 reproduces, in the same order, eight to nine distinct clauses paralleling 1 Corinthians 13:4-8: 'suffereth long,' 'is kind,' 'envieth not,' 'is not puffed up,' 'seeketh not her own,' 'is not easily provoked,' 'thinketh no evil,' 'rejoiceth not in iniquity but rejoiceth in the truth,' and the 'beareth/believeth/hopeth/endureth all things ... never faileth' close.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The density, sequence, and near-verbatim wording make coincidence implausible; this is a genuine literary or translation-level relationship to the English text of 1 Corinthians 13, not an independently composed devotional passage that merely resembles Paul.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compatible with an authentic ancient discourse rendered through Joseph Smith's memorized King James idiom, an ancient shared wisdom/virtue-list tradition loosely paralleling Paul, revelatory harmonization during translation, or direct nineteenth-century reuse of 1 Corinthians 13.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a translation model, an ancient discourse on charity/love could plausibly be rendered by a translator steeped in the King James Bible, producing exactly this kind of close verbal echo without the underlying Nephite text being a quotation of Paul. On a modern-composition model, the same closeness follows even more directly. Both reconstructions are live; the note does not adjudicate between them.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports a direct textual/literary relationship at the level of the English text; does not indicate which translation or composition model produced it, and is part of the corpus's broader, already-registered New Testament/KJV-mediation pattern rather than independent additional weight for any one model.

**Inspiration:** Nothing about the parallel's mechanism bears on whether the underlying teaching is inspired; a KJV-mediated or modern-composed passage can still convey a spiritually meaningful teaching.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish whether the underlying Nephite record (if any) actually contained a comparable discourse, whether the resemblance originated in ancient composition, in translation, or in 1829-30 dictation, or the antiquity of the discourse.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
**What the text itself requires:** The text only requires that Mormon/Moroni deliver a discourse on charity; it does not itself claim dependence on Paul, so the intertextual claim is an inference from the English wording, not something the text asserts about itself.

- **Tight-control ancient-source translation** (*plausible*) — predicts: An underlying discourse rendered into King James cadence during translation, producing close but not necessarily word-for-word correspondence. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: Consistent with the corpus's broader pattern of KJV-shaped translation of putatively ancient material
- **Loose/dynamic translation with revelatory harmonization** (*plausible*) — predicts: Translator's own biblical vocabulary bleeding into rendering of a looser underlying idea. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: Matches how translators of ancient wisdom material commonly default to familiar target-language idiom
- **Nineteenth-century composition** (*plausible*) — predicts: Exactly this kind of close, memorized-KJV-quality reuse. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: Joseph Smith's demonstrated deep King James familiarity elsewhere in the corpus
- **Independent ANE/Near Eastern virtue-list tradition** (*ad hoc*) — predicts: Looser, less sequential resemblance than observed. Fit with this finding: *some difficulty*. Support for the model: No independently attested ancient virtue list matches this specific clause order
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Ancient discourse translated through King James idiom — *plausible*: Fits the corpus-wide KJV-mediation pattern (e.g., bom-3nephi-matthew-kjv-dependence) but has no independent attestation
- Direct nineteenth-century reuse of 1 Corinthians 13 — *plausible*: Simplest explanation given period King James familiarity; not independently distinguishable from translation-mediated reuse on textual grounds alone
- Independent virtue list not derived from Paul — *ad hoc*: Does not explain the specific clause order and near-identical phrasing
- Faith-framework parallel/independent revelation of the same teaching — *possible*: Coherent within a devotional framework but has no independent evidentiary support
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A biblical author reproducing another biblical passage's wording this closely (e.g., Chronicles reusing Kings) is labeled literary dependence without controversy; the same evidentiary caution, not a presumption of either forgery or special pleading, is applied here.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->

The extensive, ordered verbal correspondence between Moroni 7:45-46 and 1 Corinthians 13:4-8 strongly supports a direct literary or translation-level relationship between the two texts, without establishing which historical mechanism produced it.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Dense, ordered, multi-clause verbal correspondence of this kind is uncommon outside deliberate quotation or echo. What would move this: The weight would rise toward the top of the strong band with mechanical confirmation that this ordered phrase cluster is rare in a matched pre-1830 English religious-prose corpus and concentrated specifically around 1 Corinthians 13; it would fall if comparable ordered charity catalogues were shown to recur commonly without dependence on that passage.
