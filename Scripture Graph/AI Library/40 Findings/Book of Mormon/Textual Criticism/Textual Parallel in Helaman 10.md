# Textual Parallel in Helaman 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Helaman 10:7 adapts the ordered earth-and-heaven authority formula of Matthew 18:18, substituting 'seal' for 'bind' while closely retaining the loosing clause. The clustered wording and preserved sequence strongly support deliberate Matthew-related or KJV-shaped mediation in Helaman's final English form. Loose translation, revelatory restatement, and nineteenth-century adaptation remain equally viable mechanisms, so the parallel does not determine date, direction, historicity, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Helaman 10:7 and Matthew 18:18 share the ordered formula 'whatsoever ye shall [seal/bind] on earth shall be [sealed/bound] in heaven' followed by 'whatsoever ye shall loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven.' The first operative verb differs ('seal' vs. 'bind'), but the earth-heaven ratification structure, the near-verbatim second (loosing) clause, and the sequence closely correspond. A comparable earth-heaven binding-and-loosing correlative appears in the Bible at Matthew 16:19 and 18:18, which are themselves near-duplicates of each other.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The shared two-clause syntax, earth-heaven correspondence, 'whatsoever ye shall' construction, loosing terminology, and preserved order make deliberate Matthew-related mediation in Helaman 10:7's final English more likely than accidental resemblance, while the substituted verb ('seal' for 'bind') shows the wording was adapted rather than quoted verbatim.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The parallel places Helaman 10:7 within a Christian scriptural tradition in which delegated earthly authority receives heavenly ratification. It is compatible with direct or indirect KJV use, translation-level harmonization, revelatory reuse, or related authority traditions expressed in common biblical English.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Matthew 18:18 presents earthly binding and loosing as ratified in heaven; Helaman 10:7 adapts that authority pattern to Nephi's commission by substituting 'seal' for 'bind' while retaining the corresponding loosing clause. On a loose-translation reconstruction, familiar KJV language supplies the English form for an ancient commission. On a nineteenth-century-composition reconstruction, Matthew's formula is adapted directly or through received Christian diction. On a revelatory-restatement reconstruction, the shared wording intentionally locates Nephi's commission within the same scriptural theology of delegated authority. The textual data fit all three equally well.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The correspondence strongly supports Matthew-related intertextuality or KJV-shaped mediation in Helaman 10:7's final English form. It gives little discrimination between loose translation of an authentic ancient concept, revelatory restatement, and nineteenth-century composition, and none of those mechanisms is independently favored by the wording alone.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: a wording match documents how the English text was shaped, not whether the priesthood authority it describes is real or whether the passage is divinely inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The parallel does not identify the direction or stage of mediation, establish direct copying of Matthew rather than indirect scriptural influence, date the underlying discourse, demonstrate ancient or modern authorship, or show that Nephi's sealing authority and Matthew's binding-and-loosing authority denote identical offices.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- A nineteenth-century author directly or indirectly adapted Matthew 18:18. — *plausible*: The KJV was available before publication, and the Book of Mormon's broader KJV-shaped language independently establishes biblical mediation generally, though direct use of this particular verse is not separately documented.
- An ancient commission was rendered or harmonized into familiar Matthew-derived English during translation. — *plausible*: This accounts for both the substituted verb and the close English syntax, though the specific translation mechanism is not independently observable.
- Both passages express related divine commissions through conventional Christian authority diction without direct dependence of Helaman 10 on Matthew 18:18. — *plausible*: Binding, loosing, sealing, and heavenly ratification are related concepts, but generic diction alone explains the preserved two-clause wording and order less completely than textual mediation.
- Revelation intentionally restated Matthew's formula for Nephi's distinct commission. — *possible*: Compatible with a believing model and the text, but the claimed revelatory mechanism is not independently established by the verbal parallel alone.
- The wording resemblance arose by chance. — *ad hoc*: The ordered earth-heaven clauses and near-verbatim loosing formula, in a construction otherwise rare in the Bible, make pure coincidence an explanation that requires an unexplained mechanism to sustain.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
