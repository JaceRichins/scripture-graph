# Intra-Textual Verbal Parallel in 1 Nephi 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 32's description of the fruit of the word reuses the unusual sweetness/whiteness/preciousness cluster from Lehi's tree-of-life vision in 1 Nephi 8, creating a real literary echo across two narratively distant speakers. This gives only weak support for deliberate coordinated design, since ancient allusion to a preserved record, editorial harmonization, and single-author composition all predict the same overlap about equally well; it does not establish direction of dependence, historical setting, or authorship.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma's discourse on the word as a seed culminates in fruit described as exceedingly sweet, white/pure above all, and precious/desirable beyond measure (Alma 32:40-42), echoing the same superlative register Lehi uses for the tree's fruit in his vision — desirable to make one happy, white, and beautiful/precious beyond all others (1 Nephi 8:10-12).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The two passages, narratively separated by roughly 500 years and attributed to different speakers, describe the fruit of the tree of life using the same unusual cluster of superlative qualities, creating a recognizable thematic and theological echo within the book's final form.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
On the narrative's own terms, Alma had access to a preserved record of Lehi's vision and could draw on it; the echo reads naturally as a homiletic callback within one continuous literary and doctrinal tradition running through the book.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the reconstruction in which Nephite record-keepers preserved Lehi's vision as a fixed text available to later teachers, Alma's fruit imagery in Alma 32 fits as a deliberate return to a shared scriptural touchstone — the same move a later biblical preacher makes when echoing a founding narrative's imagery to teach a new audience. Read together, the two passages let the tree of life's fruit and the developed fruit of Alma's seed-of-faith parable interpret and reinforce each other as a single sustained symbol running through the book.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
