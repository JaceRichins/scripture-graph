# Verbatim Textual Parallel in 3 Nephi 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Across 3 Nephi 10:4–6, the gathering language, hen-and-chickens image, wings, Jerusalem and Israel setting, refusal motif, and threefold temporal restructuring closely engage the Jerusalem lament represented in Matthew 23:37 and paralleled in Luke 13:34. This strongly supports deliberate final-form intertextuality while leaving historical repetition, KJV-shaped translation, revelatory harmonization, and nineteenth-century adaptation unresolved; it adds no independent weight beyond the registered issue.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Across 3 Nephi 10:4–6, the speaker repeatedly combines gathering language, a hen gathering her chickens under her wings, Israel or Jerusalem, and refusal, then restructures the lament into past, counterfactual-present, and future appeals. Matthew 23:37 supplies the closest cited English parallel, while Luke 13:34 preserves the same Synoptic Jerusalem-and-hen lament.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The concentrated wording, imagery, setting, refusal motif, and temporal restructuring strongly support deliberate final-form engagement with the Synoptic lament represented most closely by Matthew 23:37. The Book of Mormon passage is an expanded adaptation rather than merely a generic maternal-protection image.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Historical repetition, KJV-shaped translation of an ancient saying, revelatory or editorial harmonization, and nineteenth-century adaptation can each explain the deliberate engagement. Historical repetition can explain reuse of the image but requires translation or stable wording to explain the close English correspondence.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Under a Matthew/KJV-mediation model, 3 Nephi 10:5 sits alongside the other registered Sermon-on-the-Mount and Gospel echoes in 3 Nephi as one more instance of the same phenomenon: whatever underlying event or statement is being rendered, it surfaces in the specific, familiar English of Matthew 23:37 rather than in independently generated wording, and that is the pattern this note documents rather than a new, separate relationship.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The correspondence strongly supports deliberate intertextual engagement in the final text but does not distinguish its historical or compositional mechanism. It is the same evidence already registered under this issue and must not be counted again when assessing broader Matthew/KJV mediation.

**Inspiration:** The relationship implies nothing by itself about inspiration, because historical repetition, inspired translation or harmonization, and ordinary literary adaptation can all produce deliberate engagement.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The parallel does not establish a second historical delivery, direct copying during composition, a particular translation or redaction process, ancient or modern authorship, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jesus historically reused the lament in a different post-resurrection setting. — *possible*: Speakers can repeat memorable teachings, but the text supplies no independent witness to the second delivery and repetition alone does not explain the received English wording.
- An ancient related saying was translated through familiar KJV wording. — *plausible*: A loose or harmonizing translation model predicts recognizable canonical language while allowing the threefold expansion.
- Revelatory or editorial harmonization intentionally connected the scenes. — *possible*: This explains the close diction and restructuring, although the verbal evidence cannot independently identify the process.
- A nineteenth-century author adapted Matthew 23:37 or its Lukan parallel directly or from memory. — *plausible*: The KJV passages were available, and 3 Nephi preserves their distinctive cluster while extending it rhetorically.
- The complete cluster arose independently from a common maternal-bird metaphor. — *ad hoc*: The generic metaphor does not economically explain the combined hen, chickens, wings, desired gathering, Jerusalem or Israel, refusal, and ordered temporal adaptation.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
