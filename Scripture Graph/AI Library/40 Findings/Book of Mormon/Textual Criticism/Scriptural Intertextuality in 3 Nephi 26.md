# Scriptural Intertextuality in 3 Nephi 26

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[3 Nephi 26#^3ne-26-4|3 Nephi 26:4]] and [[3 Nephi 26#^3ne-26-5|3 Nephi 26:5]] pair good and evil works with resurrection to everlasting life or damnation in the same order as [[John 5#^john-5-28|John 5:28]] and [[John 5#^john-5-29|John 5:29]]. The distinctive balanced correspondence strongly supports an intertextual or shared-tradition relationship, with 3 Nephi adapting and expanding the formulation. Historical repetition, common tradition, translation-level harmonization, revelatory biblical idiom, and direct modern adaptation remain viable mechanisms.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
John 5:29 pairs those who have done good with "the resurrection of life" and those who have done evil with "the resurrection of damnation." Third Nephi 26:4–5 preserves the same antithetical order and nearly the same formulations, changing the first result to "the resurrection of everlasting life" and expanding the surrounding judgment theology.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The paired conduct categories, matched resurrection outcomes, preserved order, and distinctive "resurrection of damnation" expression strongly support an English-text or shared-tradition relationship. The variation and expansion indicate adaptation rather than uninterrupted verbatim quotation.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The relationship is compatible with Jesus repeating a stable teaching in two settings, a shared Christian tradition rendered similarly, translation-level Johannine harmonization, modern revelation, and direct modern adaptation. The English parallel alone cannot locate the relationship at the event, tradition, composition, or translation stage.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
John 5 and 3 Nephi 26 organize final judgment through the same balanced contrast: good conduct leads to resurrection of life, while evil conduct leads to resurrection of damnation. On a historical-repetition or shared-tradition reconstruction, both passages preserve substantially the same teaching of Jesus. On translation-harmonization, revelatory-expression, or modern-composition reconstructions, John 5 supplies the received English formulation that 3 Nephi adapts and expands.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The evidence strongly supports Johannine or closely related Christian mediation in the final text. It provides little discrimination between ancient material expressed through biblical English and nineteenth-century composition, and therefore should not be converted into independent support for either antiquity or modern origin.

**Inspiration:** This verbal and conceptual relationship implies nothing by itself about whether 3 Nephi's teaching is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The parallel does not establish direct copying from John, the historical priority of either reported discourse, ancient Book of Mormon authorship, or the historicity of Jesus's speech in 3 Nephi.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Historical repetition by Jesus** — predicts: The same teacher may deliver substantially the same antithetical resurrection teaching in more than one setting.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Shared early Christian tradition** — predicts: Two accounts may preserve a common saying tradition without direct literary copying between their final texts.. Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
- **Translation-level Johannine harmonization** — predicts: Related underlying teaching will be rendered through familiar wording from John 5.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Modern revelation in biblical idiom** — predicts: Revealed teaching may employ and expand the received language of John 5.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century adaptation** — predicts: A modern writer could reproduce John's balanced formula and add further theological explanation.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent coincidence** — predicts: General resurrection and judgment language without the same paired categories, outcomes, and order.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jesus historically repeated substantially the same resurrection teaching in two settings. — *possible*: Teachers commonly repeat core teachings, but the extant texts do not independently verify the second setting.
- Both passages reflect a shared early Christian saying tradition. — *possible*: The balanced teaching could circulate independently of John's final text, though no separate source for this exact formulation is identified here.
- A translator or revelatory process rendered related material through John 5's familiar KJV wording. — *possible*: This predicts close English agreement without requiring direct dependence in an ancient source language.
- A nineteenth-century author directly adapted John 5:29. — *plausible*: The KJV wording was available, and the preserved antithetical order is straightforward to adapt.
- The parallel is an accidental result of common resurrection vocabulary. — *ad hoc*: Generic vocabulary does not readily explain the matched good/evil categories, paired resurrection outcomes, distinctive damnation phrase, and preserved order.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->

The matched good-and-evil contrast, paired resurrection outcomes, distinctive damnation wording, and preserved order strongly support a relationship between 3 Nephi 26:4–5 and John 5:28–29, without resolving repetition, shared tradition, translation, or modern adaptation.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Resurrection and final judgment are common Christian subjects, and good-versus-evil antitheses are widespread. The complete ordered pairing with "resurrection of life" and the comparatively distinctive "resurrection of damnation" is less generic, but no matched-corpus frequency or number of searched candidate passages was reported; the evidence supports a strong rather than near-certain relationship.

What would move this: Assessment dated 2026-09-04. The weight would rise if matched-corpus testing found the full ordered formula highly distinctive to John 5 and texts demonstrably using it; it would fall if the same pairing and terminology proved common in independent Christian resurrection discourse or if earlier textual witnesses lacked the diagnostic wording.
