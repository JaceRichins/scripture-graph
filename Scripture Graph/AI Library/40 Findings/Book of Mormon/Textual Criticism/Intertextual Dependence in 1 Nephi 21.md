# Intertextual Dependence in 1 Nephi 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The full sequence and close wording of [[1 Nephi 21#^1ne-21-1|1 Nephi 21:1-26]] strongly support deliberate dependence on or descent from [[Isaiah 49#^isa-49-1|Isaiah 49:1-26]]. The correspondence securely identifies an Isaiah/KJV textual relationship, while ancient quotation, translation-level harmonization, revelatory adaptation, and nineteenth-century reuse remain competing explanations of how that relationship arose.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
First Nephi 21 follows Isaiah 49 across the chapter's full twenty-six-verse sequence with extensive shared content, order, imagery, and close English wording, while also containing some differences and an expanded opening.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter is a conscious quotation, adaptation, or textual descendant of Isaiah 49 rather than an independently composed passage that happens to resemble it.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The dependence places 1 Nephi 21 within the Isaiah textual and interpretive tradition. The close English form particularly requires an account of KJV mediation, whether at the level of translation, revelatory harmonization, editing, or modern composition.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
First Nephi presents this chapter as another reading from Isaiah, and its final English form follows Isaiah 49 continuously rather than merely borrowing isolated phrases. On an ancient-record reconstruction, Nephi quotes Isaiah and the translation uses or harmonizes with familiar KJV language. On a modern-composition reconstruction, the KJV chapter supplies the direct literary base. The shared result across these reconstructions is secure Isaiah textual dependence; their disagreement concerns the stage and mechanism of mediation.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The sustained correspondence strongly supports Isaiah/KJV textual mediation, reusing the canonical 0.84 assessment for this issue. It does not independently favor ancient quotation over loose translation, revelatory reuse, or nineteenth-century adaptation.

**Inspiration:** Textual dependence implies nothing by itself about inspiration, since inspired quotation or translation and ordinary literary reuse can produce the same correspondence.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The correspondence does not determine when the dependence arose, whether an underlying plate text contained Isaiah 49, who supplied the English wording, or which broader model of Book of Mormon origins is correct.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient Nephite quotation with tight translation** — predicts: An ancient writer may quote Isaiah 49 in full, though the close KJV English requires a translation account connecting the result to the received biblical text.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Ancient quotation with KJV harmonization** — predicts: The final text should preserve Isaiah's sequence while using familiar KJV wording and allowing expansions or adjustments.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Revelatory adaptation** — predicts: Received Isaiah wording may be reproduced and expanded for a new audience.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Nineteenth-century KJV-based composition** — predicts: A full Isaiah chapter can be copied or adapted with close wording and limited alterations.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent accidental composition** — predicts: Only scattered generic similarities, not twenty-six verses of corresponding order and wording.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient Nephite author consciously quoted Isaiah 49 from a scriptural record. — *plausible*: This is the text's own narrative framing and explains the chapter-scale sequence, though the existence and wording of the proposed ancient record lack independent attestation.
- An ancient quotation was rendered or harmonized into familiar KJV Isaiah during translation. — *plausible*: This explains both an underlying ancient quotation and the close English wording without requiring that the English reproduce the ancient source mechanically.
- The chapter was adapted directly from the KJV during nineteenth-century composition. — *plausible*: The KJV was available and directly predicts the sustained order and close wording.
- The resemblance is accidental or derives only from generic prophetic vocabulary. — *ad hoc*: Generic vocabulary cannot plausibly account for the complete ordered chapter correspondence.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
