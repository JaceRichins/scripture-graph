# Textual Intertextuality in Mosiah 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[Mosiah 12#^mosiah-12-21|Mosiah 12:21]], [[Mosiah 12#^mosiah-12-22|12:22]], [[Mosiah 12#^mosiah-12-23|12:23]], and [[Mosiah 12#^mosiah-12-24|12:24]] reproduce [[Isaiah 52#^isa-52-7|Isaiah 52:7]], [[Isaiah 52#^isa-52-8|52:8]], [[Isaiah 52#^isa-52-9|52:9]], and [[Isaiah 52#^isa-52-10|52:10]] in the same sequence and nearly the same wording. This strongly supports Isaiah/KJV textual mediation and makes peace, Zion's restoration, and God's salvation central to the priests' dispute with Abinadi, while leaving direct modern reuse and an ancient citation rendered through KJV language as viable mechanisms.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 12:21–24 reproduces the complete sequence of Isaiah 52:7–10—herald, watchmen, Jerusalem's joyful waste places, the Lord's revealed arm, and worldwide salvation—with nearly the same KJV wording and order
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
A deliberate textual relationship is effectively certain. The strongest critical reading is direct modern use of the KJV; an ancient quotation translated or harmonized into KJV language also explains the final English correspondence, while coincidence does not.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage securely establishes Isaiah's role in Mosiah 12's final literary dispute. Its close KJV form belongs to the English text's transmission history and cannot alone show whether an ancient Nephite source quoted Isaiah or whether the quotation entered at translation or composition.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the critical reconstruction, the KJV passage is inserted directly as the scriptural text around which Abinadi and the priests dispute prophetic meaning. On an ancient-source reconstruction, the priests cite an Isaiah unit available in their record tradition and the translator renders it using the received KJV wording. In either reconstruction, the quotation makes the meaning of peace, Zion's restoration, and God's salvation—not recognition of the quotation itself—the issue that drives the ensuing exchange.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The four-verse correspondence strongly supports Isaiah/KJV textual mediation but supplies little independent discrimination between an ancient citation rendered in KJV language and direct nineteenth-century reuse. The score is reduced from 0.97 to the canonical `bom-isaiah-textual-dependence` weight of 0.84 because the relationship lacks independent external attestation and should not outrank comparable chapter-length Isaiah and Malachi correspondences.

**Inspiration:** The quotation's presence implies nothing by itself about inspiration, because both inspired translation and ordinary literary composition can reproduce earlier scripture.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The correspondence does not establish an ancient Nephite manuscript, the wording of any source-language quotation, the method by which the KJV form entered the text, ancient authorship, modern fabrication, historicity, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
**What the text itself requires:** Mosiah 12 presents the priests reciting the four-verse unit while asking Abinadi for its meaning. It requires deliberate quotation within the final narrative but does not specify the ancient manuscript's exact wording, the translator's procedure, or whether the English was obtained by consulting, recalling, or revelatorily reproducing the KJV.

- **Nineteenth-century composition using the KJV** (*plausible*) — predicts: An author constructing a scriptural controversy could copy an intact KJV unit and build the ensuing interpretation around it.. Fit with this finding: *supports*. Support for the model: The KJV was historically available, and the near-verbatim four-verse sequence is exactly what direct reuse predicts.
- **Ancient Isaiah citation with KJV-shaped translation** (*independently supported*) — predicts: An ancient source could quote Isaiah while an English translator renders the entire block through familiar KJV wording.. Fit with this finding: *supports*. Support for the model: [Royal Skousen's survey of Book of Mormon biblical quotations](https://byustudies.byu.edu/article/the-history-of-the-book-of-mormon-text-parts-5-and-6-of-volume-3-of-the-critical-text/) reports that the vast majority of their phraseology is closest to the KJV, independently motivating KJV-shaped mediation.
- **Revelatory harmonization** (*possible*) — predicts: A revealed English text could supply the familiar canonical wording for an underlying Isaiah quotation.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: This fits some faithful translation frameworks but is not independently testable from the quotation itself.
- **Strict verbal equivalence to an ancient-language source** (*possible*) — predicts: The English wording should closely track the source-language form without systematic dependence on a seventeenth-century English translation.. Fit with this finding: *some difficulty*. Support for the model: The text does not require strict equivalence, and the nearly exact KJV surface form requires an additional account of how that English arose.
- **Independent composition without Isaiah mediation** (*ad hoc*) — predicts: The same four-unit sequence and wording arose without literary contact.. Fit with this finding: *strongly challenged*. Support for the model: No credible generative mechanism explains four consecutive corresponding verses by coincidence.

**Considered and set aside:**
- Reverse dependence of extant Isaiah on Mosiah — Isaiah 52 and its manuscript tradition demonstrably predate the Book of Mormon's 1830 publication.
- Coincidental independent composition — Four consecutive, same-order, near-verbatim verses cannot reasonably be attributed to chance.
- Ancient quotation alone explains the exact KJV English — An ancient citation may explain content, but a separate translation or compositional mechanism is required for the seventeenth-century English form.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- A nineteenth-century composer copied or recited Isaiah 52:7–10 from the KJV. — *plausible*: It directly predicts the same-order, near-verbatim English block.
- An ancient source quoted Isaiah and the translator used the KJV as a translation base. — *plausible*: It explains both the narrative's represented quotation and the received English form, though this passage does not independently establish the source layer.
- Revelation supplied canonical KJV wording for an underlying quotation. — *possible*: It is compatible with a revelatory translation framework but cannot be distinguished from ordinary literary reuse here.
- Both texts independently reflect a common source without Isaiah or KJV mediation. — *ad hoc*: The sequential and English-verbal identity is more economically explained through the extant Isaiah tradition.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, discrimination 0.84) for the proposition: *Mosiah 12:21-24's reproduction of Isaiah 52:7-10 reflects deliberate textual/translation-level dependence on the Isaiah 52 tradition in the final English text.*

The complete, same-order, near-verbatim four-verse block strongly supports Isaiah/KJV textual mediation in Mosiah 12, while leaving direct modern reuse and an ancient citation rendered through KJV language unresolved.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: No matched-corpus rate was supplied, but chance reproduction of four consecutive verses in substantially identical order and wording is far rarer than isolated phrase overlap. The candidate passage is also explicitly deployed as a quotation unit, sharply reducing boundary flexibility; the remaining limitation concerns mechanism and external attestation rather than whether the relationship exists.

What would move this: The relationship weight would fall only if the textual correspondences proved materially misstated; it would not meaningfully rise without independent attestation because the relationship is already secure. KJV-specific readings or errors would favor later English mediation, while ancient variants independently attested before the KJV and shared uniquely by Mosiah would strengthen an ancient-source component.
