# Intratextual Narrative Continuity in Mormon 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mormon 2:10 explicitly interprets the inability to retain possessions as fulfillment of Samuel's warning in Helaman 13 that, because of iniquity, riches would become insecure or lost. This is strong evidence for deliberate narrative continuity between the Samuel account and Mormon's history.

**Does not establish:** This internal correspondence does not independently establish ancient authorship, supernatural prediction, or the historical accuracy of the reported losses.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A single author or final editor could have constructed prophecy and fulfillment as part of one literary design.
- The later narrator could have retrospectively interpreted social collapse through an already familiar prophecy.
- Literary continuity does not require that the predicted and reported events be independently historical.
