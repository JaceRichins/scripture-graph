# English Textual Dependence in Mosiah 23

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mosiah 23:13 closely reproduces the distinctive wording and syntax of Galatians 5:1, strongly supporting Galatians or KJV-shaped mediation in the Book of Mormon's final English text. The relationship can arise through loose translation, revelatory or editorial harmonization, conscious adaptation, or remembered biblical language, so it does not determine the narrative's historical origin and should not be counted independently from the broader KJV-mediation pattern.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 23:13 reads 'I desire that ye should stand fast in this liberty wherewith ye have been made free,' closely matching Galatians 5:1's 'Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free,' sharing the distinctive sequence 'stand fast ... liberty wherewith ... made free.'
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The shared multi-word sequence, including the uncommon relative construction 'liberty wherewith ... made free,' is unlikely to arise independently and indicates some direct relationship between the two English texts.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This fits the corpus's broader, well-documented pattern of KJV New Testament phrasing appearing in Book of Mormon narrative and discourse.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Within the corpus's broader pattern of KJV language appearing throughout the Book of Mormon, this match fits a reconstruction in which Limhi's liberation speech was rendered, by whatever mechanism, into the closest available King James idiom for a covenant of freedom from bondage.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
As with other tight NT parallels in the corpus, this strongly supports KJV-shaped mediation in the final English text. Because Galatians postdates the narrated Limhi-era setting by centuries, this raises the same translation-versus-composition question as comparable registry entries, without resolving it.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: a shared distinctive English phrase is equally compatible with inspired translation into available religious idiom and with uninspired literary borrowing.

<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
**What the text itself requires:** The text requires only that the English wording of Mosiah 23:13 matches Galatians 5:1's distinctive clause; it does not require a particular translation theory or any claim about the historical antiquity of the political liberation narrated in the chapter.

- **Tight-control translation into KJV-register English** (*plausible*) — predicts: A distinctive KJV clause appears where judged an apt rendering of an ancient liberation theme. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: General LDS translation theory
- **Loose/dynamic translation or revelatory paraphrase** (*plausible*) — predicts: Reuse of a ready-to-hand KJV liberty formula. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: Corpus's broader KJV-mediation pattern
- **Direct 19th-century composition borrowing Galatians 5:1** (*plausible*) — predicts: The same distinctive clause drawn from an available King James Bible. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: Demonstrated general presence of KJV language throughout the Book of Mormon

**Considered and set aside:**
