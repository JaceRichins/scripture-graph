# Literary Intertextuality in 1 Nephi 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentrated whore/many-waters/dominion-over-nations cluster in 1 Nephi 14 moderately supports a deliberate literary relationship with Revelation 17. But "many waters" as a Babylon epithet already appears in Jeremiah 51:13, so the parallel may reflect shared older OT tradition rather than dependence on Revelation specifically, and it does not determine the historical direction or mechanism of the relationship, ancient authorship, or the identity of Revelation's John.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
1 Nephi 14:9-17 depicts a "great and abominable church" as "the whore of all the earth" who "sat upon many waters" and had "dominion over all the earth, among all nations," gathering multitudes to fight the Lamb and incurring God's wrath — paralleling Revelation 17:1 ("the great whore that sitteth upon many waters"), 17:5 ("MOTHER OF HARLOTS"), and 17:14-15 (war against the Lamb; the waters as "peoples, and multitudes, nations").
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The final English text draws on Revelation 17's whore-of-Babylon imagery and vocabulary to describe Nephi's vision.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compatible with independent parallel revelation, translation-stage supply of Revelation's English, ancient shared apocalyptic tradition, or direct modern literary borrowing.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Ancient Jewish and early Christian apocalyptic writers regularly recycled a stock Babylon-as-harlot topos (Isaiah 47, Jeremiah 51, Nahum 3) that Revelation 17 itself continues. One reconstruction: 1 Nephi 14's vision is rendered, at the translation or composition stage, in the vocabulary this shared topos had already made familiar in English scripture — a proposal that accounts for the concentrated match without requiring direct one-way dependence on Revelation.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Moderately supports a deliberate Revelation-linked textual/literary relationship in the final form. It is invoked in both directions (apologetic revelatory harmony vs. critical evidence of biblical borrowing); the parallel itself does not settle direction.

**Inspiration:** Nothing independently; the parallel bears on literary/textual relationship, not on whether either text is divinely inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The parallel does not establish the direction or date of dependence, identify the historical John, show that two independent visions disclosed the same future, or establish ancient authorship, historicity, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Independent/parallel ancient revelation to Nephi and John** — predicts: convergent content but not necessarily verbatim shared phrasing. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
- **KJV-mediated translation using Revelation's familiar English** — predicts: exactly this kind of surface phrase match without implying source-language dependence. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Direct modern literary borrowing from Revelation 17** — predicts: the same phrase-level match. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Shared reuse of older OT Babylon-harlot imagery (Isaiah 47, Jeremiah 51)** — predicts: overlapping vocabulary mediated through a common OT source rather than Revelation specifically. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Both visions independently and supernaturally depict the same future entity. — *possible*: Theologically motivated and textually permitted but unfalsifiable.
- Translation supplies Revelation's familiar English for Nephi's vision. — *plausible*: Consistent with the Book of Mormon's broader pattern of KJV-inflected diction elsewhere.
- Direct modern-composition dependence on Revelation. — *plausible*: Revelation 17 was widely known and available to any 19th-century author.
- Shared OT Babylon imagery (Isaiah 47, Jeremiah 51) rather than direct Revelation dependence. — *independently supported*: "Many waters" as a Babylon epithet is independently attested earlier in Jeremiah 51:13, and Revelation 17 is itself already a reuse of that OT tradition, so the match may run through common OT vocabulary rather than Revelation exclusively.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Moderate** (supports, evidence_strength 0.55) for the proposition: *The final text of 1 Nephi 14 deliberately evokes the imagery and language represented in Revelation 17.*

The concentrated whore/many-waters/wrath cluster gives moderate support to a deliberate literary relationship with Revelation 17, but because "many waters" as a Babylon epithet already circulated in Jeremiah 51, the parallel may run through shared older tradition rather than direct Revelation dependence, and it settles neither the direction nor the mechanism of the relationship.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Whore-of-Babylon imagery is a recurring apocalyptic topos (Isaiah 47, Jeremiah 51, Nahum 3, Revelation 17-18); the "sits on many waters" phrase itself traces to Jeremiah 51:13, so part of the cluster reflects a widely-circulating OT motif rather than a coinage unique to Revelation.

What would move this: Identifying additional syntactically distinctive phrasing unique to Revelation 17 (not traceable to Jeremiah 51/Isaiah 47) would raise the weight toward direct Revelation dependence; showing the vocabulary is fully explained by generic OT Babylon imagery would lower it toward "weak."
