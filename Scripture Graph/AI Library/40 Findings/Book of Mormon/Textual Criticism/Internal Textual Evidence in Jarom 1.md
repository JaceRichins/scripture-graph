# Internal Textual Evidence in Jarom 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Jarom 1:1-2, 14-15's named inheritance from Enos, stated brevity rationale, distinction between record collections, and transfer to Omni strongly support (0.84) that the final text deliberately constructs Jarom as a self-conscious editorial persona, the same evidence class registered at bom-1nephi6-editorial-persona and bom-3nephi5-editorial-persona. This bears only on final-form design; it gives no independent discrimination between an ancient record-keeper and literary characterization, and none-band support for the archive's actual historical existence.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Jarom 1:1 states he writes by commandment of his father Enos to keep the genealogy. Verse 2 cites the plates' small size and the intended Lamanite audience as reasons to write briefly. Verse 14 distinguishes these plates from 'the other plates of Nephi' containing war records. Verse 15 records the transfer of the plates to his son Omni.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The final text presents Jarom as a self-conscious link in a described chain of record custodians, giving reasons for brevity, distinguishing record types, and naming his successor.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This is the same evidence class already registered for 1 Nephi 6 and 3 Nephi 5 (source, purpose, space, and succession statements constructing an editorial persona), recurring here in a shorter but structurally comparable form.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read alongside 1 Nephi 6 and 3 Nephi 5, Jarom 1 fits a consistent editorial pattern running through the small plates: each custodian names his source, states why he writes little, and names his successor. On a straightforward reading this gives the small-plates section a continuous internal chain of custody; whether that chain reflects a real ancient archive or a literary device building narrative continuity is the open question this pattern cannot resolve on its own.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
It strongly supports that the final text deliberately builds a coherent, source-conscious narratorial voice for Jarom, matching the registered 0.84 weight for the same construction elsewhere in the corpus. It gives no independent traction on whether an actual ancient record-keeper produced it.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: persona construction of this kind is equally available to an ancient author and a later literary author.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Deliberate final-form persona construction does not establish the plates' antiquity, Jarom's historical existence, or the historical existence of the described multi-record archive; on the historicity question specifically, the same models predict this cluster about equally well (none-band, ~0.1), matching bom-2nephi11-historical-editorial-selection.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Authentic ancient record-keeping preserved in translation** — predicts: A real custodian genuinely citing real constraints (plate size, audience, prior records) and a real succession. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Later editorial/redactional construction** — predicts: A compiler retrofitting a plausible-sounding succession and material constraints onto disparate source material. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century literary characterization** — predicts: An author manufacturing the same succession/constraint details to give the small-plates section internal continuity. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- A literary author can construct a coherent succession of narrators and records without the represented record history being external fact. — *plausible*: The same techniques (stated source, purpose, space limits, succession) are available to any author regardless of period.
- The administrative detail serves narrative realism or stitches together preplanned sections of the book. — *possible*: Consistent with the detail's function but not independently evidenced over the ancient-record alternative.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
Comparable colophon-like notices in biblical redaction criticism are treated as evidence of a constructed editorial voice, not proof of the editor's historicity; the same standard applies here.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->
