# Repeated Textual Motif in Abraham 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Forms of "obey" recur across Abraham 4, making responsiveness to divine ordering a deliberate motif. The refrain marks the successful unfolding of the Gods' plan through organization, observation, and compliance, culminating in the completed creation being described as "very obedient."
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Across Abraham 4:10, 12, 18, 21, 25, and 31, forms of "obey" describe either the Gods' commands and ordering being obeyed or created things being expected to obey. The language culminates in the completed arrangement being described as "very obedient."
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The recurrence makes reliable responsiveness to divine ordering a deliberate motif. In context, obedience marks conformity to the creative plan and need not imply humanlike moral agency in plants, animals, matter, or natural forces.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The motif belongs to Abraham 4's distinctive portrayal of creation through planning, organizing, ordering, watching, and obtaining compliance. It gives the account a process-oriented emphasis in which the success of successive creative acts is observed.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a literary-theological reconstruction, the obedience refrain marks successive stages at which the Gods' organization becomes effective. Commands are issued, results are watched, and the completed arrangement is pronounced obedient to the plan, presenting creation as orderly and reliably responsive to divine direction.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
