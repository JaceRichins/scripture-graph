# Book of Mormon Assessment

The contested findings of the Book of Mormon corpus, weighed together. One stable assessment per issue lives in [[Assessments]]; the findings themselves are under [[Book of Mormon Findings]]. This page is a judgment, not a sum — no weight here is added to another — and it is not a verdict on the book: the project is not here to prove or disprove scripture. It exists so that no finding is dismissed one at a time as coincidence when it belongs to a line, and none is counted twice when it shares a cause. The same page exists for every corpus.

## How to read this page
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-to-read -->
This page judges the assessed Book of Mormon findings together. It is not arithmetic, a probability of historicity, a survey of all scholarship, or a verdict on the book. The registry is presently dominated by literary and intertextual questions; witnesses, production history, archaeology, populations, anachronisms, and several other central questions remain undeveloped. Strong local assessments therefore establish only their named propositions unless independent evidence connects them to provenance.

Independence is determined by causal explanation, not by a shared disclaimer. Extended biblical quotations and shorter KJV-shaped echoes can jointly establish pervasive English biblical mediation, although their individual specificity differs. Prediction and fulfillment under one final narrative controller establish coordination, not two independent historical witnesses. By contrast, South Arabian epigraphy, manuscript syntax, and literary structure use substantially different data and failure modes, so Nahom, if-and conditionals, and Alma 36 chiasmus remain separate weak lines.

The mirror-image errors are counting every biblical echo, callback, editorial notice, or narrated witness as another vote, and dismissing genuinely independent weak findings one at a time without noticing their limited convergence. Neither error is corrected by adding weights. Issue-specific doctrinal, ethical, and literary interpretations remain valid at their registered strength but are not silently converted into evidence for or against ancient authorship.
<!-- SG:END how-to-read -->

## Independent lines
<!-- SG:BEGIN lines -->
- **Biblical and KJV-shaped mediation — strong support for mediation; mechanism unresolved.** Extended blocks such as [[Assessments#bom-isaiah-textual-dependence|Isaiah/KJV dependence]] and [[Assessments#bom-3nephi-matthew-kjv-dependence|Matthew/KJV sermon mediation]], distinctive passage-level relationships such as [[Assessments#bom-3nephi15-john10-intertext|John 10 engagement]] and [[Assessments#bom-ether4-mark16-intertext|Mark 16 mediation]], and shorter portable formulas do not have identical denominators or diagnostic value. Together, however, their distribution strongly establishes pervasive biblical, often specifically KJV-shaped, mediation of the received English. They strongly challenge a source-transparent English translation independent of the KJV. Direct modern reuse, revelatory restatement, editorial harmonization, and ancient material rendered through biblical English remain different mechanisms; mere compatibility does not establish equal predictive fit. Intratextual reprises and callbacks are classified under final-form design and add no second provenance line.

- **Nahom/NHM — weak support.** [[Assessments#bom-nahom-nhm-geography|Nahom and South Arabian NHM]] is an independent epigraphic/toponymic line. The right-period tribal attestation is a genuine correlation, but it does not identify the narrated burial locality, fix the vocalization or route, or supply the missing candidate-name denominator.

- **If-and conditional syntax — weak support.** [[Assessments#bom-if-and-semitic-source|If-and conditionals]] is an independent manuscript-linguistic line. Early attestation and later regularization modestly favor a translation-calque reading, while English precedent, dictation mechanisms, a small sample, and missing matched frequencies keep it weak.

- **Alma 36 chiasmus — weak support.** [[Assessments#bom-chiasmus-antiquity|Alma 36 chiasmus]] is an independent structural line. Its inversion and verse-18 hinge are real, but natural descent-and-return symmetry, non-Hebrew ring composition, analyst-flexible units, and the absent control denominator prevent more than weak support for specifically ancient Hebrew literary origin.

- **Biblically available setting and prophetic-call conventions — none to weak for antiquity and not an additional independent line.** [[Assessments#bom-1nephi1-callnarrative-biblical-parallel|The call-narrative parallels]] and [[Assessments#bom-1nephi-zedekiah-synchronism|Zedekiah synchronism]] fit an ancient setting, but the relevant genre, ruler, crisis, and captivity setting were available through the Bible. They should not inflate the convergence supplied by Nahom, syntax, and chiasmus.

- **Deliberate final-form literary and documentary design — strong for design; none for provenance.** Editorial personae such as [[Assessments#bom-1nephi6-editorial-persona|Nephi's]] and [[Assessments#bom-3nephi5-editorial-persona|Mormon's]], epistemic calibration, integrated arguments, contrasts, typologies, thematic patterns, and internal reprises are not one local phenomenon. Collectively they strongly establish repeated deliberate final-form craft. For provenance, one authorial, editorial, redactional, or revelatory controller is a sufficient shared cause, so these patterns cannot be multiplied into historical witnesses. No corpus-wide error rate or claim of complete consistency is justified without a denominator of attempted and failed correspondences.

- **Prophecy, fulfillment, and long-range callbacks — strong for final-form coordination; none for genuine prediction by themselves.** The correspondences represented by [[Assessments#bom-alma16-prophecy-fulfillment-correspondence|Alma 16]], [[Assessments#bom-mosiah11-mosiah21-bondage-fulfillment|Limhite bondage]], [[Assessments#bom-3nephi1-samuel-signs-correspondence|Samuel's birth signs]], and related pairs are often explicit and strong. Prediction, outcome, and fulfillment notice nevertheless reach the reader through the same final literary tradition. This line overlaps causally with final-form design and is separated only to make the historical-independence failure explicit; it adds no independent evidence of pre-event foreknowledge.

- **Internal witnesses, miracles, and self-attestation — none for external corroboration.** [[Assessments#bom-3nephi17-collective-witness-claim|The 2,500-witness claim]], [[Assessments#bom-2nephi11-witness-corroboration|the three-witness formula]], [[Assessments#bom-alma3-record-accuracy-self-attestation|record self-attestation]], and related claims are different narrative assertions, not one causal event. Each is nevertheless transmitted by the one record being assessed. Their repetition cannot convert narrated witnesses into independently preserved testimony, so they remain issue-level none-band findings rather than a cumulative historicity line.

- **Internal New World spatial coherence — moderate for deliberate world construction, none to weak for historicity.** [[Assessments#bom-alma22-geographic-coherence|Alma 22]] presents an interconnected relative geography. Remembered terrain, editorial systematization, and careful worldbuilding all predict that result, so it does not independently identify a historical location.

- **Unique modern geographic identification — strong challenge to that narrow claim, not to historicity generally.** [[Assessments#bom-3nephi8-geographic-identifiability|3 Nephi 8]] lacks the distances and external anchors needed to select a unique modern setting. This closes overconfident site-identification arguments, not every limited-geography historical model.

- **First Red Sea campsite — none-band support for identification.** [[Assessments#bom-lehi-red-sea-campsite-identification|The Old World campsite description]] is geographically and causally separate from New World geography. It is compatible with a real journey but too underdetermined to identify a site or materially support antiquity.

- **Isaiah variants as a distinct ancient Vorlage — none-band support.** [[Assessments#bom-2nephi7-isaiah50-variants|The 2 Nephi 7 variants]] securely establish a distinct English form, but no cited ancient witness corroborates the relevant additions. The established KJV-mediation background makes translation-level or editorial expansion at least as available as a divergent Hebrew Vorlage.

- **Pre-Christian Christian knowledge, genealogies, and ancient-source assertions — no single cumulative line.** These are different claims, including [[Assessments#bom-2nephi6-predictive-prophecy|Jacob's crucifixion prophecy]], [[Assessments#bom-alma39-preadvent-christology|Alma's pre-advent Christology]], [[Assessments#bom-1nephi13-plain-precious-losses|plain-and-precious losses]], and [[Assessments#bom-helaman8-mulek-zedekiah-genealogy|Mulek's genealogy]]. Their explicitness establishes what the final text claims, not when the information originated. Each remains none-band for its external historical proposition because ancient revelation, later shaping, translation expansion, and modern composition remain viable.

- **Local theological, ethical, and contextual interpretations — retained but not counted toward provenance.** Assessments such as [[Assessments#bom-2nephi24-human-royal-referent|the human royal referent]], [[Assessments#bom-alma27-ammonite-pacifism-universality|Ammonite pacifism]], [[Assessments#bom-ether4-father-son-language|Father-and-Son language]], and [[Assessments#bom-alma32-experiment-epistemology|Alma 32's epistemology]] answer distinct passage-level questions. Their registered conclusions stand at full strength without becoming authorship evidence.

## What the whole picture shows
<!-- SG:BEGIN synthesis -->
The current registry converges strongly on two narrow conclusions: the received English Book of Mormon is pervasively mediated through biblical, often KJV-shaped, language, and its final form contains extensive deliberate literary, editorial, and prophecy-fulfillment design. Those are substantial findings about the text. They do not become equally substantial evidence of antiquity, historicity, or inspiration. Biblical mediation strongly lowers a source-transparent translation model, while one final narrative controller sufficiently explains internal callbacks, editorial personae, witnesses, and fulfillment notices under several ancient and modern production models.

Three presently independent findings give limited support in the ancient-source direction: Nahom/NHM, if-and syntax, and Alma 36 chiasmus. Their different data and failure modes matter, so they should not be dismissed as one phenomenon. Their convergence nevertheless remains weak rather than cleanly moderate because every line lacks an important denominator or has a plausible modern pathway. The biblically available Zedekiah setting and prophetic-call genre do not add independent force to that cluster.

The picture is asymmetric only in specified respects. It strongly challenges an English text substantially independent of KJV shaping and arguments that internal testimony or narrator-controlled fulfillment independently establish history. A nineteenth-century KJV-informed production family currently explains the observed English mediation and final-form control economically, but the registry has not sufficiently assessed oral dictation, source use, collaboration, the physical witnesses, archaeological expectations, anachronisms, populations, or the translation process to establish comparative fit for the whole book. An ancient substrate rendered or expanded through biblical English therefore remains possible, though an unconstrained translation-harmonization model cannot be treated as equally predictive merely because it can accommodate every result after the fact. The present registry supports no verdict on the Book of Mormon's overall historicity.
<!-- SG:END synthesis -->

## Frameworks — what the whole picture leaves open
<!-- SG:BEGIN frameworks -->
- **Final-form literary work with pervasive biblical/KJV mediation — independently supported.** Extended quotation blocks, distinctive phrase sequences, explicit interpretation, source personae, callbacks, and integrated arguments strongly establish this origin-neutral baseline. It raises every framework containing substantial conscious, editorial, or revelatory biblical mediation and lowers accidental-overlap and KJV-independent-English models.

- **Ancient historical record rendered through KJV-shaped revelatory or loose translation — plausible but only weakly independently supported.** The straightforward narrative presents ancient migrations, peoples, records, abridgers, and historical events; it does not require source-transparent English or explain the degree and distribution of translation harmonization. Nahom, if-and syntax, and chiasmus modestly raise this family. KJV mediation is compatible with it, but compatibility alone does not show that extensive harmonization was prospectively expected. The model needs independent criteria identifying where close rendering ends and harmonization begins.

- **Ancient core with substantial translation-level, revelatory, or modern expansion — possible.** This layered model accommodates ancient narrative material alongside later Christian and KJV formulations. Textual transmission can contain multiple stages, but no surviving Book of Mormon source manuscript independently identifies such layers. The model becomes ad hoc if expansion is invoked only wherever a difficulty appears rather than by prior linguistic or textual criteria.

- **Ancient record with ancient editorial or redactional layers — possible as a stage, not a complete rival model.** The text portrays abridgment and succession, but those notices establish final-form characterization rather than the historical existence of the claimed editors. Ancient redaction must still be combined with a translation and English-mediation model.

- **Nineteenth-century KJV-informed composition — plausible.** Known access to the KJV, pervasive biblical English, final-form literary control, and narrator-controlled fulfillments fit this family economically. The registry does not establish Joseph Smith as sole planner or rule out oral composition, sources, scribal interaction, collaboration, or revelatory self-understanding. Nahom, syntax, and chiasmus remain weak residual challenges rather than unexplained facts.

- **Nineteenth-century oral-formulaic dictation or source-mediated/collaborative production — possible and underassessed.** Such models predict recurring biblical register and may produce substantial long-range organization through memorized formulas, prior planning, source use, scribal interaction, or later correction. The present registry lacks the production-history and matched oral-composition assessments needed to rank these variants against single-author literary composition.

- **Modern inspired or revelatory scripture without a recoverable ancient substrate — possible.** This model predicts biblical idiom, theological synthesis, and coherent sacred narrative while interpreting the surface ancient-history presentation nonliterally, accommodatively, or as a revelatory vehicle. The textual findings cannot independently test divine causation, and this model incurs a real interpretive cost because it revises the narrative's ordinary historical presentation.

- **Limited-geography ancient history within an already populated Americas — possible, nested within an ancient-history model.** The text supplies relative geography but no unique modern map and does not expressly require exclusive hemispheric descent from Lehi. This avoids claims the text does not require, but the assessed registry supplies no independent New World anchor that raises the model beyond possibility.

- **Source-transparent close translation largely independent of KJV shaping — ad hoc in light of the received English.** Sustained KJV order, wording, and New Testament intertexts strongly lower this model. Repeated exceptions for biblical language effectively transform it into the KJV-shaped translation model.

**Considered and set aside or withheld:** independent composition with no biblical or translation-level relationship is strongly challenged by extended ordered wording; internal fulfillments as independent pre-event evidence fail because prediction and outcome share one literary tradition; unique New World identification from internal geography alone is underdetermined. A hemispheric-exclusive ancestry model and specific Spalding, *View of the Hebrews*, or *Late War* dependence claims should not control this synthesis until their demographic and source-dependence questions are formally assessed; the supplied context does not establish those broader models here.
<!-- SG:END frameworks -->

## What a believer can hold
<!-- SG:BEGIN believer -->
A believer can honestly hold that the Book of Mormon preserves an ancient historical record delivered through a revelatory translation that freely uses familiar KJV language. The text does not require a hemispheric ancestry model, a uniquely recoverable modern map, or an English rendering transparent to the source language. Nahom and the structurally independent Semitic-form observations give that historical framework real, cumulative support, even though they do not establish it.

Honest belief should not convert internal witnesses into external witnesses, literary fulfillments into independently dated predictions, or final-form complexity into proof of antiquity. It can instead acknowledge that the book's literary intelligence, moral argument, and scriptural synthesis are genuine features while treating historicity as supported unevenly and faith as carrying weight the registry cannot measure. The physical witnesses and rapid dictation history may matter, but they require their own calibrated assessments before being added to this page.
<!-- SG:END believer -->

## What a skeptic can hold
<!-- SG:BEGIN skeptic -->
A skeptic can honestly regard skilled nineteenth-century KJV-informed composition as the most economical ordinary historical explanation of the assessed English text. Corpus-wide biblical mediation is strong, while internal editorial personae, miracles, witnesses, and fulfillment sequences are all controlled by the same narrative source. The current registry contains no strong, independently attested New World historical anchor, and its positive ancient-provenance cluster is only moderate when denominators and alternatives are included.

That conclusion does not justify saying that a specific source book has been shown to be copied, that DNA refutes every geography the text permits, that literary artistry is unreal, or that Nahom and the Semitic-form findings are worthless. Nor does it turn a parsimonious naturalistic account into proof of fraud or a judgment about spiritual value.
<!-- SG:END skeptic -->

## What would move this
<!-- SG:BEGIN would-move -->
**Assessment dated 2026-09-06.** The strongest observed line is corpus-wide biblical/KJV mediation. It would become more discriminating toward direct nineteenth-century composition if controlled work established repeated dependence on KJV-specific renderings, secondary passages, or English errors that an ancient source could not plausibly motivate, especially in pre-Christian sections, with directionality demonstrated against pre-1830 controls. It would weaken as an authorship discriminator if early manuscript evidence or independently dated ancient witnesses repeatedly anticipated the Book of Mormon's distinctive non-KJV readings, or if matched nineteenth-century translated scripture showed that the same degree of KJV harmonization routinely coexisted with demonstrably ancient sources.

The ancient-provenance convergence would strengthen substantially through an independently dated inscription, manuscript, or archaeological complex tied by several specific names, chronology, and material details to the Book of Mormon; a securely dated NHM burial locality on a tightly constrained route would also help. It would weaken if candidate-rate studies showed that NHM-like matches are common along many plausible routes, blind matched-corpus tests reproduced the alleged Hebraisms and chiasms at comparable rates, or a specific pre-1830 source explained the relevant distinctive features together rather than merely resembling them generally.
<!-- SG:END would-move -->

## Inspiration
<!-- SG:BEGIN inspiration -->
These findings do not determine whether the Book of Mormon is inspired. Historical provenance, translation mechanism, literary quality, spiritual power, and divine origin are distinct questions. An ancient source would not by itself establish revelation, and nineteenth-century language or composition would not by itself exclude inspiration. The registry can clarify what historical and literary models fit the text and where each incurs costs; accepting divine involvement, revelation, or the book's spiritual authority remains a theological and experiential judgment rather than an inference secured by these cumulative textual findings.
<!-- SG:END inspiration -->
