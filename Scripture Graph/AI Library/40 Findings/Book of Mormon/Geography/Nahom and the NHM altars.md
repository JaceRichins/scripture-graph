# Nahom and the NHM altars

> [!evidence] The observation
> 1 Nephi 16:34 says Ishmael was buried "in the place which was called
> [[Nahom]]" ([[1 Nephi 16#^1ne-16-34|1 Ne. 16:34]]). Votive altars from the
> Bar'an temple near Wadi Jawf, Yemen (c. 7th–6th century BC), carry the
> South-Arabian tribal name **NHM** (Nihm/Naham), and a tribal region of that
> name still exists in roughly the area where Lehi's party would turn east.

<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
1 Nephi 16 describes Ishmael's burial at a pre-existing Nahom, and South Arabian inscriptions independently attest an NHM tribal name near Ma'rib in roughly the right era — a real and interesting correlation. But the inscriptions fix a tribal designation, not a burial locality or the Book of Mormon's intended vocalization, and no search denominator or ruled-out map-mediation evidence has been supplied, so this remains weak rather than moderate support for authentic ancient Arabian geographic knowledge.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
1 Nephi 16:34 states Ishmael was buried at a pre-existing place called Nahom. South Arabian inscriptions conventionally transliterated NHM occur on altars from the Bar'an temple excavation near Ma'rib and attest a tribal affiliation in roughly the relevant broad period; a later NHM/Nihm placename survives in western Yemen. The inscriptions do not themselves name Lehi, Ishmael, a burial place, the Book of Mormon's intended vocalization, or a precise location.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The apologetic identification links the unvocalized consonantal NHM with Nahom and situates it near a reconstructed southward route with an eastward turn toward a proposed Bountiful in Dhofar. This is geographically possible and more specific than a generic Arabian setting, but the route, vocalization, territorial extent, burial association, and Bountiful endpoint are reconstructed rather than directly given by the inscriptions.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compatible with an ancient NHM-associated people in South Arabia and an itinerary passing through their territory; equally compatible with a modern author encountering a related map name, or with a chance match found after searching many Arabian names and route configurations.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The right-period NHM attestation is genuinely interesting and weakly raises the plausibility of authentic Arabian knowledge, especially if nineteenth-century access to the name was unlikely. Its force is reduced by uncertain vocalization, the gap between a tribal designation and a confirmed burial locality, flexible route reconstruction, an unreported search denominator, and documented pre-1830 maps carrying a related name.

**Inspiration:** This geographic correlation says nothing by itself about divine inspiration, which is not entailed by either an ancient identification or a modern-source explanation.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
That Nahom is the same place as the NHM inscriptions, that Ishmael was buried there, that the proposed route or Bountiful identification is correct, or that Lehi's journey and the surrounding narrative are historical.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Nahom and South Arabian NHM are a chance consonantal resemblance selected from a large field of Arabian names. — *plausible*: The vocalization is uncertain, and no comprehensive denominator reports how many names, spellings, routes, and locations were available for comparison.
- A pre-1830 map or derivative geographic source containing Nehhm, Nehm, or a related spelling ultimately supplied the name. — *possible*: Related names appeared on pre-1830 European maps, although direct evidence that Joseph Smith or a close associate accessed and used a relevant source has not been supplied.
- The inscriptions name a tribe whose territory included the narrative burial location. — *possible*: The inscriptions independently attest an NHM affiliation, but an offering at Ma'rib does not by itself map that group's territory or identify a locality called Nahom.
- Travel constraints and the eastward turn narrow the route to NHM-associated territory. — *possible*: Proposed routes use real Arabian terrain and travel constraints, but their stopping points, territorial boundaries, and coastal endpoints retain appreciable retrospective flexibility.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
An unvocalized toponym/tribal-name match in a biblical narrative would receive only limited weight unless location, referent, route, and search denominator were independently constrained; treating the inscription as though it securely supplied the narrative's exact place would be too generous there too.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Weak** (supports, evidence_strength 0.3) for the proposition: *The name Nahom and its narrative location preserve authentic pre-exilic South Arabian geographic knowledge that is materially more expected from an ancient source than from nineteenth-century composition.*

The right-period South Arabian NHM inscriptions are an interesting, weakly corroborating detail for 1 Nephi's Arabian geography, but they identify a tribe rather than a burial site, and without route or search-denominator controls this stays weak evidence for ancient authenticity rather than moderate or strong.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: No matched-control study or search denominator is supplied; many Arabian names, transliterations, tribal designations, routes and coastal endpoints could be compared. Ancient Near Eastern texts commonly preserve consonantal place/tribal names, and three-consonant approximate matches across Semitic languages are not rare enough to be decisive on their own.

What would move this: Would rise with securely provenanced pre-exilic evidence locating an inhabited or burial locality vocalizable as Nahom at a narrowly predicted route junction, plus a comprehensive matched-name analysis; would fall with demonstrated pre-1830 access to the relevant map name or matched controls showing comparable three-consonant route matches are common.

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the proposal that Lehi's party traveled a caravan route down Arabia's western/interior corridor, a stop remembered as 'Nahom' near the NHM tribe's territory around Ma'rib before turning east toward a candidate Bountiful is a workable reconstruction consistent with known incense-route geography and the Bar'an altar attestation. This is a live possibility, not a demonstration: the altars fix a tribal name and rough locale, not a burial ground or a narrative's exact path, so the reconstruction sits alongside chance resemblance and map mediation as one of several live accounts.
