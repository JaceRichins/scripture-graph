# Intertextual Legal Formula in Ether 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ether 5:2-4's promise that three future witnesses will be shown the plates and that 'in the mouth of three witnesses shall these things be established' closely tracks the biblical two-or-three-witnesses legal-testimony formula (Deuteronomy 19:15, Matthew 18:16, 2 Corinthians 13:1) — strong evidence (0.72) that the final English text deliberately draws on that tradition. Because the formula recurs at multiple KJV loci available to any KJV-literate writer, and because ancient composition rendered through translation, revelatory/editorial harmonization, and nineteenth-century authorship (contemporaneous with Joseph Smith's own arrangement of the historical Three Witnesses) all predict the same wording about equally well, this gives only none-band support for identifying the promised witnesses specifically with the 1829 Three Witnesses as fulfilled foreknowledge, or for ancient authorship generally.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Ether 5:4 reads: 'in the mouth of three witnesses shall these things be established... and this work... shall stand as a testimony against the world.' This tracks the 'at/in the mouth of two or three witnesses shall the matter/word be established' clause in Deuteronomy 19:15, repeated at Matthew 18:16 and 2 Corinthians 13:1.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The verse is Moroni's own first-person address to a future reader of the finished compilation (not a translated excerpt of the ancient Jaredite record itself), invoking a recognized Mosaic legal-testimony formula to authorize the book's forthcoming witnesses.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The wording is compatible with an ancient editor consciously drawing on Deuteronomic legal language later rendered in familiar KJV cadences, with revelatory/editorial harmonization toward the KJV formula during translation, or with a 19th-century author reusing well-known biblical phrasing; the wording alone does not discriminate among these.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Under the reconstruction where Moroni's source-conscious editorial voice (attested elsewhere, cf. bom-alma40-epistemic-persona) drew on his knowledge of Mosaic legal procedure and phrased his appeal to future readers as a two/three-witness legal establishment, the verse fits comfortably into the corpus's recurring pattern of biblical-legal-formula reuse (cf. bom-2nephi11-witness-corroboration) without needing to name specific historical individuals. Whether the translation process rendered this in near-verbatim KJV cadence, or Joseph Smith supplied the wording contemporaneously with organizing the actual 1829 witnesses, is not decidable from the verse alone — both are live, unresolved reconstructions.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Believers commonly read this verse as Moroni's foreknowledge of the specific 1829 Three Witnesses (Cowdery, Whitmer, Harris), whose signed statement now prefaces the book — a stronger, more specific claim than mere formulaic reuse. Critics note the verse names no individuals, date, or particular signing event, and that Joseph Smith received D&C 17 (designating three witnesses) within the same general period as this portion of the translation, so contemporaneous composition predicts the passage without requiring ancient foreknowledge. The formulaic-reuse observation itself is not seriously disputed; the predictive reading is.

**Inspiration:** Nothing directly: whether Moroni's phrasing echoes a familiar biblical legal formula says nothing about whether the underlying record or its translation was divinely inspired; that question turns on evidence outside this verbal parallel.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The parallel does not establish ancient authorship, the specific translation mechanism, that Ether 5:4 foretold the named historical Three Witnesses rather than restating a generic legal formula, or the reliability of the witnesses' later testimony.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
**What the text itself requires:** Ether 5:2-3 explicitly predicts three specific future persons who will be shown the plates 'by the power of God' and will know of their truth with a surety, distinct from the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost separately named in v.4 as bearing record. Proposal A's suggestion that the promised witnesses could instead be 'the Godhead' or 'future readers generally' misreads the passage and is removed; identifying the promised witnesses with the final text's forthcoming three human witnesses is required by the wording, not an inference brought to it. What the text does not require is that those witnesses be identified specifically with the named historical individuals (Cowdery, Whitmer, Harris) or a particular 1829 signing event, and it does not require that the wording preserve a verbatim ancient legal formula rather than a KJV-mediated one.

- **Ancient Moroni compiling/editorializing, later rendered in KJV-style English by translation** (*possible*) — predicts: Distinctive but not necessarily verbatim legal-testimony language loosely paralleling Deuteronomy's formula.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: Moroni's stated source-conscious editorial voice elsewhere in the corpus (cf. bom-alma40-epistemic-persona, bom-3nephi5-editorial-persona).
- **Translation/harmonization pulls Moroni's underlying idea into near-exact KJV Deuteronomy/2 Corinthians wording** (*plausible*) — predicts: Close but imperfect verbal match, similar to the corpus's other registered KJV-mediation intertexts.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: Corpus-wide KJV-mediation pattern (Isaiah, Malachi, and New Testament dependence issues in the registry).
- **19th-century composition, contemporaneous with arranging the historical Three Witnesses** (*plausible*) — predicts: Exact reuse of familiar KJV phrasing, timed near D&C 17 and the actual 1829 witnessing event.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: Documented chronological proximity of Ether's translation (in traditional accounts, among the later portions translated) to D&C 17 and the June 1829 witnessing.

**Considered and set aside:**
- Ether 5:4 as literal fulfilled prophecy naming the historical 1829 Three Witnesses — Adds specifics (named individuals, a date, a signing event) the verse does not state; treated as an apologetic overreading of an otherwise well-supported intertextual observation.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Direct modern reuse of Deuteronomy 19:15/2 Corinthians 13:1 by the translator/author. — *plausible*: Exact-formula reuse of widely known KJV verses requires only ordinary familiarity with the Bible, no additional assumptions.
- Generic ancient Near Eastern multiple-witness legal convention independently available to Moroni. — *possible*: Multi-witness requirements are attested across ANE law codes, so the underlying idea need not derive from Deuteronomy specifically, though the closeness of the English wording to KJV Deuteronomy/2 Corinthians is still a translation-level fact needing explanation.
- Ether 5:4 as literal fulfilled prophecy specifically naming the historical 1829 Three Witnesses. — *ad hoc*: Requires reading in specific individuals, a date, and a printed signing event that the verse's wording does not state.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
When 2 Corinthians 13:1 itself reuses Deuteronomy 19:15 almost verbatim, scholars treat it as an unremarkable deliberate citation reflecting Paul's familiarity with Torah law, not as evidence of anything beyond that. The same restraint applies here: the citation is treated as an intertextual fact, and the stronger predictive reading is held to the same evidentiary bar a comparable claim would face in the other corpus.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, discrimination 0.72) for the proposition: *Ether 5:2-4 deliberately reuses the biblical two-or-three-witnesses legal-testimony formula (Deuteronomy 19:15 / 2 Corinthians 13:1) in framing the plates' three promised future witnesses, in the text's final English form.*

The verbal overlap is strong, readily verifiable evidence that Ether 5:4's witness clause deliberately echoes the biblical two-or-three-witnesses legal formula, but it gives essentially no independent traction on whether this reflects ancient foreknowledge of the specific historical Three Witnesses or a KJV-literate author's use of familiar legal language around the time those witnesses were actually arranged.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: The 'two or three witnesses ... established' formula recurs at least three times in the KJV itself (Deuteronomy 19:15, Matthew 18:16, 2 Corinthians 13:1), so any KJV-literate writer had ample exposure to it. This is lower-distinctiveness than single-source, longer passage-level matches elsewhere in the registry (e.g., the six-verse Malachi 4 correspondence at 0.83), supporting a strong-but-lower-end weight for the intertextuality claim.

What would move this: Independent, pre-1829 documentary evidence that this wording (or a Near Eastern legal-formula parallel not mediated by KJV English) predates Joseph Smith's own arrangement of the Three Witnesses would raise the weight for ancient design. Evidence that the phrasing or its placement tracks distinctively 19th-century legal-testimony rhetoric contemporaneous with D&C 17 would push further toward modern composition, though it would not lower the (already separate) intertextuality weight itself.
