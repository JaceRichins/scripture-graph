# Internal Narrative Evidence in Mosiah 26

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[Mosiah 26#^mosiah-26-33|Mosiah 26:33]] depicts Alma recording the revealed instructions so that they can govern his judgments, and [[Mosiah 26#^mosiah-26-34|verses 34]]-[[Mosiah 26#^mosiah-26-36|36]] show their subsequent application. Within the chapter's institutional world, written revelation functions as a continuing standard for discipline and membership.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 26:33 says Alma wrote down the words he had received so that he could judge the church according to them. Verses 34-36 then depict him applying that word through confession, repentance, continued enrollment, and the blotting out of names.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter presents a case-specific revelation becoming a written and repeatedly applied standard for church discipline.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The sequence connects claimed revelation, written preservation, adjudication, and membership records within the narrative's picture of an organized religious community.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the chapter's administrative reconstruction, Alma does not treat the revelation as guidance for one hearing only: he records it as precedent, applies it to subsequent cases, and enforces the resulting decisions through the community's membership roll. The writing and recordkeeping details therefore explain how charismatic authority becomes continuing institutional practice.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
