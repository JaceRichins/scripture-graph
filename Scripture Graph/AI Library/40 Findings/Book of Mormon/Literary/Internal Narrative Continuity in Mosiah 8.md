# Internal Narrative Continuity in Mosiah 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mosiah 8 introduces the twenty-four plates and the question of the destroyed people they describe; Mosiah 28 reports their translation and promises that their account will appear later. Ether 1 fulfills that notice by identifying the Book of Ether with those same plates. **Scripture:** [[Mosiah 8#^mosiah-8-9|Mosiah 8:9]], [[Mosiah 8#^mosiah-8-12|Mosiah 8:12]], [[Mosiah 28#^mosiah-28-11|Mosiah 28:11]], [[Mosiah 28#^mosiah-28-13|Mosiah 28:13]], [[Mosiah 28#^mosiah-28-17|Mosiah 28:17]], [[Mosiah 28#^mosiah-28-19|Mosiah 28:19]], [[Ether 1#^ether-1-1|Ether 1:1]], [[Ether 1#^ether-1-2|Ether 1:2]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 8 introduces twenty-four gold plates found by Limhi's people and asks whether they preserve knowledge of a destroyed people. Mosiah 28 reports Mosiah's translation of the plates, says that they recount a destroyed people back to the great tower, and postpones presentation of the account. Ether 1 then identifies Moroni's source as the same twenty-four plates found by Limhi's people.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The present text uses a question, a reported translation, a deferred-account notice, and a later source identification to connect the Limhi expedition with the Book of Ether.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The sequence explains within the narrative how the Jaredite account entered the Nephite record tradition and why its contents are introduced in Mosiah but narrated only later.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the final-form literary reconstruction, Mosiah 8 creates an unresolved archival question, Mosiah 28 supplies the translation and announces a later account, and Ether 1 delivers that account while explicitly naming its source. The result is a long-range source-history bridge between the Mosiah and Ether narratives.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
