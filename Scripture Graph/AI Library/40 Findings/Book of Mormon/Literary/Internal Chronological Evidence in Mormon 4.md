# Internal Chronological Evidence in Mormon 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mormon 4 organizes the Nephite collapse with named year-notices spanning the 363rd through 375th years, including a gap after the 367th year rather than a strictly consecutive year-by-year account. The notices, city changes, retreats, and renewed assaults form a coherent internal chronology, and Mormon's retrieval of the records from Shim fits this worsening sequence as an archive-preservation decision made amid disintegration.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mormon 4 anchors the Nephite collapse with year-notices across a wider span than a short consecutive run: the 363rd year (4:1), the 364th-year attack on Teancum (4:7), renewed war after the 366th year (4:10), reversals from the 367th year (4:15-16), and, after an unmarked gap, further losses and Mormon's retrieval of the records extending into the 375th year (4:19-23).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The sequence is internally well-organized and free of contradiction, showing deliberate chronological care in the final text, whether that care originated in genuine record-keeping or in careful compilation or composition.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compatible with authentic ancient annalistic transmission, with Mormon's own editorial systematization of his sources (smoothing over any original inconsistencies), or with careful modern literary construction, since internal consistency is trivial to maintain under any of the three.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On an authentic-transmission model, Mormon is abridging a genuine annalistic Nephite record that already used a stable era-count, and the coherence reflects that source directly. On an editorial model, Mormon himself imposes or preserves this coherence while compiling from multiple sources, smoothing minor gaps as any careful ancient historian would. Both reconstructions fit the observed pattern equally; nothing in the chronology itself favors one over the other.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
