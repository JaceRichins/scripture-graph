# Narrative Framing in 3 Nephi 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[3 Nephi 24#^3ne-24-1|3 Nephi 24:1]] presents Malachi's words through a deliberate sequence: Jesus commands that they be written, the people write them, and Jesus expounds them. This framing treats the passage as authoritative, preserved, and relevant to the assembled audience. It also supplies the narrative bridge by which Malachi enters the record and the discourse.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
In 3 Nephi 24:1, Jesus commands the assembled people to write words attributed to the Father through Malachi; after the words are written, the narrator says that Jesus expounds them before introducing the passage.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The sequence of command, inscription, exposition, and recitation presents Malachi's words as authorized scripture requiring both preservation and interpretation for this audience. The framing integrates the ensuing quotation into the discourse rather than presenting it as an unexplained insertion.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The verse illuminates a model of scriptural transmission in which authoritative words are dictated or recited, committed to writing, and interpreted within a teaching assembly. It also identifies the narrative rationale for including material attributed to Malachi in the people's record.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a historical-event reconstruction, the risen Jesus supplies and explains scripture absent from the audience's inherited records. On a literary-framing reconstruction, the command and exposition authorize the inserted Malachi passage and establish its relevance to the surrounding discourse. Both readings recognize that writing and interpretation jointly define the passage's narrative function.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
