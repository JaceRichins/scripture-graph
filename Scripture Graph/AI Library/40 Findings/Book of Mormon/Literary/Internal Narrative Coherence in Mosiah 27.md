# Internal Narrative Coherence in Mosiah 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The angel's explicit instruction in Mosiah 27:16 to remember the earlier communal bondage-and-deliverance narrative, echoed in Alma's own bondage/deliverance vocabulary in vv.28-29, strongly supports deliberate final-form narrative coherence between the two episodes. Evidence_strength is revised to 0.78 -- within the strong band and modestly above the registered sibling bom-alma38-zoramite-narrative-coherence (0.72) -- correcting both the prior score's excess over the band ceiling and its reliance on a composite phrase not actually present in the text. This coherence is compatible with genuine ancient history, later editorial shaping, or modern literary composition, and gives only none-band support for the historicity of either underlying event.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
In Mosiah 27:16 the angel explicitly commands Alma to 'remember the captivity of thy fathers in the land of Helam, and in the land of Nephi... they were in bondage, and he has delivered them,' directly invoking the communal deliverance narrated in Mosiah 21:5 and 24:16-17. Alma's own account in vv.28-29 then describes his conversion with bondage-adjacent imagery -- 'redeemed from the gall of bitterness and bonds of iniquity' (v.29) and twice reports being 'snatched' by the Lord's mercy (vv.28, 29) -- echoing, not verbatim-repeating, the earlier deliverance vocabulary.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
This is a deliberately coordinated final-form pairing: an explicit command to remember a specific earlier episode, followed by the protagonist's own language echoing it. The echo is close enough to compare favorably with the registered bom-alma38-zoramite-narrative-coherence (0.72); the original 0.86 both exceeded the strong band's ceiling for evidence lacking independent external attestation and rested on an unattested composite phrase ('snatched... out of bondage'), which does not appear verbatim in either verse -- both corrected here.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compatible with genuine ancient theological reflection linking two real historical episodes, with later editorial shaping designed to draw the parallel, and with deliberate modern literary composition; the coherence itself does not distinguish these.
