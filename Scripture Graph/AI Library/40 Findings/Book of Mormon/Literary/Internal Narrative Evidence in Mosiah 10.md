# Internal Narrative Evidence in Mosiah 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
As internal narrative evidence, Mosiah 10 strongly supports the narrow conclusion that the text represents inherited grievance, parental teaching, and royal persuasion as mechanisms perpetuating and mobilizing group conflict. Zeniff reports, rather than omits, the Lamanite grievance tradition before rejecting it, so an authentic record and one-sided wartime framing are not mutually exclusive explanations. The account does not establish the historical accuracy of Zeniff's reconstruction or the validity of the grievances he dismisses.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 10:6,12,17-19 has Zeniff narrate King Laman stirring rebellion, the Lamanite tradition of grievance about being wronged in the wilderness (reported by Zeniff himself, though rejected as false), deliberate parental teaching of hatred, and King Laman's persuasion of Zeniff -- concluding with Zeniff mobilizing his own people to battle.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage presents a specific causal chain (inherited belief, taught hatred, royal cunning) as the explanation for renewed conflict, voiced entirely through Zeniff's own retrospective narration, which reports but reframes the Lamanite grievance tradition as false rather than omitting it.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compatible with an authentic historical account of real intergenerational grievance transmission that is also one-sided in Zeniff's favor, or with a literary construction illustrating how such conflicts perpetuate themselves; these are not mutually exclusive.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read as a piece of first-person wartime narration, Zeniff's account reports the Lamanites' own claimed grievance and then explains why he rejects it, before mobilizing his people around his version of events. That the mechanism (reported grievance, rejection, taught hatred, royal persuasion, mobilization) is narratively tidy is worth noting as a feature of the text's construction, distinct from and prior to any judgment about which side's grievances were historically justified.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports that the final text deliberately represents inherited grievance and persuasion as conflict-mobilization mechanisms, the same evidence class registered strong elsewhere (Alma 54 0.78, Alma 55 0.78). It does not establish the historical accuracy of Zeniff's reconstruction, the uniform beliefs of all Lamanites, or the validity of the Lamanite grievances Zeniff reports and rejects.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: how a wartime narrator frames a rival account he reports says nothing about whether the text is divinely inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the historical accuracy of Zeniff's reconstruction, the uniform beliefs of all Lamanites, or the validity of the Lamanite grievances Zeniff himself reports and dismisses as false tradition.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Authentic historical record, told from one interested side** (*plausible*) — predicts: A genuine participant's account that both reflects real events and frames them favorably to the narrator.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: The text presents itself as Zeniff's own first-person record and explicitly reports, then rejects, the rival Lamanite account.
- **Literary construction illustrating a conflict-transmission mechanism** (*possible*) — predicts: A clean causal chain (belief, teaching, persuasion, mobilization) shaped for narrative or didactic clarity.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: General narrative-craft technique; not independently evidenced beyond the passage's tidy structure.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Zeniff may simplify a complex conflict by attributing it primarily to inherited falsehoods. — *plausible*: A single interested narrator has reason to simplify a multi-causal conflict, even while accurately reporting the rival account he rejects.
- The account may employ conventional wartime framing that privileges the narrator's own people's history over the reported rival tradition. — *plausible*: Widely attested pattern in ancient and modern first-person war narratives.
- Material interests, political power, and territorial pressures may have contributed even though Zeniff emphasizes tradition. — *possible*: The text does not quantify these factors, so their relative weight cannot be checked from Mosiah 10 alone.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
One-sided grievance narratives in the Bible that nonetheless report the opposing side's claims before rejecting them receive the same treatment in this corpus: read as constructed conflict narration by one interested party, not as impartial history, matching the Internal Narrative Evidence convention applied to Alma 54 and Alma 55.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, discrimination 0.75) for the proposition: *Zeniff's account of inherited grievance, parental teaching, and royal persuasion in Mosiah 10 reflects the text's deliberate construction of a conflict-mobilization narrative mechanism, distinct from whether that account accurately and completely explains the historical causes of the conflict.*

Mosiah 10 is strong evidence that its final text deliberately represents inherited grievance and royal persuasion as mechanisms mobilizing conflict, but because a single interested narrator's account predicts the same reported-then-rejected structure whether or not it is historically complete, it gives only weak, non-discriminating support for that account being an accurate history of the conflict's causes.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Single-narrator wartime accounts that attribute conflict entirely to the other side's inherited hatred are common across ancient and modern historiography, which limits how diagnostic this pattern is for authenticity versus polemic versus literary construction.

What would move this: An independent account corroborating or contradicting Zeniff's grievance narrative would raise or lower confidence in its historical accuracy; internal admission by Zeniff of Nephite-side provocation beyond what is already narrated would strengthen the one-sided-framing reading.
