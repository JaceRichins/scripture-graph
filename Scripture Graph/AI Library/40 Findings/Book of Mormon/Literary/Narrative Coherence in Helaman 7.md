# Narrative Coherence in Helaman 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The tower, garden gate, market highway, passersby, and gathering crowd form one coherent account of how Nephi's private lament becomes a public sermon: visibility attracts travelers, their report spreads, and the crowd gathers.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Helaman 7:10 places Nephi on a tower in his garden beside a highway leading to Zarahemla's chief market and near the garden gate. Verse 11 describes passersby seeing his lament, reporting it to others, and a multitude gathering; verse 12 has Nephi rise and see the assembled crowd.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The spatial and social details form a coherent sequence: a nominally private lament occurs in a visible location connected to public traffic, passersby spread the report, and the resulting crowd becomes the audience for Nephi's sermon.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The scene illuminates the text's representation of urban movement and communication. A household or garden boundary adjoining a traveled route provides an interface between domestic space and the market-going public.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a spatial reconstruction, Nephi's garden occupies household space beside a road carrying traffic toward the chief market. Its tower and gate are visible from that route, allowing travelers to observe his conspicuous mourning, alert others, and create a crowd at the boundary between domestic and public space.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
