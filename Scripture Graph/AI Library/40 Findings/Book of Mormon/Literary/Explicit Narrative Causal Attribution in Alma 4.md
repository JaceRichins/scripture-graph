# Explicit Narrative Causal Attribution in Alma 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 4 explicitly portrays misconduct within the church as damaging its public witness — wickedness becomes a stumbling-block, halts the church's progress, and, in Alma's own view, encourages further wrongdoing among unbelievers. This connects the chapter's internal religious ethics to its account of wider communal consequences.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 4:10 calls the church's wickedness "a great stumbling-block" to those outside it and links it to the church's failure to progress. Verse 11 reports Alma's judgment that the church's example was leading unbelievers "from one piece of iniquity to another."
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The narrator and Alma treat the conduct of professed believers as affecting both the community's public credibility and the behavior of people outside it — an explicit internal moral and causal assessment, not an independently measured account of the unbelievers' own motives.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage illuminates the narrative's model of religious community life: institutional reputation, internal inequality, and visible misconduct are treated as having consequences beyond the offending members themselves.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the chapter's own account, prosperity produces pride and inequality within the church; that conduct becomes a stumbling-block to outsiders and contributes to wider social decline. Alma's subsequent decision to give up the judgment seat and devote himself to preaching responds directly to this narrated chain of cause and effect.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
