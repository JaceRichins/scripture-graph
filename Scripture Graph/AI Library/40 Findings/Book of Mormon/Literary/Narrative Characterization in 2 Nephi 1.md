# Narrative Characterization in 2 Nephi 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 1:24-27 preserves accusations that Nephi sought authority, spoke sharply, and demanded obedience, framed and answered entirely within Lehi's own defense of him. This characterizes leadership, motive, and manner of speech as fault lines in the narrated family, while remaining, by its very construction, a partisan account rather than an independently corroborated record of the dispute.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Lehi reports that Nephi's brothers have accused him of seeking power and authority, speaking sharply, becoming angry, and commanding their obedience (2 Nephi 1:25-26). Lehi answers each charge in turn, attributing Nephi's conduct to concern for their welfare, divine truth, God's power, and the Spirit's utterance (vv.25-27).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The speech characterizes an established narrative conflict over leadership, motive, and tone, and simultaneously characterizes Lehi as Nephi's defender: the accusations are named, but only as Lehi restates and reframes them, not as an independent account from the brothers themselves.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage gives the coming family division a developed narrative background rather than a sudden, unexplained rupture, and it reveals the competing categories through which authority is understood in the story — domination and anger from the reported accusation, service and divine commission from Lehi's reply.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
A useful reading of the narrated conflict traces repeated dependence on Nephi for survival and guidance, followed by resentment of his authority and manner of correction. In vv.24-27, Lehi responds as an involved, partisan patriarch rather than a neutral arbiter: he grants the substance of the complaints (commands, sharp speech) while disputing their motive. The scene thereby sets up the coming division by showing the parties already disagree about whether Nephi's authority is coercive or divinely commissioned, entirely through the voice of the son whose record preserves it.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
