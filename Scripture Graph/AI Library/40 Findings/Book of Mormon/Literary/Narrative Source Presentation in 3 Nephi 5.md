# Narrative Source Presentation in 3 Nephi 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 5:8-11 present severe compression, an identified intermediary record (Nephi's plates), and self-manufactured plates; 5:16-18 distinguish inherited from eyewitness material and add truth and linguistic-limitation claims. Together these strongly support deliberate construction of a self-conscious editorial persona for Mormon — the same evidence class already registered for Nephi in 1 Nephi 6 — with no independent traction on whether an actual ancient record-keeper stands behind it.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 5:8-18: the narrator states the book cannot contain a hundredth part of twenty-five years of events, identifies fuller records and Nephi's shorter intermediary account, names the plates of Nephi as his source, states he made his own plates, distinguishes inherited accounts from personally witnessed events, and asserts the record's truthfulness while noting linguistic limitation.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Together these notices construct a self-conscious editorial persona: an abridger who names sources, distinguishes source-based history from eyewitness reporting, explains compression and omission, and claims responsibility for the physical record.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage illuminates the final text's historiographical self-presentation. Its narrator claims material constraints, source succession, personal manufacture of the writing medium, temporal proximity to part of the narrative, and responsibility for selecting a much shorter account from more extensive records.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the record-keeper reconstruction presented by the text, Mormon compiles earlier accounts until reaching his own lifetime, then supplements them with eyewitness material while compressing a much larger archive onto plates he manufactured. As literary presentation, the same notices construct an editorial persona whose authority rests on named sources, proximity to events, declared selectivity, and awareness of the limits of his medium and language. The result is a carefully articulated model of abridged historiography within the narrative.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Bears only on final-form literary construction of an editorial persona, not on whether that persona corresponds to an actual ancient record-keeper.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Mormon's historical existence, the physical plates, the accuracy of the omitted history, or ancient authorship.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->
