# Internal Literary Evidence in Mosiah 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[Mosiah 3#^mosiah-3-5|Mosiah 3:5]] through [[Mosiah 3#^mosiah-3-10|Mosiah 3:10]] form a deliberately ordered prophecy covering Jesus's incarnation, ministry, suffering, named identity and mother, rejection, crucifixion, third-day resurrection, and judgment. The cluster securely establishes the final text's detailed pre-birth presentation, but it gives only none-band support for genuine pre-Christian prediction because revelation, later Christianizing redaction, loose translation, and nineteenth-century composition can all produce it. Independent pre-Christian dating would be needed for the details to carry substantial historical weight as foreknowledge.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 3:5–10 presents an ordered future-tense account in which the Lord descends among mortals, performs miracles and exorcisms, suffers bodily anguish, is named Jesus Christ and identifies his mother as Mary, is rejected, accused of having a devil, scourged, crucified, rises on the third day, and judges the world.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The final text deliberately constructs a coherent and unusually detailed prophecy of Jesus Christ. Its sequence gathers incarnation, ministry, passion, resurrection, and judgment into a compact Christian proclamation placed before Jesus's birth in the narrative.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage is compatible with genuine pre-Christian revelation, but its detailed Christology is also highly compatible with composition, redaction, or translation after Christian traditions were available. Internal future-tense narration establishes the book's claimed chronology, not the external date at which the prophecy's wording or contents originated.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Under the narrative's genuine-revelation model, the angel supplies King Benjamin with a compact preview of Jesus's mortal mission. Under an ancient-source model with later translation or redaction, the final Christian specificity can arise when older material is expressed through post-event language. Under nineteenth-century composition, the passage functions as a retrospective Christian proclamation cast in prophetic future tense. All three reconstructions explain the observed coherence; independent dating is therefore the load-bearing missing discriminator.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
If the pre-Christian date of the passage were independently established, the named and sequenced details would be significant evidence for unusual foreknowledge. Without such dating, the details provide none-band support for genuine pre-Christian prediction because post-Christian composition and Christianizing mediation predict them at least as readily. This recalibration from 0.30 to 0.12 aligns the note with the registry's analogous assessments of pre-advent prophecy in 2 Nephi 6 and Alma 39.

**Inspiration:** The datum by itself implies nothing determinative about inspiration: revelation is one possible theological interpretation, while historical analysis cannot infer divine origin from an internally assigned date.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The internal placement of the speech does not establish its pre-Christian external date, the occurrence of the angelic visitation, supernatural foreknowledge, ancient authorship, or the historical truth of the narrative frame.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
**What the text itself requires:** The narrative requires that King Benjamin receives and delivers these details before Christ's birth within the story world. It does not independently require that the surviving English wording be pre-Christian, that every detail was present at the earliest compositional stage, or that the narrative chronology is externally historical.

- **Genuine pre-Christian revelation** (*possible*) — predicts: A prophet could receive specific information about Jesus before the events, including names and an ordered account of ministry, death, and resurrection.. Fit with this finding: *supports*. Support for the model: The model is directly permitted by the narrative's angelic-disclosure claim, but this passage supplies no independent dating or external attestation.
- **Ancient core with later Christianizing redaction** (*possible*) — predicts: An earlier sermon or prophecy could acquire explicit Christian names and fulfillment details during a later compositional stage.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: Layered composition is a general mechanism in ancient textual transmission, although no manuscript layers independently document it here.
- **Ancient source rendered through loose or revelatory translation** (*plausible*) — predicts: A translator or revelatory process could express an ancient prophecy through later Christian terminology and harmonize it with familiar accounts of Jesus.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: The registry documents extensive biblical and KJV-shaped mediation elsewhere in the final English Book of Mormon, making translation-level harmonization a serious corpus-wide model.
- **Nineteenth-century Christian composition** (*plausible*) — predicts: A writer familiar with Christian scripture and preaching would readily produce a compact prophecy containing Jesus, Mary, miracles, rejection, crucifixion, third-day resurrection, and judgment.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: The English text arose in a nineteenth-century Christian environment where the relevant biblical narratives and vocabulary were readily available.

**Considered and set aside:**
- Specific detail by itself proves supernatural prediction — That inference assumes the pre-event date that the detail is being used to establish, making the argument circular without independent dating.
- The entire cluster is merely generic pre-Christian messianism — The names Jesus and Mary, crucifixion, and third-day resurrection are too specifically Christian for a generic-motif explanation to account for the passage adequately.
- An otherwise unattested pre-Christian source contained the complete cluster — This is possible in the abstract but ad hoc without a manuscript, quotation history, or other independent trace of the proposed source.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- The details were disclosed supernaturally before the events. — *possible*: This is the passage's own explanation, but the internal claim is not independent evidence for its external date or supernatural cause.
- The final wording reflects loose or revelatory translation that renders less explicit ancient material in familiar Christian language. — *plausible*: KJV-shaped mediation is well represented elsewhere in the corpus, although this mechanism cannot be verified for these verses individually.
- A later Christian redactor expanded an earlier prophetic core. — *possible*: The mechanism is historically ordinary in layered texts, but no surviving Book of Mormon manuscript tradition identifies such a layer.
- The passage was composed after the New Testament traditions it summarizes were available. — *plausible*: This straightforwardly explains the full cluster without requiring foreknowledge, and the relevant traditions were available before the Book of Mormon's English publication.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**None** (supports, discrimination 0.12) for the proposition: *Mosiah 3:5-10 preserves genuinely pre-Christian prophetic foreknowledge of the specific circumstances of Christ's earthly ministry, rather than details introduced through later Christianizing redaction, translation harmonization, or nineteenth-century composition.*

Mosiah 3:5–10 unquestionably presents a detailed pre-birth prophecy of Jesus within its narrative, but without independent pre-Christian dating that internal placement gives almost no discrimination between genuine foreknowledge and later Christian composition, redaction, or translation.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Compact summaries of Jesus's ministry, suffering, resurrection, and judgment are ordinary in post-event Christian discourse, so the cluster is highly expected under Christian composition or mediation. No matched pre-Christian control corpus or search denominator is supplied; the six adjacent verses were selected as a unit, reducing a look-elsewhere problem but not resolving the dating problem.

What would move this: The weight would rise sharply if a securely dated pre-Christian manuscript or independently traceable textual stratum contained this distinctive cluster, especially the names, crucifixion, and third-day resurrection. It would fall further if source analysis established that distinctive wording or sequencing depends on post-New Testament or specifically KJV forms unavailable to a pre-Christian source.
