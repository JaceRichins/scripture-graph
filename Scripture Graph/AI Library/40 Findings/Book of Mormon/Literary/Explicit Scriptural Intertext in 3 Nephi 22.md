# Explicit Scriptural Intertext in 3 Nephi 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The verse-for-verse correspondence between [[3 Nephi 22#^3ne-22-1|3 Nephi 22:1–17]] and [[Isaiah 54#^isa-54-1|Isaiah 54:1–17]], reinforced by the reference to what is written, strongly supports deliberate dependence on the Isaiah 54 textual tradition. Ancient quotation, KJV-shaped translation or harmonization, and direct modern reuse all accommodate that relationship, so this instance does not identify the historical mechanism. It belongs to the broader Isaiah-dependence issue and should not be counted independently from the other extended Isaiah quotations.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
[[3 Nephi 22#^3ne-22-1|3 Nephi 22:1–17]] corresponds verse for verse with [[Isaiah 54#^isa-54-1|Isaiah 54:1–17]], preserving the chapter’s sequence, imagery, argument, and much of its English wording. It begins by introducing the material as “that which is written.”
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter is an intentional quotation or close textual reuse of Isaiah 54. The chapter-length sequence and wording are much more readily explained by literary or traditional dependence than by independent composition.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The correspondence securely places 3 Nephi 22 within the Isaiah textual tradition. It is compatible with ancient quotation, translation into familiar biblical English, revelatory or editorial harmonization, and direct nineteenth-century reuse; the correspondence alone does not locate the dependence at one particular historical stage.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The strongest supportive conclusion is that the final text knowingly and extensively incorporates Isaiah 54 rather than merely sharing a few generic prophetic images. That conclusion is strong, but it does not separately favor an ancient Nephite quotation model over KJV-mediated translation or nineteenth-century composition. As one instance of the broader Isaiah-quotation phenomenon, it should not be counted as an independent authorship argument in addition to the canonical Isaiah-dependence issue.

**Inspiration:** Literary dependence on Isaiah, without evidence identifying how or why that dependence arose, implies nothing by itself about whether the Book of Mormon is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The correspondence does not establish the discourse’s date, the historical mechanism or direction of transmission, ancient Book of Mormon authorship, the existence of a particular Nephite Isaiah manuscript, or a unique fulfillment of Isaiah 54.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient Nephite quotation** — predicts: A speaker or record keeper with access to an Isaianic text quotes and applies Isaiah 54 at length.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **KJV-shaped translation of an ancient source** — predicts: An ancient quotation is rendered in English closely aligned with the familiar King James wording.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Revelatory or editorial harmonization** — predicts: The received English form is aligned with Isaiah during revelation, translation, or final textual shaping.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century composition** — predicts: A composer with access to the King James Bible reproduces Isaiah 54 and embeds it in the discourse.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Independent composition without Isaiah dependence** — predicts: Seventeen verses independently reproduce Isaiah’s distinctive language, images, and order.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- A historical speaker quoted an Isaianic text available within the narrative’s ancient textual tradition. — *possible*: The narrative provides access to Isaiah, and extended scriptural quotation is historically ordinary; this note supplies no independently recovered witness to the proposed Nephite source.
- An ancient quotation was rendered or harmonized into familiar King James language during translation. — *possible*: This predicts the English resemblance while retaining an ancient source, but the translation mechanism is not independently identified by this correspondence.
- A nineteenth-century composer directly reused Isaiah 54 from the King James Bible. — *plausible*: The KJV was readily available in the production environment, and direct reuse straightforwardly predicts the sustained English agreement.
- The resemblance arose independently without quotation, shared tradition, or textual reuse. — *ad hoc*: Independent coincidence does not credibly account for seventeen verses preserving the same progression and extensive distinctive wording.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *Book of Mormon Isaiah-quotation chapters deliberately depend on the Isaiah textual tradition rather than independently reproducing the same wording, sequence, and imagery.*

The chapter-length, ordered correspondence strongly supports deliberate dependence on the Isaiah 54 textual tradition, while leaving ancient quotation, translation-stage harmonization, and direct nineteenth-century reuse unresolved.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Brief biblical expressions and prophetic images are common, but verse-for-verse reproduction of an entire chapter in the same order is rare without quotation or shared textual ancestry. The proposed source and boundaries are constrained by the continuous chapter correspondence rather than selected from scattered matches. The Book of Mormon’s numerous Isaiah quotations nevertheless share a common source-access mechanism and cannot be multiplied as independent authorship evidence.

What would move this: Weight for dependence would rise toward the maximum if the earliest recoverable text preserved the full correspondence and a preregistered verse-level comparison against matched religious prose confirmed that such ordered agreement virtually never occurs without textual dependence. It would fall if the earliest recoverable Book of Mormon text lacked the passage or if systematic comparison showed that the apparent continuity resulted from selective matching of commonplace language. Distinctive non-KJV variants linked to an independently dated ancient Isaiah witness would chiefly discriminate among mechanisms rather than alter the already strong dependence finding. ## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Within the 3 Nephi discourse, Isaiah functions as authoritative scripture whose restoration imagery is applied to the covenant future being described. On an ancient-quotation reconstruction, the speaker cites an inherited Isaianic text; on translation or harmonization reconstructions, that quotation reaches the English text in familiar biblical wording; on a modern-composition reconstruction, Isaiah 54 supplies the passage directly. Every serious model involving access to Isaiah explains the intertext, while a no-dependence model does not.
