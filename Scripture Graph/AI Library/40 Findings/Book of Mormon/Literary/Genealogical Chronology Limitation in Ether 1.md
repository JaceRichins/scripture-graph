# Genealogical Chronology Limitation in Ether 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ether 1 alternates the specific expression "son of" with the broader "descendant of" in [[Ether 1#^ether-1-6|Ether 1:6]], [[Ether 1#^ether-1-16|Ether 1:16]], and [[Ether 1#^ether-1-23|Ether 1:23]]. Because "descendant" can span more than one generation, the genealogy does not supply an exact generation count; a responsible chronology must allow for possible compression at those links.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Ether 1 generally links names with "son of," but Ether 1:6, 1:16, and 1:23 instead call Ether, Aaron, and Morianton a "descendant of" an earlier figure. The text supplies no dates or generation counts for these links.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The broader term "descendant" permits, but does not require, one or more unlisted generations. Treating every named person as the immediate child of the preceding person is therefore not a secure basis for calculating an exact chronology.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Biblical and other premodern genealogies can be selective rather than exhaustive, so genealogical compression supplies an appropriate comparative context. That comparison establishes the plausibility of compression, not that Ether's particular English alternation identifies an ancient compositional practice or that any specific generation was omitted.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a compressed-genealogy reconstruction, "descendant of" marks links at which intermediate ancestors may be unlisted while the line of descent is preserved. On a complete-genealogy reconstruction, the same expression can describe an immediate child as stylistic variation. Both readings fit the wording, so a chronology based on Ether 1 should preserve a range rather than silently assign exactly one generation to every link.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
