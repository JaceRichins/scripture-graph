# Narrative Theological Evidence in Alma 62

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 62:41 places two responses to prolonged war side by side: many became hardened because of the war's length, while many others were softened by their afflictions and humbled themselves before God ([[Alma 62#^alma-62-41|62:41]]). The verse treats adversity as spiritually non-deterministic, directing attention to divergent human responses rather than presenting suffering as an automatic means of sanctification.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 62:41 attributes hardening to the war's exceedingly great length and, separately, attributes softening and humility to those same people's afflictions. Both outcomes are named as arising among the same war-weary population.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The verse portrays adversity as spiritually non-deterministic: comparable wartime suffering accompanies both hardening and humility rather than reliably producing one moral outcome. The wording is compatible either with affliction itself doing causal work or with adversity simply revealing pre-existing differences in disposition.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The statement reflects a common form of theological historiography that interprets a community's fate morally while acknowledging that people respond to shared circumstances in different ways. The verse's compressed summary leaves room for prior character, social context, and individual agency alongside the war itself.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
A useful reading takes the verse as the narrator's retrospective assessment of a population transformed in divergent ways by prolonged war, using balanced parallelism (hardened / softened) to make the contrast itself the point: shared adversity creates conditions in which responses diverge, rather than functioning as a mechanical instrument of spiritual improvement.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
