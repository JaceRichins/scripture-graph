# Behavior Under Lethal Coercion in Mosiah 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
In [[Mosiah 17#^mosiah-17-9|Mosiah 17:9]]–[[Mosiah 17#^mosiah-17-10|Mosiah 17:10]], Abinadi refuses to retract his message even at the cost of death, and [[Mosiah 17#^mosiah-17-20|Mosiah 17:20]] interprets his execution as the culmination of that fidelity. The episode therefore portrays him as sincere, steadfast, and willing to bear the ultimate cost for his testimony.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
In [[Mosiah 17#^mosiah-17-9|Mosiah 17:9]]–[[Mosiah 17#^mosiah-17-10|Mosiah 17:10]], Abinadi refuses to recall his words and declares his willingness to die rather than deny them. [[Mosiah 17#^mosiah-17-20|Mosiah 17:20]] reports his death by fire and explicitly interprets it as sealing his words through his refusal to deny God's commandments.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Within the narrative, Abinadi is characterized as unwaveringly committed to his message. His costly refusal functions as character evidence for sincerity and fidelity, while the narrator advances the further theological claim that his death seals the truth of his words.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The scene uses lethal coercion to distinguish expedient profession from costly commitment. It belongs to the recognizable narrative logic of martyrdom, in which steadfastness under threatened death establishes exemplary fidelity for the story's audience.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Within the scene's narrative logic, recalling his words would preserve Abinadi at the price of repudiating his commission. His refusal and execution consequently transform the confrontation from a dispute over claims into a model of fidelity under coercion, explaining why the account became central to the book's portrayal of prophetic witness.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
