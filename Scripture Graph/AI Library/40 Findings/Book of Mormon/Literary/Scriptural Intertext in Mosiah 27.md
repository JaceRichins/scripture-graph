# Scriptural Intertext in Mosiah 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mosiah 27:37 clusters publish-peace, good-tidings-of-good, and Lord-reigneth language in Isaiah 52:7's order, strongly supporting a deliberate scriptural echo in the final text. As a condensed single-verse paraphrase rather than a sustained quotation, it sits below the registry's chapter-length Isaiah-quotation ceiling and gives no independent traction on translation mechanism or authorship.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 27:37 clusters three phrases in the same order as Isaiah 52:7: 'publish peace,' 'good tidings of good,' and a declaration that 'the Lord reigneth' (vs. Isaiah's 'thy God reigneth'). This is a shorter, allusive echo, not a verbatim multi-verse quotation like Mosiah 12:21-24.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The ordered three-clause match is distinctive enough that independent convergence on the same wording and sequence is unlikely; the final text is applying recognizable Isaianic missionary-proclamation language to Alma's converted sons.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Consistent with a KJV-mediated final English text; compatible with an ancient underlying report phrased in translation using familiar biblical cadence, or with a modern author echoing a well-known verse.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the standard model of a KJV-mediated translation, familiar proclamation language from a heavily-quoted Isaiah verse resurfacing in an unrelated missionary-success narrative is unsurprising; the same fit is available under direct modern reuse.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Moderately-to-strongly supports deliberate scriptural echo in the final form, but the phrase is common missionary/proclamation idiom and the match is one verse, not a sustained quotation, so it supports the echo more than it discriminates any origin model.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: an echo of well-known scriptural phrasing says nothing about whether the surrounding narrative or doctrine is divinely inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the date, translation mechanism, or ancient versus modern authorship of Mosiah 27, nor that a specific prophecy is being consciously fulfilled rather than idiom being reused.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Independent convergence on common missionary/proclamation idiom without conscious Isaiah reuse. — *possible*: The phrases are short and could reflect generic biblical register rather than targeted echo.
- Translator supplied the Isaiah phrasing during rendering of a differently-worded original. — *plausible*: Consistent with the corpus's broader KJV-mediation pattern and not independently falsifiable from this verse alone.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
