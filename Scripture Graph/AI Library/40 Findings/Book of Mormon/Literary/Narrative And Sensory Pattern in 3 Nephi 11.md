# Narrative And Sensory Pattern in 3 Nephi 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 11 moves the crowd from unintelligible hearing through sight, touch, and collective declaration, culminating in a wound-touching scene that closely echoes John 20:27. This illuminates the chapter's deliberate emphasis on personal, embodied confirmation; the wording's relationship to John 20 and the historicity question raised by any large witness claim are both already carried elsewhere in the registry rather than rescored here.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
The chapter narrates escalating sensory confirmation: the multitude hears a voice it does not at first understand (v.3), deliberately attends until it understands the voice on a third hearing (vv.5-6), sees a man descend from heaven (v.8), and is invited to come forward one by one to feel the prints of the nails and the wound in his side, seeing and feeling for themselves before crying out together (vv.14-16). Verse 14's invitation to 'thrust your hands...into my side, and...feel the prints of the nails' echoes the wording of John 20:27's 'reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side.'
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The sequence moves from initial incomprehension through hearing, sight, and touch to shared declaration, making individual sensory confirmation the means by which the crowd arrives at collective recognition.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This is a recognizable ancient resurrection-appearance confirmation scene (compare Luke 24:39-43, John 20:20-27): a returning, physically present figure overcomes doubt through invited touch. The specific wording shared with John 20:27 is already addressed under bom-3nephi11-biblical-english-mediation and is not rescored here; the broader question of what a narrated collective witness claim establishes about historicity is likewise already carried at none-band by bom-3nephi7-collective-witness-claim and bom-3nephi17-collective-witness-claim, since all three scenes reach the reader through the same narrative source.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read as a type-scene, the passage fits the pattern found elsewhere in resurrection narratives: hearing gives way to seeing, seeing to touching, and touching to public testimony, each step overcoming the doubt or confusion of the step before. That progression accounts for the scene's structure regardless of the source of its English wording.
