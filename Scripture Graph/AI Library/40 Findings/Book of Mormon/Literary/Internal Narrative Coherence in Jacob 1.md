# Internal Narrative Coherence in Jacob 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Jacob's description of the small-plates/full-history division in Jacob 1:1-4 matches the same two-record rationale given earlier in 1 Nephi 6:3-6 and 1 Nephi 9:2-3, strongly supporting deliberate coordination of this narrative device across the text's final form. This coordination does not discriminate between an authentic ancient record system, careful single-author planning, and later editorial harmonization, and it does not by itself establish that physical plates or the described transfer of custody were historical.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Jacob 1:1–4 says that Nephi entrusted Jacob with the small plates, instructed him to preserve and transmit them, limited ordinary history to brief treatment, and prioritized sacred preaching, revelation, and prophecy. This continues the selective sacred-record policy described in 1 Nephi 6 and the two-record distinction in 1 Nephi 9.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Jacob's opening is both a succession notice and a restatement of the small plates' editorial policy. It carries custody, purpose, and narrative voice across the transition from Nephi to Jacob.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
These passages create a continuous narrated framework of record selection, custody, and succession. The continuity makes Jacob's role and the book's changing first-person voice intelligible within its record world, whether that framework reflects inherited records, editorial shaping, or planned literary construction.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On an inherited-record reconstruction, Jacob accepts both an archive and Nephi's selection policy. On an editorial or planned-literary reconstruction, the opening preserves the established distinction between the selective sacred record and fuller history while authorizing a new narrator. In either reconstruction, Jacob 1 serves as the hinge carrying the record's custody and purpose into the next book.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
