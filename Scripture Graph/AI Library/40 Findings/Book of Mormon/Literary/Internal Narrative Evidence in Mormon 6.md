# Internal Narrative Evidence in Mormon 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mormon 6:10-12 adopts a strongly situated survivor perspective: the narrator reports being wounded and passed over, names Moroni among twenty-four survivors, and describes surveying the dead from Cumorah the next day. This is strong evidence of deliberate eyewitness-style narrative construction, matching the corpus's Internal Narrative Evidence class, but it gives only none-band support for the account's external historicity, since a crafted literary voice predicts the identical detail.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mormon 6:10-12 has the first-person narrator report being wounded and passed over by the attackers, name twenty-four survivors including his son Moroni, and describe surveying the ten thousand dead from the top of Cumorah the following day.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage supplies specific sensory and positional detail (wounded, passed over, hilltop vantage, next-day timing) characteristic of a deliberately constructed eyewitness voice, independent of whether the underlying event occurred.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Consistent with an authentic survivor's memoir, a literary narrator built to read as a survivor, or a later retrospective account shaped for dramatic effect; internal detail alone cannot distinguish these.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read purely as narrative craft, this passage does what situated survivor accounts do in any tradition: ground the reader in one body, one vantage point, one day-after view of a battlefield. That construction is worth noting as a deliberate literary choice in its own right, independent of whether the underlying battle is historical, which the passage cannot settle by itself.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports that the final text deliberately constructs a situated eyewitness narrator, the same evidence class already registered strong elsewhere in the corpus (Alma 54 0.78, Alma 55 0.78, Mosiah 26 0.9). This is a distinct and much weaker claim than the battle's external historicity.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: a well-constructed eyewitness voice says nothing about whether its content is divinely inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish that the battle occurred, that Mormon and Moroni are historical figures, or that any specific reported detail is historically exact; eyewitness-style narration is produced by a crafted literary voice as readily as by an actual eyewitness.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- A literary narrative can simulate an eyewitness viewpoint without a real underlying eyewitness. — *plausible*: Fully sufficient to produce the same detail.
- The details reflect retrospective narrative shaping for theological or dramatic emphasis. — *possible*: Plausible but unverifiable from the text alone.
- Internal specificity independently verifies the event occurred. — *ad hoc*: Specificity is exactly what a crafted narrative voice would also produce; it cannot on its own verify occurrence.
