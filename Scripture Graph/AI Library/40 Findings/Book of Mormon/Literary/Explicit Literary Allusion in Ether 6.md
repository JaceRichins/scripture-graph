# Explicit Literary Allusion in Ether 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ether 6 explicitly compares the Jaredite vessels to Noah's ark, using the familiar ark image to explain their protection amid surrounding waters and to frame the voyage as divinely sustained communal deliverance.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Ether 6:7 explicitly says that the Jaredite vessels were "tight like unto the ark of Noah" while describing their protection when surrounded and submerged by water.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The comparison invites readers to understand the vessels through the familiar ark image of a divinely preserved community carried through overwhelming waters. Its immediate point is watertightness, while the surrounding voyage develops preservation and deliverance.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The final text places the Jaredite voyage within the biblical flood-and-ark tradition, giving readers a scriptural analogy for the vessels' function and the travelers' dependence upon God.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
The comparison connects an unfamiliar vessel narrative to a familiar scriptural pattern: a sealed conveyance preserves a community through destructive waters while its occupants rely upon God. The immediate analogy explains tightness, and the larger voyage develops the pattern of communal deliverance.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
