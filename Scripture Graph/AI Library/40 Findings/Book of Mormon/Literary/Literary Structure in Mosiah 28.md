# Literary Structure in Mosiah 28

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mosiah 28 marks two narrative threads for later treatment: the mission of the sons of Mosiah and the newly translated record of a destroyed people. The notices in [[Mosiah 28#^mosiah-28-9|Mosiah 28:9]] and [[Mosiah 28#^mosiah-28-19|Mosiah 28:19]] orient readers toward the later accounts beginning at [[Alma 17#^alma-17-1|Alma 17:1]] and [[Ether 1#^ether-1-1|Ether 1:1]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 28 twice announces that an account will be supplied later: verse 9 postpones the proceedings of the sons of Mosiah among the Lamanites, and verse 19 postpones the record Mosiah had just translated by the gift and power of God using the interpreters (vv.11-17). The later narrative resumes the sons' mission at Alma 17:1 and opens the translated record's contents, Moroni's abridgment of the Jaredite history, at Ether 1:1.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The two notices function as prospective narrative signposts, telling the reader that a storyline or record introduced here will reappear later in a specific, identifiable place rather than being narrated on the spot.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Such signposting is ordinary in sustained narrative and compilation. It is compatible with advance planning by a single composer, editorial arrangement of inherited accounts, or later insertion of transitions once the larger sequence had taken shape.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a compilation reading, Mosiah 28 is a junction where two threads -- the sons' mission and the newly translated Jaredite record -- are introduced but deferred so the present narrative can continue; Alma 17 and Ether 1 later reopen them, making the postponement notices a practical roadmap through the book's final form.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
