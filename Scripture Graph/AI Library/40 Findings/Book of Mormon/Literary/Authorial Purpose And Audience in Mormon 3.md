# Authorial Purpose And Audience in Mormon 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[Mormon 3#^morm-3-16|Mormon 3:16]]–[[Mormon 3#^morm-3-22|Mormon 3:22]] repeatedly identifies the intended readers and explains why the narrator writes to them. These addresses frame the history as a witness intended to persuade Gentiles, Israel, the remnant, and ultimately all humanity to believe, repent, and prepare for judgment.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mormon 3:16 identifies the narrator as a witness; verses 17–20 repeatedly say "I write" while naming Gentiles, Israel, the remnant, and ultimately everyone as the audience. Verses 20–22 expressly connect the writing with judgment, belief in Christ, repentance, and preparation.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The repeated addresses organize the passage as a deliberate appeal to future readers. The narrator presents the surrounding history as witness-bearing material selected for theological and moral persuasion, not as an uninterpreted military chronicle.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage securely reveals the communicative design of Mormon 3 in its final form: narrated events are subordinated to an explicit program of witness, covenant identity, judgment, and repentance.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a final-form literary reading, verses 16–22 function as an editorial address within the military narrative. The shift from what Mormon saw to why he writes shows how the book asks readers to understand the narrated wars: they are being presented as part of a theological testimony directed beyond the story's immediate participants.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
