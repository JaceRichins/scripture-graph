# Internal Literary Coherence in Alma 25

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 25 explicitly recalls Abinadi's Mosiah 17 predictions in closely matching language, marking deliberate final-form integration. This strongly supports intentional narrative coordination, but because prediction and fulfillment share one continuous source, it gives only none-band support for genuine pre-event prophecy or historicity.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 25:9-12 explicitly names Abinadi and declares his words 'verified,' closely echoing Mosiah 17:15-18's death-by-fire, scattering, and 'driven and slain by wild beasts' language.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The correspondence is not incidental background reuse but an explicit, verse-level callback naming Abinadi and quoting his prediction's substance, marking deliberate cross-reference between the two passages in the received text.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This kind of explicit internal cross-reference is compatible with genuine fulfilled prophecy transmitted faithfully, with an editor shaping the later account to echo the earlier one, or with a single author/redactor composing both in a planned sequence.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Ancient historiography regularly closes a narrative arc by explicitly noting that an earlier prophet's words 'came to pass' in specific, matching language (the Deuteronomistic history does this repeatedly, e.g. 1 Kings 13 and its fulfillment in 2 Kings 23). Alma 25's callback to Mosiah 17 fits that same historiographic convention: a plausible reconstruction is that the Nephite record-keeper (Mormon, abridging Alma's account) is deliberately using this well-attested prophecy-fulfillment framing device to structure the narrative, exactly as biblical editors did with their own received material -- which accounts for the tight verbal match without requiring, on this evidence alone, that the underlying prediction was genuinely made in advance.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The clustered, close verbal correspondence strongly supports reading the two passages as a deliberately coordinated final-form unit, matching the registry's comparably strong internal prophecy-fulfillment issues (e.g. bom-alma16-prophecy-fulfillment-correspondence, 0.84). Because prediction and fulfillment reach the reader through one continuous narrative source, this gives only none-band support for genuine pre-event prophecy, the historicity of the described persecution, or ancient provenance.

**Inspiration:** Nothing about inspiration follows from the correspondence itself; a single-source fulfillment notice is predicted equally by real prophecy, editorial shaping, and retrospective composition.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish that Abinadi made a historical prediction, that the reported fulfillment occurred as described, or the record's ancient provenance.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Authentic prophecy later fulfilled and recorded** — predicts: a later notice recalling the earlier prediction in matching terms. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Later redaction shaping the fulfillment notice to match** — predicts: the same close verbal echo, produced editorially after the fact. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Retrospective/unified composition of both passages** — predicts: the same coordinated pair, since one author writing both would naturally match wording. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- A single later author could construct both prediction and fulfillment as one planned narrative. — *plausible*
- An editor could shape the later account to echo an inherited earlier passage. — *plausible*
- The retrospective description could be formulated from the earlier prophecy's wording rather than independent memory. — *plausible*
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A biblical fulfillment notice explicitly recalling an earlier prophet's specific words (1 Kings 13's prophecy and its fulfillment in 2 Kings 23:16-18) would be treated the same way: strong evidence of deliberate coordination, none-band evidence of genuine advance accuracy, since one source transmits both halves.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Explicit 'thus the word was fulfilled' notices tying a later event back to a named earlier prophecy are a common and recognizable feature of both ancient historiography (e.g., Kings/Chronicles) and constructed narrative; the pattern itself does not discriminate between them.
