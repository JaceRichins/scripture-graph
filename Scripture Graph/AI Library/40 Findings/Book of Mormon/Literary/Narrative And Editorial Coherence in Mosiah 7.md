# Narrative And Editorial Coherence in Mosiah 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mosiah 7 frames the retrospective history of Mosiah 9-19 by compressing Zeniff's settlement, Lamanite deception, tribute, prophetic preaching, and the prophet's death into Limhi's account of his people's present bondage. Compare [[Mosiah 7#^mosiah-7-9|Mosiah 7:9]], [[Mosiah 7#^mosiah-7-21|Mosiah 7:21]], [[Mosiah 7#^mosiah-7-22|Mosiah 7:22]], [[Mosiah 7#^mosiah-7-26|Mosiah 7:26]], and [[Mosiah 7#^mosiah-7-28|Mosiah 7:28]] with [[Mosiah 9#^mosiah-9-10|Mosiah 9:10]], [[Mosiah 11#^mosiah-11-20|Mosiah 11:20]], [[Mosiah 17#^mosiah-17-20|Mosiah 17:20]], and [[Mosiah 19#^mosiah-19-15|Mosiah 19:15]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Limhi's speech in Mosiah 7 compresses several elements later narrated at greater length: Zeniff's settlement and kingship, King Laman's deceptive grant of land, the tribute of one half, a prophet's preaching, and that prophet's execution. Mosiah 9-17 and 19 supply the corresponding episodes, including the verbatim phrase 'cunning and craftiness of king Laman' at 9:10, and identify Limhi's unnamed prophet as Abinadi.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Mosiah 7 functions as a narrative frame and preview for the retrospective history that follows: Limhi's present-tense predicament gives readers the outcome and his theological interpretation before the narrative turns back to Zeniff's earlier perspective and unfolds the intervening events.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Retrospective framing of this kind is compatible with a compiler arranging inherited accounts, a later editor harmonizing wording, or a single composer controlling the story's disclosure; it clarifies the narrative's temporal architecture without requiring one particular production sequence.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a compilation reading, Limhi's account of present bondage creates the question the Zeniff-Noah-Abinadi narrative then answers. Mosiah 7 supplies the dynastic setting, exploitation, tribute, prophetic condemnation, and death; Mosiah 9-19 then narrates how those conditions arose, converting Limhi's exposition into an extended retrospective account, with the tribute detail carried through to Mosiah 19:15.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
