# Literary Coherence in Ether 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Across Ether 3–4, the veil moves from the barrier crossed by the brother of Jared to a figure for the unbelief later audiences must overcome. This development connects the vision with its application to readers and makes the earlier encounter a model for receiving revelation.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Ether 3 describes the veil being removed from the brother of Jared's eyes and his entry into divine presence through faith and knowledge. Ether 4 recalls his experience as a model for future revelation and identifies later audiences' unbelief, hardness of heart, and blindness as a veil that must be rent.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The narrative develops the veil from the barrier crossed by the brother of Jared into a metaphor for the unbelief separating later readers from revelation. Ether 4 thereby interprets the earlier vision and directs its imagery toward the audience.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The development binds the vision narrative to the ensuing exhortation and shows how the final text transforms a narrated encounter into a reusable model of faith, revelation, and spiritual perception.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Ether 4 explicitly returns to the brother of Jared's experience and applies its logic to later readers: as his faith brought him within the veil, they are called to rend a "veil of unbelief" so hidden revelations may be unfolded. The developing image forms the literary bridge from narrated vision to direct exhortation.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
