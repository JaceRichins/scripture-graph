# Explicit Internal Intertext in Alma 36

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 36:22 explicitly and deliberately recalls Lehi's throne vision from 1 Nephi 1:8, making intentional literary cohesion in the final text nearly certain. Steelmanning the critical reading, this kind of clean, explicit cross-reference is at least as easy — arguably easier — to produce under single or collaborative modern authorship as under any real ancient transmission chain, so it gives essentially no independent support to ancient origin and should not be read as corroborating antiquity even as a minor data point.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 36:22 explicitly says "even as our father Lehi saw" and closely recalls the throne-and-angels sequence in 1 Nephi 1:8. The named comparison establishes deliberate literary recall in the received text.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Alma's conversion vision is intentionally placed within the visionary tradition inaugurated by Lehi, linking Alma's personal deliverance with the book's foundational prophetic experience.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The callback shows deliberate final-form coordination between widely separated narratives and presents later Nephite religious experience as being interpreted through remembered ancestral records.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->
## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a family-record reconstruction, Alma knows Lehi's account and understands his own experience through that inherited prophetic narrative. On an editorial reconstruction, the compiler preserves or sharpens the comparison to connect two major conversion-and-commission scenes, giving the book a continuous vocabulary of prophetic encounter.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
