# Internal Epistemology in Alma 32

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 32 stages the 'experiment upon the word' as a gradual, self-assessed process and is candid that the resulting knowledge is partial. But its own stated failure condition (unbelief, v.28) makes the test hard to disconfirm from the inside, and ordinary confirmation-bias mechanisms predict the same reported inward changes about as well as genuine discernment does. This gives only none-band, non-discriminating support for the process being independently reliable evidence beyond what a believer's own desire and social context would produce.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 32:27 invites 'an experiment upon my words' beginning from mere desire to believe; vv.28-36 describe the word 'swelling,' 'enlarging,' and 'enlightening' the understanding as signs the seed is good, while v.28 attributes the seed being 'cast out' specifically to the hearer's own unbelief; v.34 explicitly limits the resulting knowledge ('your knowledge is perfect in that thing'); full fruit arrives only later (vv.41-42) through sustained diligence, faith, and patience.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The text stages a gradual, participant-evaluated experience rather than a controlled test, and is self-aware about the partiality of its own early-stage knowledge claim.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Bears on how the passage should be read as an epistemology, not on any historical or compositional question; compatible with either an ancient or modern origin.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read against known first-person accounts of religious conversion generally, the described sequence (desire, noticed inward change, sustained cultivation, delayed full confirmation) is a familiar structure that appears across traditions independent of any one tradition's truth. On that reconstruction Alma 32 is best read as an honest, self-aware description of lived religious experience rather than as a claim to have devised an independently verifying test.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Critics have long argued this structure is functionally unfalsifiable: any perceived inward change counts as confirmation, and any absence of effect is attributed to the hearer's own unbelief rather than to the proposition being false (v.28), so the method cannot in principle fail on its own terms. Because ordinary expectation and confirmation-bias effects predict the same self-reported 'swelling' and 'enlightening' about as well as genuine spiritual discernment would, and the text supplies no way to distinguish the two from inside the experience, this gives only none-band support for the proposition that the process independently verifies truth.

**Inspiration:** Nothing follows about whether the text is divinely inspired; a self-aware but confirmation-susceptible epistemology is a textual-rhetorical feature available to any author, ancient or modern.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish that the reported effects have a supernatural cause, that the method discriminates against a naturalistic-psychological explanation, or that it verifies any specific doctrinal proposition a hearer applies it to. Textual self-awareness about partial knowledge (v.34) is a rhetorical feature available to any author and does not itself answer the falsifiability critique.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Genuine spiritual/epistemic discernment** — predicts: inward swelling and enlightenment tracking the actual truth of the tested proposition. Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
- **Expectation/confirmation-bias account** — predicts: the same reported inward effects, driven by prior desire to believe and self-assessed, not externally checked, criteria. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Mixed account** — predicts: the same surface effects with any genuine signal unrecoverable from the text alone. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Expectation and confirmation effects produce the described inward changes, and the method's own failure condition (v.28's 'unbelief') forecloses disconfirmation. — *plausible*: The passage's stated criteria for success (felt swelling/enlightening) and for failure (the hearer's unbelief) are exactly what a self-sealing confirmation loop would predict.
- Social belonging or persuasive rhetoric contributes to the reported experience. — *plausible*: Alma addresses a socially marginalized group (excluded from synagogues), for whom belonging to a new community carries independent motivational pull.
- General ethical practice yields real benefits without verifying the specific theological claims tied to it. — *plausible*: Moral improvement and truth-verification are logically separable.
- The described effects do track a genuine, discernible spiritual reality. — *possible*: Cannot be excluded by literary analysis alone, but the passage's own careful staging does not supply independent verification distinguishing it from confirmation bias.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A structurally identical 'trust the process, judge by inward fruits, blame failure on the participant's own unbelief' appeal in another tradition would be read by outside observers as an ordinary confirmation-susceptible, unfalsifiable-by-design practice, not evidence for that tradition's specific truth-claims; the same standard applies here.
