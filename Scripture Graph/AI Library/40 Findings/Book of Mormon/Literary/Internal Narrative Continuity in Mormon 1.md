# Internal Narrative Continuity in Mormon 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ammaron's concealment of the archive and instructions to Mormon develop into a coherent sequence of succession, record-making, and emergency preservation. Mormon first takes the plates of Nephi as instructed and later removes the entire collection when the Lamanite advance threatens Shim. **Scripture:** [[4 Nephi 1#^4ne-1-48|4 Nephi 1:48]], [[Mormon 1#^morm-1-2|Mormon 1:2]], [[Mormon 1#^morm-1-3|Mormon 1:3]], [[Mormon 1#^morm-1-4|Mormon 1:4]], [[Mormon 2#^morm-2-17|Mormon 2:17]], [[Mormon 2#^morm-2-18|Mormon 2:18]], [[Mormon 4#^morm-4-23|Mormon 4:23]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
4 Nephi 1:48 reports Ammaron concealing the sacred records. Mormon 1:2–4 then has Ammaron commission the young Mormon to retrieve the plates of Nephi at age twenty-four, add his observations, and leave the other records at Shim. Mormon 2:17–18 reports Mormon carrying out the first part of those instructions, while Mormon 4:23 reports his later removal of the entire archive when military defeat threatened it.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
These references form a deliberate archival through-line: concealment, succession planning, partial retrieval and record-making, and finally emergency recovery of the larger archive.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The sequence makes the final narrative's account of record custody and editorial succession internally intelligible. It also distinguishes the plates on which Mormon writes from the larger archive he initially leaves at Shim.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the narrative's own reconstruction, Ammaron responds to instability by preserving the archive and designating a successor; Mormon first retrieves only the working record needed for his own history and later evacuates the whole collection when the land is about to be overrun. The several notices therefore describe successive stages of one record-preservation process rather than duplicate retrievals.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
