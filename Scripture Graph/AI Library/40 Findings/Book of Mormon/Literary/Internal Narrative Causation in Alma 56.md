# Internal Narrative Causation in Alma 56

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 56 places divine favor alongside provisions, reinforcements, stratagem, forced movement, a timely rear attack, renewed allied action, and encirclement. Its account makes logistics and battlefield decisions integral to the victory while allowing Helaman to understand those ordinary means within a providential framework.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 56 attributes Nephite preservation to the Lord while also narrating provisioning, reinforcements, deception, forced marches, pursuit, Helaman's return, a rear attack, enemy reorientation, renewed allied action, and encirclement.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
A coherent reading treats the chapter's providential interpretation and its ordinary military explanations as concurrent rather than mutually exclusive. Divine favor does not make logistics or tactics narratively irrelevant; preservation occurs amid decisions, supplies, movement, timing, and battlefield positioning.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The chapter exemplifies religious historical narration in which ordinary causal mechanisms and divine governance operate at different explanatory levels. Its causal chain also clarifies that the victory is not narrated as resulting from the young soldiers' faith alone.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the chapter's providential reconstruction, preservation, timely supplies, reinforcements, and tactical opportunities are means through which divine favor operates. On a military reconstruction, deception, divided attention, rapid movement, a rear assault, renewed allied action, and encirclement explain the reversal. Alma 56 accommodates both levels by supplying tactical causes while Helaman assigns the outcome theological meaning.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
