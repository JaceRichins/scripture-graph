# Internal Verbal And Thematic Parallel in Mosiah 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mosiah 5:8 closely reprises Mosiah 3:17's teaching that salvation comes through no other name. In the covenant scene, that earlier doctrine becomes an enacted commitment as the people take upon themselves the name of Christ. **Scripture:** [[Mosiah 3#^mosiah-3-17|Mosiah 3:17]], [[Mosiah 5#^mosiah-5-8|Mosiah 5:8]]
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 3:17 teaches that no other name, way, or means brings salvation except the name of Christ. Mosiah 5:8 resumes the same exclusive formula—"There is no other name given whereby salvation cometh"—and applies it to the people taking Christ's name upon themselves in covenant.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The later verse deliberately recalls a central assertion from Benjamin's sermon and converts it into covenantal application: the saving name proclaimed in the sermon becomes the name assumed by the responding people.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The repetition clarifies the literary and ritual movement of the episode. Mosiah 5 is not an isolated covenant scene; its language answers and enacts the teaching delivered in Mosiah 3.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the passage's literary reconstruction, Benjamin first identifies Christ's name as the exclusive means of salvation and then uses the people's covenant response to show what accepting that teaching entails. The verbal reprise binds proclamation and response together and makes the covenant scene the practical culmination of the sermon.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
