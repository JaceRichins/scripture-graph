# English Verbal And Sequential Correspondence in Moroni 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The ordered baptismal criteria in [[Moroni 6#^moro-6-1|Moroni 6:1]]–[[Moroni 6#^moro-6-3|Moroni 6:3]] closely correspond to [[D&C 20#^dc-20-37|Doctrine and Covenants 20:37]], strongly supporting deliberate reuse or a proximate shared English source. Doctrine and Covenants 20 may reuse Moroni, both may reflect the same producer or ecclesiastical formula, or a revelatory model may account for stable diction; the correspondence alone does not choose among them.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
[[Moroni 6#^moro-6-1|Moroni 6:1]]–[[Moroni 6#^moro-6-3|Moroni 6:3]] and [[D&C 20#^dc-20-37|Doctrine and Covenants 20:37]] share an ordered cluster of baptismal qualifications: coming with a broken heart and contrite spirit, witnessing before the church that one has truly repented of all sins, taking or being willing to take Christ's name, and demonstrating determination to serve him to the end. Doctrine and Covenants 20 expands the sequence but preserves its principal vocabulary and order.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The density and order of the shared criteria strongly favor deliberate reuse or a proximate shared linguistic source over independent assembly from generic Christian vocabulary. The wording alone does not establish which text or production stage supplied the formulation.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The correspondence links two early Restoration formulations of baptismal admission and shows that their English ecclesiastical language belongs to the same immediate textual environment. It is relevant to the development and reuse of Restoration ritual language.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
The two passages present substantially the same initiation pattern in nearly the same sequence, with Doctrine and Covenants 20 expanding the formulation for church governance. On an ancient-Moroni reconstruction, the later revelation or church document adopts the translated record as a norm; on a modern-composition reconstruction, common producer involvement or an emerging ecclesiastical formula accounts for both. The shared English is therefore highly informative about textual proximity but only weakly informative about Moroni's antiquity.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The cluster strongly supports an English textual relationship. It is compatible with Doctrine and Covenants 20 deliberately adopting Moroni as an authoritative church pattern, with the same revelatory diction operating in both, or with Joseph Smith's compositional habits shaping both texts; consequently it offers little discrimination between ancient-origin and modern-origin accounts of Moroni.

**Inspiration:** A textual relationship is compatible with both revelatory and ordinary compositional processes and therefore does not independently establish or challenge inspiration.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The parallel does not establish dependence direction, whether Moroni's underlying material is ancient, whether the shared language was revealed, or whether one person composed both passages.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
**What the text itself requires:** Each passage requires repentance, contrition, public witness, commitment to Christ, and perseverance as baptismal qualifications. Neither passage identifies the other as its source or explains whether the common wording arose through revelation, reuse, translation, shared practice, or common authorship.

- **Ancient Moroni translated first, followed by deliberate D&C reuse** (*plausible*) — predicts: The later church instruction adopts Moroni's baptismal criteria and much of its wording as an authoritative ecclesiastical pattern.. Fit with this finding: *supports*. Support for the model: The ordinary English production sequence places Moroni's wording before Doctrine and Covenants 20 and makes subsequent reuse available.
- **Joseph Smith's compositional participation in both texts** (*plausible*) — predicts: A stable cluster of preferred baptismal language recurs when the same producer formulates related ecclesiastical requirements.. Fit with this finding: *supports*. Support for the model: Joseph Smith participated in producing both English texts, providing an economical proximate source for recurring diction.
- **Independent revelations using stable revealed diction** (*possible*) — predicts: Closely related divine instructions may be expressed through the same ordered vocabulary without ordinary literary borrowing.. Fit with this finding: *consistent*. Support for the model: This accords with a believing account of both texts, but the verbal match does not independently verify revelation as the mechanism.
- **Shared early Restoration ecclesiastical formula** (*possible*) — predicts: Both texts draw upon an already circulating formulation of baptismal qualifications.. Fit with this finding: *not ruled out*. Support for the model: Formulaic church language can circulate orally or in drafts, although no specific independent source for the complete sequence is identified here.
- **Independent use of biblical and revival-era vocabulary** (*plausible*) — predicts: Individual elements such as repentance, contrition, witness, and perseverance recur, but the full ordered cluster is less expected.. Fit with this finding: *some difficulty*. Support for the model: The component concepts were broadly available in Christian discourse, though their availability does not fully explain the sequence.

**Considered and set aside:**
- Reverse direct dependence of the originally dictated Moroni passage on the later D&C text — The ordinary production sequence runs from Moroni's English text to Doctrine and Covenants 20; reverse dependence would require otherwise evidenced later revision or retrojection.
- Coincidental assembly from generic Christian vocabulary alone — The common vocabulary explains components but not economically the full density, close phrasing, and preserved sequence.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Doctrine and Covenants 20 deliberately reused Moroni's baptismal wording as an authoritative pattern. — *plausible*: The production order permits this economical direction, and the later passage expands rather than merely duplicates the criteria.
- Joseph Smith's own stable religious vocabulary produced both formulations. — *plausible*: Common producer involvement readily explains both the close wording and adaptation to a new document.
- Both passages independently express the same revealed requirements through divinely stable language. — *possible*: This fits a revelatory model but is not independently distinguishable from reuse or common authorship by the wording itself.
- Both drew from a shared oral, manuscript, or ecclesiastical formula. — *possible*: A common proximate source would explain the correspondence, but none is identified in the supplied evidence.
- Generic biblical and revival-era baptismal language independently produced the entire match. — *possible*: It explains individual elements better than it explains their density, close wording, and preserved order.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
