# Narrative Outcome in Mosiah 29

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mosiah 29:43–44 presents Alma's righteous administration and the land's continual peace as the favorable beginning of the reign of the judges. The transition is narrated through the character of its first chief judge and a concise report of public order.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 29:43 describes Alma as keeping God's commandments, judging righteous judgments, and presiding during "continual peace through the land"; verse 44 identifies this as the commencement of the reign of the judges and Alma as the first chief judge.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The narrator closes the political transition by presenting Alma's initial administration favorably and connecting public peace with the conduct of the first chief judge.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The notice illuminates the narrative's evaluation of political change through concise reports about an officeholder's conduct and the condition of the land.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
A plausible narrative reconstruction treats verses 43–44 as a transition notice: after Mosiah's constitutional argument and the people's acceptance of judges, the narrator summarizes the favorable beginning of Alma's administration before commencing the history of the new era. The notice evaluates that beginning, particularly Alma's administration, rather than offering a comprehensive appraisal of the judgeship's later history.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
