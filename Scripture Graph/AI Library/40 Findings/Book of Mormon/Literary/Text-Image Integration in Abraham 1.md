# Text-Image Integration in Abraham 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Abraham 1:12–14 explicitly directs readers to figures at the beginning of the record for visual information about the altar and named gods ([[Abraham 1#^abr-1-12|Abraham 1:12]], [[Abraham 1#^abr-1-13|1:13]], [[Abraham 1#^abr-1-14|1:14]]). The presented work thus asks its audience to read prose and image together: the narrative supplies an interpretation, and the figures supply the referenced visual forms. This integrated reading strategy is present whether the association originated with an ancient record, later editing, or composition around an available image.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Abraham 1:12 directs readers to a representation at the beginning of the record for knowledge of the altar, and verse 14 directs them to figures there for the appearance of the named gods.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
In its presented form, Abraham 1 treats prose and figures as an integrated communicative unit: the prose narrates and labels, while the figures are invoked as visual supplementation.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The cross-references establish the reading relationship that the final text assigns to Abraham 1 and its accompanying figures. Questions about the figures' original Egyptian function, antiquity, or historical connection to Abraham are separate disputed issues.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On an originally integrated-record reconstruction, the references preserve an author's directions to an illustration belonging with the narrative. On an editorial or nineteenth-century compositional reconstruction, prose was written or shaped to interpret an available image. Both reconstructions explain why verses 12–14 require readers to move between verbal description and visual representation as part of reading the presented work.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
