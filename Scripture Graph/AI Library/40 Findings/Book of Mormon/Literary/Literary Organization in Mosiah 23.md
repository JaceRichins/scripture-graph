# Literary Organization in Mosiah 23

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mosiah 23 recalls the people's deliverance from Noah as a lesson about trusting God under testing (vv.1-2, 12-13, 21-24), immediately before a new Lamanite captivity begins under Amulon (vv.25-39) that the record resolves only in Mosiah 24. The shared vocabulary of liberty, bondage, trust, and deliverance links the chapter's two episodes without describing the Amulonite bondage as already ended within Mosiah 23 itself. See [[Mosiah 23#^mosiah-23-1|Mosiah 23:1-2]], [[Mosiah 23#^mosiah-23-12|Mosiah 23:12-13]], [[Mosiah 23#^mosiah-23-21|Mosiah 23:21-24]], and [[Mosiah 23#^mosiah-23-29|Mosiah 23:29]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Mosiah 23 links warning and divine protection during the flight from Noah (vv.1-2), Alma's reminder to his settled people that they were delivered 'even out of the hands of king Noah' and should stand fast in that liberty (vv.12-13), and his teaching that God tries the faith and patience of 'whosoever putteth his trust in him,' illustrated by 'thus it was with this people' (vv.21-24) -- a retrospective reference to the deliverance already narrated in vv.1-2, not a promise about events still to come. The Lamanite army, led by Noah's former priest Amulon, then discovers the settlement at Helam and the people are placed under a new bondage (vv.25-39), which the record resolves only in Mosiah 24.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Read this way, vv.21-24 are Alma's use of the already-completed escape from Noah as a teaching illustration about trust under testing, delivered just before that trust is tested again by the Lamanite discovery in v.25. The chapter's liberty/bondage/trust vocabulary does double duty: it interprets a past deliverance and immediately precedes a new captivity whose own deliverance the narrative defers to the following chapter.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This kind of retrospective teaching illustration, followed immediately by a new test of the same trust, is compatible with a participant's remembered address, a later editor's arrangement of Alma's teaching alongside the narrative, or literary composition; the pattern illuminates the narrator's theological framing without indicating which of these produced the final text.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a straightforward literary reading, Alma's people have just experienced one deliverance (from Noah) and are about to face a second test (capture by Amulon's Lamanites) whose resolution the record defers to Mosiah 24. Alma's reflection in vv.21-24 supplies the interpretive key -- trust under testing -- before the new bondage begins, so the same vocabulary of liberty, bondage, trust, and deliverance can serve both episodes across Mosiah 23-24 without either chapter needing to resolve both bondages on its own.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
