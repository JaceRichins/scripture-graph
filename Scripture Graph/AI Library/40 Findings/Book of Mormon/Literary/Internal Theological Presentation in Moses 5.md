# Internal Theological Presentation in Moses 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[Moses 5#^moses-5-5|Moses 5:5-9]], [[Moses 5#^moses-5-14|Moses 5:14-15]], and [[Moses 5#^moses-5-57|Moses 5:57-59]] portray sacrifice, repentance, belief in the Son, the Holy Ghost, preaching, and ordinances as parts of one gospel operating from humanity's beginning. The chapter thereby reads Adam's sacrifice as a christological rite and integrates primeval history into a continuous Christian salvation narrative.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Moses 5:5-9 presents Adam offering sacrifice by commandment, receiving an angelic explanation that the sacrifice symbolizes the Only Begotten, being instructed to repent and act in the Son's name, and receiving the Holy Ghost. Verses 14-15 and 57-59 extend this presentation through preaching, belief, repentance, salvation, and holy ordinances.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter constructs a continuous Christian salvation history beginning with Adam: primordial sacrifice is interpreted christologically, and later gospel teaching and ordinances continue the same divine program.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This presentation places distinctively Christian concepts inside a primeval Genesis setting. It is important for understanding the Restoration-scripture portrayal of Adamic religion and its relationship to later Christianity.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Within the literary world of Moses 5, Adam does not initially understand the sacrificial command; the angel's explanation supplies its christological meaning, and the Holy Ghost then universalizes the promise of redemption. On a Restoration reading, this is recovered knowledge of primordial religion. On a literary-historical reconstruction, it is a Christian expansion of Genesis that makes later Christian doctrine the interpretive key to Adam's worship. Either reconstruction clarifies the chapter's organizing theological movement from command, through explanation, to a preached and ritually confirmed gospel.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
