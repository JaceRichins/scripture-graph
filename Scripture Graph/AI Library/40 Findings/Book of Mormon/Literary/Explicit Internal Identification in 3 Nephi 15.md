# Explicit Internal Identification in 3 Nephi 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 15 has Jesus explicitly identify his immediate hearers as the 'other sheep' of John 10:16. Steelmanning the critical case, this is a textbook example of an internal narrative stating its own intended interpretation, a move equally available to genuine historical report, ancient theological development, nineteenth-century revelation, or literary composition — so despite its explicitness, it discriminates between none of them and remains none-band evidence for the identification's historicity.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Within 3 Nephi 15, Jesus directly identifies his immediate hearers as the "other sheep" mentioned in the saying associated with John 10:16 and appeals to their having heard and seen him. The identification is explicit rather than inferred.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage identifies the Nephite audience as at least one referent of the other-sheep saying and uses Jesus's direct speech to authorize that interpretation within the narrative.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The identification expands the sheep discourse beyond its Jerusalem setting and integrates Jesus's appearance to the Nephites into the Book of Mormon's larger account of scattered Israel receiving his ministry.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->
## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the passage's narrative reconstruction, the risen Jesus visits an Israelite community outside Jerusalem and explains that this community belongs to the additional flock anticipated by the earlier saying. The identification connects the New World ministry with the gathering and covenant themes developed throughout the surrounding discourse.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
