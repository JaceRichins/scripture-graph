# Reported Miracle And Theological Attribution in Alma 57

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[Alma 57#^alma-57-19|Alma 57:19]]–[[Alma 57#^alma-57-27|Alma 57:27]] reports that all 2,060 soldiers survived despite many wounds and that two hundred fainted from blood loss; [[Alma 57#^alma-57-26|verse 26]] explicitly attributes their preservation to God's miraculous power and their faith. This firmly establishes the speaker's theological interpretation. Because the casualty figures and interpretation come through one narrative source without independent medical, tactical, or comparative evidence, they provide only none-band support for supernatural causation as the best historical explanation.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 57 reports that all 2,060 soldiers fought, received many wounds, and survived; two hundred had fainted from blood loss, while one thousand other Nephite soldiers were slain. The first-person speaker says that the army found this astonishing and "justly" attributed it to God's miraculous power because of the young soldiers' faith.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The text unmistakably presents the survival as extraordinary and gives Helaman's theological explanation for it. The strongest critical reading distinguishes that clear narratorial attribution from its cause: the only casualty account comes through the same interested narrative, while wound severity, exposure, treatment, selection, reporting conventions, and comparison data are unavailable.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The report is compatible with a sincerely remembered battle interpreted providentially, a historical tradition shaped to teach covenant fidelity, stylized sacred historiography, or deliberate modern composition. The zero-death outcome would be remarkable if every quantitative and medical detail were independently verified, but the passage itself is the sole evidence for those details.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Sacred and military narratives often interpret unexpected survival through a community's theology. On a historical reconstruction, Helaman's unit experienced an extraordinary outcome and interpreted it through the soldiers' covenantal faith; ordinary tactical factors and divine agency need not be mutually exclusive within that reading. On a literary-shaping or modern-composition reconstruction, the contrast between universal wounding, zero deaths, and one thousand other fatalities creates an exemplary narrative of obedience and providential protection.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

It does not require readers to treat the report as an independent casualty audit, to infer that no survivor died later, or to generalize the episode into a universal promise that faithful combatants will receive physical protection.

Fit with this finding: *consistent*. **Considered and set aside:**
- The narrator's certainty independently verifies the miracle — Sincerity and explicit attribution establish what the narrator claims, not which causal explanation is historically best.
- Modern trauma terminology validates the narrative — Retrospective medical labels cannot reconstruct wound severity, treatment, exposure, or mortality risk from the brief report.
- The episode teaches universal physical immunity for faithful people — The text describes a particular preservation and elsewhere acknowledges faithful suffering and death; the universal proposition is not textually required.
- A copying mistake generated the outcome — The survival claim is integrated with the casualty comparison, astonishment, and theological conclusion rather than resting on one isolated numeral.

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Chance, differing battlefield exposure, armor, tactics, evacuation, or medical care produced the survival pattern. — *plausible*: Such factors materially affect mortality, but the narrative supplies too little tactical or medical information to determine whether they suffice in this case.
- The report counted survivors or nonfatal wounds in a way that obscures later deaths, wound severity, or the population at risk. — *possible*: Reporting definitions can change apparent casualty rates, although the text's categorical assertion that none perished resists simply redefining the claim away.
- The episode preserves a historical core but was selected or shaped to emphasize exemplary faith. — *plausible*: The explicit moral interpretation and repeated emphasis on obedience give the account a clear didactic function, and literary shaping need not entail wholesale invention.
- The numbers and total survival are stylized rather than actuarially exact. — *possible*: War narratives can use rounded or rhetorically organized numbers, but no textual variant or independent source specifically demonstrates numerical stylization here.
- A later scribal error accidentally created the complete-survival claim. — *ad hoc*: The figures, astonishment, comparison with one thousand deaths, and theological attribution form a coordinated passage that a simple copying error does not explain.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
