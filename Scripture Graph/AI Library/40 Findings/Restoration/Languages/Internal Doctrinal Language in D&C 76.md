# Internal Doctrinal Language in D&C 76

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated contrast between the sons of perdition and "all the rest" is strong textual evidence that the chapter envisions an exceptionally broad scope for Christ's redemptive victory. That breadth includes resurrection for all others and a form of salvation even for telestial heirs, while still preserving unequal glories and exclusion from the dwelling place of God and Christ.

**Does not establish:** This language does not establish universalism, equal glory for all people, or eventual access by every person to the presence of God and Christ.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The word "saved" may describe several graded conditions rather than one uniform final state.
- Resurrection and release from hell need not imply admission to the presence of the Father.
- The sons of perdition remain an explicit exception to the chapter's broad redemptive language.
