# Verbal And Thematic Intertext in D&C 104

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The unusually close wording and shared rich-poor judgment theme make biblical intertextuality with Luke 16 highly likely, while leaving the exact mechanism and purpose of the reuse open.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a formal quotation, a unique source of composition, or that every detail of Luke 16 should control interpretation of D&C 104.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The wording could arise from general familiarity with King James biblical diction rather than a deliberate allusion to the whole parable.
- The parallel may intensify the warning without importing every feature of the rich man and Lazarus narrative.
