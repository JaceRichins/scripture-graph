# Literary Intertext in D&C 56

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close wording shared by D&C 56:16 and Jeremiah 8:20 is strong evidence that the revelation's language consciously reuses or evokes the biblical lament. It is not evidence by itself for any particular theory of authorship or revelatory mechanism.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish ancient authorship, the identity of the speaker, or whether the wording arose through direct quotation, memory, composition, or revelation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The wording may have entered the revelation through Joseph Smith's familiarity with the King James Bible.
- A believer may understand familiar biblical language as the idiom through which revelation was expressed.
- The phrase could function as an already familiar religious lament without requiring a carefully designed literary allusion.
