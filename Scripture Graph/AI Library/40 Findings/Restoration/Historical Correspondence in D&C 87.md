# Historical Correspondence in D&C 87

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Several particulars in D&C 87:1–3 correspond notably with the later American Civil War: South Carolina led secession, Northern and Southern states fought one another, the war caused immense death and misery, and the Confederacy sought recognition and assistance from Great Britain and other European powers. The correspondence is meaningful, but the wording about war upon all nations extends beyond the Civil War and should not be treated as fully exhausted by it.

**Does not establish:** These correspondences do not independently prove supernatural foreknowledge, uniquely identify the American Civil War as the whole fulfillment, or verify every prediction in the chapter.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The 1832 Nullification Crisis already centered on South Carolina and made sectional violence conceivable.
- Southern reliance on cotton exports and ties to Britain made an appeal to Britain historically foreseeable.
- Some correspondences may result from reading broad wording retrospectively through a familiar later event.
- The global and eschatological portions may remain unfulfilled, be hyperbolic, or refer to a longer sequence of conflicts.
