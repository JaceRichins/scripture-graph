# Internal Theological Argument in D&C 20

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the chapter's argument, the Book of Mormon functions as a corroborating witness for existing scripture and for the claim that God still inspires and calls people. This is strong evidence for the chapter's own theology of continuing revelation, but the document's assertions are not independent proof that every historical or supernatural claim is true.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently establish the Book of Mormon's ancient origin, the historicity of angelic ministry, or the truth of all claims attributed to the witnesses.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language may function as confessional or institutional rhetoric rather than as an argument intended to supply independent historical verification.
- A reader may accept that the Book of Mormon reinforces biblical teachings while rejecting the chapter's inference about its divine origin.
- The witnesses invoked in the passage may carry evidentiary weight primarily for readers who already accept their credibility.
