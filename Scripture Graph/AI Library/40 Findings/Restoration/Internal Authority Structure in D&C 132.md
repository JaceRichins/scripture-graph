# Internal Authority Structure in D&C 132

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter identifies Joseph as the exclusive contemporary holder of sealing power, authorizes relationships that directly concern him, promises his exaltation, and commands others not to oppose him. This is strong textual evidence of a self-authorizing structure and a potential conflict of interest, regardless of whether the revelation claim is accepted.

**Does not establish:** The structure does not by itself prove fraud, insincerity, divine authorization, or the historical manner in which every provision was implemented.

**Alternative explanations:**
- If Joseph's prophetic authority and the revelation are genuine, personal benefit does not by itself invalidate the commands.
- Defenders may view the promises and protections as divine reassurance during a costly prophetic obligation rather than self-serving legitimation.
- Some provisions may be read as general priesthood rules merely applied to Joseph's immediate circumstances.
