# HistoricalPredictive in D&C 130

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The 'eighty-five years' statement (D&C 130:14-17) is explicitly conditional ('if thou livest') and Joseph Smith himself immediately states he could not decide what kind of appearing was meant and merely 'believed' the Second Coming would not be sooner than that date; Smith died in 1844 at age 38, decades short of 85.

**Does not establish:** A settled verdict on whether this was a conditional personal impression or an unconditional revealed prophecy; the text itself supports both readings.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The statement is explicitly conditional ('if thou livest') and Smith's own gloss (v.16-17) frames it as an unresolved impression he only 'believed' rather than a certain, unconditional prediction, so his death before 85 does not straightforwardly constitute a failed unconditional prophecy.
- Critics read the verse as a plain, dated prediction that failed on its own terms regardless of the surrounding hedges, since the text does present it as a voice 'repeating' specific words rather than Smith's own inference.
