# Biblical Intertextuality in D&C 71

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of two distinctive Isaianic formulations in verses 8–10 is strong internal evidence that the passage frames opposition through Isaiah's imagery of legal challenge and eventual vindication. It is meaningful literary evidence but weak evidence for any larger claim about ancient or divine authorship.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish ancient authorship, independent antiquity, divine revelation, or a single demonstrable method by which the biblical wording entered the text.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The wording may reflect direct familiarity with the King James Bible without a more elaborate literary design.
- Biblical phrases could have entered the passage through the general religious language of its nineteenth-century setting.
- The two echoes may be locally meaningful without organizing the entire section.
