# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 109

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The prayer repeatedly incorporates recognizable biblical phrases and images: protection against weapons, Pentecostal wind and fire, sealing the law and testimony, the kingdom becoming a world-filling mountain, the woman fair as moon and clear as sun, transformed terrain at the divine advent, and meeting the Lord in the cloud. This is strong evidence that the chapter was composed in a densely biblical idiom.

**Does not establish:** Biblical dependence does not establish supernatural inspiration, ancient authorship, or the precise compositional process behind the prayer.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some expressions may have become ordinary religious language rather than being freshly borrowed from a specific passage.
- Several images may derive from a shared biblical vocabulary rather than a single direct source.
- The density may reflect conscious literary construction, memorized scripture, revelatory diction, or a combination of these.
