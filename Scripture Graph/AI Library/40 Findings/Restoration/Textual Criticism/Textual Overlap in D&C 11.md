# Textual Overlap in D&C 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
D&C 11 shares extended wording and sequence with D&C 6 and shorter harvest-language parallels with D&C 12 and D&C 14. This is strong evidence that these revelations participate in a common scriptural and revelatory idiom, but the historical or compositional explanation for that idiom requires evidence beyond textual overlap.

**Does not establish:** Textual overlap alone does not establish supernatural origin, independent corroboration, ancient authorship, or the precise human and divine contributions to composition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The repetitions may reflect deliberate divine adaptation of the same counsel to different recipients.
- They may reflect Joseph Smith's recurring dictation vocabulary and compositional formulas.
- They may arise from shared dependence on biblical language and early Restoration discourse.
- More than one of these explanations may be true simultaneously.
