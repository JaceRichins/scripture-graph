# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 110

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of parallels with Revelation 1 is strong evidence that the recorded account deliberately participates in biblical apocalyptic and christological language. It is not independent evidence for the historical or supernatural occurrence of the vision.

**Does not establish:** It does not determine literary dependence mechanisms, the date of composition, ancient authorship, or whether a supernatural appearance occurred.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The narrators may have described an experience through familiar scriptural language.
- The wording may reflect conscious literary composition or later editorial shaping.
- Both passages may employ a shared stock of biblical theophanic imagery.

**Scripture:** [[D&C 110#^dc-110-3|D&C 110:3-4]], [[Revelation 1#^rev-1-14|Revelation 1:14-18]]
