# Textual Differentiation in D&C 124

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The text strongly distinguishes two public houses: a boarding house for strangers, later named the Nauvoo House, and a holy house in which ordinances and priesthood-related revelations are to be given. The distinction is established by their different stated functions and separately repeated construction instructions.

**Does not establish:** The distinction does not establish that the two projects form an intentional symbolic pair or balanced literary architecture.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Because both projects are repeatedly called a house, an inattentive reading may merge them.
- The earlier boarding-house instructions do not supply the name Nauvoo House until later in the chapter, although the repeated purpose and builders strongly support continuity.
