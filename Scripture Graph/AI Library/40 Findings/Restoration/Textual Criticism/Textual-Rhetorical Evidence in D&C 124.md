# Textual-Rhetorical Evidence in D&C 124

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Repeated conditional language is strong textual evidence that many commendations, offices, protections, and promised blessings in the chapter are contingent rather than unconditional forecasts. Phrases such as 'if he receive counsel,' 'if he continue,' 'if he will do my will,' and 'if he will hearken' explicitly condition several individual promises.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish whether any named person later fulfilled the stated conditions or how every promise should be evaluated historically.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some promises in the chapter lack an adjacent explicit condition and should be evaluated individually.
- A reader might understand general covenant obedience as an implicit condition even where no conditional clause appears.
