# Internal Textual Evidence in D&C 131

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The explicit assertion that spirit is matter is strong textual evidence that this chapter teaches a material conception of spirit, but the brief statement is insufficient to reconstruct a complete metaphysical system.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish what particles, laws, or mechanisms spiritual matter involves, nor does it by itself settle every question about embodiment, consciousness, or divine nature.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The word "matter" may be used analogically or in a nineteenth-century sense that does not correspond exactly to modern physical terminology.
- The passage may make only a limited claim about the substantiality of spirit rather than endorsing every implication of philosophical materialism.
