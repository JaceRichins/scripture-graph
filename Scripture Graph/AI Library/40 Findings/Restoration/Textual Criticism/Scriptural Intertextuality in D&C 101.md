# Scriptural Intertextuality in D&C 101

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The explicit use of the unjust judge and wheat-and-tares parables, together with recognizable biblical formulations concerning Abraham, salt, stillness before God, and instantaneous change, is strong evidence that the chapter deliberately frames its instructions through biblical language and narratives.

**Does not establish:** These parallels establish deliberate biblical framing more securely than they establish the precise mechanism of dependence, the direction of influence, historical authenticity, or divine origin.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The biblical register may reflect Joseph Smith's scriptural environment and vocabulary.
- Believers may understand the same language as revelation intentionally recalling earlier scripture.
- Some verbal correspondence may have been affected by transmission or editorial standardization.
