# Scriptural Intertextuality in D&C 106

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
D&C 106:4–5 closely parallels the clustered language of 1 Thessalonians 5:2–5: the day of the Lord, a thief in the night, being overtaken by that day, and children of light. The clustering makes a New Testament literary echo substantially more likely than a resemblance based on only one common phrase.

**Does not establish:** The verbal relationship does not by itself establish a particular theory of revelation, independent ancient origin, or the date of the Second Coming.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The revelation may deliberately quote or adapt familiar King James Bible language.
- The resemblance may reflect Christian language deeply familiar to Joseph Smith and his contemporaries.
- A believer may understand the parallel as revelatory reuse of an earlier divine warning.
