# Ethical And Intra-Textual Tension in D&C 134

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Verse 12 is strong textual evidence that the declaration accommodated governments and masters who permitted human servitude: it prohibited preaching to or baptizing bond-servants contrary to their masters’ wishes and justified the restriction partly by appeal to public peace. This creates a serious moral tension with the chapter’s language of inherent rights.

**Does not establish:** The verse alone does not establish that servitude was declared morally good or divinely mandated; neither does a safety-based explanation remove the restriction it places on enslaved persons’ religious agency.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The restriction can be read as a temporary safety policy intended to avoid retaliation or civil violence rather than as approval of slavery.
- Its defenders may argue that the verse addresses unauthorized interference with legally recognized relationships, not the morality of servitude itself.
- The language may reflect an effort to answer contemporary accusations that missionary activity would provoke slave unrest.
