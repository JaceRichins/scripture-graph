# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 33

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of close phrase-level parallels with Hebrews, John, Matthew, and Revelation is strong evidence that the chapter deliberately frames its commission in biblical language and imagery.

**Does not establish:** These parallels do not by themselves establish divine origin, prophetic authority, supernatural revelation, or the fulfillment of the chapter's eschatological expectations.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The phrasing may arise from conscious reuse of familiar King James Bible language.
- Some combinations may reflect a shared Christian preaching vocabulary rather than a carefully designed literary program.
- Individual parallels do not by themselves identify the human or divine mechanism of composition.
