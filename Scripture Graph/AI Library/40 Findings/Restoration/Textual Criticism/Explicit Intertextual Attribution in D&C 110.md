# Explicit Intertextual Attribution in D&C 110

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The explicit attribution to Malachi and the close correspondence between D&C 110:14-15 and Malachi 4:5-6 provide very strong evidence that the chapter deliberately frames Elijah's appearance as a fulfillment or renewed application of Malachi's prophecy.

**Does not establish:** The textual relationship alone does not independently establish the historicity, supernatural cause, or exclusive interpretation of the reported vision.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passage may represent literary appropriation of Malachi without demonstrating that the narrated appearance occurred.
- Its fulfillment language may reflect the narrators' or an editor's theological interpretation of an experience.

**Scripture:** [[D&C 110#^dc-110-14|D&C 110:14-15]], [[Malachi 4#^mal-4-5|Malachi 4:5-6]]
