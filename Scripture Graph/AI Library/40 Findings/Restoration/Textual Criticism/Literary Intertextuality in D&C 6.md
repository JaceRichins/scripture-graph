# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The density and closeness of biblical phrasing provide strong evidence that D&C 6 presents itself through the language of the English Bible. They do not, without further argument, identify whether particular expressions arise through direct quotation, remembered scriptural idiom, revelatory accommodation, or nineteenth-century composition.

**Does not establish:** The parallels alone do not establish divine origin, fraud, ancient provenance, or dependence on one uniquely identifiable biblical passage in every instance.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The speaker may intentionally quote or adapt earlier scripture.
- The wording may reflect Joseph Smith's familiarity with the King James Bible.
- Believers may understand the biblical idiom as divine accommodation to the recipients' scriptural language.
- Some shorter parallels may arise from shared religious vocabulary rather than dependence on one specific verse.
