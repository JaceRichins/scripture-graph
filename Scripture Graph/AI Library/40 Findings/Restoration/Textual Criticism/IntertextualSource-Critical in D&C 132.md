# IntertextualSource-Critical in D&C 132

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The claim in D&C 132:34 that God commanded the Hagar arrangement is an interpretive addition relative to Genesis 16, where the initiative is Sarah's alone and no divine command to Abraham is recorded; this is a documentable textual divergence between the two accounts.

**Does not establish:** This gap does not by itself establish that the D&C 132 claim is false; it establishes only that the Genesis text, as it stands, supplies no divine-command narration for the Hagar episode, so D&C 132 is doing interpretive/additive work rather than simply restating Genesis.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The command could have been an unrecorded oral tradition or extra-canonical detail not preserved in the Genesis narrative but genuinely known to Joseph Smith by revelation.
- D&C 132:34 may be read as an interpretive gloss applying the chapter's own theological logic ('it was the law') retroactively to Genesis 16 rather than as an independent historical report.
- The verse may reflect typical harmonizing exegesis common in 19th-century biblical interpretation rather than a claim of new historical information.
