# EthicalTextual in D&C 132

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The consent and sanction structure of verses 61-65 is asymmetric by sex: a husband's plural marriages are validated by an initial consent from the first wife and by divine appointment, while a wife's transgression (vv.63-64) triggers destruction with no parallel plural-spouse allowance offered to her.

**Does not establish:** This observation does not establish the text's intent or resolve whether the asymmetry reflects a permanent doctrinal position or a historically contingent regulation; it establishes only that the stated conditions and sanctions are not symmetric between husband and wife as written.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The asymmetry may reflect the specific ancient and 19th-century patriarchal marriage forms the text is regulating rather than asserting a timeless principle of unequal moral standing.
- Some readers hold the differential roles reflect distinct priesthood/administrative functions rather than differential worth, though the text does not itself make this distinction explicit.
- The passage may be a limited, situational instruction ('let this suffice for the present,' v.66) rather than a complete or final statement of principle.
