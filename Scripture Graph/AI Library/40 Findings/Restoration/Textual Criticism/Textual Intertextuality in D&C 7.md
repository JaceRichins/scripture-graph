# Textual Intertextuality in D&C 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The unusually close wording and shared subject matter strongly support the conclusion that D&C 7 intentionally interprets John 21:20–23. The additional cluster of language shared with Hebrews 1:7,14 supports deliberate or scripturally mediated biblical intertextuality.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not by themselves establish the revelation's supernatural origin, the historical survival of John, or the antiquity of any underlying document.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The biblical wording may reflect the revelator's familiarity with the English Bible rather than dependence by an ancient source.
- The parallels may be understood within the text's own claims as revealed expansion of the biblical account.
- The Hebrews imagery could be conventional biblical diction rather than a direct quotation.
