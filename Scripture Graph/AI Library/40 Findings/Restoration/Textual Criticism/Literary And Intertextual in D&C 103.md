# Literary And Intertextual in D&C 103

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The clustered references to a Moses-like leader, Israelite and Abrahamic identity, deliverance from bondage by power and a stretched-out arm, angels, divine presence, and a goodly land provide strong textual evidence that the redemption of Zion is deliberately framed as a renewed Exodus.

**Does not establish:** The typology does not establish that the contemplated expedition would reproduce every feature of the ancient Exodus, nor does literary dependence by itself establish divine origin or ancient authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language could draw on conventional biblical diction without implying a detailed one-to-one correspondence with the ancient Exodus.
- Readers may disagree over which details are typological and which are simply inherited scriptural vocabulary.
