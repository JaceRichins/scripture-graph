# Canonical Intertextual Parallel in D&C 50

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
D&C 50:41–44 contains a cluster of Johannine themes and expressions: Christ overcoming the world, people given to the Son by the Father, reciprocal indwelling, and the good shepherd. This strongly supports conscious or culturally mediated use of biblical language, while the precise route of dependence remains uncertain.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not by themselves establish a single literary source, a method of composition, ancient authorship, or the revelation's divine or non-divine origin.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The parallels may arise from the general biblical vocabulary of Joseph Smith and his religious environment rather than deliberate combination of particular passages.
- Several themes could have been mediated through preaching, memory, or broader Christian discourse rather than direct textual consultation.
- A believer may understand the resemblance as continuity of divine teaching rather than ordinary literary dependence.
