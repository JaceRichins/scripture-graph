# Literary-Contextual in D&C 122

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the chapter's argument, verse 8 is strong textual evidence that the catalogue of suffering is intended to lead into humility and endurance: the Son of Man's descent below all is followed immediately by the conclusion, 'Therefore, hold on thy way.'

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself identify a particular episode of Christ's suffering, define the mechanics of the Atonement, or prove that every instance of suffering produces spiritual benefit.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The word 'Therefore' may gather the entire preceding revelation rather than verse 8 alone.
- The comparison with the Son of Man may emphasize the addressee's limited status more directly than it explains how suffering produces endurance.

**Scripture:** [[D&C 122#^dc-122-8|D&C 122:8]], [[D&C 122#^dc-122-9|D&C 122:9]]
