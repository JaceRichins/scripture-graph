# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 63

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
D&C 63 repeatedly reuses recognizable New Testament language: signs following believers, faith pleasing God, a well springing to everlasting life, rendering to Caesar, blessing those who die in the Lord, and an eternal weight of glory. These correspondences are strong evidence that the chapter is deliberately framed in a King James biblical register and invites intertextual reading.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not by themselves establish ancient authorship, supernatural origin, originality, or any single theory of how the wording was produced.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The phrasing may reflect conscious imitation or quotation of the King James Bible.
- Some expressions may have belonged to the shared scriptural vocabulary of nineteenth-century English-speaking Christians.
- A believing interpretation may understand the parallels as divine reuse of earlier revelation rather than literary dependence alone.
