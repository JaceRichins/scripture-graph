# Textual Parallel in D&C 100

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
D&C 100:5-6's promise that speech will be supplied 'in the very hour, yea, in the very moment, what ye shall say' closely echoes Matthew 10:19-20 ('take no thought how or what ye shall speak: for it shall be given you in that same hour what ye shall speak'). The overlap in phrase structure ('shall be given you... in that hour... what ye shall say/speak') is a verifiable textual parallel.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish that D&C 100 is a conscious quotation of Matthew, nor does it establish or refute the revelation's claimed divine origin.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Joseph Smith and Sidney Rigdon were both immersed in KJV language and could naturally reproduce its idiom without conscious quotation.
- The phrase may reflect common early-19th-century American religious speech about extemporaneous, Spirit-led preaching rather than direct literary dependence on Matthew.
- Shared theme (promised inspired utterance) does not require shared wording origin; independent revelation using similar imagery is also possible for a believer.
