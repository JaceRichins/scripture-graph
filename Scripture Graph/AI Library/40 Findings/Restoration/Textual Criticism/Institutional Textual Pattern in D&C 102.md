# Institutional Textual Pattern in D&C 102

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The juxtaposition of appointment by revelation with acknowledgment or sanction by a collective voice strongly supports the limited conclusion that this chapter envisions both revelatory and communal elements in church governance.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish modern democracy, equal authority among all participants, or the divine origin of any particular decision.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The collective voice may function primarily as ratification rather than as an independent source of authority.
- Different provisions may address distinct offices and should not be collapsed into one general theory of governance.
- The recorded unanimity of the organizing meeting does not imply that all later decisions required unanimity.
