# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 29

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The gathering-under-wings image and the final separation at Christ's right and left closely connect D&C 29 with biblical teachings attributed to Jesus. This is strong evidence of biblical intertextuality, but its historical or revelatory significance depends on assumptions not settled by the parallels themselves.

**Does not establish:** The parallels alone do not establish divine origin, independent antiquity, or a particular mechanism of composition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language may reflect direct use of the King James Bible.
- The imagery may have entered the revelation through the recipients' shared Christian vocabulary.
- A believer may understand the recurrence as divine reuse or expansion of earlier teachings.
