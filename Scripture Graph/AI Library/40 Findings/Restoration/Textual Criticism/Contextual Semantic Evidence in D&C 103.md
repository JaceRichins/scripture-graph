# Contextual Semantic Evidence in D&C 103

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Although Zion's redemption is said to come 'by power,' the chapter's specified means include purchasing land with consecrated money, gathering an organized company, establishing the community upon laws and commandments, and seeking victory through diligence, faithfulness, and prayer. This supports a broader reading of 'power' than military force alone.

**Does not establish:** These operational instructions do not establish that the expedition was pacifist, that force was categorically forbidden, or that 'power' has only a spiritual meaning.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Land purchase, organization, and prayer could coexist with preparation to use physical force.
- The language of cursing, vengeance, and laying down life may imply that violent confrontation was regarded as possible.
- Power may intentionally include several forms of divine, institutional, legal, and physical action rather than excluding any one of them.
