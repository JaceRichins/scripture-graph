# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 40

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The clustering of receiving the word with gladness, persecution, temptation, and worldly cares is evidence that D&C 40 frames Covel's experience through language and categories associated with the parable of the sower. The verbal and thematic correspondence is strong, but intentional allusion remains an interpretation rather than a directly stated fact.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish which Gospel account, if any, was the direct literary source, nor does it establish the revelation's historical or divine origin.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The shared expressions may arise from general biblical diction rather than a deliberate reference to one particular Gospel passage.
- The revelation may combine familiar Christian themes without intending a systematic correspondence to the parable.
- The wording may reflect the language habits of the revelatory recorder or later textual transmission.
