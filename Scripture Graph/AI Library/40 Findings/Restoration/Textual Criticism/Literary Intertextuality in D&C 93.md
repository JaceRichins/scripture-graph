# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 93

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The cluster of corresponding motifs and vocabulary in D&C 93:7-11 provides strong textual evidence that this passage consciously engages John 1. The density and sequence of the parallels make accidental resemblance unlikely.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not establish that the embedded material is an ancient lost record written by John, nor do they establish authorship independent of the King James Bible.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The wording may reflect direct use of, or memory of, the King James Bible rather than an independent ancient source.
- Some parallels could arise from general Christian vocabulary already shaped by John 1.
- A faith-based reading may posit a shared revelatory source, but textual similarity alone cannot verify that explanation.
