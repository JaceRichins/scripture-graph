# Textual Intertextuality in D&C 76

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The explicit use of John 5 and the clustered parallels with 1 Corinthians and Hebrews provide strong evidence that D&C 76 communicates its vision through sustained biblical intertextuality. These parallels illuminate how the chapter enlarges familiar biblical language into a distinctive account of post-resurrection glory.

**Does not establish:** The verbal parallels do not determine whether the vision was supernatural, psychological, literary, or some combination, and they neither prove nor disprove the witnesses' historical claims.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The biblical diction could reflect revelation communicated in the scriptural vocabulary familiar to the witnesses.
- It could reflect conscious literary composition or interpretation by Joseph Smith and Sidney Rigdon.
- Some wording could arise from memorized biblical phrases without a planned literary structure.
