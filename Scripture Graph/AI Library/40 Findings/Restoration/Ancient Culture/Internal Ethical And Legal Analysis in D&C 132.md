# Internal Ethical And Legal Analysis in D&C 132

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The grammar, sanctions, and allocation of decision-making power in verses 41–65 are strong textual evidence that the chapter's plural-marriage regulations are structurally gender-asymmetric and that the stated consent mechanism operates under coercive religious pressure.

**Does not establish:** The text alone does not reconstruct every participant's lived choices, determine whether each historical relationship followed these rules, or settle the revelation's divine authenticity; it does establish the asymmetrical and threatening language of the stated rules.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passage may address a particular male priesthood office and specific marital crisis rather than offer a complete account of women's spiritual authority.
- A theological reading may treat the threatened sanctions as divine judgment applicable to disobedience generally rather than as interpersonal coercion by a husband.
- The precise force of 'destroyed' is uncertain and may not denote physical death.
