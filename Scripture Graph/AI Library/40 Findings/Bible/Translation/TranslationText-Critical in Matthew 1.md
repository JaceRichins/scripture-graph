# TranslationText-Critical in Matthew 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Matthew 1:23 quotes Isaiah's sign of a 'virgin' who 'shall be with child' and bear a son called Emmanuel/Immanuel. The underlying Hebrew term in Isaiah 7:14 (almah) denotes a young woman of marriageable age and does not by itself specify virginity; the rendering 'virgin' follows the Greek Septuagint's parthenos. This translation choice sits at the center of a longstanding Jewish-Christian and text-critical debate over Isaiah's original sense.

**Does not establish:** Does not by itself resolve whether Isaiah's original oracle envisioned a virgin birth; that is an independent exegetical question from Matthew's own theological claim about Mary.

**Alternative explanations:**
- In its original 8th-century-BC context, Isaiah 7:14 is widely read by scholars (including many believing scholars) as a near-term sign to king Ahaz involving an ordinary young woman, not a prediction of a distant miraculous virgin birth.
- Matthew may be applying a typological or sensus plenior reading of Isaiah rather than asserting that Isaiah's original human audience understood the prophecy as being about a virgin conception centuries later.
