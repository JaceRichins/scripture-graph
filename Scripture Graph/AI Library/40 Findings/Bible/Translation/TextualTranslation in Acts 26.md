# TextualTranslation in Acts 26

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Agrippa's reply in v.28 is a well-known translation crux. The KJV rendering 'Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian' is often popularly read as near-conversion, but the underlying Greek is genuinely ambiguous and has been rendered by various translators/commentators as sarcastic deflection, a statement about the brevity of Paul's argument ('in a short time...'), or a noncommittal courtly reply — not unambiguously a sincere near-conversion.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish Agrippa's actual inner spiritual state or that he came close to converting; the verse should not be used as firm evidence of a near-conversion without noting the translation dispute.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Agrippa's statement is sincere but guarded admiration, not full persuasion.
- The statement is polite, non-committal deflection typical of a monarch avoiding a direct answer in a public legal hearing.
- The Greek idiom behind 'almost' may concern degree of persuasion or time rather than emotional closeness to conversion, altering the popular reading entirely.
