# TextualInterpretive in Micah 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The phrase 'whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting' (Micah 5:2) is read two distinct ways: (a) as an assertion of the Messiah's premortal, eternal existence, the traditional Christian/LDS reading; or (b) as Hebrew idiom pointing to the antiquity of the Davidic royal line rooted in Bethlehem (David's ancestral town), without implying literal personal eternity.

**Does not establish:** The Hebrew phrasing alone does not settle, independent of later theological tradition, whether personal premortal existence is in view.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The phrase functions as a stock idiom for 'ancient lineage' (comparable to how 'of old' is used elsewhere of ancestral houses), pointing back to David rather than asserting literal preexistence of the future ruler.
- Some historical-critical readers apply the verse to an anticipated near-term Davidic king (e.g., Hezekiah) rather than a distant messianic figure at all.
