# Literary Intertextuality in Joel 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close verbal inversion between Joel 3:10 and Isaiah 2:4/Micah 4:3 is strong evidence of a meaningful literary relationship among these prophetic formulations, although direction of dependence cannot be established from wording alone.

**Does not establish:** The verbal relationship alone does not establish which passage came first, direct quotation, common authorship, or the date of Joel.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The prophets may independently adapt a shared traditional saying.
- Joel may reverse Isaiah or Micah.
- Isaiah or Micah may transform an earlier war summons resembling Joel.
- The surviving books may preserve later editorial interaction.
