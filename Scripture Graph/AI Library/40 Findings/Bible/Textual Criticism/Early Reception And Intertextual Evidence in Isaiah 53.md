# Early Reception And Intertextual Evidence in Isaiah 53

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The independent New Testament uses of Isaiah 53 are strong evidence that first-century Christians interpreted the chapter through the suffering, death, healing ministry, and redemptive work of Jesus. They establish early Christian reception of the chapter more directly than they establish the exclusive meaning intended in Isaiah's original historical setting.

**Does not establish:** This evidence does not by itself prove predictive fulfillment, exclusive reference to Jesus, or the absence of an earlier corporate or historical referent.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The New Testament authors may be offering typological or christological rereadings rather than recovering the passage's sole original referent.
- Shared Christian tradition may account for some overlap among the New Testament applications.
- A passage can acquire a later canonical application without that application exhausting its earlier historical meaning.
