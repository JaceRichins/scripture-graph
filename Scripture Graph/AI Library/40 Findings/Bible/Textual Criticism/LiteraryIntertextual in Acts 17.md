# LiteraryIntertextual in Acts 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The line Paul cites ('we are also his offspring') closely matches a phrase from the Hellenistic poet Aratus's Phaenomena ('for we are also his offspring'), and a similar idea appears in Cleanthes' Hymn to Zeus; this is a widely noted point in New Testament commentary on Acts 17.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish that Paul had formal training in Greek literature, nor exactly which text he had in mind; it only shows the speech (as recorded) engages recognizable Hellenistic poetic language.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The phrase may reflect a general Stoic commonplace about human kinship with Zeus/the divine rather than a precise citation of one identifiable poet.
- Luke, writing decades after the event, may have supplied or polished the literary allusion in composing Paul's speech rather than reporting Paul's exact words verbatim.

**Scripture:** [[Acts 17#^acts-17-28|Acts 17:28]]
