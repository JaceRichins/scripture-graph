# Canonical Intertextual Reception in Hosea 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Romans 9:25-26 and 1 Peter 2:10 reuse the Hosean movement from 'not my people' and absence of mercy to divine belonging and mercy. This is strong evidence for the passage's importance in later scriptural reflection on the formation of God's people.

**Does not establish:** Later reuse does not establish that Hosea's original historical audience understood Hosea 2:23 primarily as a prediction about the Christian church or Gentile conversion.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The New Testament authors may be making a typological or analogical application rather than claiming that Gentiles were Hosea's only original referent.
- Romans combines Hosean passages and places them within Paul's own argument about Jews and Gentiles.
