# Literary And Intertextual in Joshua 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The divided waters, passage on dry ground, water standing as a heap, and explicit comparison of Joshua with Moses provide strong textual evidence that the Jordan account is meant to recall Israel's earlier deliverance through the sea.

**Does not establish:** The literary correspondence does not by itself establish the date of composition, eyewitness authorship, the precise physical mechanism, or the historicity of either crossing.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some shared vocabulary may arise naturally because both narratives describe passage through divided water.
- The correspondence may reflect later literary or editorial shaping rather than the wording of an eyewitness report.
- The author may employ a conventional deliverance pattern without intending every detail as a direct allusion.
