# Textual Criticism in 1 John 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Trinitarian formula in verse 7 ('there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one') is widely regarded by New Testament textual critics as a later addition (the 'Johannine Comma'), absent from all early Greek manuscripts and from the earliest versions, entering the Latin tradition first and only appearing in a handful of very late Greek manuscripts before being included in the Textus Receptus underlying the KJV.

**Does not establish:** The manuscript history of this clause concerns textual transmission only; it neither proves nor disproves any particular doctrine of the Godhead, which rests on the wider witness of scripture rather than this verse alone.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A minority of defenders of the Textus Receptus/Majority Text tradition argue the clause could reflect a genuine early reading that simply dropped out of surviving Greek copies rather than being a later insertion; this view is held by very few professional textual critics today.

**Scripture:** [[1 John 5#^1jn-5-7|1 John 5:7]]
