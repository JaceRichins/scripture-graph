# Later Textual Practice in Deuteronomy 25

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Paul's report that he received “forty stripes save one” five times is consistent with a later practice of stopping below Deuteronomy's forty-blow ceiling. It is useful evidence for cautious observance of the numerical limit, although it does not identify why thirty-nine became the administered number.

**Does not establish:** It does not prove that every later Jewish court used thirty-nine blows or that avoidance of accidental transgression was the sole reason for the practice.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Thirty-nine may have become a fixed disciplinary convention for reasons not stated in either passage.
- Paul's brief report does not describe the counting procedure or the authorities' explicit rationale.
