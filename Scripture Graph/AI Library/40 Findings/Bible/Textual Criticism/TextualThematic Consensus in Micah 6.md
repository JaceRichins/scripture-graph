# TextualThematic Consensus in Micah 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Micah 6:8 is widely regarded, across Jewish and Christian tradition, as one of the Old Testament's clearest summations of prophetic religion: ethical and relational obedience (justice, mercy, humility) as the substance God requires, with cultic sacrifice notably absent from the answer.

**Does not establish:** That the Law of Moses' sacrificial system was considered obsolete or wrong; only that ritual without justice, mercy, and humility is insufficient.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The verse may emphasize inward disposition as a necessary complement to (rather than an outright replacement of) prescribed Levitical sacrifice, which the Torah elsewhere commands.
- The silence about sacrifice in v.8 could reflect rhetorical compression rather than a theological claim that sacrifice is unnecessary.
