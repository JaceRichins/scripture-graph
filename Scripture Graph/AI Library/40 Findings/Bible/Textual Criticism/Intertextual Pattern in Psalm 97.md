# Intertextual Pattern in Psalm 97

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The parallels with Sinai imagery, Micah's melting mountains, Psalm 114's presence formula, and Psalm 30's thanksgiving clause support the conclusion that Psalm 97 participates in a wider scriptural repertoire of divine-presence language.

**Does not establish:** These parallels do not establish direct dependence, direction of borrowing, common authorship, or a precise date for Psalm 97.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The authors may independently employ conventional Israelite theophany and worship language.
- Shared wording may reflect common liturgical diction rather than direct literary borrowing.
- Even a direct relationship would not by itself determine which text influenced the other.
