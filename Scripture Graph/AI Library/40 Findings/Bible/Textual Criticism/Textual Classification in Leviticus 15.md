# Textual Classification in Leviticus 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Because the chapter assigns uncleanness to ordinary bodily events, including seminal emission, intercourse, and menstruation, without commanding repentance or describing those events as transgressions, its language supports distinguishing ritual impurity from straightforward personal moral guilt. The prescribed sacrifices after prolonged discharges complicate but do not erase that distinction.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that ritual impurity was spiritually insignificant, nor does it settle the meaning of every occurrence of "sin offering" or "atonement" in Leviticus.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The terms "sin offering" and "atonement" may be read as implying some form of culpability, even though the chapter states no deliberate offense.
- Ritual and moral categories may overlap within the larger theology of Leviticus rather than forming an absolute binary.
