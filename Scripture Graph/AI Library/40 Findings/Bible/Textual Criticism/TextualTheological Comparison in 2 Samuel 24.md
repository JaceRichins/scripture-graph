# TextualTheological Comparison in 2 Samuel 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The parallel account of this same event in 1 Chronicles 21:1 attributes the incitement to 'Satan' rather than to the LORD, a well-documented divergence between the Samuel and Chronicles versions of the same episode.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish which account is theologically definitive, or whether Chronicles is correcting, supplementing, or simply re-narrating Samuel through a different theological lens.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Chronicles reflects later Second Temple-era theological development that distances YHWH from directly authoring evil or temptation
- Both texts may reflect an ancient Hebrew idiom of ultimate divine sovereignty/permission over all events, even those proximately caused by an adversarial agent
- The two accounts may derive from independent source traditions rather than one deliberately revising the other
