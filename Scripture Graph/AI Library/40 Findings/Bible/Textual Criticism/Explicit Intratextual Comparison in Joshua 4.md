# Explicit Intratextual Comparison in Joshua 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter's explicit comparison of the Jordan crossing with the Red Sea is strong evidence that the narrator intends readers to understand the event as a continuation or renewal of the Exodus pattern.

**Does not establish:** The literary parallel alone does not establish the historicity, date, authorship, or supernatural mechanism of either crossing.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The comparison may be primarily theological and literary rather than a claim that every detail of the two events corresponds.
- A later narrator or editor could have shaped the account to resemble the Exodus tradition.

**Scripture:** [[Joshua 4#^josh-4-22|Joshua 4:22-24]], [[Exodus 14#^ex-14-21|Exodus 14:21-22]]
