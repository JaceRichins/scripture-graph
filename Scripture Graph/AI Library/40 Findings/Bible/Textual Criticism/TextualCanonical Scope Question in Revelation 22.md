# TextualCanonical Scope Question in Revelation 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
It is disputed how far the referent of 'this book' in verses 18-19 was ever meant to extend: the most defensible reading restricts it to the book of Revelation itself (as internally described, e.g. 1:11, 22:7,10), and treating it as a warning against altering the whole Bible is a later canonical application rather than John's demonstrable original scope.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish John's own awareness of, or intention regarding, a fixed 66-book biblical canon that did not yet exist in his lifetime.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some readers hold that because Revelation stands last in the canonical ordering, its warning functions providentially (if not authorially) as a seal on the whole Bible regardless of John's original intent.
- The warning could be a stock ancient scribal-protection formula applied to any single manuscript, without implying a developed theory of 'canon' at all at the time of writing.
