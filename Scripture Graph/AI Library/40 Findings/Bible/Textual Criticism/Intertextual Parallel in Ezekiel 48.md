# Intertextual Parallel in Ezekiel 48

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ezekiel's square city, twelve tribe-named gates, and concluding emphasis on God's presence closely parallel the city vision in Revelation 21. The correspondence strongly supports a literary and theological relationship, although the evidence alone cannot settle whether every shared feature represents direct dependence, broader scriptural tradition, or an identical fulfillment scheme.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not by itself establish a literal architectural blueprint, a precise chronology, or complete identity between Ezekiel's city and Revelation's New Jerusalem.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Revelation may combine Ezekiel with other Jewish restoration and temple traditions.
- Some similarities may arise from shared symbolic conventions rather than line-by-line dependence.
- Literary reuse does not require both visions to describe every detail of fulfillment in the same way.
