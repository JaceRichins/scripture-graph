# Contextual And Messianic Interpretation in Haggai 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The phrase "the desire of all nations shall come" supports a traditional Christian messianic reading, but the immediate reference to the nations, silver, gold, and the filling of the house also supports a reading involving the nations' desirable or valuable things. The KJV wording alone does not resolve the question.

**Does not establish:** The phrase by itself does not establish that Haggai's immediate audience understood it as a direct personal prediction of Jesus Christ.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The expression may refer collectively to wealth or precious objects brought from the nations.
- It may describe the nations themselves coming with tribute.
- A Christian canonical reading may treat the language as ultimately fulfilled in Jesus Christ even if its nearer reference concerns temple wealth or international homage.
