# Intertextual-Historical Correspondence in Ezekiel 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The prince's departure, capture, transport to Babylon, and inability to see Babylon in Ezekiel 12:12-13 closely correspond to accounts of Zedekiah's nighttime flight, capture, blinding, and deportation in 2 Kings 25:4-7 and Jeremiah 39:4-7. This strongly supports identifying the unnamed prince with Zedekiah and reading the oracle in relation to his fate.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence alone does not establish the oracle's date, independent predictive composition, or the historical independence of the biblical accounts.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The wording or final form of the oracle may have been shaped after Zedekiah's fate was known.
- Ezekiel and the historical narratives may preserve a shared literary tradition rather than independent testimony.
- Flight and capture were foreseeable siege outcomes, although the correspondence involving blindness and Babylon is more specific.
