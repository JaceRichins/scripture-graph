# Literary And Intertextual Pattern in Mark 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The combined motifs of a mountain, selected companions, Moses, an overshadowing cloud, transformed appearance, and a divine voice provide substantial textual evidence that the transfiguration is presented with Sinai-like theophanic imagery.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not by themselves establish direct literary dependence, the precise historical location of the event, or that every detail was designed as a one-to-one reenactment of Sinai.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Mountain, cloud, light, and heavenly speech are general biblical theophany conventions rather than references to one particular Sinai account.
- The cluster may reflect inherited oral tradition rather than deliberate literary construction by Mark.
- Moses may be present principally as a representative of the law, independent of the other Sinai correspondences.
