# Internal Textual And Pastoral Evidence in 2 Corinthians 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter provides strong textual evidence that Paul evaluates sorrow by what it produces rather than treating emotional pain as an end in itself: he denies rejoicing merely because the Corinthians were made sorry, rejoices because their sorrow led to repentance, contrasts salvation with death, and enumerates the responses their sorrow produced.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a fixed sequence of stages, a universal test for another person's sincerity, or that sorrow earns salvation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The list in verse 11 may be rhetorical praise tailored to one crisis rather than a universal checklist for repentance.
- The phrase "after a godly manner" may concern the sorrow's orientation or relation to God as well as its subsequent results.
- Several items in verse 11 describe attitudes or communal responses rather than easily measurable behavior.
