# Cumulative Textual Argument in 1 Corinthians 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated contrast between unintelligible and intelligible speech strongly supports the conclusion that, in this chapter, a claimed spiritual origin does not by itself make public speech beneficial; benefit to the church requires communicable meaning.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every genuine spiritual experience must be immediately intelligible, nor does it disprove private or interpreted use of tongues.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Private speech to God may still possess devotional value even when other people do not understand it.
- Interpretation, rather than abandonment of tongues, is Paul's stated remedy in public worship.
