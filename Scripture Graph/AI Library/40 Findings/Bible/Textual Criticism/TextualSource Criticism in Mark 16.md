# TextualSource Criticism in Mark 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Because Mormon 9's 'signs following believers' passage closely matches the King James wording of Mark 16:17-18 — a passage (16:9-20) that mainstream textual scholarship regards as likely absent from the earliest recoverable Greek text of Mark — the parallel is worth weighing honestly as part of the broader, well-documented phenomenon of KJV-derived language appearing throughout the Book of Mormon's English translation.

**Does not establish:** This observation does not establish anything about the historicity of the Nephite record or the mechanism of Joseph Smith's translation; it only flags a wording parallel worth disclosing rather than glossing over.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The 'signs following believers' teaching may reflect an authentic early Christian catechetical saying transmitted independently of Mark's manuscript tradition (e.g., orally, or via other lost sources), so the underlying content need not depend on the disputed ending of Mark even if the English wording does.
- Consistent with the traditional LDS view that Joseph Smith rendered the translation into the biblical idiom available to him, matching KJV phrasing here does not by itself indicate literary dependence on Mark's specific (and possibly late) textual tradition, since the same phenomenon occurs across many Book of Mormon passages regardless of the source text's critical status.
- The scholarly dating of Mark's longer ending as 'later' is a majority position, not a certainty; some manuscripts and patristic citations attest the passage very early, so the premise that this content postdates the apostolic era is itself contested.
