# Literary And Contextual in Isaiah 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The combination of a named judgment on Babylon with references to the world, the darkening of heavenly bodies, and the shaking of heaven and earth supplies meaningful literary evidence for reading Babylon’s fall as a pattern of a more comprehensive divine judgment. The evidence supports a typological reading but does not prove that every detail refers directly or exclusively to the Second Coming.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish an exclusive Second Coming referent, a detailed end-times chronology, or that every statement awaits literal future fulfillment.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The cosmic language may be conventional prophetic hyperbole for the collapse of a political order.
- The universal language may intensify the Babylon oracle without introducing a second, future fulfillment.
- A later reader may validly apply the imagery typologically even if the oracle’s primary horizon is historical Babylon.
