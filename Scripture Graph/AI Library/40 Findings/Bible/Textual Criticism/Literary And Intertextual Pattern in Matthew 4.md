# Literary And Intertextual Pattern in Matthew 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The combination of forty days in the wilderness and three replies drawn from Deuteronomy 6–8 provides substantial literary support for reading Jesus as reenacting Israel's wilderness testing while remaining obedient. This is a well-grounded interpretation, although Matthew does not explicitly explain the forty days as a symbol of Israel's forty years.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not by itself establish the episode's precise historical form, the author's sources, or that every narrative detail was designed as an Israel parallel.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The forty-day period may primarily signify an extended fast without specifically representing Israel's forty years.
- The Deuteronomy cluster may reflect the suitability of those commandments for resisting temptation rather than a developed Israel typology.
- Some elements of the perceived pattern may arise from later readers combining separate allusions.
