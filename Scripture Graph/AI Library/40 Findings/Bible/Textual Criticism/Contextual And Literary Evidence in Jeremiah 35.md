# Contextual And Literary Evidence in Jeremiah 35

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter strongly supports reading the Rechabite wine rule as a particular ancestral command used to demonstrate obedience, rather than as a universal command imposed on Judah or all readers. The text consistently attributes the rule to Jonadab, joins it to several other Rechabite practices, and identifies Judah's required response as repentance from evil and idolatry.

**Does not establish:** It does not determine whether abstinence may be required by another covenant or revelation, nor does it establish a direct historical connection to the Word of Wisdom.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The divine commendation may approve the Rechabite practices themselves as well as the group's fidelity.
- Later religious communities may legitimately apply the example to their own abstinence commitments without claiming that Jeremiah 35 creates a universal law.
