# Internal Textual Pattern in Deuteronomy 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
As internal literary evidence, the prescribed speakers, locations, loud proclamation, fixed response, and twelvefold repetition strongly support identifying the passage as a formal communal ceremony rather than an unstructured list of prohibitions.

**Does not establish:** The literary form does not by itself establish when the passage was composed, how its present form developed, or whether the ceremony occurred historically exactly as prescribed.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passage may preserve an ideal or literary ritual prescription rather than a transcript of an event actually performed.
- The repeated formula may reflect editorial standardization of diverse laws.
- Public verbal assent does not establish each participant's inward agreement.
