# Literary Intertextuality in Isaiah 51

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The clustered references to a dried sea, a path through its depths, redeemed people, and return to Zion provide substantial textual evidence that the hoped-for restoration is being depicted through an exodus-shaped pattern of deliverance.

**Does not establish:** The imagery does not establish that the promised return is a literal repetition of the Exodus, nor does it by itself determine the date or single historical fulfillment of the oracle.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Sea-drying imagery could function as general poetic language for divine power rather than as a detailed typological program.
- Rahab and the dragon may contribute broader chaos-conflict imagery alongside, rather than simply identifying, the Exodus event.
