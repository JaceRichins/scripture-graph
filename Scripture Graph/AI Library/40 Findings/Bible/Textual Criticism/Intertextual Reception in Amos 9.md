# Intertextual Reception in Amos 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The explicit use of Amos 9:11-12 in Acts 15 is strong evidence that an early Christian author understood the oracle as relevant to the incorporation of Gentiles into God's people. It is evidence about Christian reception and application, not by itself a conclusive reconstruction of the oracle's sole original historical meaning.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that Amos's original audience understood the passage exactly as Acts applies it, nor that every detail has only one fulfillment.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Acts may present a contextual apostolic application rather than claim that Gentile inclusion exhausts every possible fulfillment of Amos.
- Differences between the wording of Amos and Acts may reflect the textual form or translation used by the author of Acts.
- The oracle may have had an initial horizon involving Israel, Davidic rule, and neighboring nations before its Christian application.
