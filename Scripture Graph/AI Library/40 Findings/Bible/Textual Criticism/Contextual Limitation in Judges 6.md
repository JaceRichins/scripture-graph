# Contextual Limitation in Judges 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter provides weak support for treating 'putting out a fleece' as a universal method of decision-making. Gideon has already received a direct commission, a promise, a miraculous sign, and the Spirit before requesting the fleece signs; the immediate issue is reassurance about an announced mission.

**Does not establish:** The episode does not by itself prove that requesting signs is always improper or always appropriate.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some readers understand God's compliance as tacit approval of Gideon's procedure.
- The account may illustrate divine patience with uncertainty without either commanding or condemning similar requests.
- The narrative does not explicitly state Gideon's motive beyond his desire to know that God will save Israel by his hand.
