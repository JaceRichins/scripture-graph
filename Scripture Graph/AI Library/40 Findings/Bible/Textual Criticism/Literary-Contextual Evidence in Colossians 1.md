# Literary-Contextual Evidence in Colossians 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The movement from reconciliation of 'all things' to the Colossians' personal reconciliation supports reading the passage as cosmic in scope but concretely concerned with transformed human relationship to God.

**Does not establish:** The wording does not by itself establish universal salvation, define the final destiny of every person or spiritual power, or eliminate the chapter's stated concern with continuing in faith.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The phrase 'reconcile all things' may emphasize Christ's universal supremacy without specifying the eventual saving condition of every individual.
- Reconciliation can include the pacification or subjection of hostile powers rather than universal forgiveness.
- Verse 23's appeal to continued faith cautions against treating the passage as an explicit statement of unconditional universal salvation.
