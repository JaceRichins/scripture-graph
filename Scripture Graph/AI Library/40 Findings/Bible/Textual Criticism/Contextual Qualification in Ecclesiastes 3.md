# Contextual Qualification in Ecclesiastes 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The juxtaposition of confidence in God's judgment with a question about the spirit's destination is evidence that the chapter holds moral accountability and epistemic limitation together; interpreting it as a simple denial of judgment or of all postmortem existence goes beyond its stated claims.

**Does not establish:** These verses do not specify when judgment occurs or provide a complete account of resurrection, the spirit world, or the state of the dead.

**Alternative explanations:**
- God's judgment in verse 17 could be understood as occurring within mortal history rather than after death.
- Verse 21 may rhetorically challenge doubt rather than express the speaker's own uncertainty.
- Different voices or stages within the book's argument may account for the tension.
