# Literary And Intertextual Pattern in Hosea 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated, nonchronological selection of Jacob and exodus traditions is strong literary evidence that Hosea is using Israel's remembered origins to interpret its present conduct: Jacob's struggle leads to an appeal to return, while earlier dependence and prophetic preservation expose Ephraim's proud self-reliance.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not by itself establish the chapter's date, single authorship, compositional history, or the historicity of the ancestral events.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The references could be traditional prophetic examples assembled without a tightly unified literary design.
- Individual ancestral episodes may carry meanings different from the dependence-and-return pattern proposed here.
- The present ordering may reflect stages of composition or transmission rather than a single rhetorical plan.
