# Textual Practice in Acts 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Acts 16 explicitly reports the baptism of Lydia and her household and, later, of the jailer and all his. These are firm textual examples of household-associated conversion, but the chapter does not give the ages of household members and cannot by itself establish either infant baptism or its absence.

**Does not establish:** The account does not establish the presence of infants, a universal rule for household baptism, or a complete theology of eligibility for baptism.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The households may have consisted entirely of people capable of hearing and responding.
- The household formula may summarize a collective event without specifying each member's age or individual experience.
- Readers from different baptismal traditions may import assumptions that the passage itself does not state.
