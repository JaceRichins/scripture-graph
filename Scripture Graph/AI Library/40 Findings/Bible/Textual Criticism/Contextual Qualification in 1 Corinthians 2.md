# Contextual Qualification in 1 Corinthians 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Paul's explicit affirmation that he speaks wisdom, together with his contrast between divine and worldly wisdom, strongly supports reading the passage as a critique of wisdom defined apart from God rather than as a wholesale rejection of thought or learning.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every intellectual system is compatible with the gospel, that education is spiritually sufficient, or that every claimed spiritual insight is reliable.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passage may still imply a radical subordination of ordinary reasoning to revelation.
- Because Paul says this wisdom is spoken among the 'perfect,' his affirmation may concern a restricted audience or level of maturity rather than learning generally.
