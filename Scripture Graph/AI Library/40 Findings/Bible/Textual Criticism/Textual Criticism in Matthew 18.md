# Textual Criticism in Matthew 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Matthew 18:11 ('For the Son of man is come to save that which was lost') is absent from the earliest surviving Greek manuscripts of Matthew and is bracketed or dropped in modern critical editions and many modern translations; mainstream textual critics generally regard it as an early scribal addition drawn from the nearly identical wording of Luke 19:10, rather than as original to Matthew.

**Does not establish:** This textual-critical observation does not affect the verse's status as scripture in traditions (including the KJV-based LDS canon) that follow the fuller Byzantine/Textus Receptus textual tradition; it bears on manuscript history, not canonical authority.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The verse could be original and instead have dropped out of some early manuscripts by scribal error (e.g., eye-skip between similar surrounding phrases) rather than being added elsewhere.
- Manuscript evidence for the Gospels before the 4th century is sparse, so absence from the earliest witnesses is not conclusive proof of non-originality.
