# TextualPhilological Argument in Genesis 49

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
'Shiloh' in Genesis 49:10 is read within Latter-day Saint and much of the broader Christian interpretive tradition as a messianic title referring to Jesus Christ, but the term's meaning is philologically disputed: it has also been read as a place name (the town of Shiloh), or reconstructed from a different Hebrew consonantal division as 'he whose it is' or 'until tribute comes to him.'

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that the original audience or author understood the term as a personal messianic title; the messianic reading is a later interpretive tradition, however venerable.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Shiloh as the geographic town where the tabernacle later stood, with the clause meaning 'until [the scepter reaches/comes to] Shiloh.'
- A revocalization of the Hebrew yielding 'until he comes to whom it belongs' (a royal-right formula rather than a proper name).
- A corrupted or obscure term whose original sense is simply not recoverable with confidence.
