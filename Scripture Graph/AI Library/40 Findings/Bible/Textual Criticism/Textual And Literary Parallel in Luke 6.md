# Textual And Literary Parallel in Luke 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Substantial correspondences connect Luke's sermon with the Sermon on the Mount and, through its close relationship to Matthew, the Sermon at the Temple: beatitudes, enemy-love, judgment and the beam, fruit imagery, and the two builders occur in comparable clusters. This is strong evidence of a shared Jesus-tradition or literary relationship, but the comparison alone does not determine direction of dependence.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not by themselves establish which Gospel form is earlier, direct literary dependence, the precise historical setting of each discourse, or independent antiquity for the 3 Nephi wording.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Matthew and Luke may independently preserve overlapping oral or written traditions.
- One evangelist may depend directly or indirectly on material also used by the other.
- Similar sayings may have been delivered by Jesus on multiple occasions.
- The 3 Nephi parallel primarily reflects its close textual relationship with Matthew and does not independently resolve the relationship between Matthew and Luke.
