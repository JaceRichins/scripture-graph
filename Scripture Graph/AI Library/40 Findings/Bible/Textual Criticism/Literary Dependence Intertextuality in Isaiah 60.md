# Literary Dependence Intertextuality in Isaiah 60

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Isaiah 60:19-20's image of a city needing no sun or moon because God himself is its light is widely noted by biblical scholars (of varying faith commitments) as source material echoed in Revelation 21:23-25 and 22:5, which describe the New Jerusalem in strikingly similar terms.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that Revelation's author consciously quoted or intended to fulfill Isaiah 60 specifically, nor does it bear on the historical dating or authorship of either book.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Both texts may independently draw on a common stock of ancient Near Eastern / Second Temple apocalyptic imagery about divine light replacing celestial bodies, rather than Revelation directly borrowing from Isaiah.
- The resemblance could reflect shared liturgical or prophetic idiom rather than conscious literary quotation by John of Patmos.
