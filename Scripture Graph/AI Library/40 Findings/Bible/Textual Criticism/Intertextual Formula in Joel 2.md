# Intertextual Formula in Joel 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The description of God in Joel 2:13 as 'gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil' closely echoes the same divine-attribute formula found in Jonah 4:2 and Nehemiah 9:17, both of which are flagged as verified text-overlap parallels for this chapter. This is a recognized recurring liturgical formula in the Hebrew Bible (rooted ultimately in the Exodus 34:6-7 self-revelation of God), reused by multiple later biblical authors.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish that Joel directly quotes Jonah or Nehemiah, or resolve the disputed date of Joel; it only establishes that the phrase was a shared theological formula across these texts.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The shared wording may reflect a common liturgical/creedal formula in wide circulation (like a known confession) rather than direct literary borrowing between these specific books.
- Direction of dependency (which text is earliest) is disputed among scholars and depends on unresolved dating of Joel.
