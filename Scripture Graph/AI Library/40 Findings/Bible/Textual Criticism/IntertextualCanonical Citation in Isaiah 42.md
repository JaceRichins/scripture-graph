# IntertextualCanonical Citation in Isaiah 42

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The New Testament directly cites Isaiah 42:1-4 in Matthew 12 (confirmed by the index's verified text-overlap data) and applies the servant description to Jesus's ministry, treating the passage as fulfilled prophecy. This is one of the strongest intertextual anchors for a messianic reading of 42:1-4 within Christian tradition, though it is a later interpretive application rather than proof of the eighth/sixth-century audience's own understanding of the referent.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that Isaiah 42:1-4's original author or audience intended an exclusively individual, future-messianic referent; it establishes only that later Christian scripture reads it that way.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The Servant may denote collective Israel or a faithful remnant, consistent with the chapter's own application of 'servant' to a blind/disobedient Jacob-Israel figure (42:19-25).
- Matthew's citation could reflect an early Christian typological/fulfillment reading rather than a claim about the prophet's original single intended referent.
- Some historical-critical proposals identify the Servant with an unnamed contemporary figure of the exilic period rather than a distant messianic individual.
