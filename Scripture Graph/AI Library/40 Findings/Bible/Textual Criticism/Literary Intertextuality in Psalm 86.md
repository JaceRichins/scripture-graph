# Literary Intertextuality in Psalm 86

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close parallels with Exodus 34:6 and Psalm 54:3 are strong evidence that Psalm 86 participates in established biblical language for describing God and petitioning against enemies. They do not by themselves determine whether the psalmist directly copied particular texts or drew upon shared worship traditions.

**Does not establish:** These parallels do not establish a date, historical setting, named author, or direction of literary dependence.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passages may independently use a common stock of Israelite prayer language.
- A later editor or collector may have shaped the wording.
- The parallels may reflect oral liturgical tradition rather than direct textual borrowing.
- The direction of any literary dependence cannot be established from verbal similarity alone.
