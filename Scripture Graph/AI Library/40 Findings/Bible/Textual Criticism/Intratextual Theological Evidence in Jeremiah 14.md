# Intratextual Theological Evidence in Jeremiah 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The passage provides strong internal evidence that religious observances do not mechanically secure divine acceptance: fasting, crying, burnt offering, and oblation are all named, yet the Lord refuses to hear or accept them in this setting.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that fasting, prayer, or sacrifice are generally ineffective, nor that God never responds to a sinful person's prayer.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The refusal may describe an exceptional point of judgment rather than a universal rule about unanswered prayer.
- The passage rejects the people's conduct and condition, not fasting, prayer, or sacrifice as practices in themselves.
