# Literary Intertextuality in Romans 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of identifiable scriptural language and explicit attributions to Moses and Isaiah is strong textual evidence that Romans 10 deliberately constructs its argument through Israel's scriptures.

**Does not establish:** The presence of deliberate quotation does not establish that Paul's applications exhaust the original passages' meanings or that every interpretive step would have been accepted by other ancient Jewish readers.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some wording may have reached Paul through established Jewish or early Christian interpretive traditions rather than originating with him.
- Individual citations may function as localized proof texts without requiring every passage to form one unified exegetical scheme.
- Differences from familiar Hebrew or English wording may reflect a Greek textual tradition rather than deliberate alteration.
