# Intratextual Narrative Causation in 2 Kings 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within Kings' own theological narrative, Manasseh's idolatry, corruption of Judah, and shedding of innocent blood are presented as major reasons for the coming catastrophe upon Jerusalem. The later statement in 2 Kings 24:3-4 explicitly recalls Manasseh and the innocent blood he shed, strongly supporting this intratextual reading.

**Does not establish:** This literary connection does not establish that Manasseh's reign was the sole historical cause of Babylon's conquest or eliminate geopolitical explanations.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The text supplies a theological interpretation of the exile rather than a complete account of its political and military causes.
- The communal history of disobedience in 2 Kings 21:15 prevents reducing the catastrophe solely to one king.
- The long interval between Manasseh and Jerusalem's fall means the narrative's moral causation should not be mistaken for simple immediate historical causation.
