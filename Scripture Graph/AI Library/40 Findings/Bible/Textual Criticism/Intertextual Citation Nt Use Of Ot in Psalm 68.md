# Intertextual Citation Nt Use Of Ot in Psalm 68

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ephesians 4:8 quotes Psalm 68:18 in connection with Christ's ascension, but changes 'thou hast received gifts for men' to 'he...gave gifts unto men,' reversing the direction of giving. This textual discrepancy is well documented and is commonly cited as the basis for reading Psalm 68:18 messianically/christologically.

**Does not establish:** That Psalm 68:18 was originally composed as a direct verbal prophecy of Jesus Christ's ascension; the New Testament application is a later inspired/typological reading, not something demonstrable from the psalm's own Old Testament setting.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Paul may be following an Aramaic Targum or paraphrase tradition that already read 'gave' rather than 'received,' rather than deliberately altering the Hebrew/Greek text himself.
- 'Received' and 'gave' may describe two stages of one ancient royal-triumph convention (a victor receives tribute/captives, then redistributes gifts to followers) rather than a contradiction.
