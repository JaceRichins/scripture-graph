# Textual-Theological in Genesis 45

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Joseph's repeated distinction between the brothers' sale of him and God's preservative purpose is strong textual evidence that the chapter presents a providential interpretation of adversity. It is not, by itself, proof that the brothers lacked agency or culpability.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a general rule that harmful human actions are divinely caused, excuse the sale of Joseph, or independently prove the historical operation of providence.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Joseph may be using emphatic language chiefly to relieve his brothers' fear and self-reproach.
- The statement may express a theological interpretation imposed on events without defining how divine and human causation relate.
- A reader may regard the speech as the narrator's or a later tradent's theology rather than a recoverable historical statement by Joseph.
