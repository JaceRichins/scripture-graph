# IntratextualLiterary in 2 Kings 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The judgment oracle of 2 Kings 21:11-16 is not left as an isolated threat: 2 Kings 24:3-4 later states that the Babylonian removal of Judah came 'for the sins of Manasseh, according to all that he did,' explicitly including the innocent blood he shed. This ties the immediate narrative of this chapter to the book's own explanation of the exile narrated many chapters later, showing a deliberate compositional link within Kings rather than a one-off oracle.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that Manasseh alone, apart from later kings' conduct, was historically sufficient to cause the exile; it establishes only that the book of Kings presents his sin as the stated theological reason.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The exile in 2 Kings 24-25 also follows from later kings' own political rebellions (e.g., Jehoiakim's and Zedekiah's revolts against Babylon), so Manasseh's sin functions as the Deuteronomistic historian's theological explanation of ultimate cause rather than the sole narrated proximate cause.
