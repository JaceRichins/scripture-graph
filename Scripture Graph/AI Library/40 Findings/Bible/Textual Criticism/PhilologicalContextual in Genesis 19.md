# PhilologicalContextual in Genesis 19

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The verb 'know' (Hebrew yada) carries a demonstrable sexual sense elsewhere in Genesis (e.g., 'Adam knew Eve his wife,' Genesis 4:1), and the narrative context here (a threatening mob, Lot's counter-offer of his daughters 'which have not known man') strongly supports reading the mob's demand as intent toward sexual assault of the guests rather than mere interrogation.

**Does not establish:** This does not by itself establish a specific ancient Near Eastern legal or social meaning beyond what the immediate narrative context supports.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A minority of interpreters read 'know' here as merely 'formally interrogate/identify' the strangers, treating the episode primarily as a violation of hospitality/xenophobia rather than a sexual threat; this reading struggles to explain why Lot's counter-offer is his daughters' bodies.
