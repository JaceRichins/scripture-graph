# Intertextual Tension in Ezekiel 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ezekiel 18:20's claim that a son will not bear his father's iniquity stands in apparent tension with Exodus 20:5 and Deuteronomy 5:9, which describe the LORD 'visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation.'

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that the two texts are logically contradictory in their original settings, nor which view (if either) is doctrinally primary; both remain canonical.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Exodus/Deuteronomy may describe corporate or natural consequences (living under conditions a parent's sin created) while Ezekiel addresses personal moral guilt before God — a harmonization many interpreters use.
- The tension may reflect genuine development in Israelite theology toward individual accountability, plausibly sharpened by the crisis of exile.
- Ezekiel 18 may be a targeted pastoral corrective to a specific fatalistic misuse of the proverb rather than a comprehensive repudiation of Exodus 20:5's principle.
