# Internal Textual Pattern in Amos 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter provides strong textual evidence that its prophetic critique targets complacent privilege joined to indifference and corrupted justice, rather than merely cataloguing expensive possessions.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that wealth, music, comfortable furniture, wine, or ointment is intrinsically sinful in every context.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The luxury items may also function as symbols of ruling-class arrogance or illegitimate political power, not only social indifference.
- The chapter does not specify how each individual acquired the listed goods, so economic exploitation should not be inferred for every person solely from verses 4–6.
