# Intertextual Authorial Framing in Matthew 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Matthew explicitly presents Jesus' withdrawal, healings, and command not to publicize him as fulfillment of Isaiah's servant passage. This is strong internal evidence that the narrator interprets Jesus through the servant portrait of Isaiah 42.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently establish the historicity of every narrated event, the date of composition, or predictive fulfillment as a conclusion binding on readers who do not share Matthew's premises.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The fulfillment formula establishes the narrator's theological interpretation but does not by itself demonstrate that Isaiah uniquely predicted these events.
- Differences between Matthew's wording and the KJV wording of Isaiah may reflect textual tradition, translation, or authorial adaptation.
