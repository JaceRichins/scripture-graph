# TextualHistorical in 2 Corinthians 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The exact nature of Paul's 'thorn in the flesh' (12:7) is not specified anywhere in the text, and the phrase has been variously proposed by interpreters across history to mean a chronic physical ailment (e.g., an eye condition, epilepsy, or recurring illness), ongoing persecution or opposition from rivals, or a recurring temptation; the passage itself supports only that it was some persistent, humbling affliction linked to demonic buffeting, not any specific identification.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish which, if any, of the traditional identifications of the thorn is correct; the text itself leaves it deliberately unspecified.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Physical illness (various specific diagnoses have been proposed, none provable from the text)
- Persistent opposition from human adversaries/rival teachers
- A recurring spiritual or moral temptation
- A rhetorical figure representing the general burden of apostolic suffering rather than one discrete affliction
