# Literary Intertextuality in Jeremiah 48

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The paired correspondences involving Moab's pride, mourning for Kir-heres, Sibmah and Jazer, the loss of harvest joy, and high-place worship constitute strong evidence of a literary or shared-traditional relationship between Jeremiah 48 and Isaiah 16.

**Does not establish:** The correspondences alone do not establish the direction of dependence, the date of either textual unit, or a single author for both passages.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Jeremiah may have adapted material from Isaiah.
- Both passages may draw upon an older shared oracle or lament concerning Moab.
- Some form of later editorial reuse or integration may account for the relationship.
- The KJV can accentuate English verbal similarity, although the shared sequence, themes, and place names remain significant.
