# Internal Textual Evidence in James 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter provides strong internal evidence that its positive model is faith acting together with works: Abraham's faith works with his deeds and is made perfect by them, while the body-spirit analogy treats works as integral to living faith. This supports a reading in which James attacks inactive profession rather than treating faith and action as independent alternatives.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself settle every later dispute about grace, merit, the order of salvation, or the precise relationship between James and Paul.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language may be read primarily as a correction of a particular opponent rather than as a comprehensive definition of saving faith.
- Different theological traditions disagree over whether the works complete faith, demonstrate genuine faith, participate in justification, or do some combination of these.
