# Textual And Intertextual Ambiguity in James 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The words introduced in James 4:5 as what scripture says do not reproduce any readily identifiable Old Testament verse. The sentence's subject and sense are also debated, making detailed doctrine built solely on this verse unusually uncertain.

**Does not establish:** The absence of an exact surviving parallel does not establish that James cited a lost book, that the text is corrupt, or that the statement lacks a scriptural basis.

**Alternative explanations:**
- James may be paraphrasing a scriptural theme rather than quoting one verse.
- The introductory formula may refer more broadly to preceding or following scriptural teaching.
- Different punctuation and choices about whether the spirit is human or divine produce different readings.
