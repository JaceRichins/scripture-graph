# Cumulative Textual Scope in Isaiah 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated references to the earth and world, the inclusion of every social rank, praise from the uttermost earth, and judgment of both high powers and earthly kings strongly support a universal literary horizon. This is stronger evidence for the scope of the chapter's rhetoric than for the geographic extent of any single historical event.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every image predicts one physically global event or that all details must be read literally.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Prophetic poetry can universalize a regional political disaster through deliberate hyperbole.
- The English terms "earth" and "land" may allow movement between worldwide and territorial senses.
- Different sections may widen the horizon beyond the immediate setting without describing one uniformly global event.
