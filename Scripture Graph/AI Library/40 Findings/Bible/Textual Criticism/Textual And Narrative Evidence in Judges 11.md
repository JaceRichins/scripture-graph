# Textual And Narrative Evidence in Judges 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
In the KJV wording and the narrative sequence, the most direct reading is that Jephthah fulfilled the promised burnt offering upon his daughter. The conclusion remains interpretively disputed because the text never explicitly describes her death and repeatedly emphasizes her virginity.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that God commanded or approved the vow, that victory depended on it, or that every detail corresponds to a recoverable historical event.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The daughter may have been permanently dedicated to religious service and perpetual virginity rather than killed; the repeated references to virginity are the principal support for this reading.
- Some interpreters understand the vow's conjunction disjunctively, allowing dedication to the LORD or a burnt offering according to what emerged.
- The narrator may be reporting the outcome euphemistically, which would explain the absence of an explicit description of killing without changing the sacrificial reading.
