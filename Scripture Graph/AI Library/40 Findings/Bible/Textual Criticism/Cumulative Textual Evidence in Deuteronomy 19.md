# Cumulative Textual Evidence in Deuteronomy 19

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Taken together, accessible refuge, a distinction between accidental and premeditated killing, the multiple-witness threshold, and diligent judicial inquiry provide strong textual evidence that the chapter is concerned with preventing wrongful death and wrongful conviction while still punishing established wrongdoing.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that ancient Israel possessed modern due-process institutions, that these laws were implemented uniformly, or that every penalty described is ethically equivalent to modern law.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The provisions may chiefly preserve communal order and bloodguilt purity rather than express an abstract theory of individual rights.
- The chapter does not describe every procedural step, so comparison with a modern judicial system can overstate the resemblance.
