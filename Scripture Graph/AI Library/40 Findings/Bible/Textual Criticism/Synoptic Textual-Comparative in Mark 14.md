# Synoptic Textual-Comparative in Mark 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
When the high priest asks 'Art thou the Christ, the Son of the Blessed?', Mark records Jesus answering unambiguously, 'I am,' whereas Matthew's parallel has the more indirect 'Thou hast said,' and Luke's parallel has 'Ye say that I am' — a checkable wording difference across the three Synoptic trial accounts.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that Mark's wording is historically more accurate than Matthew's or Luke's, nor resolve the underlying Aramaic/Hebrew idiom Jesus may have used.

**Alternative explanations:**
- All three may be free paraphrases of a single underlying Aramaic exchange, so surface wording differences may not reflect a real difference in what was said
- Matthew's and Luke's more indirect phrasing could reflect a Jewish idiom of affirmation-by-repetition rather than evasion, making the accounts less divergent in meaning than the English wording suggests
- Mark's directness may reflect his generally blunter narrative style rather than superior historical precision
