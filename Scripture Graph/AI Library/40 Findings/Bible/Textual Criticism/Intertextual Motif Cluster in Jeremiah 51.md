# Intertextual Motif Cluster in Jeremiah 51

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Revelation 17-18 shares a concentrated cluster of Jeremiah 51 motifs: Babylon's golden cup, nations made drunk, the announcement that Babylon has fallen, sins reaching heaven, the command for God's people to leave her, and a stone cast into water to dramatize irreversible collapse. The density and order of these motifs strongly support literary reuse of Jeremiah's Babylon oracle in Revelation's portrayal of Babylon.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not establish that every detail of Jeremiah 51 predicts one later city or that Revelation preserves Jeremiah's original historical referent unchanged.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Revelation may draw on a wider collection of Babylon traditions, including Isaiah, rather than on Jeremiah 51 alone.
- Some individual motifs, such as intoxication or falling cities, could arise independently; the cumulative cluster is more probative than any one parallel.
