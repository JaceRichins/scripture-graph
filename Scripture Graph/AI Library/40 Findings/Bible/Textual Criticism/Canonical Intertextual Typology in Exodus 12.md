# Canonical Intertextual Typology in Exodus 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Later Christian scripture explicitly applies Passover language to Christ, and John's passion narrative associates Jesus' unbroken bones with scriptural fulfillment. Together with Exodus 12:46, these texts provide strong evidence that early Christians read Jesus through Passover imagery, though they do not by themselves prove that every detail of Exodus 12 was originally composed as a prediction of Jesus.

**Does not establish:** This intertextual pattern does not establish that Exodus 12 originally described Jesus explicitly, that every Passover detail has a Christian counterpart, or that typology independently proves theological claims about Jesus.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The Christian authors may be applying Israel's Passover imagery retrospectively rather than identifying the sole original meaning of Exodus 12.
- John 19:36 may also evoke Psalm 34:20, so its immediate scriptural background need not be limited to Exodus 12:46.
- Some similarities may arise from deliberate literary presentation in the later texts rather than from independent historical correspondence.
