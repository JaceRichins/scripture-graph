# Textual Criticism in John 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The account of the woman taken in adultery in John 8:1-11 is absent from several of the earliest and most important Greek witnesses, and manuscripts that include it sometimes place it elsewhere. Textual critics therefore broadly regard the passage as secondary to the earliest recoverable form of John, while its historical origin remains a separate question.

**Does not establish:** The manuscript evidence does not establish that the narrated event never occurred, nor does it decide how a faith community should assess the passage's canonical or devotional authority.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passage could have been original and omitted early because readers feared that it appeared too lenient toward adultery.
- A circulating account about Jesus may have been inserted into John after the Gospel's initial composition.
- Its unstable manuscript position may reflect liturgical handling rather than a simple one-stage interpolation.
