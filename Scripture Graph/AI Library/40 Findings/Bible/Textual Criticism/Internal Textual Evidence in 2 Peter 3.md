# Internal Textual Evidence in 2 Peter 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Second Peter 3:15-16 is strong internal evidence that the literary setting represented by the epistle knew a plurality of Pauline letters and accorded them scriptural significance. It is evidence about the status of Paul's writings in that setting, not sufficient proof of a closed New Testament canon or of the epistle's date and authorship.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a closed New Testament canon, the exact contents of the Pauline collection, the date of composition, or Petrine authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- If the epistle is later or pseudonymous, the passage may reflect a post-Petrine stage in the reception of Paul's letters.
- The category called scripture here need not have had all the fixed boundaries associated with a later closed canon.
- The comparison may emphasize similar distortion of the two bodies of writing without defining the precise canonical status of every Pauline letter.
