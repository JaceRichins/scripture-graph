# Textual And Contextual in Romans 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Romans 13:1–7 is strong textual evidence that Paul regarded submission, tax payment, and respect for governing authorities as normal Christian obligations. It is substantially weaker evidence for unconditional obedience to every governmental command because the passage does not explicitly address commands that conflict with obedience to God.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every ruler or policy has divine approval, that government is morally infallible, or that obedience remains required when authorities command disobedience to God.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The statements may be read as an absolute doctrine of submission rather than a description of normal civic duty.
- The counsel may respond primarily to the practical circumstances of Christians living under Roman rule.
- The morally positive description of rulers may function as an ideal or generalization rather than a claim about every regime.
