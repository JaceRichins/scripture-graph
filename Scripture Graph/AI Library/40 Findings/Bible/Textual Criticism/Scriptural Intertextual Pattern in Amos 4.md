# Scriptural Intertextual Pattern in Amos 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of hunger, drought, blight, mildew, crop loss, pestilence, sword, and devastation closely parallels the covenant-warning catalogues of Leviticus 26 and Deuteronomy 28. This strongly supports reading Amos 4:6-11 within an Israelite covenantal framework.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not establish direct dependence on a particular written edition, a precise date of composition, or that every reported disaster can be matched to a known historical event.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Agricultural failure, warfare, and disease were common ancient experiences and could produce similar lists without direct textual borrowing.
- Amos and the legal texts may draw independently upon a shared covenant tradition.
- Later editors may have shaped either passage, so literary direction cannot be inferred from resemblance alone.
