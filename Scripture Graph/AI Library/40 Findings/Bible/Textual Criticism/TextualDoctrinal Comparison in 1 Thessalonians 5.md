# TextualDoctrinal Comparison in 1 Thessalonians 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Paul's prayer that "your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless" (5:23) uses a threefold anthropological formula (spirit, soul, body) that differs from the combination given in D&C 88:15, "the spirit and the body are the soul of man," where soul denotes the union of spirit and body rather than a third, distinct component.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that Paul held a formal doctrine of three separable human components, nor does it resolve the wording against the different soul/spirit/body relationship taught in D&C 88:15; the comparison is worth noting but the verse alone cannot settle the question.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Many New Testament scholars read "spirit, soul, and body" as an emphatic rhetorical triad meaning simply 'the whole person,' not a technical, ontological trichotomy Paul intended to teach as doctrine.
- Ancient Greek anthropological vocabulary (pneuma, psyche, soma) did not have fixed, universally agreed technical meanings in Paul's day, so building a precise doctrine of composition from this one verse may overread the language.
