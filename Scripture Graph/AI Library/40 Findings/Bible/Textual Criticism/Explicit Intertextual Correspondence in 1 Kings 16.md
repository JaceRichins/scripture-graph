# Explicit Intertextual Correspondence in 1 Kings 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The matching firstborn/foundation and youngest/gates pattern, together with the explicit attribution to Joshua, is strong evidence that 1 Kings 16:34 invokes the Joshua tradition deliberately. The literary link is much firmer than conclusions about the historical mechanism or relative dates of the texts.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish the dates of composition, predictive chronology, or whether Abiram and Segub died through accident, sacrifice, violence, or another mechanism.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The Kings notice may have been composed or edited retrospectively in light of the Joshua wording.
- Both passages may draw on a shared tradition whose development cannot be reconstructed from these verses alone.
- The report may use theological literary patterning to interpret losses associated with construction.
