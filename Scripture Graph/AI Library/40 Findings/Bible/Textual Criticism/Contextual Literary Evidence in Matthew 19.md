# Contextual Literary Evidence in Matthew 19

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The disciples interpret the camel-and-needle saying as raising the question of who can be saved, and Jesus answers with a contrast between human impossibility and divine possibility. This context strongly supports reading the image as severe hyperbole about the incapacity of wealth or human effort to secure entry into God's kingdom, rather than as a merely difficult maneuver.

**Does not establish:** The passage does not establish that possessions are intrinsically evil, that poverty earns salvation, or that every disciple must dispose of property in precisely the same manner as this young man.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The saying is deliberate hyperbole and need not mean that no rich person can ever become a disciple.
- Jesus's statement about impossibility may address salvation generally rather than wealth alone.
