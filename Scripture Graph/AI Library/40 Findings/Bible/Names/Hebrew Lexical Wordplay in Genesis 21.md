# Hebrew Lexical Wordplay in Genesis 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Hebrew narrative develops a sustained wordplay on the consonants ṣ-ḥ-q: the name Isaac, Sarah's laughter, and the activity of Hagar's son translated "mocking" in the KJV are lexically related. The precise force of the word in verse 9 is disputed, ranging from play or laughter to ridicule, so the wordplay is clearer than the nature of the offense.

**Does not establish:** The wordplay does not prove a particular motive for Sarah, a single compositional history, or the narrative's historical accuracy.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The lexical repetition does not determine whether verse 9 describes harmless play, ridicule, or a perceived threat to Isaac.
- The motif could have been strengthened during oral transmission or editorial composition rather than originating in a single act of authorship.
