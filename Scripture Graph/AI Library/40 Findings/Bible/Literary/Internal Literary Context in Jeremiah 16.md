# Internal Literary Context in Jeremiah 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The immediate context provides substantial evidence that the fishers and hunters function primarily as agents or images of inescapable judgment rather than being identified straightforwardly as benevolent gatherers: they hunt people from hiding places because no conduct or iniquity is concealed from the LORD, who next speaks of recompense.

**Does not establish:** The contextual sequence does not prove that the imagery has only one intended sense, nor does it rule out later typological application to missionary gathering.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Because verses 14-15 promise restoration, fishing and hunting may be read as searching for dispersed Israel in order to gather them.
- The imagery may be deliberately polyvalent, joining forced exposure and judgment with an eventual gathering.
- A transition not made explicit in the text could separate the restorative promise of verses 14-15 from a renewed judgment oracle in verses 16-18.
