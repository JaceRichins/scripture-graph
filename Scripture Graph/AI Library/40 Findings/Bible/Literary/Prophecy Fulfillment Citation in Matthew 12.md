# Prophecy Fulfillment Citation in Matthew 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Matthew introduces a quotation of Isaiah 42:1-4 with an explicit fulfillment formula ('that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet'), applying the Servant Song's description of a gentle, non-contentious servant who brings judgment to the Gentiles directly to Jesus' quiet healing ministry.

**Does not establish:** Does not by itself establish that Isaiah 42 was written as a specific, singular prediction of Jesus; establishes that Matthew's Gospel presents Jesus' non-self-promoting healing ministry as fulfilling the pattern of the Isaianic Servant.

**Alternative explanations:**
- In its own historical setting, Isaiah's 'servant' is read by many scholars as ambiguous between an individual figure and personified/corporate Israel, not a name-specific prediction of Jesus centuries in advance.
- Matthew's application reflects early Christian typological (pesher-style) exegesis, reading Israel's earlier scripture through the lens of Jesus' life, rather than the text functioning as an explicit, unambiguous single-fulfillment prophecy.
