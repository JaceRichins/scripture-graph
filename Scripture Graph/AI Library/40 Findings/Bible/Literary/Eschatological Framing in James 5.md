# Eschatological Framing in James 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
James 5:3's phrase "Ye have heaped treasure together for the last days" (i.e., hoarding wealth is itself evidence against the rich in an imminent day of judgment) reflects an apocalyptic expectation that the Lord's coming was near, consistent with 5:8's "the coming of the Lord draweth nigh." Whether this reflects a genuine expectation of an imminent Parousia in the earliest Christian communities, or a more general exhortational use of imminence-language without a fixed timetable, is disputed among scholars.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish a specific timetable James held for the Second Coming, nor settle broader NT debates about early Christian expectations of an imminent Parousia.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The imminence language may function rhetorically (to motivate patience and ethical behavior now) rather than reflecting a precise chronological belief about timing.
- "Last days" language has Old Testament prophetic roots (e.g., Isaiah, Micah) and may be inherited idiom rather than a claim unique to James's own eschatology.
