# Internal Literary Characterization in 2 Kings 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The juxtaposition of divine commendation in verse 30 with explicit condemnation in verses 29 and 31 is strong internal evidence that the narrative does not present Jehu's role as an unqualified endorsement of his whole reign or character.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish which individual killings or tactics, beyond the judgment on Ahab's house expressly named in verse 30, received divine approval.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Verse 30's broad wording may be read as approving all of Jehu's treatment of Ahab's house, even though it still does not commend his calf worship.
- The positive and negative assessments may reflect distinct theological concerns placed together by the compiler rather than a deliberate comment on every violent act.
- Some readers may treat Jehu's religious failure as chronologically or morally separate from the methods by which he established his rule.
