# Narrative Comparison in Ezekiel 23

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sequence in which Aholibah observes Aholah's destruction and then acts more corruptly supports a reading of Jerusalem as especially accountable for refusing a visible warning.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the individual culpability of every inhabitant of Jerusalem or a universal formula for measuring guilt.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The comparison may primarily be a literary device for intensifying Jerusalem's guilt rather than a formal doctrine about degrees of accountability.
- The sisters' stories compress complex histories and do not demonstrate what every member of either population knew.
