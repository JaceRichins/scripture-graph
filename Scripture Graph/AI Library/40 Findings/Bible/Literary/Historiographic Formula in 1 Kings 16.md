# Historiographic Formula in 1 Kings 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated referral to royal chronicles is strong internal evidence that the received narrative presents itself as selective rather than exhaustive. It is only limited evidence about the identity, date, contents, or historical independence of the cited work.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the cited records survive, were contemporaneous with every event, or independently confirm the chapter.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The references may be conventional literary formulas rather than indications that the final writer directly consulted the work.
- The cited chronicles may themselves have contained royal ideology or other biases.
