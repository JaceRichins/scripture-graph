# Literary And Thematic Correspondence in Deuteronomy 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The correspondence between Deuteronomy's royal restrictions and the Solomon narrative strongly supports reading Solomon as exemplifying the dangers those restrictions identify, but it provides only limited evidence for any particular direction of literary dependence or date of composition.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence does not by itself establish the date of Deuteronomy 17, the historical sequence of the texts, or direct borrowing in either direction.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The Kings narrative may evaluate Solomon using an already existing Deuteronomic law.
- The royal law may have been shaped or edited in response to remembered royal abuses.
- Both passages may draw on a shared theological tradition without direct literary dependence.
