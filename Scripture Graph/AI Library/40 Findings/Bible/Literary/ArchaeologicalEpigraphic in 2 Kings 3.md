# ArchaeologicalEpigraphic in 2 Kings 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone), a ninth-century BCE inscription commissioned by King Mesha of Moab, independently attests Mesha's historical existence and describes conflict and territorial recovery from the dynasty of Omri (Ahab's father), providing external corroboration for the general geopolitical setting of 2 Kings 3.

**Does not establish:** The Stele does not establish the historicity of the water miracle, Elisha's oracle, the specific three-king coalition route, or the sacrifice of Mesha's son as narrated in 2 Kings 3; it only supports that a Moab-Israel conflict involving Mesha and the house of Omri occurred in roughly this period.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The Stele's own account of the conflict differs in sequence and emphasis from 2 Kings 3, and may reflect Mesha's own propagandistic framing rather than a neutral record reconcilable with the biblical narrative.
- The Stele does not mention Jehoram, Jehoshaphat, Elisha, the coalition route through Edom, or the water/oracle episode, so it corroborates the broad setting, not this specific chapter's events.
