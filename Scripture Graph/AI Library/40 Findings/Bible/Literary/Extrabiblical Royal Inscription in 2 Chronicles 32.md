# Extrabiblical Royal Inscription in 2 Chronicles 32

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Taylor Prism, a Neo-Assyrian royal inscription produced about 691 BCE, names Hezekiah and describes tribute received from him during Sennacherib's 701 BCE campaign. This is strong extrabiblical support for the historical Assyrian-Judah conflict presupposed by the chapter.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the reported speeches, the angelic destruction, the precise size or manner of Assyrian losses, or divine causation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- As an Assyrian royal inscription, the prism presents events through imperial propaganda and emphasizes submission and tribute.
- Chronicles may preserve or reshape information transmitted through earlier biblical texts and traditions rather than an independent archival account.

**Scripture:** [[2 Chronicles 32#^2chr-32-1|2 Chronicles 32:1]], [[2 Chronicles 32#^2chr-32-9|2 Chronicles 32:9]]

**Sources:**
- British Museum — *The Taylor Prism (Sennacherib Prism), British Museum object 91032* (691 BCE)
