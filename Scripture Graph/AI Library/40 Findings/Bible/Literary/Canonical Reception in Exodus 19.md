# Canonical Reception in Exodus 19

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Deuteronomy 4 and Hebrews 12 preserve recognizable clusters of Sinai imagery—including fire, cloud or darkness, trumpet, voice, and fear. These parallels strongly support the importance of Exodus 19 as a foundational scriptural pattern for describing overwhelming divine revelation.

**Does not establish:** The literary continuity does not independently prove the historical circumstances, date, or natural mechanism of the Sinai event.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some shared imagery can occur generally in storm-theophany traditions.
- The later passages interpret Sinai for their own rhetorical purposes and need not preserve an independent historical account.
