# Historical Chronology in Daniel 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Daniel 1 dates Nebuchadnezzar's arrival at Jerusalem to Jehoiakim's third year, while Jeremiah 25 dates Nebuchadnezzar's first year to Jehoiakim's fourth year. Accession-year versus non-accession-year counting can reduce the apparent one-year discrepancy, but the surviving Babylonian chronicle for 605 BCE records campaigning in the Hatti-land without explicitly naming a siege of Jerusalem. The historical synchronization is therefore plausible under some reconstructions but not directly confirmed in every detail by the extant external record.

**Does not establish:** The discrepancy neither proves that the chapter is historically worthless nor confirms that a siege and deportation occurred exactly as a modern reconstruction describes.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Different Judean and Babylonian regnal-year conventions may account for the third-year/fourth-year difference.
- Daniel 1 may compress several stages of Babylonian pressure, deportation, and court recruitment into one opening scene.
- The narrative may employ a schematic historical setting rather than intend modern chronological precision.
- The Babylonian chronicle's silence about Jerusalem is not proof that no action involving Jerusalem occurred.
