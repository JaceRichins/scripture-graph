# Explicit Literary Typology in 1 Corinthians 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The explicit designation of Israel’s experiences as “examples” and “ensamples,” together with their application to the Corinthian audience, is strong textual evidence that Paul is deliberately reading Israel’s history as morally and theologically instructive for the church.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish the historical accuracy of every narrated detail, a one-to-one symbolism for every Exodus element, or any larger claim about biblical authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Paul may be using historical analogy or moral exempla without intending a comprehensive typological system.
- Individual details need not carry a distinct symbolic meaning merely because the overall narrative is exemplary.
