# Narrative Theological Attribution in Judges 20

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The text strongly supports the limited claim that the narrator attributes Benjamin’s third-day defeat to the LORD. It provides substantially less support for claiming that God approved every Israelite decision, especially the later killing of city populations and animals, because no divine command for that broader destruction is reported.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish independent historical proof of supernatural intervention, nor does it establish divine approval of every act committed by the victorious army.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Readers may infer that the promised victory implicitly authorized the entire campaign, including its aftermath.
- The narrator may expect an unstated warfare convention to supply authorization not repeated in this chapter.
- The attribution may be understood as the narrator’s theological interpretation of the outcome rather than independently verifiable historical causation.
