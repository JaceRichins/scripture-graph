# Narrative And Theological Argument in Luke 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the narrative's argument, the paralytic's visible and immediate healing functions as evidence for Jesus's otherwise invisible claim to possess authority on earth to forgive sins. This is strong evidence for Luke's intended theological portrayal of Jesus's authority, not independent proof that the event occurred historically.

**Does not establish:** The narrative logic by itself does not establish the miracle's historicity, the independence of Luke's source, or a general causal relationship between personal sin and physical illness.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The healing may function primarily as a literary-theological sign within the story.
- A reader may accept the narrative's theological meaning without treating it as independent historical corroboration.
