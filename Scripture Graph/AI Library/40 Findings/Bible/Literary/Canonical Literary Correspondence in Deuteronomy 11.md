# Canonical Literary Correspondence in Deuteronomy 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Deuteronomy 11 appoints Gerizim and Ebal as the locations of blessing and curse, and Joshua 8 later narrates a covenant ceremony involving those mountains and the reading of blessings and curses. This is strong evidence of an intentional canonical narrative link.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence alone does not independently establish the ceremony's historicity, the date of either text, or the direction and stages of literary dependence.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Joshua may have been composed or edited with direct literary dependence on Deuteronomy.
- Both accounts may draw on a shared covenant-ceremony tradition.
- The correspondence may reflect historical observance subsequently expressed through literary shaping.
