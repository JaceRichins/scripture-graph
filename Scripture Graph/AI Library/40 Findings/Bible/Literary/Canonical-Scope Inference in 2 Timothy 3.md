# Canonical-Scope Inference in 2 Timothy 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Because verse 15 identifies scriptures known from Timothy's childhood, the immediate referent of the following statement includes, and most clearly points to, the Jewish sacred writings. Verse 16 strongly supports a theology of divinely originated and useful scripture, but by itself it does not provide a table of contents for either Testament.

**Does not establish:** The passage alone does not identify the complete biblical canon, date its closure, or prove the inspired status of each later canonical book individually.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The broader wording of verse 16 may intentionally extend beyond the childhood scriptures to every writing recognized by the community as scripture.
- Some early Christian writings may already have been treated as scripture, even though this passage does not identify them.
- Interpreters differ over whether the Greek construction should be rendered 'all scripture is God-breathed' or 'every God-breathed scripture is also profitable.'
