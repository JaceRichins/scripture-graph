# Literary Allusion in Isaiah 43

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The combination of a way through the sea, mighty waters, chariot and horse, an army brought forth, and enemies who do not rise strongly supports reading Isaiah 43:16–17 as an evocation of Israel's sea deliverance; verses 19–20 then portray the promised new act with reversed geography—a way in the wilderness and water in the desert.

**Does not establish:** The imagery does not by itself identify a single date, route, political event, or exhaustive fulfillment of the promised new act.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some individual expressions could derive from generic imagery of divine victory over armies and waters.
- The wilderness-water imagery may signify restoration broadly rather than a detailed prediction of a second historical exodus.
