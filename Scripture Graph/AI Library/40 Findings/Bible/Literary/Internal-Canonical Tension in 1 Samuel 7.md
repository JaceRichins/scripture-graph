# Internal-Canonical Tension in 1 Samuel 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
1 Samuel 7:13's claim that the Philistines 'came no more into the coast of Israel' and that 'the hand of the LORD was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel' stands in apparent tension with later chapters that narrate active Philistine-Israelite conflict during Samuel's lifetime — e.g., Philistine control restricting Israelite ironworking (1 Samuel 13:19-20), the Philistine army mustered against Saul with Goliath's challenge (1 Samuel 17:1), and Samuel's death occurring only shortly before David's continued Philistine wars (1 Samuel 25:1).

**Does not establish:** that 1 Samuel 7:13-14 is factually inaccurate or a late/inserted claim; only that its totalizing language functions rhetorically as a summarizing 'the land had rest' type formula rather than as an exhaustive historical record, given the Philistine conflicts narrated later within Samuel's lifetime.

**Alternative explanations:**
- 7:13's language may describe a real, more limited cessation — Philistines no longer raiding 'into the coast of Israel' proper — distinguishable from later border battles fought nearer Philistine-controlled territory.
- The 'all the days of Samuel' formula may be a summary specifically bounding this particular Mizpeh-generation peace rather than an unqualified claim covering Samuel's entire subsequent career.
