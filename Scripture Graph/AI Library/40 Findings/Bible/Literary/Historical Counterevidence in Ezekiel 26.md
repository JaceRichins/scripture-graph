# Historical Counterevidence in Ezekiel 26

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Tyre's substantial later history and its continued existence as an inhabited place make an exhaustive literal reading of 'built no more' and 'never be found again' historically difficult. This is strong counterevidence to a maximal interpretation in which no settlement could ever again exist at Tyre, though it is not decisive against interpretations limited to the ancient city's political form, former commercial supremacy, or the conventional totality language of prophetic judgment.

**Does not establish:** The later city does not by itself establish the intended scope of the ancient wording or settle the theological status of the prophecy.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The statements may concern the ancient island-city configuration rather than every later settlement in the vicinity.
- The language may denote permanent loss of Tyre's former sovereignty and commercial dominance.
- Absolute language is common in prophetic judgment poetry and may function rhetorically rather than as a cadastral prediction.
- Different clauses may address the mainland dependencies, the island center, and the larger polity rather than one identical geographic object.
