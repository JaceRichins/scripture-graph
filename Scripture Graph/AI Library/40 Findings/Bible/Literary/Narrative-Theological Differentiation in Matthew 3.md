# Narrative-Theological Differentiation in Matthew 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The baptism scene simultaneously differentiates Jesus, the descending Spirit of God, and the heavenly speaker who calls Jesus his Son. It therefore strongly supports a narrative distinction among Father, Son, and Spirit, while leaving their precise metaphysical relationship unstated.

**Does not establish:** The scene alone does not establish a complete doctrine of the Trinity, a complete Latter-day Saint doctrine of the Godhead, or the bodily form of the Spirit.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Trinitarian readers understand the scene as a manifestation of three persons within one God.
- Non-Trinitarian readers understand it as interaction among more sharply distinct divine beings.
- Some analysis may emphasize the scene's christological and literary function without deriving a complete doctrine of divine ontology.

**Scripture:** [[Matthew 3#^matt-3-16|Matthew 3:16]], [[Matthew 3#^matt-3-17|Matthew 3:17]]
