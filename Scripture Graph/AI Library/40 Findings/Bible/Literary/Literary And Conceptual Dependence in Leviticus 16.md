# Literary And Conceptual Dependence in Leviticus 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Hebrews 9 explicitly recalls the high priest's annual entry into the inner sanctuary with blood for himself and the people, then uses that ritual pattern to interpret Christ's priestly work. The close correspondences strongly establish that Leviticus 16 or its continuing ritual tradition informs Hebrews' argument.

**Does not establish:** The literary relationship alone does not prove every later Christian identification was intended by the human author of Leviticus or independently prove larger claims about authorship and historicity.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Hebrews may draw on the practiced Day of Atonement tradition rather than directly quoting the written form of Leviticus 16.
- Some details in Hebrews reflect the author's theological adaptation rather than a simple restatement of Leviticus.
