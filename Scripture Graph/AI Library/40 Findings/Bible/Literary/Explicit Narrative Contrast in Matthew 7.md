# Explicit Narrative Contrast in Matthew 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The rejected speakers claim prophecy, exorcism, and wonderful works in Jesus's name, yet are identified as workers of iniquity. This is strong textual evidence that spectacular religious claims or works are not presented as sufficient proof of faithful discipleship.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every miracle claim is false or that spiritual gifts are unimportant; it establishes their insufficiency as the sole test of discipleship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The speakers may be making false or exaggerated claims about their works.
- The works may have occurred without reflecting the workers' character or obedience.
- The passage does not explain whether the works were authentic, deceptive, or merely alleged.

**Scripture:** [[Matthew 7#^matt-7-21|Matthew 7:21]], [[Matthew 7#^matt-7-22|Matthew 7:22]], [[Matthew 7#^matt-7-23|Matthew 7:23]]
