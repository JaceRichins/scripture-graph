# Characterization And Moral Reasoning in Genesis 39

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Joseph's explicit appeal to both entrusted responsibility and sin against God, followed by repeated refusal and physical flight, gives strong narrative evidence that the chapter portrays his conduct as principled fidelity rather than mere avoidance of detection.

**Does not establish:** The episode does not establish that faithful conduct always prevents false accusation, punishment, or other immediate harm.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A reader may emphasize Joseph's loyalty to Potiphar more than the theological dimension of his refusal.
- The account is literary characterization and does not independently verify every historical detail of the event.
