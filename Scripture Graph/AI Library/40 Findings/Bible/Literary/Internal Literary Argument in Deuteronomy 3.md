# Internal Literary Argument in Deuteronomy 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the chapter's literary argument, the reports of victories over Sihon and Og serve as evidence offered to Joshua and Israel that they need not fear the kingdoms ahead. This is strong evidence for the text's rhetorical strategy, but it is not independent evidence that every narrated event occurred exactly as described.

**Does not establish:** The literary use of remembered victories does not independently establish the historicity of the conquest details or the theological claim of divine causation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The victories may be included primarily as chronological history rather than as a deliberately shaped rhetorical preparation.
- The arrangement may reflect later editorial composition while still performing the same literary function.
