# Literary-Rhetorical Pattern in Acts 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The contrast between the creation-and-providence appeal in Acts 14 and the scriptural-history address in Acts 13 is substantial evidence that Acts intentionally portrays missionary speech as adapted to an audience's religious background.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that either speech is a verbatim transcript or independently prove the historicity of every narrated detail.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The contrast may reflect the narrator's literary composition more than the missionaries' exact historical wording.
- Both addresses may draw upon conventional Jewish modes of argument available to the author.
