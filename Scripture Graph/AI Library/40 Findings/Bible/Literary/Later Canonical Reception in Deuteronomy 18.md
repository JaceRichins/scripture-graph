# Later Canonical Reception in Deuteronomy 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Acts 3 applies the prophet-like-Moses passage within Peter's proclamation of Jesus, Acts 7 repeats it in Stephen's account, and 3 Nephi 20 explicitly identifies Jesus as the one of whom Moses spoke. This is strong evidence for a christological reception of Deuteronomy 18 in later scripture.

**Does not establish:** Later christological application does not by itself prove that Deuteronomy's first audience could only have understood the promise as one distant messianic prediction or that recurring prophets were excluded.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Later inspired application can develop or culminate an earlier institutional pattern rather than exhaust the passage's immediate legal meaning.
- Acts 7's repetition contributes to Stephen's wider argument and is less explicit by itself than Acts 3 or 3 Nephi 20.
