# Internal Narrative Pattern in Mark 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within Mark’s narrative, several predictions receive explicit fulfillment: the two disciples find the Passover arrangements as Jesus described; the disciples flee after the shepherd saying; and Peter’s three denials conclude with the second cockcrow and his recollection of Jesus’s words. This is strong evidence that the narrator presents Jesus as foreknowing and interpreting the coming events.

**Does not establish:** The literary pattern alone does not historically prove supernatural foreknowledge or establish that every prediction was transmitted verbatim before the events.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The evangelist may have shaped inherited material retrospectively so that prediction and fulfillment correspond.
- Some predictions may reflect established Christian interpretation of events rather than independently preserved advance statements.
- Narrative coherence by itself cannot determine how each saying originated historically.
