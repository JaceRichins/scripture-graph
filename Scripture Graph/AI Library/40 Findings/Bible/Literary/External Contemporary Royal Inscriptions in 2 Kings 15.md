# External Contemporary Royal Inscriptions in 2 Kings 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Assyrian inscriptions report devastation and deportation in Israel and associate Pekah's overthrow with Hoshea's installation. This converges substantially with verses 29-30, while the sources frame political agency differently: Kings emphasizes Hoshea's conspiracy, whereas the Assyrian king claims decisive authority over the succession.

**Does not establish:** The convergence does not prove the precise sequence, agency, city list, chronology, or theological meaning assigned by Kings.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Hoshea may have carried out the coup locally while Tiglath-pileser subsequently confirmed him, allowing both accounts to emphasize different aspects of one transition.
- The Assyrian monarch may exaggerate his personal control over events as part of royal propaganda.
- The fragmentary wording leaves uncertainty over whether Israelites or Tiglath-pileser claimed to have killed Pekah.
