# LiteraryForm-Critical in Jeremiah 20

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The abrupt shift from praise (20:13) to a birth-curse (20:14-18) supports the view that this passage preserves raw, theologically unresolved emotional expression rather than a tidy devotional resolution. It does not establish a single coherent 'moral' for the chapter, nor that the curse language is meant as normative prayer instruction.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish Jeremiah's actual psychological state, nor whether vv7-13 and vv14-18 were composed as a single unit.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The two sections (vv7-13 and vv14-18) may originally have been separate laments later combined editorially, which would make the 'reversal' a compositional artifact rather than a single speaker's emotional arc.
- The curse may function as conventional hyperbolic lament rhetoric (a recognized ancient Near Eastern wisdom/lament form) rather than sincere despair or doubt.
