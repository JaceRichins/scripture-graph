# Internal Literary Pattern in Luke 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within Luke 4, the placement of healings, liberation from unclean spirits, and preaching to additional cities after the Nazareth mission statement is strong internal evidence that the narrator intends these actions to exemplify Jesus's Spirit-anointed proclamation and liberating mission.

**Does not establish:** The literary correspondence does not by itself establish the historical sequence of the events, the precise scope of Isaiah's fulfillment, or every later theological claim about Jesus's mission.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The subsequent episodes may be arranged primarily by inherited chronology or tradition rather than as deliberate illustrations of the Nazareth declaration.
- Some elements of the Isaiah passage may function metaphorically or eschatologically rather than mapping one-to-one onto the reported healings and exorcisms.
