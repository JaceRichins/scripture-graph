# Canonical Narrative Correspondence in Deuteronomy 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Joshua 8:30–35 narrates a closely corresponding event involving an altar on Mount Ebal, unworked stones, offerings, writing the law on stones, the Gerizim–Ebal arrangement, and a reading of blessings and curses. This is strong evidence that the Deuteronomic command and the Joshua narrative were understood as corresponding texts.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence alone does not establish historical performance, the dates of either text, or the direction of literary dependence.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Joshua's account may have been composed or edited to portray fulfillment of Deuteronomy's command.
- The two accounts may draw on a shared ritual tradition rather than one directly depending on the other.
- Literary correspondence does not require that every narrated detail be a recoverable historical event.
