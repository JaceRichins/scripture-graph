# Narrative Characterization in 2 Samuel 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated peace formula, the statement that David did not know of Joab's recall, David's public repudiation of bloodguilt, his participation in mourning, and the people's resulting conclusion collectively provide strong literary evidence that this narrative seeks to dissociate David from Abner's assassination.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish David's historical innocence or exclude unreported prior knowledge; it establishes how the received narrative portrays him.

**Alternative explanations:**
- David's mourning and fasting could have served political reputation management as well as expressing genuine grief.
- A later narrator or editor may have emphasized David's innocence to defend the legitimacy of his dynasty.
- The narrator's portrayal cannot independently verify every historical circumstance surrounding Abner's death.
