# Thematic And Lexical Evidence in Isaiah 66

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The gathering of all nations and tongues, proclamation among distant Gentiles, and worship by all flesh strongly support a universal horizon for the chapter's vision. The simultaneous judgment language prevents this observation from establishing universal salvation.

**Does not establish:** The universal language does not establish that every person is saved or that distinctions between faithful servants and transgressors disappear.

**Alternative explanations:**
- All flesh may function as prophetic hyperbole for a very broad international assembly rather than a claim about every individual.
- The nations may be portrayed primarily as witnesses to Israel's restoration rather than as identical participants in every covenant promise.
