# Literary Structure in Genesis 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The six days exhibit substantial literary correspondence: days 1 and 4 concern day, night, and light; days 2 and 5 concern the waters and the open heaven with their creatures; days 3 and 6 concern dry land, its life, and food. This is strong evidence that the account has deliberate literary organization, although the third and sixth days are not exact mirror images.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not by itself establish the duration, physical mechanism, scientific sequence, historical genre, or authorship of creation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some correspondences may arise naturally from narrating environments before their inhabitants.
- The arrangement need not make the days merely figurative or nonchronological.
- Readers may impose a more rigid framework than the uneven details warrant.
