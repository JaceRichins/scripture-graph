# Canonical Literary-Theological Correspondence in 1 Kings 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Solomon's extraordinary gold, multiplication of horses and chariots, and acquisition of horses from Egypt correspond closely to matters restricted for Israel's king in Deuteronomy 17:16-17. This creates a substantial canonical tension, although 1 Kings 10 itself does not explicitly pronounce these activities sinful.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence does not by itself establish that every item of wealth violated the law, that the narrator condemns Solomon in this chapter, or that the number 666 carries an intended symbolic meaning here.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The chapter may primarily celebrate royal magnificence and international prestige rather than signal criticism.
- Individual descriptions of trade, gold, horses, and chariots may be conventional royal reporting without deliberate allusion to Deuteronomy.
- The narrator may reserve explicit criticism for Solomon's conduct in the following chapter.
