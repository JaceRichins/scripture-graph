# Burial And Witness Continuity in Matthew 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter names Joseph of Arimathaea as the person who obtains and buries Jesus' body, describes a new rock-hewn tomb and its stone, and places Mary Magdalene and the other Mary opposite the tomb. These details provide strong internal narrative continuity between Jesus' death, burial, and the resurrection account that follows.

**Does not establish:** Narrative specificity alone does not independently establish the burial account or the resurrection as historical events.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The specificity may preserve historical memory.
- Some or all details may have been selected or developed to provide narrative and theological continuity.
- Named participants make the account concrete but are not equivalent to independently surviving testimony.
