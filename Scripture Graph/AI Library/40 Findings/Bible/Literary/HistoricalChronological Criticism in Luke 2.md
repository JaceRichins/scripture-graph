# HistoricalChronological Criticism in Luke 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Luke's stated dating anchor — the census 'first made when Cyrenius was governor of Syria' (2:2) — sits in tension with the wider historical record: the only census under Quirinius independently attested (by Josephus, Antiquities 18.1.1, and implicitly by Acts 5:37's reference to unrest under 'Judas of Galilee' in the days of 'the taxing') occurred in AD 6, roughly a decade after the death of Herod the Great (c. 4 BC), during whose reign Luke (1:5) and Matthew (2:1) both place Jesus's birth.

**Does not establish:** This tension does not by itself establish that the nativity is unhistorical or that Luke fabricated the census; it establishes that reconciling Luke's stated date with the independently attested AD 6 Quirinius census remains a genuinely unresolved historical-critical problem rather than a settled contradiction or a settled harmonization.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Quirinius may have held an earlier administrative or military post in the Syria-Cilicia region (sometimes associated with an undated, unnamed inscription, the 'Lapis Tiburtinus'), during which an earlier registration could have begun — though no ancient source names him as governor of Syria before AD 6.
- The Greek of 2:2 (protē) has occasionally been rendered 'this census took place before Quirinius was governor,' removing the conflict, though this is a minority reading not favored by most grammarians.
- Luke, writing decades later from oral and written tradition, may have loosely associated the nativity with the empire-wide Augustan registrations without intending precise decade-level chronology, consistent with ancient historiographic conventions.
- An otherwise unattested local Herodian registration, distinct from the AD 6 provincial census Josephus describes, could have occurred nearer 6-4 BC.
