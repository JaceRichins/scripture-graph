# Internal Literary Structure in Psalm 89

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close juxtaposition of an emphatic oath concerning David's enduring throne with a lament that the throne has been cast down is strong internal evidence that the chapter is designed to confront the perceived conflict between divine promise and historical experience.

**Does not establish:** The contrast does not identify the historical crisis, date the psalm, prove that God actually broke the covenant, or determine how the promise should be fulfilled.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The oracle and lament may have originated separately and been joined by an editor.
- The accusation that the covenant was made void may be rhetorical language of grief rather than a literal theological conclusion.
- The disaster may concern a temporary humiliation rather than the permanent end of the Davidic promise.
