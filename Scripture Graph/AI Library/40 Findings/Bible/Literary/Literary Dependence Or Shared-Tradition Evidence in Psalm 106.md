# Literary Dependence Or Shared-Tradition Evidence in Psalm 106

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The psalm recounts numerous episodes in highly compressed form, often naming only a person, place, or decisive act. This strongly supports the modest conclusion that the poem presupposes an audience familiar with a larger body of Israelite historical tradition.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the exact written sources used, the direction of literary dependence, the date of composition, or the historicity of every narrated episode.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The audience's knowledge could have come from oral tradition rather than written sources.
- The psalmist and surviving narratives could draw independently on shared communal traditions.
- Some allusions may have been more self-explanatory to an ancient audience than they are to a modern reader.
