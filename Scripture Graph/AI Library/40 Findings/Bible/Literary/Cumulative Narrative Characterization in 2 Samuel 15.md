# Cumulative Narrative Characterization in 2 Samuel 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the narrative, the concentration of public display, disputed access to judgment, claims upon Israel's hearts, a trumpet proclamation, elite counsel, and competing loyalty networks strongly supports reading the rebellion as a contest over political legitimacy rather than merely a sudden military coup.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the historical scale of Absalom's support, the objective condition of David's judicial administration, or the moral purity of either faction.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some details may primarily heighten dramatic tension rather than supply a systematic account of political legitimacy.
- Absalom may have combined genuine reform concerns with self-interested ambition; the narrator's hostile summary does not independently resolve every grievance.
- Expressions such as 'all Israel' and 'the hearts of the men of Israel' may be conventional generalizations rather than statistical descriptions.
