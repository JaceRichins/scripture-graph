# Theological Anthropology in Psalm 139

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The prenatal imagery strongly supports the poem's claim that divine knowledge and involvement extend to life in the womb. Because the description is poetic and does not present a biological sequence, it is weak evidence for detailed scientific or chronological claims about embryonic development.

**Does not establish:** These verses alone do not establish a scientific account of development, the precise moment personhood begins, or a complete legal or ethical position on every question concerning pregnancy.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language may primarily celebrate divine craftsmanship rather than address questions about prenatal moral or legal status.
- The imagery of being wrought in the lowest parts of the earth may be poetic analogy rather than a literal description of gestation.
- The book and written members may symbolize divine foreknowledge or ordering rather than a biological record.
