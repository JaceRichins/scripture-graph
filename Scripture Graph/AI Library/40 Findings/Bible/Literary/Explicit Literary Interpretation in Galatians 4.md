# Explicit Literary Interpretation in Galatians 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Paul explicitly calls his treatment of Abraham's two sons and their mothers an allegory and associates the women with two covenants. This is strong evidence that this particular argument intentionally gives the Genesis figures an additional covenantal significance beyond merely retelling their story.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the Genesis narrative is nonhistorical, that all scripture should be read allegorically, or that Paul's application exhausts the narrative's meaning.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The term translated 'allegory' may describe a typological or figurative application rather than a denial that Paul regarded the Genesis narrative as historical.
- Paul's specific rhetorical use does not prove that every detail of the Genesis account was intended by its original author to carry the same covenantal symbolism.
