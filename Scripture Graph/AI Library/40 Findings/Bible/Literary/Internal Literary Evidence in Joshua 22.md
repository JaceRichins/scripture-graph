# Internal Literary Evidence in Joshua 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated denials of sacrificial use, followed by Phinehas's acceptance and Israel's abandonment of war, provide strong internal evidence that the narrative expects readers to distinguish the altar of witness from a competing sanctuary.

**Does not establish:** This literary pattern does not establish the event's historicity, the builders' private motives, the altar's date, or the precise circumstances in which the account was composed.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The repetition may partly reflect conventional legal or rhetorical emphasis rather than a deliberately developed literary contrast.
- The narrative reports the eastern tribes' defense and its acceptance but does not independently test their motives.
- A later writer or editor could have shaped the account to address disputes about legitimate worship.
