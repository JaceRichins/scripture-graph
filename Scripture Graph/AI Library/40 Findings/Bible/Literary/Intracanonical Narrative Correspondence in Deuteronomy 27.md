# Intracanonical Narrative Correspondence in Deuteronomy 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Joshua 8 presents a later narrative enactment of this chapter's instructions: Joshua builds an unhewn-stone altar on Ebal, offers sacrifices, writes a copy of the law, positions Israel in relation to Ebal and Gerizim, and reads the blessing and curse.

**Does not establish:** The literary correspondence does not by itself establish that the described ceremony occurred historically or identify the dates and compositional relationship of Deuteronomy and Joshua.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Joshua's account may have been composed or edited with Deuteronomy's command deliberately in view.
- Literary correspondence alone cannot determine the date or historical circumstances of either passage.
