# Literary Structure in Amos 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated oracle openings, followed by a much longer Israel oracle containing accusation, saving-history recital, rejection of prophets and Nazarites, and an extended defeat scene, strongly support reading Israel as the rhetorical climax and principal target of the chapter.

**Does not establish:** The structure does not by itself establish the oracle’s date, precise compositional history, authorship of every line, or fulfillment in one uniquely identifiable event.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The unequal lengths could partly reflect the amount of surviving material rather than a consciously designed climax.
- Modern chapter division accentuates the Israel oracle even though the larger series begins in Amos 1.
- Israel’s longer treatment might arise simply because the prophet addresses his immediate audience in greater detail.
