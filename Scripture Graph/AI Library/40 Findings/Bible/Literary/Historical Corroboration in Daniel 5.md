# Historical Corroboration in Daniel 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Cuneiform evidence independently establishes Belshazzar as a real Babylonian royal figure, the son of Nabonidus, who exercised substantial authority during his father's prolonged absence. This supports the conclusion that Daniel 5 preserves authentic knowledge of a once-obscure late Babylonian figure, but the degree to which it confirms the chapter's detailed narrative is limited.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish early composition, prophetic foreknowledge, the miracle of the writing, or the historical accuracy of every narrative detail.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A later author could have acquired the name and political memory from surviving records or traditions.
- Historical knowledge of Belshazzar does not demonstrate that the feast, supernatural writing, interpretation, or death scene occurred as narrated.
- The designation of Belshazzar as king may reflect literary usage or delegated authority rather than his formal Babylonian title.
