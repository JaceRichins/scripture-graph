# EthicalHistorical in Ephesians 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ephesians 6:5-9 regulates conduct within the master-servant relationship (mutual accountability, no threats, no partiality before God) but does not call for the abolition of the institution of slavery itself.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish Paul's (or the author's) considered view on whether slavery as an institution is just; the text is silent on that question.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some argue v.9's insistence on identical divine accountability and 'no respect of persons' plants a theological seed later used by abolitionists, even if not intended as abolition.
- The passage may reflect pragmatic missionary accommodation to Greco-Roman social structure rather than either endorsement or critique.
