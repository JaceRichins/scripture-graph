# Hebrew Lexical Clarification in Isaiah 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The word translated “hell” in the KJV at Isaiah 5:14 is Hebrew Sheol. In this verse it is personified as opening its mouth without limit while Jerusalem's glory and multitude descend. The immediate image is the voracious realm of death; the verse alone does not define a later Christian doctrine of final punishment.

**Does not establish:** The lexical identification does not settle every biblical or theological question about the state of the dead.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Readers may integrate Sheol into a broader theology of postmortem judgment using other passages, but that theology is not specified here.
- The image can encompass national catastrophe and mass death without requiring a detailed geography of the afterlife.
