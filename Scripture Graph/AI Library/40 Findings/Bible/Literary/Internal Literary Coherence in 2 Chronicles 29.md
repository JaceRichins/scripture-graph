# Internal Literary Coherence in 2 Chronicles 29

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
As internal literary evidence, the opening and repair of the temple, the reversal of discontinued worship, and the recovery of vessels discarded by Ahaz form a deliberate narrative reversal of the actions attributed to Ahaz in the preceding chapter. This strongly supports cross-chapter narrative coherence but provides only limited independent evidence about the historical details.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently establish the chronology, completeness, or historical precision of every reported act, nor the narrative's claim of divine causation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The contrast may reflect retrospective theological shaping rather than a complete record of either reign.
- The author may have inherited a reform tradition and arranged its details to create a sharp contrast between Ahaz and Hezekiah.
- Some actions could be conventional motifs used in accounts of apostasy and restoration.
