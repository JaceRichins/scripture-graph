# Internal Narrative Attestation in Mark 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within Mark's narrative, Jesus's death and burial receive multiple concrete checkpoints: a centurion is present at the death, Pilate asks the centurion to confirm it, Joseph receives and entombs the body, and named women observe the burial place. This is strong evidence for what Mark intends to assert and provides historically relevant detail, though internal narrative detail alone cannot independently prove every event.

**Does not establish:** These details do not by themselves prove the historicity of the burial circumstances, the empty tomb, or the resurrection.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The checkpoints may reflect literary construction intended to prepare for the empty-tomb narrative.
- Named characters and official confirmation can add narrative verisimilitude without independently verifying an account.
- Historical evaluation also depends on source dating, transmission, genre, and comparison with other evidence.
