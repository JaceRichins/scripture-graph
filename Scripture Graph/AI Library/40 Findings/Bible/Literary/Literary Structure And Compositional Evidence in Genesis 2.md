# Literary Structure And Compositional Evidence in Genesis 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Genesis 1:1-2:3 and Genesis 2:4-25 offer distinguishable but deliberately adjacent presentations of creation: the first is cosmic and highly patterned, while the second concentrates on land, garden, vocation, command, companionship, and marriage. Their juxtaposition strongly supports reading them comparatively, but it does not by itself settle whether they originated as one composition or as traditions brought together by an editor.

**Does not establish:** The literary differences alone do not prove contradiction, separate authorship, a specific documentary source theory, or the precise historical process by which the present text arose.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The second presentation may be a purposeful topical expansion composed with the first from the outset.
- The presentations may preserve distinct earlier traditions joined by a later editor.
- Apparent differences in sequence may reflect Hebrew narrative conventions rather than competing chronological claims.
