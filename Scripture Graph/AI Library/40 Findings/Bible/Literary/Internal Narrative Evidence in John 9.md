# Internal Narrative Evidence in John 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the narrative, the restoration of congenital sight functions as evidence for Jesus’ divine authorization: even divided observers acknowledge the reported deed, and the healed man uses it to challenge the assertion that Jesus is a sinner.

**Does not establish:** The episode by itself does not prove historicity, divine sonship, or the correctness of every premise in the healed man’s argument.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A reader may accept the literary function of the miracle without accepting it as a historical event.
- The authorities can interpret Sabbath conduct as counterevidence because they prioritize their legal judgment over the sign.
- A narrated miracle does not independently verify its own occurrence outside the text.
