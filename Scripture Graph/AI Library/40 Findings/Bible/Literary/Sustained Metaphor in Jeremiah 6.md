# Sustained Metaphor in Jeremiah 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The closing verses form an extended metallurgical image involving brass, iron, bellows, lead, a founder, refining fire, and rejected silver. The concentration and sequence of these terms strongly support reading the conclusion as an assay or refining process that fails because the wicked are not removed.

**Does not establish:** The imagery does not establish the date of the passage, the historical sophistication of Judahite metallurgy, or the exact technical process envisioned.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some individual terms in Jeremiah 6:27 are textually or translationally difficult, so the precise role assigned to the addressee remains uncertain.
- The image may emphasize testing and exposure more than an attempted purification.
- The metaphor may combine the people's corrupt composition with the foundry's failed separation process rather than describe one technical procedure exactly.
