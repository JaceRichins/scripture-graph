# Epigraphic And Historical Context in 2 Kings 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The ninth-century BCE Mesha Stele independently names Mesha as king of Moab and describes Israelite domination under Omri's dynasty followed by Moabite recovery. It strongly supports the chapter's broad geopolitical setting and the historical existence of a Moabite ruler named Mesha, but it does not clearly attest this particular expedition or its reported wonders and ending.

**Does not establish:** The stele does not establish the sheep-tribute figures, Jehoram's route, Elisha's oracle, the water event, the royal sacrifice, or the detailed chronology of 2 Kings 3.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The inscription and 2 Kings may describe different campaigns or different phases of a longer conflict.
- Both sources are ideologically shaped accounts that select events to advance national and theological claims.
- Kings may preserve accurate general political memory without preserving every detail of this campaign.
