# Biblical Source Criticism in Micah 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The literary relationship between Micah 4:1-3 and Isaiah 2:2-4 is a long-standing text-critical question: the two passages are nearly identical in wording but appear in different surrounding contexts in each book, and neither text explicitly quotes the other.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish which book (if either) is the original source, nor does it establish or disprove the antiquity of the oracle itself; it only establishes that the two texts are textually related.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Micah is the original source and Isaiah (or a later editor of Isaiah) incorporated it
- Isaiah is the original source and Micah (or a later editor of Micah) incorporated it
- Both prophets, or later editors of both books, drew independently on a shared older oracle or liturgical formula not preserved elsewhere
- The passage was inserted into one or both books during later exilic/post-exilic editing
