# Internal Narrative Evidence in 2 Kings 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the chapter's narrative sequence, Ahaz's Assyrian appeal is accompanied by the transfer of temple wealth and followed by changes to the temple associated with Damascus and, in one case, explicitly with the king of Assyria. This is substantial textual evidence that his foreign policy had material and religious consequences, but it is not proof that every cultic change was demanded by Assyria.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that Tiglath-pileser ordered the new altar, that Ahaz abandoned worship of the LORD entirely, or that all temple changes had a single motive.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Ahaz may have adopted the Damascus altar primarily from personal admiration rather than Assyrian coercion.
- Some temple alterations may have been practical, political, or financial measures rather than a unified religious program.
- Narrative sequence does not by itself prove that the alliance caused every later action.
