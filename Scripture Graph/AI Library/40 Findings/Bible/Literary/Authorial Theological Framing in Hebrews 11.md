# Authorial Theological Framing in Hebrews 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Applying 'the reproach of Christ' to Moses, who lived many centuries before Jesus's birth, is theologically significant but historically contested: it can be read either as the author of Hebrews' own retrospective Christian vocabulary describing Moses's suffering for God's people, or (in some traditions) as a claim that Moses himself possessed explicit knowledge of a coming Messiah.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish what the historical Moses personally believed or knew about a future Messiah.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some traditions, including Latter-day Saint readings informed by the Book of Mormon's portrayal of pre-Christian prophets, hold that Moses possessed explicit knowledge of and consciously anticipated Christ, making the phrase a literal description of his own understanding.
- Mainstream historical-critical scholarship generally treats 'the reproach of Christ' as the author of Hebrews's own retrospective vocabulary applied to Moses's suffering, not evidence of what the historical Moses believed or knew.
