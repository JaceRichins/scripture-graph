# Literary Sequencing in Acts 23

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The placement of the promise about Rome immediately before the assassination plot strongly supports a literary reading in which the subsequent rescue advances a declared divine purpose. The rescue itself occurs through human information, decisions, and military protection.

**Does not establish:** The narrative arrangement does not independently establish supernatural causation or demonstrate how the Lord communicated with Paul.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The sequence may simply report events chronologically without making every human action a special act of providence.
- A reader may regard the divine appearance as a literary-theological framing supplied by the narrator rather than as independently verifiable history.
