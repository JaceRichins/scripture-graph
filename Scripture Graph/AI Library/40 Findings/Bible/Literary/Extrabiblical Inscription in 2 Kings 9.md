# Extrabiblical Inscription in 2 Kings 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III contains an Assyrian tribute notice conventionally understood to refer to Jehu of Bit-Humri, the land or house of Omri. This is substantial extrabiblical support that a ruler identified as Jehu belonged to the ninth-century BCE political setting of Israel, although it does not attest the coup or killings narrated in this chapter.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the chapter's supernatural claims, prophetic speeches, precise sequence of events, or moral interpretation of Jehu's coup.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Assyrian royal inscriptions are propagandistic presentations of submission and tribute.
- The Assyrian dynastic label Bit-Humri does not imply that Jehu was literally Omri's descendant.
- The inscription supplies no independent evidence for Jehu's prophetic anointing or the deaths of Joram and Jezebel.
