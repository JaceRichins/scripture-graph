# Literary Portrayal Of Civil War in 2 Samuel 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter strongly presents the conflict as destructive violence among kin: the initial contestants kill one another, Asahel's death intensifies family vengeance, and Abner finally warns that the sword will produce bitterness while calling the opposing forces brethren.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the chapter rejects all warfare, assigns equal responsibility to every participant, or denies David's claim to kingship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Abner's appeal may be principally tactical because his defeated force is in danger.
- The designation brethren may express shared Israelite identity without condemning every use of force in the conflict.
- The narrative may lament the consequences of this battle without teaching absolute pacifism.
