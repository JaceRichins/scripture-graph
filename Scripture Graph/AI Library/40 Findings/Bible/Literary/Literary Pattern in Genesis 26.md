# Literary Pattern in Genesis 26

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Genesis 26 clusters motifs previously associated with Abraham: famine and a wife-sister claim, dealings with Abimelech, a covenant, contested water access, and the naming of Beer-sheba. This is substantial literary evidence that the canonical narrative presents Isaac in continuity with Abraham, although explanations of how the parallel arose remain disputed.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not by itself establish the narratives' historicity, their date, Mosaic authorship, direct literary dependence, or that the Abraham and Isaac accounts describe one duplicated event.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Similar events may have recurred within the same extended family and region.
- The parallels may reflect independent ancestral traditions later placed together.
- Editorial shaping may account for some correspondence without every repeated detail being deliberate.
- Shared social pressures involving famine, migration, marriage, and water access could naturally produce similar episodes.
