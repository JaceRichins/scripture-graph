# Literary And Thematic Parallel in Colossians 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Colossians 3 and Ephesians contain a concentrated sequence of parallels: putting off the old man and putting on the new, psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, thanksgiving in Christ's name, and instructions to wives, husbands, children, fathers, servants, and masters. The cumulative correspondence strongly supports a literary relationship or shared instructional tradition.

**Does not establish:** The parallels alone do not establish which text came first, direct copying, or the authorship of either letter.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Both letters may draw on common early Christian catechesis or worship language.
- A common author could naturally reuse characteristic themes and expressions.
- One letter or a later author may have adapted the other.
- Some household similarities may reflect wider ancient conventions rather than direct dependence.
