# Literary Repetition And Contrast in Ezekiel 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated descriptions of rebellion, the duplicated hear-or-forbear formula, and the direct contrast between the rebellious house and the prophet provide strong textual evidence that faithful reception and delivery of God's word under anticipated rejection are central concerns of the chapter.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not by itself establish a detailed compositional structure, the historical circumstances of the commission, the audience's eventual response, or any conclusion about authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some repetition may reflect conventional prophetic commissioning language rather than a specially designed literary pattern.
- The precise prominence of repeated terms can be affected by translation choices.
- The chapter may emphasize Israel's culpability at least as strongly as the prophet's fidelity.
