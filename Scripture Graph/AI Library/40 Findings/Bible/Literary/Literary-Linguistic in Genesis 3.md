# Literary-Linguistic in Genesis 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The similar-sounding Hebrew terms commonly transliterated as 'arummim' (naked, Genesis 2:25) and 'arum' (subtil or crafty, Genesis 3:1) form a wordplay across the chapter boundary. This is moderate evidence of deliberate literary shaping: human openness or vulnerability is placed beside the serpent's craftiness.

**Does not establish:** The wordplay does not establish the narrative's date, single authorship, historical genre, or any larger theological conclusion.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The phonetic similarity could be exploited by a final editor rather than originating with the earliest form of the story.
- Recognizing wordplay does not require the proposed thematic contrast to exhaust its meaning.
- A literary pun can occur in narrative understood as history, theology, folklore, or a combination of genres.
