# NumericalHistorical Analysis in Matthew 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Using standard first-century monetary equivalences (a talent commonly reckoned around 6,000 denarii, a denarius roughly a day's wage), the first servant's debt of ten thousand talents is on the order of hundreds of thousands of times larger than the hundred pence owed by his fellow servant, indicating the parable is using deliberately incalculable, hyperbolic figures rather than a realistic financial comparison.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish precise first-century values; establishes only that the contrast was almost certainly intended as extreme rather than proportionate.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Ancient talent-to-denarius conversion rates varied by region, era, and metal (gold vs. silver talent), so any specific ratio is approximate, not precise.
- The numbers may simply signal 'largest conceivable sum' versus 'ordinary small sum' rather than encode a deliberate exact ratio for the audience to calculate.
