# Internal Narrative Tension in 2 Samuel 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The song's retributive claim of 'clean hands' and unbroken righteousness (vv.21-25) sits in tension with the Bathsheba and Uriah episode recorded earlier in the same book, where the narrative explicitly convicts David of adultery and murder, yet the song offers no qualification or acknowledgment of that failure.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that the text is hypocritical, ironic, or intentionally critical of David; the editors' purpose in placing it here is not stated in the text.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Retribution language in royal psalms is often conventional genre rhetoric about covenant loyalty rather than a literal claim of sinless perfection.
- The underlying poem may predate the Bathsheba episode chronologically even though it is placed editorially near the close of 2 Samuel.
- The 'righteousness' in view may be relative to David's enemies/rivals rather than an absolute moral claim.
