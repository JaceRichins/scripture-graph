# Synoptic Comparison in 2 Chronicles 35

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Chronicler's account of Josiah's death (disguise, Necho's warning explicitly framed as coming 'from the mouth of God,' archers wounding Josiah, transfer to a second chariot, and burial) is considerably more detailed and theologically framed than the parallel account in 2 Kings 23:29-30, which reports only that Necho 'slew him at Megiddo' without mentioning a warning, a disguise, or divine speech through Necho.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish which account is chronologically or historically prior, nor does it establish that the Chronicler invented the divine-speech framing rather than transmitting an existing tradition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Kings may simply have compressed a fuller tradition also known to the Chronicler, rather than the Chronicler having elaborated a sparser Kings account.
- Both accounts may draw independently on a common source (e.g., the 'book of the kings of Israel and Judah' referenced in 2 Chronicles 35:27) that differed in what each author chose to preserve.
