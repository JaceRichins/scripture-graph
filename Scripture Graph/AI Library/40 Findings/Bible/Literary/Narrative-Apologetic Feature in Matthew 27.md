# Narrative-Apologetic Feature in Matthew 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chief priests and Pharisees remember Jesus' prediction of rising after three days and ask Pilate to secure the tomb because they anticipate a claim that the disciples stole the body. The sealing and watch therefore function narratively as a preemptive response to a theft explanation.

**Does not establish:** Because the narrative supplies both the precaution and the rebuttal, it is not independent proof that guards and a seal were historically present.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The precautions may preserve memory of an actual historical measure.
- The account may have developed in controversy over an existing stolen-body allegation.
- Historical memory and later apologetic shaping are not mutually exclusive.
