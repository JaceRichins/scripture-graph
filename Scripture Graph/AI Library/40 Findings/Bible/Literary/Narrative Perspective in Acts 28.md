# Narrative Perspective in Acts 28

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
First-person plural language continues through the Melita and travel narratives, so the narrator presents these scenes from the perspective of someone within Paul's traveling company. This is meaningful internal evidence for a participant perspective, but only limited evidence for actual eyewitness authorship.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish the traditional identity of the author, direct observation of every event, or the historical accuracy of every narrative detail.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The author may have incorporated a travel source written by another participant.
- First-person narration can function as a literary convention rather than a literal autobiographical claim.
- Even genuine participation would not independently verify every reported speech or miracle.
