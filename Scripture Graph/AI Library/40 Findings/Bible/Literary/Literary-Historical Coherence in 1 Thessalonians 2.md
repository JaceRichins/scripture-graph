# Literary-Historical Coherence in 1 Thessalonians 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The reference to prior suffering and mistreatment at Philippi coheres with Acts' account of Paul and Silas being beaten and imprisoned there.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence does not establish literary independence, the complete historicity of every detail in Acts, or Pauline authorship by itself.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Acts may preserve Pauline traditions already known within early Christian communities.
- One account could be shaped by knowledge of the other or by a shared literary tradition.
- General memories of persecution could have been developed differently by the two authors.
