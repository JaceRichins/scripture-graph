# Internal Literary Pattern in Matthew 23

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated contrast between visible righteousness and hidden corruption is strong internal evidence that moral incongruity is a central organizing concern of the chapter: public works conceal inconsistent conduct, a clean exterior conceals extortion and excess, and beautiful tombs conceal uncleanness.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not by itself establish the chapter's compositional history, the precise stages of Gospel transmission, or a single formal outline intended by the author.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The recurring contrasts may reflect several independently preserved sayings arranged together because of their common subject.
- The sequence need not imply a formally designed literary structure beyond ordinary thematic repetition.
