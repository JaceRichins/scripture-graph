# Doctrinal Tension in Mark 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Jesus states that 'when they shall rise from the dead, they neither marry, nor are given in marriage; but are as the angels which are in heaven' (Mark 12:25). Read at face value, this statement is in tension with the doctrine of eternal, sealed marriage; it is honestly one of the more difficult verses for a theology that teaches marriage continues after resurrection, and deserves to be presented as a genuine difficulty rather than explained away by default.

**Does not establish:** The verse alone does not settle, in either direction, whether previously formed marriage covenants persist after resurrection; it directly answers the Sadducees' narrower legal question about competing husbands.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Mainstream Christian reading: there is no marriage relationship at all after the resurrection; earthly marriage is a temporal institution that simply ends.
- A reading that limits the saying to the specific question asked (repeated remarriage/being 'given' in marriage anew, i.e. the mortal social act of arranging marriages) rather than to the continuation of an already-existing sealed relationship.
- The verse may be addressing only civil/legal marital claims of the kind the Sadducees posed (whose wife is she 'of them'), not making a general metaphysical claim about all marriage bonds.
