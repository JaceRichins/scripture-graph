# Internal Narrative Evidence in Acts 20

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The first-person plural narration presents part of the journey from a participant's perspective and includes concrete travel stages and durations. This is meaningful internal evidence for access to an itinerary or participant-shaped travel tradition, but it does not by itself prove that the final author personally witnessed the journey.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently establish the identity of Acts' final author or prove that every reported speech and event derives from direct eyewitness memory.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The author may have incorporated a travel diary or source written by someone else.
- The first-person plural may be a literary convention rather than an autobiographical signal.
- A later narrator could have adapted an earlier participant account.
