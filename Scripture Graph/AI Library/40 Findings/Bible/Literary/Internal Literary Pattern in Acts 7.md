# Internal Literary Pattern in Acts 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated movement from rejection to divine authorization provides moderate internal evidence that Stephen presents Moses as a precursor to the rejected Just One. This supports a Moses-Christ typological reading, although the speech does not explicitly map every feature of Moses' life onto Jesus.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish an exhaustive Moses-Christ typology, the intended meaning of every narrative detail, or the independent historicity of the reported events.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The pattern may primarily illustrate Israel's recurring resistance to prophets rather than a detailed Moses-Christ typology.
- Stephen may cite Moses chiefly to answer accusations concerning Moses and the law.
- The resemblance may reflect broad narrative shaping rather than a correspondence intended in every detail.
