# Literary Command-Fulfillment Correspondence in Exodus 38

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close correspondence between earlier construction commands and the completed-work report, together with the statement that Bezaleel made all the LORD commanded Moses, provides strong textual evidence that Exodus 38 functions as deliberate narrative closure to the tabernacle instructions.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence does not by itself establish single authorship, an early date, eyewitness testimony, or the historical accuracy of every measurement.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The repeated wording could result from editorial reuse of an earlier specification list.
- Literary closure need not imply that every detail derives from one author or historical stage.
- A compiler could use duplication to emphasize obedience without preserving an eyewitness construction report.
