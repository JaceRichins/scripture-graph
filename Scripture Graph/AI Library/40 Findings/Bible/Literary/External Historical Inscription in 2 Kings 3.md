# External Historical Inscription in 2 Kings 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The ninth-century BCE Mesha Stele names Mesha as king of Moab and describes conflict between Moab and the Omride dynasty. It therefore supplies independent evidence for the broad geopolitical setting reflected in the chapter's account of Mesha's subjection and rebellion, although it cannot securely be equated with every event in this particular campaign.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the water episode, the oracle, the child sacrifice, the chapter's exact chronology, or supernatural causation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The biblical narrative and the inscription may describe different phases or campaigns within a longer Israelite-Moabite conflict.
- The chapter may preserve authentic geopolitical memory while reshaping individual events for theological and literary purposes.
- Chronological correlations between the inscription and the kings named in biblical sources remain debated.
