# Narrative And Theological Pattern in 2 Kings 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the narrative, the LORD's action through Naaman, Naaman's healing, and his subsequent confession provide strong textual evidence that divine favor and acknowledgment of Israel's God are not portrayed as confined to ethnic Israelites.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish a complete doctrine of Gentile salvation, prove the narrative's historicity, or erase the chapter's emphasis on Israel as the location of the LORD's prophet.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The episode may primarily exalt the LORD and Israel's prophet rather than articulate a general doctrine of inclusion.
- Naaman's case could be presented as an exceptional event rather than a programmatic rule.
- The account may distinguish access to divine mercy from full incorporation into Israel's covenant community.
