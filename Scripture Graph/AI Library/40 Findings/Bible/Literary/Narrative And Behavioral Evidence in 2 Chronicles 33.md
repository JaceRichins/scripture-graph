# Narrative And Behavioral Evidence in 2 Chronicles 33

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Manasseh's post-restoration conduct supports reading his repentance as a behavioral reversal rather than as prayer alone: he removes foreign gods, the temple idol, and unauthorized altars, repairs the Lord's altar, offers sacrifices, and commands Judah to serve the Lord.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every earlier practice was eliminated, that all damage was repaired, or that this sequence is an exhaustive definition of repentance.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some reforms may also have served political consolidation after Manasseh's restoration.
- The narrator may have selected actions that fit a theological pattern of apostasy, affliction, humility, and restoration.

**Scripture:** [[2 Chronicles 33#^2chr-33-15|2 Chronicles 33:15]], [[2 Chronicles 33#^2chr-33-16|2 Chronicles 33:16]]
