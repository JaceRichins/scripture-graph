# Literary Correspondence in 2 Chronicles 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sequence after Elijah's writing substantially corresponds to its announced judgments: invaders remove royal property and family members, and Jehoram develops the predicted bowel disease. A distinct plague upon the people, although announced, is not separately narrated.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence alone does not establish when the oracle was composed, how it was delivered, independent corroboration, or supernatural foreknowledge.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The account may have been retrospectively arranged so that the announced judgment anticipates the disasters already known to the writer.
- The writing and fulfillment account may derive from a received tradition subsequently shaped by the Chronicler.
- The unitemized plague upon the people may be understood as part of the invasion, but the text does not explicitly make that identification.
