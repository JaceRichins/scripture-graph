# Narrative Pattern in Exodus 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The narrative repeatedly pairs advance announcements with reported fulfillment: the livestock disease occurs at the appointed time, the hail follows its warning, and the storm ceases after Moses announces what he will do. This strongly supports the observation that the chapter presents the disasters as controlled signs rather than unconnected accidents within its own narrative world.

**Does not establish:** The narrative pattern does not by itself establish the events' historicity, supernatural causation, precise chronology, or the authorship and date of the account.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The pattern may be a literary-theological arrangement by the narrator rather than a recoverable sequence of historical prediction and fulfillment.
- Natural events could be interpreted retrospectively as fulfillment within a theological account.
