# Literary And Referential Evidence in Ezekiel 28

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Edenic fall, cherub, beauty, and pride imagery can support a typological association with Satan's fall, but the passage explicitly presents itself as a lamentation over the king of Tyrus and repeatedly relates his corruption to merchandise and traffic. The chapter alone therefore does not establish that Satan is its direct or exclusive referent.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the passage narrates Satan's premortal history, that Satan is the sole addressee, or that every image is literal.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The imagery is a poetic depiction of the historical Tyrian monarch's privilege and fall.
- The king is compared with a traditional primordial guardian figure without being identified as that figure.
- The oracle intentionally allows the earthly ruler to typify a broader pattern of rebellious spiritual pride.
