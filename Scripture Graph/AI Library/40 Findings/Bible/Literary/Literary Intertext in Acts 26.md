# Literary Intertext in Acts 26

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The language of opening eyes, turning from darkness to light, and bringing light to Gentiles closely aligns Paul's commission with Isaiah's servant-mission imagery. This supports reading the Gentile mission as presented in continuity with Israel's prophetic scriptures.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not establish that Acts preserves the commission verbatim, nor do they by themselves settle who composed or shaped the wording.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language may draw broadly on common scriptural imagery rather than deliberately invoking one specific Isaiah passage.
- Luke may have shaped the wording of the narrated speech to emphasize themes developed elsewhere in Luke-Acts.
- Paul or an earlier Christian tradition may already have applied Isaiah's servant language to Gentile mission.
