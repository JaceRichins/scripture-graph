# Internal Literary-Rhetorical Evidence in Deuteronomy 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated denial of Israelite righteousness, followed by a catalogue of Israel's rebellions, is strong internal evidence that this chapter resists a triumphalist reading in which military success demonstrates the conquerors' superior virtue.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the chapter rejects conquest, denies the nations' wickedness, or offers a complete moral justification for the violence it describes.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The rehearsal may primarily motivate obedience rather than formulate a general critique of triumphalism.
- The text still distinguishes Israel from the displaced nations through covenant election and the charge of those nations' wickedness.
