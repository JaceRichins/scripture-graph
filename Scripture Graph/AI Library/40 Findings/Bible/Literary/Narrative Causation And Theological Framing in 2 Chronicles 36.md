# Narrative Causation And Theological Framing in 2 Chronicles 36

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter reports political and military actions but also supplies an explicit theological interpretation: prophetic rejection precedes judgment, the desolation fulfills Jeremiah's word, and Cyrus's decree likewise fulfills the divine word.

**Does not establish:** The presence of theological interpretation does not by itself prove or disprove divine causation, nor does it make the political dimensions of the events irrelevant.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A reader may treat the theological explanations as the narrator's interpretation rather than as independently verifiable historical causation.
- Political factors such as imperial expansion and Zedekiah's rebellion can explain the immediate mechanics of Jerusalem's fall without addressing the narrator's theological claims.
