# LogicalApologetic in Acts 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Gamaliel's maxim—that a movement 'of men' will come to nought while one 'of God' cannot be overthrown (5:38-39)—is sometimes cited apologetically as evidence for the divine origin of the church based on its subsequent survival, but the text presents it as one Pharisee's prudential political advice, not a divine oracle; as a general inference rule it is logically weak, since institutional endurance can result from social, political, or cultural causes unrelated to divine origin.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that longevity or survival of a movement proves it originates from God; it records a rhetorical argument for restraint, not a doctrinal principle taught by Peter or the narrator.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Many long-enduring human institutions and ideologies are not divinely inspired, so survival alone is not diagnostic of divine origin.
- Gamaliel's counsel reflects pragmatic Pharisaic caution (avoiding the fate of the Theudas and Judas revolts) rather than a theological test he expects to be generally applicable.
