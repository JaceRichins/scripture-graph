# Literary And Source Formula in 2 Kings 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Repeated notices direct readers to chronicles of the kings of Judah or Israel for additional acts. This is strong evidence that the chapter presents itself as a selective royal account rather than an exhaustive history.

**Does not establish:** The formulas do not establish that the cited chronicles survive, that they are identical with biblical Chronicles, or that every narrated detail can independently be verified from them.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The notices may be conventional literary formulas rather than proof that the final writer directly consulted each named record.
- The references may serve to signal credibility or omitted detail without identifying the exact compositional process.
