# Comparative Literary Evidence in 2 Chronicles 35

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Compared with the shorter account in 2 Kings, Chronicles supplies Necho's warning, identifies it as coming from God, describes Josiah's disguise and wounding, and records the national lament. These additions strongly support the conclusion that Chronicles offers a distinctive theological and literary presentation of Josiah's death.

**Does not establish:** The comparison does not by itself establish which additional details are historically earlier, whether the Chronicler invented any detail, or the precise source relationship between the accounts.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Chronicles may preserve independent historical traditions rather than deliberately expanding the Kings account.
- Some differences may result from abbreviation in Kings rather than theological elaboration in Chronicles.
- A combination of inherited source material and authorial shaping is possible.
