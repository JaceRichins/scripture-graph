# Internal Source Citation in 1 Kings 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The narrator refers readers to books of the chronicles of the kings of Judah and Israel for additional royal acts. These notices strongly establish that the narrative presents itself as selective and invokes broader royal records, but they provide only modest evidence that the final author directly consulted surviving contemporary archives.

**Does not establish:** Because the cited works are not extant, the notices do not establish their contents, dates, historical accuracy, or the compiler's precise mode of access.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The notices reflect actual royal annals used directly or indirectly by the compiler.
- They are conventional bibliographic formulas inherited from earlier source material.
- They function primarily as a literary claim to selectivity and authority.
