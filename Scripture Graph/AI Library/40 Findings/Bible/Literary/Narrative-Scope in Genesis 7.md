# Narrative-Scope in Genesis 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter's English narrative depicts the catastrophe in comprehensive terms: all hills under the whole heaven are covered, all moving flesh on earth dies, and only Noah and those with him remain alive. This is strong evidence for the totalizing scope of the narrated judgment, but internal narrative language alone cannot settle the event's physical geography or its relationship to modern geological reconstruction.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently prove a planet-wide geological flood, disprove a regional-flood interpretation, or determine the narrative's genre and historical method.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Universal language may be read as ancient phenomenological description of the narrator's known world.
- The rhetoric may emphasize the completeness of judgment without functioning as a modern scientific geographic specification.
- Some readers understand the language as describing a literally global event.
