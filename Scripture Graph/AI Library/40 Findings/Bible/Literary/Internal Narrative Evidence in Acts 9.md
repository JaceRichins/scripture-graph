# Internal Narrative Evidence in Acts 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within Acts 9, the Aeneas and Tabitha episodes explicitly connect publicly known restorations with turning to or believing in the Lord. This is strong evidence for the narrator's theological presentation of miracles as signs supporting faith, but only internal evidence for the events' historical occurrence and effects.

**Does not establish:** The narrative alone does not independently establish the historicity, medical character, or precise numerical effects of the reported miracles.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The conversion summaries may be literary compression rather than exhaustive social reporting.
- Readers may understand the miracle accounts primarily as theological narratives rather than independently corroborated historical reports.
- Other unreported social factors could have contributed to the community's response.
