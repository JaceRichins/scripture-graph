# Historical-Correlation in Daniel 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The little horn's described actions—suppressing the daily sacrifice, desecrating the sanctuary, warring against 'the mighty and the holy people,' and being destroyed by no human hand—closely match the historically documented actions of the Seleucid king Antiochus IV Epiphanes, who in 167 BCE banned Jewish sacrifice and desecrated the Second Temple, an identification most scholars extend from the parallel, more detailed account in Daniel 11.

**Does not establish:** Does not resolve or depend on the disputed question of Daniel's composition date (traditional 6th-century BCE authorship versus critical dating to the Antiochus crisis itself, c. 167-164 BCE); the historical-action match is compatible with either view of when the text was written.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Devotional and some Restoration-tradition readings extend the little horn typologically or additionally to a future 'time of the end' antichrist figure, drawing on 8:17 and 8:19's explicit end-time framing rather than a purely 2nd-century BCE referent.
- Some conservative interpreters resist any Antiochus identification altogether, reading the whole vision as strictly eschatological.
