# LiteraryApologetic Function in Matthew 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sealing and guarding of the tomb (found only in Matthew) appears to anticipate and rebut a specific counter-claim — that the disciples stole the body — which Matthew makes explicit later (Matthew 28:11-15); this suggests the detail served an apologetic function for Matthew's original audience.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish whether the guard narrative is historical reportage, theological composition, or some combination; it only notes the narrative's apparent function within Matthew's Gospel.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The account could reflect straightforward historical memory rather than being shaped primarily by later apologetic needs.
- The guard detail is absent from Mark, Luke, and John, which some scholars take as evidence it is a distinctly Matthean addition rather than independently attested history.
- The episode may serve a theological purpose (underscoring the reality and permanence of the tomb's sealing before the resurrection) apart from any specific polemical exchange.
