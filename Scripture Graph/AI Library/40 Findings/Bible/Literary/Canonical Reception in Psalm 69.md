# Canonical Reception in Psalm 69

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated New Testament reuse of Psalm 69 provides strong evidence that early Christian authors read portions of the psalm christologically and typologically. It is not sufficient evidence that every verse is direct first-person prediction by a sinless Messiah.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the entire psalm is direct speech by Jesus, that its human author consciously foresaw every later application, or that the New Testament events are independently verified by the verbal parallels.

**Alternative explanations:**
- New Testament authors may apply the psalm typologically without treating every line as direct predictive prophecy.
- Some expressions belong to a wider vocabulary of biblical lament and may recall more than one psalm.
- Passion narratives may describe events with scriptural language, so verbal correspondence alone is not independent historical corroboration.
- The psalm may first describe a historical sufferer whose language was subsequently reapplied to Jesus.
