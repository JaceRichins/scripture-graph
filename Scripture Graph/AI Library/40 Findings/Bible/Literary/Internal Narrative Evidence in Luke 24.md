# Internal Narrative Evidence in Luke 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
As evidence of Luke's representational intent, the invitation to inspect and handle Jesus's hands and feet, the explicit reference to flesh and bones, and the eating of food strongly establish that this narrative depicts the risen Jesus as bodily rather than as a disembodied spirit. These literary details do not by themselves verify the event historically.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently establish that the narrated appearance occurred historically or settle every metaphysical question about a resurrected body.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The physical demonstrations may be literary and theological devices emphasizing bodily resurrection.
- The account may preserve a tradition that developed or was shaped during transmission.
- A reader may distinguish the narrative's intended claim from the historical circumstances behind it.
