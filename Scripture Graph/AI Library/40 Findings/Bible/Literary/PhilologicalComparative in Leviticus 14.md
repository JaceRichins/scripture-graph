# PhilologicalComparative in Leviticus 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter's own summary groups 'leprosy' of a person, of a garment, and of a house together under one legal category, which supports the widely held scholarly reading that the Hebrew term tzaraat (translated 'leprosy') denotes a broader category of visible degenerative conditions (skin disease, fabric mildew/discoloration, and structural mold or efflorescence) rather than specifically Hansen's disease.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish the precise modern clinical identity of tzaraat in every instance, which remains debated among historians and biologists.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some traditional readers take 'leprosy' at face value as a single literal disease that can also afflict inanimate objects through a shared supernatural cause, rather than as an umbrella legal category for visually similar but medically distinct phenomena.
