# ArchaeologicalEpigraphic in 2 Kings 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Jehu is one of the few Israelite kings independently attested outside the Bible: the Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III (Assyria, mid-9th century BC) depicts a kneeling figure, labeled in the accompanying inscription 'Jehu, son of Omri,' presenting tribute — even though 2 Kings 9 itself identifies Jehu as son of Jehoshaphat, son of Nimshi, and as the man who violently ended Omri's dynasty. The Assyrian label most likely uses 'House of Omri' as a political/dynastic name for the kingdom of Israel rather than a genealogical claim.

**Does not establish:** Confirms Jehu's historical existence and Assyrian contact/tribute; it does not corroborate the specific narrative details of the coup, the killings, or the prophetic oracles in 2 Kings 9.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Minor scholarly debate exists over whether the depicted figure represents Jehu personally or a royal emissary, and over the precise year of the tribute scene.
- 'Son of Omri' could reflect Assyrian record-keeping conventions that continued to label Israel by its most prominent prior dynasty regardless of the actual ruling house.
