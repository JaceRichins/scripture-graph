# HermeneuticalGenre Analysis in Proverbs 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Sayings such as 16:3 ('Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established') and 16:7 ('When a man's ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him') are examples of the wisdom-literature genre's general, observational principles rather than unconditional individual guarantees; treating them as ironclad promises sits in tension with the qualifying voice the wisdom corpus itself supplies elsewhere (e.g., Job and Ecclesiastes on the suffering of the righteous).

**Does not establish:** does not establish that these sayings are unreliable or non-binding as moral counsel, only that Proverbs' own genre conventions caution against reading them as unconditional individual guarantees

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some readers and traditions treat these as covenantal promises conditioned on obedience rather than as merely statistical generalizations
- Ancient Near Eastern wisdom literature was intended as reliable practical guidance, so framing these sayings as loose 'tendencies' may understate the force their original audience gave them
