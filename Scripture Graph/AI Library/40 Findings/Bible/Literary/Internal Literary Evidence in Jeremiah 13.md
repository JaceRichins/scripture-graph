# Internal Literary Evidence in Jeremiah 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Because the text itself explains how the girdle corresponds to Judah's pride, disobedience, and failed relationship with God, the passage provides strong internal literary evidence that the episode functions as a symbolic prophetic act.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every narrated action occurred historically, nor does it identify the precise location intended by the place-name rendered Euphrates.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The account may be a literary construction or vision rather than a report of a physically enacted journey.
- Even if enacted, some narrative details may have been shaped to clarify the sign's theological meaning.
