# LiteraryRedactional Comparison in 2 Chronicles 29

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Chronicles devotes an entire extended narrative (2 Chronicles 29) to the cleansing, staffing, and re-dedication of the temple under Hezekiah, including named Levitical genealogies and a detailed liturgical program, whereas the Deuteronomistic evaluation of Hezekiah's reign (2 Kings 18:1–8) offers only a brief formulaic notice of his righteousness and cultic reforms without any comparable cleansing narrative. This difference in scale and focus is a real, checkable literary fact and reflects the Chronicler's characteristic emphasis on temple, priesthood, and cultic order.

**Does not establish:** This comparison does not establish which account is historically more primary, nor does it establish that the specific liturgical details (Levitical rosters, day counts, offering totals) are independent eyewitness records rather than Chronicler-shaped composition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The author of Kings may have known of a comparable cleansing but omitted it for reasons of space, source availability, or differing narrative priorities, not because it did not occur.
- The Chronicler may have elaborated or schematized an underlying tradition for theological and liturgical purposes (e.g., legitimizing later temple personnel and practices) rather than transmitting an independent, equally detailed historical record.
