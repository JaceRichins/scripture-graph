# Literary-Christological Correspondence in Mark 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Jesus tells the restored man to report what the Lord has done for him, while the narrator says that the man reports what Jesus has done for him. This close verbal correspondence supports a christologically significant identification of Jesus’ action with the Lord’s compassionate action, although agency language is also possible.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence alone does not establish a complete doctrine of Jesus’ divine identity or settle how Mark understands the relation between Jesus and God.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The man may simply attribute an act performed through Jesus to God as its ultimate source.
- The wording may emphasize divine agency without asserting an ontological identification between Jesus and the Lord.

**Scripture:** [[Mark 5#^mark-5-19|Mark 5:19]], [[Mark 5#^mark-5-20|Mark 5:20]]
