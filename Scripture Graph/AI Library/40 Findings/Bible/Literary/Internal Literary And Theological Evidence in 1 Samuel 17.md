# Internal Literary And Theological Evidence in 1 Samuel 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the narrative, the strongest evidence for its governing theological interpretation is David's explicit pre-combat declaration followed by victory with a sling and stone and the repeated notice that he initially had no sword. This strongly supports the literary claim that the account attributes deliverance to the Lord rather than to conventional military superiority.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish the event's historicity, prove divine intervention outside the narrative, or imply that preparation and skilled action are irrelevant.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A reader may understand the outcome primarily through David's skill with a sling and tactical advantage over a heavily armed opponent.
- The narrator may combine divine causation and effective human action rather than opposing them absolutely.
- The narrative's theological attribution does not independently verify supernatural causation as a historical conclusion.
