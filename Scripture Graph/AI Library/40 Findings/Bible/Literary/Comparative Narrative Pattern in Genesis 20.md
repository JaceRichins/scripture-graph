# Comparative Narrative Pattern in Genesis 20

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The substantial parallels among Genesis 12, Genesis 20, and Genesis 26 strongly support reading the episodes comparatively as a recurring literary motif. They do not, by themselves, determine whether the accounts describe repeated events, variant traditions, deliberate thematic repetition, or some combination of these.

**Does not establish:** The parallels alone do not establish source divisions, direct dependence, fabrication, historicity, or a particular date or authorship model.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The narratives may preserve separate historical incidents involving similar survival strategies.
- They may derive from variant oral or written traditions about one underlying type of event.
- A compiler or author may have retained deliberate repetitions to compare Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Rebekah, and foreign rulers.
- Similar social dangers could have produced similar episodes without direct literary dependence.
