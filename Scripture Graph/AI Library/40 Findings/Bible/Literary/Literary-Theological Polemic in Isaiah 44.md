# Literary-Theological Polemic in Isaiah 44

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The extended wood-idol satire is strong evidence for the chapter's theological opposition to worship directed toward manufactured images. It is less reliable as a complete description of how every ancient image-worshipper understood an idol.

**Does not establish:** The satire does not establish that all practitioners of ancient image worship held the philosophically crude belief attributed to them by the polemic.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Ancient worshippers could regard an image as a representation, embodiment, or cultic dwelling of a deity rather than believe the raw material itself exhausted the deity's identity.
- The passage is intentionally satirical and therefore emphasizes contradiction rather than supplying neutral ethnography.
