# Literary Organization in Jeremiah 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The progression from a general summons to royal justice through judgments on Shallum, Jehoiakim, and Coniah, reinforced by repeated motifs of gates, cedar, oppression, refusal to hear, and exile, provides substantial internal evidence that the chapter has been shaped as a coherent critique of Judah's monarchy.

**Does not establish:** The literary coherence does not establish single-stage composition, exact chronology, sole authorship, or the absence of later editorial arrangement.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Originally separate royal oracles may have been grouped by a later editor because they shared a subject.
- Recurring language can result from Jeremiah's general prophetic vocabulary without implying composition in one sitting.
- The present thematic unity does not require every passage to have originated at the same time.
