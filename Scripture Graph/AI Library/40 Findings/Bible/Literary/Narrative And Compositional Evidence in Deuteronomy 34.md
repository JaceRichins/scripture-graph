# Narrative And Compositional Evidence in Deuteronomy 34

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The report of Moses' death and the statement that his burial place was unknown 'unto this day' provide strong textual evidence that Deuteronomy 34, at least in its presented narrative form, speaks from a perspective after Moses' death.

**Does not establish:** It does not identify the narrator or editor, date the chapter precisely, or determine the authorship and compositional history of the rest of Deuteronomy.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A later scribe or editor may have appended or finalized the chapter without composing the preceding material.
- A traditional faith reading may propose that Moses prophetically described his own death, although the retrospective phrase 'unto this day' still requires explanation.
- The expression may be a conventional narrator's formula and cannot by itself indicate how much time had elapsed.
