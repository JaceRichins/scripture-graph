# Narrative And Theological Pattern in Exodus 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The placement and repetition of Israel's multiplication support the interpretation that the narrator presents the people's growth as resistant to Pharaoh's power and associated with divine favor.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not by itself prove miraculous population growth, reconstruct population numbers, or establish the historicity of the account.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The growth notices can be read principally as demographic background rather than as an implicit covenantal claim.
- Only the favorable treatment of the midwives is explicitly attributed to God in this chapter; divine causation of every multiplication notice is inferred.
