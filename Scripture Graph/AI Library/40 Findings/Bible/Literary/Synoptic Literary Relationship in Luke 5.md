# Synoptic Literary Relationship in Luke 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The leper, paralytic, Levi, fasting controversy, and old-new sayings occur in closely corresponding Synoptic passages. Their shared sequence and content provide strong evidence of literary relationship or common tradition among the accounts, while leaving the precise direction and form of dependence open to source-critical analysis.

**Does not establish:** These parallels do not establish that the Gospel accounts are independent eyewitness reports, nor do they alone prove or disprove the historicity of the narrated events.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The evangelists may have drawn on overlapping oral traditions rather than one directly copying another.
- A combination of written sources, oral transmission, and editorial arrangement may explain the parallels.
- The correspondence does not by itself determine whether Markan priority or another source model is correct.
