# Narrative And Theological Complexity in 2 Kings 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The narrative simultaneously attributes Ahab's fall to the Lord's word and depicts identifiable human choices: Jehu plots and commands, frightened officials comply, and soldiers execute the killings. This layered presentation supports study of divine judgment without erasing human agency or responsibility.

**Does not establish:** The coexistence of divine and human causation does not by itself determine the moral status of every killing or reveal every participant's motive.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A reader may regard the agents as straightforward instruments of divine judgment and see little tension between divine and human causation.
- Some details may primarily explain Jehu's political success rather than invite a sustained moral analysis of responsibility.
