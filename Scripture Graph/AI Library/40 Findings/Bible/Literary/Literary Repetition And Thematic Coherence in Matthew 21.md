# Literary Repetition And Thematic Coherence in Matthew 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Repeated fruit language links the fruitless fig tree with the vineyard parable and the declaration about a nation producing the kingdom's fruits. This is meaningful internal evidence for a chapter-level concern with productive response and accountable stewardship.

**Does not establish:** The repetition does not establish that the fig tree represents Israel, nor does it determine the ethnic or institutional identity of the fruit-bearing nation in verse 43.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Fruit is common agricultural vocabulary, and some repetition may arise independently from the traditions Matthew arranged.
- The fig-tree episode's explicit lesson concerns faith and prayer, so reading it primarily as judgment may subordinate the explanation supplied by the text.
- The apparent unity may reflect Matthew's editorial arrangement rather than a single original discourse.
