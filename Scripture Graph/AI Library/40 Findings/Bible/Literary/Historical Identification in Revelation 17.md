# Historical Identification in Revelation 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
In the first-century setting, the combination of seven mountains, worldwide royal influence, luxury, and violence against Jesus's witnesses strongly supports Rome as the woman's primary historical referent. Calling the city Babylon continues an established Jewish and early Christian practice of using an earlier imperial oppressor as a symbolic name for Rome. This historical identification need not exhaust the image's broader application to oppressive imperial systems.

**Does not establish:** The evidence does not establish that every detail has a single Roman equivalent, that the symbol applies only to ancient Rome, or that one disputed enumeration of the seven kings is correct.

**Alternative explanations:**
- An archetypal reading treats Babylon principally as the recurring world-city or oppressive civilization rather than one historical city.
- Jerusalem-centered readings appeal to biblical prostitution imagery and the city's association with persecution, although the seven mountains and rule over earthly kings fit Rome more naturally.
- Futurist readings identify the woman with a still-future political or religious center.
- The image may intentionally combine Rome with earlier scriptural portraits of Babylon, Tyre, and faithless Jerusalem.
