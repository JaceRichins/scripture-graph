# Historical-Prophetic Naming in Isaiah 44

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The explicit naming of Cyrus alongside the rebuilding of Jerusalem and the temple is a major datum in discussions of the passage's date and prophetic character. It is certain that the received text makes this association, but whether it represents advance prophecy, exilic-period composition, later updating, or another transmission history cannot be decided from the naming alone.

**Does not establish:** The occurrence of Cyrus's name by itself establishes neither supernatural foreknowledge nor a late date, and it does not settle the compositional history of Isaiah.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Traditional readings attribute the specific prediction to an eighth-century Isaiah through divine revelation.
- Many critical scholars assign this material to an exilic prophet writing when Cyrus's rise was known or foreseeable.
- A transmission model could posit an earlier core with later editorial specification or expansion.
