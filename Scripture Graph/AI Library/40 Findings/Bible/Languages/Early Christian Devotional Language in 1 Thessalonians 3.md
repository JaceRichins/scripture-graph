# Early Christian Devotional Language in 1 Thessalonians 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The coordinated petition to God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ is strong primary-text evidence that Jesus is included in the devotional and divine-agent language of this Christian letter.

**Does not establish:** This verse alone does not establish the later creedal formulation of the Trinity, define the ontology of the Father and Son, or provide an exhaustive rule governing Christian prayer.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The wording may express delegated messianic agency without defining the precise metaphysical relationship between the Father and Jesus.
- A formal epistolary prayer may preserve communal devotional convention rather than present a complete theological formulation.

**Scripture:** [[1 Thessalonians 3#^1thes-3-11|1 Thessalonians 3:11]]
