# Narrative And Divine-Messenger Language in Genesis 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The narrative closely associates the angel of the LORD with the LORD: the angel speaks the multiplication promise in the first person, and Hagar subsequently calls the speaker "the LORD." This strongly supports a literary identification between messenger and sender within the episode, while leaving the messenger's precise ontology unresolved.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish a complete doctrine of the angel's identity or prove that every biblical appearance of the angel of the LORD has the same referent.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A commissioned messenger may speak in the sender's first person without being ontologically identical to the sender.
- The passage may intentionally leave the distinction between the LORD and the angel of the LORD unresolved.
