# Legal Intertext in 1 Kings 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Joab's grasping the altar and Solomon's refusal to grant him sanctuary can reasonably be read alongside the law that a presumptuous killer may be taken from God's altar to die. This makes the outcome compatible with a stated legal limit on altar asylum, although 1 Kings does not explicitly cite that law or describe a judicial hearing.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that Joab received due process, that all of Solomon's motives were judicial, or that killing him at the altar was the only lawful response.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Joab's action may have been a general appeal for ritual protection rather than a deliberate invocation of Exodus 21.
- The narrator may assume the legal principle without consciously constructing an intertext.
- Compatibility with the rule does not show that every procedural requirement was observed.

**Scripture:** [[1 Kings 2#^1kgs-2-28|1 Kings 2:28-34]], [[Exodus 21#^ex-21-12|Exodus 21:12-14]]
