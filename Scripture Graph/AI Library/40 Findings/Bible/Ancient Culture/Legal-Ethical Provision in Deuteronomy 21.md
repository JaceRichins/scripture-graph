# Legal-Ethical Provision in Deuteronomy 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The captive-wife law imposes a mandatory month-long mourning/transition period and explicitly forbids selling her or treating her as merchandise, 'because thou hast humbled her'—a real but limited restraint on a victor's treatment of a war captive, operating within, rather than abolishing, the underlying practice of taking captive women as wives.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish gender equality or full personal autonomy for the captive woman by modern standards; the law mitigates but does not eliminate the underlying institution of wartime captive-taking.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The provision can also be read as protecting the male captor's household interests (against treating a woman he has taken as disposable property) rather than as a protection centered primarily on the woman's own autonomy or wellbeing.
