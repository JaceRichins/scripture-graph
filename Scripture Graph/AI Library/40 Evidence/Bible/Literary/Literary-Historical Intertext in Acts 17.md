# Literary-Historical Intertext in Acts 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The wording about humanity being God's offspring closely corresponds to a line in Aratus's Greek poem Phaenomena. Together with Paul's explicit reference to “your own poets,” this is strong evidence that the speech, as narrated, deliberately uses Greek cultural language as a point of contact.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish that Acts preserves a verbatim transcript, that Paul endorsed the poem's theology as a whole, or that he personally read a particular manuscript of Aratus.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The narrator may have selected or shaped the wording while composing a condensed version of the speech.
- Similar divine-offspring language circulated more broadly in Greek philosophical and poetic traditions, including Cleanthes, so dependence on one particular text cannot be proved solely from the phrase.

**Scripture:** [[Acts 17#^acts-17-28|Acts 17:28-29]]

**Sources:**
- Aratus — *Phaenomena, opening invocation* (third century BCE)
