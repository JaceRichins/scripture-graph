# Literary Structure in Ezekiel 35

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The adjacency and thematic reversals between Ezekiel 35 and 36 strongly support reading the chapters as a coordinated literary contrast: the mountains of Seir are judged for coveting desolated Israel, while the mountains of Israel are addressed with promises of renewed fruitfulness and habitation.

**Does not establish:** The literary pairing does not by itself establish a single compositional stage, an exact date for either oracle, or any larger historical or theological conclusion beyond the textual contrast.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The contrast may result from later editorial arrangement rather than a single original composition.
- Some verbal similarity may arise from Ezekiel's recurring vocabulary for land, desolation, possession, and restoration.
