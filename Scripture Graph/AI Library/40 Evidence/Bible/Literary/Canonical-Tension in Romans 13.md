# Canonical-Tension in Romans 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Romans 13:1-2's counsel of submission to civil authority, which states no exception clause, sits in tension with other apostolic precedent for disobeying civil or religious commands on grounds of conscience, such as Acts 5:29.

**Does not establish:** That Paul intended Romans 13:1-7 to include an implicit exception for unjust or idolatrous commands; the text itself states no such exception, so this is a tension raised by reading the passages together, not a claim about Paul's own intent.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Romans 13 may be addressing routine civic duties (taxes, public order) rather than matters of worship or conscience, a distinction the text does not make explicit but which many interpreters supply.
- The epistle may reflect the specific, comparatively mild circumstances of Rome under early Nero (c. AD 57) rather than a timeless claim valid under every form of government.
- Some interpreters read Romans 13 and Acts 5:29 as addressing different domains (civil order versus direct religious command) that Paul did not intend as being in tension.
