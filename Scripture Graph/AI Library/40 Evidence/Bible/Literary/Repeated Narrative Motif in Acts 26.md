# Repeated Narrative Motif in Acts 26

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The declaration of Paul's innocence in Acts 26 continues a sequence of official findings in Acts that the dispute concerns religious questions rather than a capital Roman offense. This supports the interpretation that Acts has a sustained apologetic interest in distinguishing Christian proclamation from punishable political crime.

**Does not establish:** The internal verdicts do not independently prove Paul's legal innocence or the historical accuracy of every reported proceeding.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The repeated verdicts may chiefly report what the author regarded as historically accurate rather than serving a deliberate literary apology.
- The officials' judgments address Paul in particular and need not imply approval of Christianity in general.
- Literary and historical explanations are not mutually exclusive.
