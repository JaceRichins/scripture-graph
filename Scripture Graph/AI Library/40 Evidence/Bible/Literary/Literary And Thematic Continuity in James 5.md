# Literary And Thematic Continuity in James 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The combination of corrupt riches, unpaid wages, the cry of the oppressed, and approaching divine judgment is strong textual evidence that James 5:1-6 participates in the ethical and rhetorical world of biblical prophetic social criticism.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not by itself identify a specific written source, prove direct literary dependence, or settle questions of authorship and date.

**Alternative explanations:**
- James may be drawing on generally shared Jewish moral teaching rather than deliberately invoking one particular earlier passage.
- Similar denunciations can arise independently wherever wealthy landowners exploit hired workers.
