# Comparative Narrative Evidence in 2 Kings 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The presence of Manasseh's repentance and reforms in Chronicles and their absence from Kings is strong evidence of different narrative selection and emphasis. Silence in Kings is not, by itself, evidence that its author denied that such events occurred.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish contradiction, complete historical compatibility, the historicity of every reported event, or one author's awareness of the other's full account.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The authors may have used different sources or possessed different information.
- Kings may deliberately omit later personal reform because its explanation of Judah's fall concentrates on enduring national consequences.
- Chronicles may shape its account around its characteristic interest in humility, prayer, restoration, and temple worship.
- Either account could contain historical compression; comparison alone cannot determine the complete sequence of events.
