# Immediate Literary Context in Philippians 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The immediate context strongly supports reading "I can do all things" primarily as Christ-enabled endurance across changing material conditions—abasement, abundance, hunger, fullness, and need—rather than as a promise of unlimited achievement.

**Does not establish:** It does not define every work Christ may empower, but it does weigh strongly against treating the verse as a promise of limitless success.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The wording can legitimately be applied more broadly to faithful responsibilities empowered by Christ.
- Themes elsewhere in the letter may enlarge the principle beyond financial circumstances, though not into a guarantee of every desired outcome.
