# Historical-Symbolic Identification in Revelation 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within Revelation's first-century setting, Babylon is widely understood as a symbolic representation of Rome, especially as an imperial city exercising influence over kings, international trade, and persecution. This is a strong historical reading, though the symbol may also portray oppressive imperial culture more broadly.

**Does not establish:** The historical case for Rome does not prove that every detail maps to a specific Roman event, nor does it exclude later or archetypal applications.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Babylon may function primarily as a transhistorical archetype of corrupt civilization.
- Some interpreters expect a future literal or newly constituted Babylon.
- The image can refer initially to Rome while intentionally exceeding Rome in scope.
