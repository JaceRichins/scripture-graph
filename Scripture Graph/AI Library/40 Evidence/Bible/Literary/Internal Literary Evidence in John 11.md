# Internal Literary Evidence in John 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The four-day burial, Martha's expectation of decomposition, the closed tomb, and Lazarus's emergence in graveclothes form strong internal evidence that the narrator intends readers to understand Lazarus as genuinely dead, not merely asleep or mistakenly diagnosed.

**Does not establish:** These narrative details establish the story's intended meaning but do not independently prove that the reported resurrection occurred historically.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The details could be literary construction designed to make the theological claim unmistakable.
- A historical memory could have been shaped or amplified in transmission.
- The narrative supplies no independent contemporary corroboration within the chapter.
