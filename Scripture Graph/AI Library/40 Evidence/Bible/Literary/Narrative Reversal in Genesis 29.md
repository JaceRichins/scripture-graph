# Narrative Reversal in Genesis 29

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Laban's secret substitution of the elder daughter for the younger creates a strong narrative reversal of Genesis 27: Jacob, the younger son who displaced his elder brother through deception, is deceived when Laban insists that a younger daughter must not precede the firstborn. This supports a measure-for-measure reading, although the narrator does not explicitly call the event divine punishment.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish that God caused or approved Laban's deception, nor that every subsequent hardship is punishment for Jacob's earlier conduct.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The reversal may be an unintended resemblance produced by the two stories' common concern with family precedence.
- Laban presents local custom, rather than retribution for Jacob's past, as his explanation.
