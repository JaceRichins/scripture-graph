# Narrative Pattern in Exodus 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The placement of growth notices after both affliction and the midwives' resistance provides strong internal evidence for deliberate narrative reversal: policies intended to suppress Israel are followed by statements that Israel multiplied. This supports a theme of frustrated tyranny within the chapter.

**Does not establish:** The literary reversal does not independently establish how rapidly the population grew or supply external historical corroboration.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The sequence may primarily preserve a traditional account of events rather than function as crafted irony.
- The growth notices may serve continuity with ancestral promises without making opposition to Pharaoh the principal theme.
