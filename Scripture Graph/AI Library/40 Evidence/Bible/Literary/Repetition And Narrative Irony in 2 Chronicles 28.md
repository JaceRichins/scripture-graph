# Repetition And Narrative Irony in 2 Chronicles 28

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated vocabulary and outcomes surrounding help provide substantial internal evidence for a deliberate critique of Ahaz's policy: neither imperial tribute nor imitating a victorious enemy's gods supplies the security he seeks.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every military setback resulted directly from one policy or that alliances are condemned in every circumstance.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Repeated references to help may arise naturally from the account's political subject matter.
- The anti-Assyrian and anti-idolatry emphases could reflect retrospective theological evaluation rather than Ahaz's own understanding.
