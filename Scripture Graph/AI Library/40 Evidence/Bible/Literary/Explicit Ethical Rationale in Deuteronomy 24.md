# Explicit Ethical Rationale in Deuteronomy 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Verses 18 and 22 explicitly ground duties toward vulnerable people in Israel's remembered slavery and redemption from Egypt. This is strong evidence that the chapter joins historical memory to social ethics: recipients of deliverance are commanded to restrain their power over others.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that these protections were consistently enforced or that ancient Israel eliminated economic hierarchy.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The exodus memory also functions as a covenantal appeal to obedience, not solely as an invitation to empathetic identification.
- The laws can simultaneously serve social stability, agricultural practice, and theological formation.
