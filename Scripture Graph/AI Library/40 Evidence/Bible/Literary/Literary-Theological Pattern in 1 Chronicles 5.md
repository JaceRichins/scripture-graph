# Literary-Theological Pattern in 1 Chronicles 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Chronicler explicitly attributes the Transjordan tribes' victory over the Hagarites to prayer and trust in God rather than to military strength alone: "they cried to God in the battle, and he was entreated of them; because they put their trust in him" (v20), and "the war was of God" (v22). This is best read as an instance of the Chronicler's recurring theology of immediate divine response to faithfulness, not as a historical claim about battlefield mechanics.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish the historical accuracy of the battle's details or numbers, only that the Chronicler frames the outcome theologically.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Ascribing military victory to a deity's favor was a common convention across ancient Near Eastern war narratives generally, not a feature unique to the Chronicler's theology.
- The statement may function rhetorically to encourage the Chronicler's post-exilic audience rather than to report the combatants' actual stated motivations.
