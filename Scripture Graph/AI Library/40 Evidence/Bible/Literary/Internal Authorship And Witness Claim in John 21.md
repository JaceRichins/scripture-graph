# Internal Authorship And Witness Claim in John 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
John 21 identifies the beloved disciple as the one who testifies about and wrote “these things,” followed by a plural affirmation that his testimony is true. This is strong evidence for the Gospel's own claimed connection to an eyewitness witness, but it does not independently verify that claim or explicitly identify the disciple as John son of Zebedee.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently prove eyewitness authorship, identify the beloved disciple by name, or establish that one person composed the Gospel in its final form.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A later editor or community may be authenticating traditions associated with the beloved disciple rather than asserting that he composed every word of the final Gospel.
- The beloved disciple may function partly as a literary ideal even if connected to a historical witness.
- The verse does not supply the disciple's personal name, so identifying him with John son of Zebedee depends on evidence beyond this chapter.
