# Internal Literary Contrast in 2 Chronicles 28

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter's juxtaposition of Israel's response to prophetic correction with Ahaz's increasing transgression supports a deliberate contrast between repentance and hardened persistence under warning or distress.

**Does not establish:** This contrast does not establish the private motives of the Ephraimite leaders, the historicity of every narrative detail, or a general claim that distress always produces either repentance or apostasy.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The episodes may have been placed together primarily because they belong to the same reign rather than to create a formal literary contrast.
- The Ephraimite leaders may act chiefly from fear of divine wrath rather than from inward repentance or compassion.
