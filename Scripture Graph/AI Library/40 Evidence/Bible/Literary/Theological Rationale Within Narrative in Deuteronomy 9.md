# Theological Rationale Within Narrative in Deuteronomy 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Deuteronomy 9 explicitly presents the conquest as divine judgment on the nations and fulfillment of a patriarchal oath, but within this chapter those theological assertions are narrated rather than independently demonstrated.

**Does not establish:** The chapter by itself does not independently verify the nations' wickedness, resolve the moral problem of conquest, or authorize later communities to label enemies wicked and dispossess them.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A confessional reading may accept the stated divine judgment as authoritative revelation.
- A historical-critical reading may understand the rationale as Israelite theological interpretation of conquest and land possession.
- The language may combine remembered conflict with conventional ancient warfare rhetoric.
