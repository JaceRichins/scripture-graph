# Literary Prediction-Fulfillment in 1 Kings 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The narrative closely matches Ahijah's prediction to its reported outcome: the child dies when Jeroboam's wife reaches the threshold, and Israel buries and mourns him. This is strong evidence that the narrator deliberately presents Ahijah's word as reliable within the story.

**Does not establish:** The internal fulfillment establishes the narrator's portrayal of prophetic reliability; by itself it does not prove supernatural prediction or independently verify the event's historicity.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The account may have been composed or shaped after the child's death so that prediction and outcome correspond.
- The correspondence can serve a theological-literary purpose without independently establishing the historical sequence.
- If the underlying events are historical, the death at the stated moment could still be interpreted without invoking supernatural foreknowledge.
