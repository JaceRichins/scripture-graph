# Internal Prediction-Fulfillment Pattern in Acts 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the narrative, Agabus's prediction corresponds substantially to the ensuing sequence: Jews seize Paul and seek his death, after which Roman soldiers take custody of him and bind him. The correspondence is not exact in every narrated detail because the Roman commander explicitly orders the chains.

**Does not establish:** The literary correspondence alone does not establish supernatural foreknowledge, precise verbal inerrancy, or the historical independence of the prediction from the arrest account.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The prediction may summarize Jewish responsibility for the chain of events rather than specify who physically applied the chains.
- The narrative may have been shaped retrospectively so that the warning anticipates the later arrest.
- The prediction could reflect a reasonable expectation based on known hostility toward Paul.
