# Internal Literary Rationale in Exodus 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The explicit appeal to Israel's former status as strangers in Egypt is strong internal evidence that the chapter connects remembered deliverance with the duty not to oppress strangers.

**Does not establish:** This internal connection does not by itself establish the date of composition, the historicity of the exodus, or the consistent observance of the command.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The rationale may function as a conventional literary or covenantal appeal rather than preserving a direct memory from the period described.
- The appeal may primarily establish group identity and reciprocity rather than articulate a universal theory of rights.
