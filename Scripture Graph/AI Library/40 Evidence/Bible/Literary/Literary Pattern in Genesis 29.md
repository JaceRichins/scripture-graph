# Literary Pattern in Genesis 29

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Genesis 29 shares a cluster of motifs with other biblical marriage narratives: a journey to another land, an encounter with a woman at a well, watering connected with hospitality or assistance, news carried to the woman's household, and an ensuing marriage. The cluster supports reading the episode as a deliberately shaped betrothal scene.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not establish direct literary dependence, the historicity of every narrated detail, or a particular date of composition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Wells were natural social centers in pastoral settings, so some similarities may arise from ordinary circumstances rather than a formal literary convention.
- The narratives may reflect a shared cultural pattern without one passage intentionally imitating another.
