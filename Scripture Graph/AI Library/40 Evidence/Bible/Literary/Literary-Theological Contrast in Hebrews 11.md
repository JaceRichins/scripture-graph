# Literary-Theological Contrast in Hebrews 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The catalogue attributes both spectacular deliverance and faithful suffering to faith. Some conquer, escape death, and receive the dead back; others reject deliverance and are tortured, imprisoned, displaced, or killed. This strongly supports reading the chapter as a theology of fidelity rather than a guarantee of temporal success.

**Does not establish:** The contrast does not show that earthly deliverance is unrelated to faith, nor does it predict the outcome of any particular believer's trial.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The suffering examples could be read primarily as a supplement to the victory examples rather than as a deliberate correction of triumphal expectations.
- The promised reward may be understood eschatologically, so the contrast concerns timing rather than whether faith is rewarded.
