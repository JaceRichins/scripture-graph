# Intra-Canonical Theological Tension in James 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
James 2:24's statement that 'by works a man is justified, and not by faith only' stands in apparent verbal tension with Paul's teaching that a person is justified by faith apart from works of the law (e.g., Romans 3:28, Galatians 2:16), a tension noted since antiquity and famously sharpened by Luther, who questioned James's canonical standing partly on this basis.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish that James and Paul are in doctrinal agreement, nor that they are in irreconcilable contradiction; it establishes only that a real, long-recognized interpretive problem exists that any harmonization must address rather than ignore.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Paul and James may use 'faith,' 'works,' and 'justified' with different technical senses: Paul often targets 'works of the law' (ritual/ceremonial boundary markers such as circumcision) as insufficient for initial acceptance before God, while James addresses whether a bare intellectual assent ('faith' with no accompanying conduct) can save, i.e. a living faith evidenced by fruit.
- The two authors may be addressing different pastoral problems (antinomian complacency in James's audience vs. legalistic works-righteousness in Paul's Galatian audience) rather than making a shared systematic claim about the mechanism of salvation.
- Some scholars hold the tension is real and reflects competing early-Christian factions or theologies that were only harmonized later by the church's canonical process.
