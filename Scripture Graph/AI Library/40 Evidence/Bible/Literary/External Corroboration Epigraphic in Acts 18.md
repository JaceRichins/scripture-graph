# External Corroboration Epigraphic in Acts 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Luke's note that Paul was brought before Gallio, proconsul ('deputy') of Achaia, ties this episode to a datable Roman official; an inscription discovered at Delphi recording a rescript of the emperor Claudius mentioning Gallio as proconsul of Achaia is used by scholars to anchor Gallio's term to approximately AD 51-52, giving one of the firmest fixed points in Pauline chronology.

**Does not establish:** That every other date in Acts or in Paul's letters can be fixed with equal precision from this single anchor point; it only constrains the Corinth ministry to roughly this window.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The Delphi inscription is fragmentary and its exact year range (AD 51 vs 52, and the precise months of Gallio's single-year term) is reconstructed and debated within a narrow band by specialists.
- The inscription confirms Gallio held the proconsulship around this time; it does not independently confirm the specific events Luke narrates (the synagogue trial, the beating of Sosthenes) actually occurred as described.
