# External Inscription in 2 Kings 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Mesha Stele independently attests a ninth-century BCE Moabite ruler named Mesha and describes Moabite conflict with Omri's Israelite dynasty and Moabite recovery. This supports the broad personal and geopolitical setting of 2 Kings 3 more strongly than it supports identification of any specific episode in the chapter.

**Does not establish:** The stele does not independently confirm this expedition, Elisha's oracle, the water event, the sacrifice, the meaning of the indignation, or supernatural causation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The biblical account and inscription may describe different phases or campaigns within a longer Israel-Moab conflict.
- Each text presents events from its own royal or theological perspective.
- Shared rulers and geopolitical conditions do not require that both texts narrate the same battle.
