# Literary And Chronological Framing in Exodus 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The references to preservation before the Testimony and to forty years of manna frame the episode from a later narrative vantage than the initial wilderness complaint. This supports a final-form reading in which the immediate event is connected to later sanctuary memory and the completed wilderness period.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish multiple authors, a particular documentary source, or a date for the chapter's composition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A narrator can summarize later outcomes without implying a separate source or editor.
- The commands may be narrated proleptically, with their implementation understood to occur after the sanctuary objects existed.
- The closing notices may reflect editorial arrangement, but the text alone cannot identify its stages or dates.
