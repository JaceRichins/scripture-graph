# Cumulative Literary Characterization in 1 Samuel 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within this episode, the combination of Jonathan's successful initiative, his concern about the army's exhaustion, the people's praise, and his rescue provides substantial internal literary evidence that the narrative presents him more favorably than Saul. This remains an interpretation of narrative emphasis rather than an explicit comprehensive verdict on either man.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every action of Jonathan is approved or that every action of Saul in the chapter is condemned.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Jonathan nevertheless violates a royal oath, and the lot identifies him.
- Saul acts to stop the people's blood violation and seeks divine counsel, details that complicate a wholly negative portrait.
- The people's defense may reflect military gratitude and pragmatic loyalty rather than the narrator's complete moral judgment.
