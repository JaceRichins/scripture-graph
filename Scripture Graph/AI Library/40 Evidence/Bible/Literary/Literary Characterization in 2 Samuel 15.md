# Literary Characterization in 2 Samuel 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The juxtaposition of Israel's transferred allegiance with Ittai's costly oath supplies strong internal evidence for a narrative contrast between mass political popularity and freely chosen personal fidelity. The contrast is especially pointed because David identifies Ittai as a recent foreign exile and releases him from any obligation to follow.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the narrator's precise compositional intention, Ittai's complete motives, or the historical accuracy of every reported speech.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The contrast may arise primarily from chronological narration rather than deliberate literary design.
- Ittai's oath may reflect military or patronage obligations not explained in the chapter.
- The narrator may be idealizing a loyal supporter of David.
