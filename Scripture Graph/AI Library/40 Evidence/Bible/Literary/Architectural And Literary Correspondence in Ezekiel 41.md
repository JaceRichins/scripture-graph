# Architectural And Literary Correspondence in Ezekiel 41

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The combination of a twenty-by-twenty inner room, multi-story side chambers kept from fastening into the main wall, cherub-and-palm decoration, and elaborately divided doors provides strong textual evidence that Ezekiel 41 presents its sanctuary in continuity with the Israelite temple tradition represented by Solomon's temple.

**Does not establish:** These correspondences do not establish direct copying from Kings, the historical construction of Ezekiel's temple, supernatural origin, or that every architectural difference carries symbolic meaning.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Ezekiel and Kings may independently reflect a shared temple-building tradition rather than direct literary dependence.
- Some similarities may arise from conventional ancient temple architecture and vocabulary.
- The vision may deliberately transform remembered temple features rather than propose a restoration of Solomon's exact plan.
