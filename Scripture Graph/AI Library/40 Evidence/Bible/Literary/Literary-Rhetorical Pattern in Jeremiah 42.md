# Literary-Rhetorical Pattern in Jeremiah 42

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter constructs an explicit reversal: Egypt is desired as an escape from war and hunger, yet the sword and famine feared by the remnant are said to pursue them there. This is strong evidence for a deliberate rhetorical argument that security sought through disobedience becomes the vehicle of the feared danger.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not by itself establish the oracle's historical fulfillment, date of composition, single authorship, or divine origin.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The matching vocabulary may partly reflect conventional prophetic judgment formulas.
- A compiler or editor could have sharpened the verbal correspondence while arranging the narrative.
