# Internal Literary Evidence in Exodus 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The prediction of specific timing, the reported fulfillment of Moses's words, the magicians' failure, and the announced protection of Goshen strongly support the literary conclusion that the narrator presents the plagues as purposeful signs of the LORD's unique authority rather than as meaningless disasters.

**Does not establish:** These internal narrative features do not independently establish the historical occurrence, miraculous mechanism, precise zoological identity, or date of the plagues.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The features may reflect literary shaping intended to communicate theology rather than a recoverable sequence of externally verified events.
- Natural phenomena could have supplied narrative materials later interpreted or arranged as signs.
- The magicians' failure may function primarily as a polemical narrative device.
