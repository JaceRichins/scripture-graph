# Internal Thematic Pattern in Exodus 23

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The adjacent seventh-year and seventh-day laws provide strong internal evidence that the chapter presents recurring rest as benefiting several vulnerable dependents of Israelite economic life: poor people, servants, strangers, domestic animals, and wild animals.

**Does not establish:** This pattern does not by itself establish a modern theory of labor rights, animal rights, environmentalism, or economic policy.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The laws may be motivated partly by agricultural sustainability and labor management rather than by a single unified social ethic.
- The beneficiaries named in the two laws differ, so the parallel should not erase their distinct purposes.
