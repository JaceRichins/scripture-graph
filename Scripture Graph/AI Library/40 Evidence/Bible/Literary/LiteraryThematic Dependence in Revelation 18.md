# LiteraryThematic Dependence in Revelation 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The angel's symbolic act of throwing a millstone into the sea to signify Babylon's irreversible destruction (18:21) parallels the sign-act in Jeremiah 51, where Seraiah is instructed to bind a stone to a scroll of judgment oracles and sink it in the Euphrates as a sign that Babylon will not rise again. The parallel is thematic (a stone cast into water as a permanence sign) rather than verbal.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish direct quotation; establishes at most a shared symbolic vocabulary for 'unrecoverable sinking' as a figure for Babylon's end.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Casting an object into water as a judgment sign may be a broader prophetic convention rather than unique dependence on Jeremiah 51.
- The two acts differ in mechanics — Jeremiah's sign is a one-time act performed by a named scribe with a scroll; Revelation's is performed by an angel with an actual millstone — so the connection is symbolic rather than a shared literary unit.
