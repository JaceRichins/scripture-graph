# Narrative And Political Contrast in 2 Samuel 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The contrasting accession accounts contribute to a favorable narrative presentation of David: the LORD directs David to Hebron and the men of Judah anoint him, whereas Abner takes Ish-bosheth to Mahanaim and makes him king. This is meaningful literary evidence about the narrator's framing, though it is not independent proof that every participant regarded David as the sole legitimate king.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently establish divine rejection of Ish-bosheth, universal contemporary acceptance of David, or the historical neutrality of the account.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The different verbs may reflect narrative compression rather than a deliberate judgment about legitimacy.
- Abner may have been implementing a recognized dynastic succession supported by constituencies not described in detail.
- The account may preserve or promote a Davidic perspective on the conflict.
