# Internal Literary Correspondence in 2 Chronicles 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The narrative closely coordinates Elijah's announced judgments with the subsequent account: the threatened loss affecting people, children, wives, and goods is followed by invasion and the removal of royal property and family, while the threatened bowel disease is followed by an incurable illness and death after two years.

**Does not establish:** The literary correspondence alone does not establish when the prophecy was composed, the mechanism of supernatural prediction, or that the account reports every relevant historical cause.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The account may have been selected or arranged retrospectively so that events correspond to the prophetic announcement.
- The correspondence may reflect conventional ancient theological historiography rather than a complete chronological record.
- Some losses and the disease could have ordinary political or medical causes even if the narrator interprets them theologically.
