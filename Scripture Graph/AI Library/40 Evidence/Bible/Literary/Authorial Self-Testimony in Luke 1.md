# Authorial Self-Testimony in Luke 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Luke's prologue is a direct authorial assertion about his method and sources — that he used material from those who "were eyewitnesses" so Theophilus could "know the certainty" of his instruction — but this claim is itself unverifiable from within the text; it tells readers what Luke asserts about his sourcing, not independent proof that the underlying events are accurately reported.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish that the specific events narrated (the annunciations, the Visitation, the Benedictus) are historically accurate — only that Luke claims a careful, eyewitness-based method.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Ancient prefaces claiming careful research and eyewitness sourcing were a conventional rhetorical topos in Greco-Roman historiography, which neither guarantees nor rules out accuracy.
- The address to Theophilus and the described research process could be, in part, a literary framing device rather than a description of an actual investigative process.
