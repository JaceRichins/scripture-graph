# Historical-Scriptural Context in Ezekiel 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The convergence of failed mobilization, sword outside, famine and pestilence inside, seizure of houses, sanctuary defilement, and collapsed leadership strongly supports reading the chapter as an oracle of comprehensive conquest or siege judgment. The fall of Jerusalem narrated in 2 Kings 25 supplies a close historical-scriptural context for such a reading.

**Does not establish:** The thematic correspondence alone does not prove that every line was composed at one moment, nor does it independently establish the chapter's exact date.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Several images are conventional descriptions of national catastrophe and need not refer exclusively to one siege.
- The oracle names the land of Israel and a city but does not explicitly name Jerusalem, Babylon, or a date within this chapter.
