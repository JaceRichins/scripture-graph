# Practice Referenced In Rhetorical Argument in 1 Corinthians 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Paul's question about people baptized for the dead is evidence that some practice bearing that description was sufficiently recognizable to function in his argument for resurrection. The verse appeals to the practice but does not explain its administration or provide a complete theology of it.

**Does not establish:** The verse alone does not establish who performed the baptisms, for whom they were performed, whether Paul approved every aspect of the practice, or what salvific effect he attributed to it.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The exact expression may refer to a practice different from later proxy-baptism systems.
- Paul may cite a practice he does not personally endorse.
- The argument could refer to a limited local custom or employ language whose precise idiom is now uncertain.
- The third-person they may distance Paul from the participants, although that inference is not certain.

**Scripture:** [[1 Corinthians 15#^1cor-15-29|1 Corinthians 15:29]]
