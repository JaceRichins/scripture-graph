# Comparative Literary Evidence in 2 Chronicles 33

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The substantial repentance-and-restoration episode distinctive to Chronicles is strong evidence that repentance, responsive prayer, and divine mercy are deliberate concerns of this version of Manasseh's story. Its absence from 2 Kings does not by itself determine whether the episode is historically authentic.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish either that the repentance account is invented or that every reported event can be independently verified as history.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Chronicles may preserve information omitted by the author of Kings.
- The Chronicler may have expanded an inherited tradition to emphasize a theology of repentance.
- Kings and Chronicles may select different events because their narrative purposes differ.
