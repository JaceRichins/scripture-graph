# Biblical-Theological in Acts 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Peter's parallel accusations — that Ananias lied 'to the Holy Ghost' (v.3) and 'unto God' (v.4) — treat lying to the Holy Ghost and lying to God as equivalent offenses, which functions as textual evidence for an identification of the Holy Ghost with God within Luke's theology in Acts.

**Does not establish:** Does not by itself establish a fully articulated doctrine of the Holy Ghost as a distinct divine personage; it shows Luke treating offense against the Spirit as offense against God in this narrative.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Luke may use 'Holy Ghost' and 'God' as loosely interchangeable ways of referring to divine presence or power rather than making a formal ontological claim about a distinct divine person.
- The equivalence could reflect Hebraic/functional idiom (God acting through his Spirit) rather than a developed doctrine of the Godhead.
