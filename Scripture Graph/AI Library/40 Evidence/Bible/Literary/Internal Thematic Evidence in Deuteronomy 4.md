# Internal Thematic Evidence in Deuteronomy 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close juxtaposition of threatened covenant judgment and promised mercy provides strong textual evidence that the chapter intentionally holds divine severity and covenant faithfulness together: apostasy has real consequences, yet dispersion does not make repentance or renewed relationship impossible.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that repentance always removes temporal losses, nor does it give a complete account of judgment, mercy, or later historical fulfillments.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A reader may emphasize the conditional character of mercy: the passage speaks of seeking, turning, and obeying rather than unconditional restoration.
- The promise is addressed to covenant Israel and should not automatically be converted into a context-free guarantee about every temporal consequence.
