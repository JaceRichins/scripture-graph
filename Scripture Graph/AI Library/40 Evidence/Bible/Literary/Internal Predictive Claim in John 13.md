# Internal Predictive Claim in John 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the narrative, Jesus states that he is disclosing the betrayal beforehand so that its occurrence may become a reason for the disciples to believe. This securely identifies the narrative's intended evidentiary function, but the passage by itself is not independent historical proof that the prediction preceded the event.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently establish supernatural foreknowledge, the date when the saying was first recorded, or the precise history of its transmission.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The evangelist may have shaped the prediction after the betrayal was already known.
- The saying may preserve an earlier prediction but have been expanded or framed by the narrator.
- Its principal function may be theological reassurance rather than provision of independently testable evidence.
