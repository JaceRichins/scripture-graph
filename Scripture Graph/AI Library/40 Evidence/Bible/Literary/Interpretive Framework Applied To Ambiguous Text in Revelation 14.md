# Interpretive Framework Applied To Ambiguous Text in Revelation 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
A common reading treats the grain harvest (14:14-16) as the gathering of the righteous and the grape/winepress (14:17-20) as judgment on the wicked, paralleling the wheat/tares separation elsewhere in the Gospels. However, Revelation 14 itself never states that the grain harvest is positive or that it gathers only the righteous; some scholars read both the harvest and the vintage as parallel images of the same climactic judgment on 'the earth,' with the wheat/tares contrast imported from Matthew 13 rather than stated here.

**Does not establish:** That Revelation 14 itself distinguishes a 'good' harvest from a 'bad' vintage; that distinction is drawn from outside the chapter.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The grain harvest represents the gathering of the righteous/elect, distinct from and preceding the wrathful vintage.
- Both harvest and vintage are two images of the same undifferentiated final judgment on 'the earth,' without a righteous/wicked split built into this chapter.
- The grain harvest could represent judgment as well, since 'harvest' language elsewhere in Revelation and the prophets is frequently used for judgment, not gathering of the saved (e.g., Joel 3:13).
