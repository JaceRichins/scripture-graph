# Historical-Philological Identification in Esther 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ahasuerus is widely identified by scholars with Xerxes I of Persia (reigned 486–465 BC); the Hebrew name Achashverosh corresponds philologically to the Old Persian royal name Xšayārša (Greek Xerxes), and the description of an empire from India to Ethiopia/Cush matches the known extent of the Achaemenid empire under Darius I and Xerxes I.

**Does not establish:** This identification does not establish that the specific banquet, Vashti incident, or decree in chapter 1 are historically documented events; it only situates the narrative plausibly within the known Achaemenid Persian empire.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The Septuagint and some later traditions instead identify the king as Artaxerxes, reflecting an alternate transmission or a different Persian-era setting.
- Some minority scholarship treats Esther as a later novella using a generic/idealized Persian court setting rather than reporting a specific historical monarch.
