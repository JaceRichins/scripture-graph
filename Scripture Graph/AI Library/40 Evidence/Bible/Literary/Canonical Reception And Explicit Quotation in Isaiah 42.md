# Canonical Reception And Explicit Quotation in Isaiah 42

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Matthew's extended quotation is decisive evidence that an early Christian canonical author interpreted Isaiah 42:1-4 christologically. It strongly supports the Christian reading of the servant as Jesus within the New Testament's own interpretive framework.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself prove that Isaiah's first audience understood the servant exclusively as the future Jesus, nor does it eliminate collective or historical levels of reference.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Matthew may employ typological fulfillment rather than claim that Jesus was the passage's only possible historical referent.
- A collective-Israel reading in Isaiah's historical setting can coexist with Matthew's identification of Jesus as the representative or ideal servant.
- Canonical Christian interpretation and reconstruction of Isaiah's original historical horizon are related but distinct questions.
