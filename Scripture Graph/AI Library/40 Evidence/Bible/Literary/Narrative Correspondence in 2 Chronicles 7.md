# Narrative Correspondence in 2 Chronicles 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The larger narrative of Chronicles later depicts apostasy, pollution of the temple, burning of the sanctuary, and exile. This is strong literary evidence that Chronicles intends the destruction account to be read as a realization of the alternatives announced in 2 Chronicles 7.

**Does not establish:** The narrative correspondence does not establish the date when the warning was composed, predictive foreknowledge, or a complete historical explanation of the Babylonian conquest.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The warning may have been shaped or emphasized retrospectively in light of the already-known exile.
- Both passages may independently apply conventional Deuteronomic covenant theology rather than form a deliberately signaled prediction-fulfillment pair.
