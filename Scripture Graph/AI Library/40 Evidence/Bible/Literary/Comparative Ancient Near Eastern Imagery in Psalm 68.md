# Comparative Ancient Near Eastern Imagery in Psalm 68

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The divine rider and storm-theophany imagery belongs to a broader ancient Northwest Semitic poetic world. Psalm 68 applies that imagery emphatically to JAH, the God of Israel. This is meaningful evidence of cultural and literary interaction, not evidence that the psalm simply identifies Israel's God with another deity.

**Does not establish:** The comparison does not establish polytheism in this psalm, dependence on a particular surviving Canaanite text, or a precise date of composition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some features, such as clouds and rain, may independently arise from ordinary observation of storms.
- The psalm may inherit already-Israelite traditional language without direct contact with non-Israelite texts.
- Shared poetic imagery need not imply shared theology.
