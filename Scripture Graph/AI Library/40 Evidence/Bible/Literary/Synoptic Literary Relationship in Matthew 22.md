# Synoptic Literary Relationship in Matthew 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The corresponding sequence of controversies in Matthew 22, Mark 12, and Luke 20 is strong internal evidence that the Synoptic accounts are literarily or traditionally related. Mark includes the tribute, resurrection, greatest-commandment, and Davidic-Lord episodes; Luke preserves the tribute, resurrection, and Davidic-Lord sequence.

**Does not establish:** The parallels alone do not determine the exact direction of dependence, settle Synoptic priority, or prove the historical details of each narrated exchange.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The evangelists may independently preserve a stable oral sequence.
- Two or more accounts may depend on a shared written source.
- One Gospel may depend directly or indirectly on another.
- Oral and literary dependence may both have contributed.
