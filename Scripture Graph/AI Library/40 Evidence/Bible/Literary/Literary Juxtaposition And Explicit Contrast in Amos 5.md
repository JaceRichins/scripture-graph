# Literary Juxtaposition And Explicit Contrast in Amos 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The placement of rejected feasts, offerings, and music after the indictment of oppression, followed by the demand for judgment and righteousness, strongly supports a critique of worship practiced amid injustice. The immediate context does not support treating the passage as a universal abolition of sacrifice, assemblies, or sacred music.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the Law of Moses, sacrifice, ordinances, communal worship, or sacred music are inherently objectionable.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The rites may also be rejected because they are associated with condemned northern sanctuaries or idolatry.
- The passage may combine several grounds for rejection rather than identifying social injustice as the sole cause.
