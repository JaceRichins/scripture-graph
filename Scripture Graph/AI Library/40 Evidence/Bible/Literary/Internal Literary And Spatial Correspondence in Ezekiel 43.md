# Internal Literary And Spatial Correspondence in Ezekiel 43

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The east-to-temple movement of divine glory in Ezekiel 43 forms a strong literary counterpart to the earlier departure of glory toward the east. This supports reading the scene as a reversal of abandonment and a portrayal of restored divine presence.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence does not establish when, how, or whether the envisioned temple was historically constructed, nor does it settle whether the vision is primarily architectural, symbolic, or eschatological.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Eastward movement may carry general symbolic or geographic significance without every stage being designed as a strict inversion.
- The correspondence may reflect continuity of Ezekiel's visionary vocabulary rather than a formally constructed reversal.
