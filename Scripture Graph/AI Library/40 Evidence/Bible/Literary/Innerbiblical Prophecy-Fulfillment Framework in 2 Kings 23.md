# Innerbiblical Prophecy-Fulfillment Framework in 2 Kings 23

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Kings 23:16-18 presents Josiah's destruction of the Beth-el altar and his burning of bones upon it as the fulfillment of a specific earlier prophecy delivered by an unnamed 'man of God' from Judah, which tradition (1 Kings 13:2) holds named Josiah by name generations before his birth. Within the book of Kings this functions as a deliberate prophecy-fulfillment structure.

**Does not establish:** The literary fulfillment structure within Kings does not by itself establish the historical date, authorship, or independence of the 1 Kings 13 prophecy from the events it purports to predict, nor does it establish supernatural predictive knowledge as the only explanation for the narrative's coherence.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Most historical-critical scholarship treats the naming of 'Josiah' three centuries in advance in 1 Kings 13 as a vaticinium ex eventu — composed or edited after Josiah's reign specifically to create this fulfillment — rather than as an independently preserved ancient prediction.
- The unusual specificity of naming a not-yet-born king, atypical of other prophetic oracles in Kings, is itself often cited as evidence of retrospective composition.
- The coordination could instead reflect deliberate Deuteronomistic editorial shaping of both passages as a unified literary arc rather than either a genuine long-range prediction or pure coincidence.
