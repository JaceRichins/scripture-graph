# NarrativeRhetorical Function in 2 Samuel 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The narrator states directly that David's visible mourning (rending clothes, sackcloth, following the bier, fasting until sundown) produced a specific political effect: 'all the people took notice of it, and it pleased them... all the people and all Israel understood that day that it was not of the king to slay Abner.' This shows the mourning functioned (at least in narrative effect) to publicly clear David of complicity, whether or not the grief itself was also sincere.

**Does not establish:** Whether David orchestrated the mourning deliberately for political cover or grieved sincerely with a fortunate side effect; the text supports both readings without adjudicating between them.

**Alternative explanations:**
- David's grief may have been entirely genuine, with the political benefit an incidental (not intended) consequence rather than the purpose of the display.
- The narrator's note about public perception could reflect the historian's own apologetic aim to exonerate David, rather than a straightforward report of the crowd's reasoning.
- Ancient mourning rites (sackcloth, fasting, public lament) were standard social obligation for a king toward a slain ally regardless of political calculation, so the display need not be read as strategic.
