# Literary Intertext in Luke 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The raising at Nain closely recalls Elijah's restoration of a widow's son: both accounts concern a bereaved widow, the restoration of her son, and the prophet delivering the living son to his mother. This is substantial literary evidence that Luke presents Jesus in continuity with, and potentially as surpassing, Israel's prophetic tradition.

**Does not establish:** The parallel alone does not establish the miracle's historicity, Jesus' divinity, or that Luke intended a detailed one-to-one typology.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Similar wording may arise naturally in two stories about restoring a widow's son.
- Luke or Luke's source may have shaped the narration with scriptural language without implying that every detail is typological.
- The correspondence can establish a literary echo without proving direct dependence on a particular written form of 1 Kings.
