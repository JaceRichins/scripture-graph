# Sacramental Intertext in John 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Passover setting and repeated commands to eat Jesus's flesh and drink his blood create meaningful resonance with the later Christian meal tradition, especially the Last Supper sayings. John 6 itself, however, does not narrate an ordinance or explicitly identify the bread and cup of that meal.

**Does not establish:** The verbal resonance does not establish that John 6 is exclusively about the sacrament, that ritual participation automatically gives life, or that the passage resolves later disputes about eucharistic presence.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The eating and drinking may primarily symbolize believing in and inwardly appropriating Jesus, as suggested by the parallel language in John 6:35 and John 6:47.
- The language may anticipate Jesus's self-giving death without directly referring to a ritual meal.
- Sacramental associations may be secondary echoes perceived by later Christian readers rather than the discourse's exclusive meaning.
