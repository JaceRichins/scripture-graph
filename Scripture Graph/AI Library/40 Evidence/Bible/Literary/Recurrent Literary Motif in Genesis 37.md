# Recurrent Literary Motif in Genesis 37

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Genesis 27 and Genesis 37 share a distinctive cluster of family-deception motifs: a favored child's garment, a kid of the goats, and a father induced to reach a false conclusion from disguised evidence. The recurrence strongly supports a literary connection in the received narrative, although identifying it as deliberate retributive justice remains interpretive.

**Does not establish:** The motif does not establish a mechanical law of retribution, a single compositional layer, or divine approval of what Joseph's brothers do.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Garments and goats are ordinary features of the shared pastoral family setting.
- A later narrator or editor may have shaped separate traditions to produce the correspondence.
- The parallel can function literarily without implying that Jacob's suffering is divinely imposed punishment.
