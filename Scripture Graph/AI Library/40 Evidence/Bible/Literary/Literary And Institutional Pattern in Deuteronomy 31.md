# Literary And Institutional Pattern in Deuteronomy 31

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The coordinated placement of public succession, written-law preservation, communal recitation, and a mnemonic song supports reading the chapter as a deliberate covenant-transition framework designed to preserve communal identity beyond Moses's death.

**Does not establish:** This internal pattern does not by itself establish Mosaic authorship, the date of composition, the historicity of every narrated event, or supernatural foreknowledge of later apostasy.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The arrangement may reflect later editorial coordination of originally distinct succession, law-reading, and song traditions.
- The elements may primarily serve the immediate narrative transition without constituting a unified institutional program.
