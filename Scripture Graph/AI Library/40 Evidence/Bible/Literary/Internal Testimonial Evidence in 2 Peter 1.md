# Internal Testimonial Evidence in 2 Peter 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
As internal testimonial evidence, the chapter explicitly appeals to first-person eyewitness and auditory experience in support of its proclamation about Jesus Christ.

**Does not establish:** The first-person wording alone does not establish Petrine authorship, the historical occurrence of the event, or the absence of literary development.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A later writer could frame inherited Transfiguration tradition as first-person testimony.
- The passage could be a literary appeal to apostolic authority rather than an independently verifiable memoir.
- Even authentic testimony can reflect memory, interpretation, and theological shaping.
