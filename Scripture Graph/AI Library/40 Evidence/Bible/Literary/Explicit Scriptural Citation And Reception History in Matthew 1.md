# Explicit Scriptural Citation And Reception History in Matthew 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Matthew's citation of Isaiah is strong evidence for the evangelist's christological reading of Israel's scriptures. Whether Isaiah 7:14 originally functioned as a direct prediction exclusively about Jesus remains disputed because Isaiah places the sign within an eighth-century BCE crisis involving Ahaz.

**Does not establish:** The citation alone does not settle Isaiah's original historical referent, the full semantic range of the underlying Hebrew term, or the historicity of the birth account.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Matthew may understand Isaiah typologically, with an earlier sign pattern reaching a fuller realization in Jesus.
- A traditional predictive reading understands Isaiah as directly foretelling the virginal conception.
- A historical-critical reading may regard Matthew as applying an older text to Jesus beyond its primary historical reference.
- Matthew's wording reflects the Greek scriptural tradition, whose rendering influences the association with virginity.
