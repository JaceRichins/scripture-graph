# Intrabiblical Verbal And Thematic Correspondence in Jeremiah 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Jeremiah 11 displays a dense cluster of language shared with Israel's covenant traditions: the curse on one who does not obey, the exodus from the iron furnace, the demand to obey the divine voice, the people-and-God formula, and the promised land flowing with milk and honey. This is strong evidence that the passage consciously speaks within that covenantal scriptural tradition.

**Does not establish:** The correspondences do not by themselves establish a direction of literary dependence, a precise date of composition, or the identity of an editor.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Jeremiah and Deuteronomy may independently draw on shared covenant formulas used in Israelite worship or instruction.
- Some of the wording may reflect later editorial shaping rather than the precise form of Jeremiah's original proclamation.
- Verbal similarity alone does not determine which text, if either, directly depends on the other.
