# Internal Social-Network Evidence in Romans 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The density of names, relationships, households, and commendations is strong internal evidence that Romans presents Paul as connected to an extensive network of coworkers and believers. It is moderately strong evidence for a corresponding historical network, although the chapter alone cannot independently verify every identity or role.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the precise office, authority, social status, or later history of each named person.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some greetings may employ conventional epistolary language rather than provide detailed descriptions of relationships.
- Names and brief labels do not reveal the duration, closeness, or institutional status of every relationship.
- The literary presentation alone cannot independently authenticate every biographical detail.
