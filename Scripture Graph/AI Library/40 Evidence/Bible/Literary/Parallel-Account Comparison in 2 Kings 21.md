# Parallel-Account Comparison in 2 Kings 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The account of Manasseh in 2 Chronicles 33 includes captivity, humility, prayer, restoration, and subsequent reforms, whereas 2 Kings 21 records his offenses, prophetic condemnation, death, and burial without narrating repentance. The contrast is a strong observation about differing narrative selection, but by itself it cannot determine whether either writer denied events omitted from his account.

**Does not establish:** The difference alone does not prove that Manasseh never repented, that Chronicles invented his repentance, or that the accounts are irreconcilable.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Kings may omit repentance because its narrative purpose is to explain Jerusalem's eventual fall.
- Chronicles may preserve additional tradition, shape inherited tradition for its own theology, or both.
- The two accounts may emphasize different periods of the same reign without intending direct contradiction.
