# Literary-Narrative Evidence in Judges 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The fable's fire imagery, the reciprocal curse, the alliance's collapse, the burning of Shechem's refuge, Abimelech's death at another tower, and the narrator's concluding explanation together provide strong internal evidence that the chapter has been shaped to present the downfall as just retribution.

**Does not establish:** The literary design does not independently establish every event's historical details or prove the narrator's theological explanation of causation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some correspondences may arise from the narrator selecting and arranging remembered events rather than from events unfolding according to a prior supernatural prediction.
- The fire and tower correspondences could partly reflect common methods and hazards of ancient siege warfare.
