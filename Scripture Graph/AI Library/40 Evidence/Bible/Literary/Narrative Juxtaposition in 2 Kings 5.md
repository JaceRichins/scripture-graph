# Narrative Juxtaposition in 2 Kings 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The immediate sequence of Elisha refusing Naaman's gift and Gehazi secretly obtaining part of it strongly supports reading Gehazi's conduct as a corruption of the prophetic mission and of the freely given healing.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a universal prohibition on religious workers receiving material support or prove that payment was Gehazi's only offense.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Elisha's refusal may have been specifically diplomatic, intended to prevent a Syrian commander from treating the healing as a purchased service.
- The phrase "Is it a time" may condemn the timing and deception rather than all receipt of material support.
- Gehazi's lies and misuse of Elisha's name may be at least as central to the judgment as his desire for wealth.
