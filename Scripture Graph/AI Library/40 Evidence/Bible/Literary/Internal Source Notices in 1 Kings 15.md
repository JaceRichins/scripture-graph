# Internal Source Notices in 1 Kings 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated references to chronicles of the kings of Judah and Israel support the observation that the narrator presents this work as a selective account drawing attention to fuller royal records. Since the cited chronicles are not extant for comparison, the notices cannot by themselves verify particular events or identify the narrator's sources.

**Does not establish:** The formulas do not establish that the cited records survive, that they were contemporary with every event, or that the present narrative reproduces them without editorial shaping.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The notices may be conventional historiographic formulas rather than precise citations in the modern sense.
- The titles may refer to royal annals, later compilations, or other records whose form cannot now be reconstructed.
