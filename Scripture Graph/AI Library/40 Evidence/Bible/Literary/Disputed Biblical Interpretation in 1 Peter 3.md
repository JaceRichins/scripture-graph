# Disputed Biblical Interpretation in 1 Peter 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The meaning of 'spirits in prison' and Christ's 'preaching' in 1 Peter 3:19-20 is one of the most disputed passages in the New Testament, with major alternative readings competing against the postmortem-evangelization interpretation.

**Does not establish:** The text alone does not establish whether the 'spirits' are human or angelic, whether repentance was offered, or the timing of the proclamation relative to Christ's death and resurrection.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Augustinian/traditional view: the pre-existent Christ preached, through Noah and by the Spirit, to Noah's living contemporaries before the flood; the 'spirits in prison' are simply the disobedient dead now awaiting judgment, with no post-mortem missionary activity implied.
- The 'spirits in prison' are read as the disobedient 'sons of God'/angelic beings of Genesis 6:1-4 (a tradition echoed in 1 Enoch, 2 Peter 2:4, and Jude 6), and Christ's 'preaching' is a proclamation of triumph and judgment over hostile spiritual powers after his resurrection, not an offer of repentance to human dead.
- The passage may function primarily as a proclamation of cosmic victory (kerygma) meant to comfort suffering readers by Christ's vindication, rather than as a doctrinal statement about the mechanics of salvation for the dead.
