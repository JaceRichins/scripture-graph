# Text-CriticalInterpretive Ambiguity in Daniel 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Whether 'one like the Son of man' in Daniel 7:13 denotes a distinct individual heavenly figure or is a symbolic personification of 'the saints of the most High' collectively (paralleling how the four beasts symbolize kingdoms rather than literal animals) is a genuinely disputed question, since the chapter's own interpretation glosses the beasts and horns but never explicitly glosses the son-of-man figure the same way.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish which reading, if either exclusively, the text originally intended; Daniel 7:15-27's own interpretation is silent on this specific point.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Individual-messianic reading: the son of man is a distinct heavenly figure who receives dominion in his own right, the reading adopted by Jesus and other New Testament authors.
- Corporate-symbolic reading: since the kingdom is given to 'the people of the saints of the most High' (Daniel 7:27) in language paralleling the son of man's own investiture (Daniel 7:14), the figure may symbolize the corporate saints rather than one individual.
- Both/and reading: an individual figure who represents or embodies the covenant community, making the two readings not strictly exclusive.
