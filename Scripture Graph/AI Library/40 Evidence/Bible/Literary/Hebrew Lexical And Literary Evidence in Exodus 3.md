# Hebrew Lexical And Literary Evidence in Exodus 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The same Hebrew first-person verb form, ʾehyeh, underlies the promise translated "I will be" in verse 12 and the repeated expression translated "I AM" in verse 14. This strongly supports reading the name disclosure in connection with promised divine presence, while the full meaning and preferred tense of the expression remain disputed.

**Does not establish:** The wordplay does not by itself establish one exhaustive translation, a complete metaphysical doctrine of God, or a specifically later Christological interpretation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The expression may stress continuing divine presence: "I will be what I will be."
- It may function as an intentionally enigmatic answer rather than a complete definition of God's nature.
- The wordplay can support more than one theological nuance without selecting a single exhaustive translation.
