# Narrative Characterization in 2 Chronicles 35

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Chronicles preserves both Josiah's goodness under the law and his fatal refusal to heed God's warning. This is evidence that the chapter's portrait is morally differentiated rather than an unqualified celebration of the king.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the narrative is politically neutral, that every detail is historical, or that one failure cancels the chapter's positive evaluation of Josiah.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The favorable closing evaluation may summarize Josiah's reign while treating his death as an isolated mistake.
- The account may primarily explain an otherwise troubling death rather than seek a broadly balanced portrait.
- The juxtaposition may reflect the combination of separate traditions rather than a deliberate literary strategy.
