# Internal Narrative Evidence in 1 Kings 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter provides strong internal narrative evidence that Solomon's accession is distinguished from Adonijah's by Davidic authorization and public installation. This supports a literary claim about legitimacy within the narrative more strongly than it independently proves every historical detail of the succession.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently prove the complete historical accuracy of the speeches, the unanimity of Israel, or the absence of other succession claims.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The account may shape remembered events to legitimate Solomon and his supporting coalition.
- Adonijah's supporters may have understood succession customs differently from the narrator's presentation.
- Public support reported in the story need not represent every group in Israel.
