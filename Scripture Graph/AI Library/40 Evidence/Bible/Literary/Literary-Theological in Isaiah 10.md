# Literary-Theological in Isaiah 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The explicit contrast between the Lord's use of Assyria and Assyria's self-directed ambition is strong internal evidence that the passage distinguishes providential use of an agent from moral approval of that agent's motives and conduct.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a complete theory of divine causation, resolve the problem of evil, or imply that every victorious empire is divinely approved.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The distinction may function primarily as prophetic rhetoric explaining successive judgments rather than as a comprehensive doctrine of providence.
- Readers may differ over how divine commissioning, human agency, and moral responsibility relate beyond this particular narrative.

**Scripture:** [[Isaiah 10#^isa-10-5|Isaiah 10:5-7]], [[Isaiah 10#^isa-10-12|Isaiah 10:12-15]]
