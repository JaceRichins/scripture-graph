# Literary Structure in Lamentations 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
In Hebrew, Lamentations 3 is a twenty-two-letter alphabetic acrostic in which each successive Hebrew letter governs a group of three lines, accounting for the chapter's sixty-six verses. This strongly supports deliberate formal composition, but the form itself does not identify the author, date, or historical mechanism of composition.

**Does not establish:** The acrostic does not establish Jeremian authorship, a particular date, eyewitness status, supernatural origin, or the historicity of every first-person image.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The acrostic may primarily serve mnemonic, aesthetic, or scribal purposes rather than encode a specific theological message.
- The formal order may impose discipline on grief without implying that the poem presents a tidy resolution.
