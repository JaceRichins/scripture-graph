# Lexical-Literary Pattern in Malachi 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Hebrew noun mal'akh means messenger. Its application to the priest in Malachi 2:7 and its repeated use in Malachi 3:1 form a lexical thread with the designation Malachi, conventionally understood as 'my messenger.' This is good evidence for an intentional messenger motif in the book, but it cannot decide whether Malachi was the prophet's personal name or a title.

**Does not establish:** The pattern does not establish whether Malachi is a personal name, a prophetic title, or the work's editorial designation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Messenger is an ordinary term appropriate to both priests and prophetic agents, so every occurrence need not be deliberate wordplay.
- Malachi may already have functioned as a personal name, making the lexical resemblance meaningful but not titular.
