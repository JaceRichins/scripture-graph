# Literary Context in Jeremiah 29

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The immediate literary evidence strongly favors reading Jeremiah 29:11 first as a promise to the exiled community: its audience is the Babylonian captives, its horizon is the seventy-year period, and its expected outcome is gathering and return.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish an unconditional guarantee of prosperity, safety, or a preferred short-term outcome for every individual believer.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Readers may apply the promise analogically to individual lives, provided that application is distinguished from the verse's primary historical and communal setting.
- The promise may express a broader principle of divine benevolence, but the chapter itself does not state that every individual hardship will end favorably during mortality.
