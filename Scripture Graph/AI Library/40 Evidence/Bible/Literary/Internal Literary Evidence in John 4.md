# Internal Literary Evidence in John 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The boundary-crossing conversation, Jesus's two-day stay with the Samaritans, their widespread belief, and their confession of him as the Saviour of the world provide strong internal literary evidence that this chapter presents his saving significance as extending beyond a narrowly local or exclusively Jewish audience.

**Does not establish:** It does not by itself establish the historical extent of Jesus's ministry, the date or authorship of the Gospel, or that every later form of universal-mission theology is already specified here.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The title Saviour of the world may express theological scope without describing the historical scale of Jesus's activity at this point.
- Samaritans were connected to Israelite traditions, so their inclusion is not identical to a mission among all Gentile nations.
- The episode could be read as a particular Samaritan exception rather than a complete missionary program.
