# Canonical Reception in 2 Samuel 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Canonical reception supports a layered reading of the promise: Solomon is identified as the temple-building son, while Luke applies the enduring throne and kingdom language to Jesus. This provides strong evidence for immediate and later canonical horizons, but it does not by itself prove that the original human speaker consciously foresaw every later application.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the precise extent of the original author's messianic expectations, the date of the oracle, or an exclusively messianic referent for every verse.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Luke may be reusing the oracle typologically rather than claiming that every detail was originally an explicit prediction of Jesus.
- The promise may have referred initially to Solomon and the continuing Davidic dynasty, with later texts extending its language after historical circumstances changed.
- Some readers understand “for ever” primarily as dynastic or covenantal duration rather than as a prediction about one immortal individual.
