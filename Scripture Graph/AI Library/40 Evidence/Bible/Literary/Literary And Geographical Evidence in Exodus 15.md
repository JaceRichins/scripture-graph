# Literary And Geographical Evidence in Exodus 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sequence Palestina, Edom, Moab, and Canaan gives the song a geographical horizon extending beyond the sea crossing toward Israel's anticipated passage and settlement. This is literary evidence for the chapter's forward-looking function, but it is not decisive evidence for when the song was composed.

**Does not establish:** The geographic horizon by itself establishes neither early prophetic composition nor later retrospective composition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passage may be presented as prophetic anticipation before Israel enters the land.
- The geographic sequence may reflect knowledge organized during or after the journey.
- A later writer or editor could have placed Israel's remembered deliverance within a retrospective national geography.

**Scripture:** [[Exodus 15#^ex-15-14|Exodus 15:14-17]]
