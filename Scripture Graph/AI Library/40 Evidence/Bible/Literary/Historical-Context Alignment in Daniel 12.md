# Historical-Context Alignment in Daniel 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the historical-critical reading of Daniel, the removal of the daily sacrifice and installation of the desolating abomination most plausibly refer in their initial historical horizon to Antiochus IV Epiphanes and the Jerusalem temple crisis of the second century BC. The repeated language within Daniel strengthens this identification, although Daniel 12 does not name Antiochus.

**Does not establish:** The historical identification does not by itself disprove typological or later theological applications, nor does it produce an uncontested calculation of the 1,290 and 1,335 days.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Traditional futurist readings assign the passage primarily to a still-future desecration and tribulation.
- A typological reading allows the Antiochene crisis to be the initial referent while also treating it as a pattern for later events.
- Some interpreters relate the language to the Roman destruction of Jerusalem or to more than one historical crisis.
