# Internal Literary Evidence in Numbers 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The report progresses from specific observations about fruit, cities, and regional populations to sweeping and self-comparative language: the land consumes inhabitants, all the people are of great stature, and the scouts are like grasshoppers. This is strong internal evidence of rhetorical escalation, but whether the speakers are deliberately exaggerating, accurately describing their fear, or reporting extraordinary conditions remains disputed.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every reported danger was false, that the scouts knowingly lied, or that the narrative supplies literal measurements for the inhabitants.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The shift may be a normal movement from reconnaissance details to a concluding risk assessment rather than deliberate exaggeration.
- The language may express sincere fear and perceived military disparity.
- Some apparent intensification may reflect idiom or the wording of the English translation.
- The conditions described could be intended literally within the narrative.
