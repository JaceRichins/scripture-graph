# DoctrinalLiturgical Pattern in Acts 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The two-stage sequence in vv.12-17 (baptism, then a separate later laying-on-of-hands for the Holy Ghost) is often read as scriptural precedent for a distinct confirmation ordinance following baptism. This is a genuine textual pattern, but its status as a universal template — rather than a one-time narrative device authenticating the new, ethnically distinct Samaritan mission to the Jerusalem apostles — is disputed.

**Does not establish:** That laying on of hands for the Holy Ghost was required or practiced identically in every apostolic-era conversion; Acts contains counterexamples with different sequencing.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Luke's purpose may be to show Jerusalem apostolic authentication/unification of the Samaritan mission into the one church, not to establish a universal two-stage sacramental sequence.
- Acts itself is not uniform: in Acts 10:44-48 the Gentiles at Cornelius's house receive the Holy Ghost BEFORE water baptism, and in Acts 2:38 the gift of the Holy Ghost is tied directly to repentance and baptism with no separate hand-laying step mentioned.
- Some traditions read the delay as due to the Samaritans' incomplete faith or the unusual social/religious tension between Jews and Samaritans, a circumstance not necessarily generalizable.
