# Cumulative Literary Evidence in Ezekiel 48

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The inclusion of every tribe in territorial portions or gate names, the participation of all tribes in city service, and the sanctuary's central location provide substantial textual evidence that the vision portrays a comprehensively reordered Israel centered on divine presence. These features do not determine whether the geography is intended as a literal survey, an idealized constitutional plan, or symbolic eschatological imagery.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a date or mechanism of fulfillment, a modern geographical map, or the identity of the prince.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The symmetry may primarily organize a visionary description rather than prescribe a realizable political geography.
- The tribal completeness may express covenant identity without forecasting a literal redistribution of territory.
- The sanctuary's centrality may be theological symbolism rather than a cartographic instruction.
