# HistoricalSocial Background in Luke 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The parable's rhetorical force trades on well-attested first-century Jewish-Samaritan mutual hostility (reflected elsewhere in the New Testament, e.g. John 4:9, and rooted in the centuries-long religious/ethnic estrangement between the two groups): Jesus casts the culturally despised outsider, not the religious professionals, as the moral exemplar, subverting the lawyer's assumption that 'neighbour' names a bounded in-group.

**Does not establish:** This background does not establish precisely how severe or uniform Jewish-Samaritan hostility was in every locale in the first century; it establishes general, well-documented mutual estrangement.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The priest and Levite's avoidance is sometimes explained by ritual purity concerns (contact with a possibly-dead body) rather than callousness, which somewhat softens but does not remove the parable's contrast with the Samaritan's action.
