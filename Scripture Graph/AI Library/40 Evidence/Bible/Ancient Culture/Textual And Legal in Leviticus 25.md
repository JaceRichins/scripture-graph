# Textual And Legal in Leviticus 25

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Leviticus 25 provides strong textual evidence for limiting Israelite debt servitude and preventing permanent loss of Israelite patrimony, but it does not abolish slavery universally because it expressly permits permanent, inheritable enslavement of foreigners.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish how consistently these laws were practiced, nor does it justify treating the permitted permanent enslavement as morally equivalent to free labor.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some theological readings treat jubilee as a moral trajectory toward universal liberation, but that extends beyond the chapter's explicit legal distinctions.
- Comparisons with other ancient legal systems may show that some protections were relatively restrictive of abuse, but relative restriction is not abolition.
