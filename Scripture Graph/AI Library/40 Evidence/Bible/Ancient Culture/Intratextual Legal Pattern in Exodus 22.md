# Intratextual Legal Pattern in Exodus 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The repeated differentiation of compensation according to what happened to property is strong textual evidence that these laws are concerned with calibrated responsibility and material redress, although the chapter also contains punitive and capital provisions and therefore cannot be reduced to a purely restorative system.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that Israel consistently enforced these rules, that every penalty was proportionate by modern standards, or that the chapter is exclusively restorative rather than punitive.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The distinctions may reflect inherited case-law traditions assembled together rather than one systematic theory of proportional justice.
- Some restitution levels may reflect the economic value or use of particular property rather than a general restorative principle.
