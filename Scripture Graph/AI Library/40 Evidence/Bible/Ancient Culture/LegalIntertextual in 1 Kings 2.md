# LegalIntertextual in 1 Kings 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Joab's flight to 'the horns of the altar' (v.28) invokes the altar-asylum custom addressed in Exodus 21:12-14, which grants a place of refuge for one who kills unintentionally but explicitly denies it to one who 'come presumptuously upon his neighbour, to slay him with guile' — that offender is to be taken 'from mine altar, that he may die.' Solomon's charge that Joab be killed for the premeditated killings of Abner and Amasa (vv.31-33) matches the Exodus exception closely enough to function as an implicit legal justification for overriding altar sanctuary.

**Does not establish:** That killing Joab at or near the altar was itself a lawful application of the Exodus statute, only that the narrative supplies a recognizable legal category (premeditated murder) that would except him from asylum.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Exodus 21:14 envisions removing the offender from the altar for due process/trial, not summary execution at or immediately after the altar; the narrative may be applying the exception more loosely than the statute contemplates.
- The connection to Exodus 21 may be the narrator's own retrospective legal framing rather than evidence that this specific procedure was actually followed as law in the historical event.
