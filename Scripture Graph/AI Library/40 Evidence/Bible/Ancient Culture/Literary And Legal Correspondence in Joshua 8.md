# Literary And Legal Correspondence in Joshua 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The narrator repeatedly presents Joshua's altar, inscribed law, tribal arrangement, and public reading as fulfillment of commands attributed to Moses. This is strong evidence for the account's literary and theological portrayal of Joshua as obedient to Mosaic instruction.

**Does not establish:** This correspondence does not by itself establish when the ceremony occurred, the account's date of composition, or the historicity of every reported detail.

**Alternative explanations:**
- An author or editor may have shaped an older conquest tradition to conform explicitly to Deuteronomic law.
- The ceremony may have circulated separately before being placed at this point in the narrative.
