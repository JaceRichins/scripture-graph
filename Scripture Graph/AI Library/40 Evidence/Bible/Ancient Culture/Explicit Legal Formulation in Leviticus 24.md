# Explicit Legal Formulation in Leviticus 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Leviticus 24 explicitly applies one legal standard to the stranger and the native. The repeated inclusion of the stranger is strong textual evidence for equal liability under the laws discussed here, including the blasphemy rule.

**Does not establish:** The passage does not establish a modern theory of universal civil rights, erase every native-alien distinction elsewhere in the law, or demonstrate that every stated penalty is ethically adequate for modern societies.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The equality asserted may be limited to the laws and penalties in this unit rather than constituting a comprehensive statement of equal social status.
- Equal liability to punishment does not by itself demonstrate equal access to every privilege within ancient Israel.
