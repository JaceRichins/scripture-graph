# Literary Structure And Covenantal Contrast in Amos 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The expansion from relatively short Moab and Judah oracles to a detailed Israel oracle supports reading Israel as the chapter's principal rhetorical target. Israel's remembered deliverance and prophetic gifts give its indictment an added dimension of violated obligation, although oracle length alone cannot measure comparative guilt.

**Does not establish:** The structure does not establish that Israel's sins were objectively worse than every offense committed by Moab or Judah, nor does it determine the chapter's compositional history.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The Israel oracle may be longer chiefly because Amos addresses an Israelite audience, without implying a formal ranking of guilt.
- The differing lengths may reflect editorial or compositional considerations not recoverable from the chapter alone.
