# Legal And Rhetorical Evidence in Deuteronomy 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The command to burn the city's spoil and retain none of the cursed thing frames the prescribed destruction as an act from which the participants must not profit materially.

**Does not establish:** This framing does not establish that the command was historically carried out, that every inhabitant was individually culpable, or that the prescribed violence is ethically unproblematic.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A prohibition on taking spoil does not prove that historical participants, if any, lacked mixed or material motives.
- The provision can also be understood as part of a broader sacred-ban tradition rather than chiefly as an anti-profiteering safeguard.

**Scripture:** [[Deuteronomy 13#^deut-13-15|Deuteronomy 13:15-17]]
