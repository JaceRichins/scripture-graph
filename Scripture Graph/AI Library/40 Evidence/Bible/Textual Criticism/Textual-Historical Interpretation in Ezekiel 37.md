# Textual-Historical Interpretation in Ezekiel 37

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The vision's own stated referent (v.11-14) is national restoration — God opening graves and bringing the exiled house of Israel back into 'the land of Israel' — using resurrection imagery as a metaphor for return from captivity, rather than the passage being framed as direct teaching about individual post-mortem bodily resurrection.

**Does not establish:** That Ezekiel himself intended to teach or reveal the doctrine of individual bodily resurrection; the text's own framing (v.11-14) presents the vision as about national restoration from captivity, with resurrection imagery serving that message.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The vision may reflect exilic-era ideas about corporate national revival that do not presuppose or teach individual post-mortem bodily resurrection as later developed.
- Later Second Temple, rabbinic, Christian, and LDS tradition increasingly read the passage as literal support for individual bodily resurrection — a reading that developed after and beyond Ezekiel's stated exilic context.
