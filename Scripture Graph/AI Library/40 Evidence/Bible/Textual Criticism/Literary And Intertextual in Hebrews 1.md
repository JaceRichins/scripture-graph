# Literary And Intertextual in Hebrews 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sustained sequence of scriptural citations strongly supports reading Hebrews 1:5-13 as a cumulative textual argument for the Son's superiority to angels. It does not by itself settle how each cited passage was understood in its earliest historical setting.

**Does not establish:** The citation sequence does not prove that every source passage was exclusively or transparently messianic in its original historical context, nor does it establish the authorship of Hebrews.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Individual passages may have referred initially to an Israelite king, to God, or to another historical setting before being applied christologically in Hebrews.
- Some details of the argument depend on the wording and interpretive conventions of the scriptural textual tradition used by the author.
