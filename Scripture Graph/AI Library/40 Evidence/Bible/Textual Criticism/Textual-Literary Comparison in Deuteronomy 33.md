# Textual-Literary Comparison in Deuteronomy 33

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The framing and sequence of differentiated tribal sayings in Deuteronomy 33 and Genesis 49 provide strong textual evidence that the chapters participate in the same broad literary type of tribal blessing or testament. This comparison is substantially stronger as a classification of form than as evidence for direct literary dependence.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish direct borrowing, common authorship, a particular compositional date, Mosaic authorship, or the historical fulfillment of every saying.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The chapters could independently employ a conventional Israelite blessing form without either borrowing directly from the other.
- One or both collections could have been assembled or arranged from separate tribal traditions by later editors.
- Similar tribal organization may arise from their shared subject matter rather than a close compositional relationship.
