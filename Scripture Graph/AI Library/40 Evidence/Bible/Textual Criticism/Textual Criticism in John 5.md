# Textual Criticism in John 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The description of an angel periodically troubling the pool's water (John 5:4, and the last clause of v3) is absent from the earliest extant Greek witnesses (including Papyrus 66, Papyrus 75, Codex Sinaiticus, and Codex Vaticanus) and is bracketed or footnoted as a probable later scribal addition in modern critical editions, even though it stands in the KJV text translated from later manuscripts.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that the tradition of intermittently stirred healing water is false, only that the specific wording of vv3b-4 is probably not what John originally wrote; the man's own reference to the water 'being troubled' in v7 is textually secure regardless.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Absence from surviving early manuscripts is not absolute proof of non-originality; an early exemplar containing the verse may simply not have survived.
- Some regard vv3b-4 as reflecting a genuine, older local Jerusalem tradition that a scribe made explicit rather than inventing from nothing.
