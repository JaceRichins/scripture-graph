# Textual-Critical in Mark 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Jesus dates David's eating of the shewbread to 'the days of Abiathar the high priest' (2:26), but the underlying Old Testament episode (David fleeing to the priest at Nob) narrates Ahimelech, Abiathar's father, as the officiating priest; Abiathar becomes high priest only afterward. This discrepancy between Mark's wording and the earlier narrative is a well-known text-critical crux.

**Does not establish:** This does not by itself establish an error in the historical account of David; the ambiguity concerns how precisely Mark's Greek wording maps onto the earlier narrative's chronology.

**Alternative explanations:**
- 'In the days of Abiathar' may function as a loose era-marker (Abiathar being the more famous priest of that household in later memory, even if not yet in office) rather than a claim about who performed that specific act.
- Some manuscripts and translations render the phrase differently, and the ambiguity may reflect transmission history of the Gospel text rather than an error about the underlying event.
- Ahimelech and Abiathar may have shared duties or the phrase may loosely denote the priestly household rather than a single office-holder.
