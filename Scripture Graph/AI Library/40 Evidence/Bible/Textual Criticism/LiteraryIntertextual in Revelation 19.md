# LiteraryIntertextual in Revelation 19

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The angel's summons for birds to "eat the flesh of kings ... and of all men" (vv.17-18) closely parallels an Old Testament oracle in which birds and beasts are invited to a sacrificial feast on the flesh of a defeated army's leaders and horses, suggesting the author drew on that earlier prophetic image to depict final judgment.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish that the author intended a one-to-one allegorical mapping onto Ezekiel's Gog oracle, only that the imagery of a judgment-banquet for scavenger birds was available and plausibly drawn upon.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The shared imagery of carrion birds feasting after battle is a common ancient Near Eastern and Greco-Roman war motif rather than evidence of direct literary dependence on Ezekiel specifically.
- The parallel could reflect general familiarity with apocalyptic convention rather than deliberate quotation.
