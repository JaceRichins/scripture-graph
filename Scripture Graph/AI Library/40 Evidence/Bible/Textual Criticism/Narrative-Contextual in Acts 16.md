# Narrative-Contextual in Acts 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The answer "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ" is followed by proclamation of the word to the jailer and his household, the jailer's care for the wounded prisoners, baptism, hospitality, and rejoicing. The immediate literary context therefore presents belief within a larger conversion response rather than documenting bare verbal assent alone.

**Does not establish:** This episode alone does not settle broader disputes about justification, baptismal efficacy, perseverance, or the salvation of one person through another's faith.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The subsequent actions may be consequences or signs of faith rather than conditions of salvation.
- Because this is a compressed narrative, it may not intend to specify a universal sequence of conversion.
- Different theological traditions distinguish differently between faith, baptism, evidence of repentance, and saving grace.
