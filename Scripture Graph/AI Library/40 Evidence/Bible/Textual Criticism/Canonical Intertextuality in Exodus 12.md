# Canonical Intertextuality in Exodus 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The New Testament explicitly applies Passover imagery to Jesus: Paul calls Christ the sacrificed Passover, and John's crucifixion account associates the preservation of Jesus's bones with scriptural fulfillment. Together with Exodus's unblemished victim and prohibition against breaking its bones, this provides strong textual evidence for a Christian typological reading of Passover.

**Does not establish:** This intertextual reuse does not by itself establish that every Passover detail was originally composed as a direct prediction of the crucifixion, nor does it independently establish the historical details of the exodus.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The New Testament authors may be creatively rereading Israel's scripture rather than identifying the sole original meaning of Exodus 12.
- John 19:36 may also evoke other biblical texts about protected bones, so Exodus 12:46 need not be its exclusive antecedent.
- Some Passover details may be typologically significant to Christians without every ritual feature functioning as a prediction of Jesus.
