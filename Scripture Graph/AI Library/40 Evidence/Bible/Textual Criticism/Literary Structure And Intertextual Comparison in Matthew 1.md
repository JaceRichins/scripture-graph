# Literary Structure And Intertextual Comparison in Matthew 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The omissions and explicit fourteen-generation arrangement strongly support the conclusion that Matthew has shaped inherited genealogical material for literary and theological purposes; the passage should not be treated as though it claimed to list every biological generation.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the historical identity of every person after the exile, nor does it demonstrate that the genealogy is a complete biological pedigree.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Ancient genealogical convention permitted the omission of intermediate ancestors, so compression need not imply carelessness or deception.
- Some differences may reflect sources or genealogical traditions no longer available rather than Matthew's independent editorial choices.
