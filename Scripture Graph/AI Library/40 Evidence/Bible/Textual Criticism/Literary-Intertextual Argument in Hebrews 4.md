# Literary-Intertextual Argument in Hebrews 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the author's argument, David's later proclamation of "To day" is strong textual evidence that the promised rest was not exhausted by Israel's entrance into Canaan: a further opportunity remained when the psalm was voiced and remains relevant to the audience.

**Does not establish:** This reasoning does not by itself determine whether the rest means heaven, final salvation, present communion with God, Sabbath observance, or one reality with several dimensions.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The rest may be understood primarily as present spiritual participation, primarily as future salvation, or as an already-present reality awaiting completion.
- The author's interpretation of the psalm is a theological rereading and need not imply that the psalm's original historical audience understood every later application.
