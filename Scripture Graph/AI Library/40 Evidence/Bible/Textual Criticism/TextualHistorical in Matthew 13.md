# TextualHistorical in Matthew 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Matthew 13:55-56 records the Nazareth crowd naming Jesus's brothers as James, Joses, Simon, and Judas, and referring to unnamed sisters. This is textual evidence bearing on later Christian debates over the nature of these relationships (full/half siblings, cousins, or Joseph's children by a prior marriage) and, by extension, on doctrines of Mary's perpetual virginity — a question the passage itself does not resolve.

**Does not establish:** The verse does not by itself establish whether Mary had other biological children or remained perpetually virgin; that conclusion depends on theological tradition, not on this text alone.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Full or half biological siblings of Jesus through Mary and/or Joseph (majority Protestant reading).
- Cousins or other close relatives, given looser ancient Semitic/Greek usage of 'brother' (traditional Catholic 'Hieronymian' view).
- Children of Joseph from a previous marriage, making them Jesus's step-siblings (traditional Eastern Orthodox 'Epiphanian' view).
