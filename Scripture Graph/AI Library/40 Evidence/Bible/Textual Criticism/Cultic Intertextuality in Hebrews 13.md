# Cultic Intertextuality in Hebrews 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sequence of blood brought into the sanctuary, bodies burned outside the camp, and Jesus suffering outside the gate is strong textual evidence that Hebrews interprets Jesus' death through Israelite sin-offering imagery, particularly the disposal rite described in Leviticus 16:27.

**Does not establish:** This correspondence does not by itself settle every question about Hebrews' doctrine of atonement or identify the disputed altar in Hebrews 13:10.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language may evoke the broader group of sin-offering regulations involving disposal outside the camp rather than only the annual Day of Atonement.
- The imagery is typological and theological; it need not imply that every detail of Jesus' death corresponds one-to-one with a single Levitical rite.
