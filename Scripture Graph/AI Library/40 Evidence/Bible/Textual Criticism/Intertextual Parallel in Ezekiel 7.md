# Intertextual Parallel in Ezekiel 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ezekiel 7:19's statement that silver and gold 'shall not be able to deliver them in the day of the wrath of the LORD' closely tracks Zephaniah 1:18's declaration that silver and gold cannot deliver 'in the day of the LORD's wrath.' The verified text-overlap index flags a one-verse match between the chapters, and both oracles belong to the wider 'Day of the LORD' judgment tradition.

**Does not establish:** This parallel does not establish the direction of literary dependence, that Ezekiel had access to a written text of Zephaniah, or that either prophet is quoting the other rather than a common tradition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Both prophets may be drawing independently on a conventional 'Day of the LORD' judgment idiom current in Israelite/Judean prophecy rather than one text borrowing from the other.
- The motif that wealth cannot buy deliverance from divine judgment is a broader wisdom commonplace (cf. Proverbs 11:4) that either author could have used without knowledge of the other's text.
- The overlap could reflect a shared oral or scribal stock phrase rather than direct literary dependence.
