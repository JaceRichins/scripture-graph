# Internal And Canonical Textual Evidence in Genesis 32

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The combined designations of man, God, and angel strongly support reading the wrestling episode as a heavenly or divine encounter, but they do not settle the figure's precise identity or the manner in which God was represented.

**Does not establish:** The wording does not by itself establish which divine or angelic personage appeared, whether the event was corporeal or visionary, or how the different designations should be reconciled.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The figure may be an angel who represents God and therefore permits Jacob to describe the encounter as seeing God.
- The story may employ deliberately fluid divine and human terminology without intending a precise ontological classification.
- The references to God may describe the encounter's significance rather than identify the opponent directly as God.
