# Internal Textual Scope in Genesis 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Genesis 7 uses strongly comprehensive language to present the flood's scope within the narrative: all high hills under the whole heaven are covered, all dry-land creatures with the breath of life die, and Noah's company alone survives. This is strong evidence for the text's portrayal of a universal catastrophe, but the wording alone cannot determine the event's geological extent.

**Does not establish:** The language does not by itself establish a recent global geological flood, its date, its mechanism, or the historical exhaustiveness of the narrative's species inventory.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The comprehensive language may be phenomenological, describing the world within the narrator's geographic horizon.
- Ancient catastrophe narratives can employ conventional or rhetorical totality without intending a modern geological claim.
- The language may describe a universal event within the narrative world while leaving the historical mechanism and physical extent unspecified.
