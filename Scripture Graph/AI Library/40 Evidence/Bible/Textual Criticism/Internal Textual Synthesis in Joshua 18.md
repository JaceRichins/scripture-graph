# Internal Textual Synthesis in Joshua 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The coexistence of a subdued land, tribes still needing to possess their inheritances, and later notices of enduring Jebusite residence strongly supports reading Joshua's conquest language as allowing phased, uneven, or incomplete local control rather than requiring total simultaneous occupation of every listed city.

**Does not establish:** This textual pattern does not establish the historical scale, date, or archaeological character of Israel's settlement.

**Alternative explanations:**
- "Subdued" may refer to regional military dominance while "possess" refers to settlement or administration.
- The differing statements may reflect separate narrative stages rather than a deliberate qualification of conquest language.
- Some scholars explain tensions among the territorial notices through source or editorial layering.
