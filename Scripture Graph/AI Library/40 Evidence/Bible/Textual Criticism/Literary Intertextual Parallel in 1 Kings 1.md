# Literary Intertextual Parallel in 1 Kings 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The unusually close correspondence between Adonijah's display and Absalom's earlier display is strong evidence that the Kings narrative invites comparison between the two rebellious sons, though the parallel alone cannot determine the author's date or full intention.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every detail of the two episodes is symbolic, nor does it independently establish the narrative's date or historicity.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Royal aspirants may have used a conventional display, so the similarity could partly reflect shared political practice.
- The resemblance may arise from a later narrator's standardized description rather than a deliberate characterization of Adonijah as another Absalom.

**Scripture:** [[1 Kings 1#^1kgs-1-5|1 Kings 1:5]], [[1 Kings 1#^1kgs-1-6|1 Kings 1:6]], [[2 Samuel 15#^2sam-15-1|2 Samuel 15:1]]
