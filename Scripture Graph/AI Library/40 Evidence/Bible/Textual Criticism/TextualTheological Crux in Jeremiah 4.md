# TextualTheological Crux in Jeremiah 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Jeremiah 4:10 is a genuine theological/textual crux: Jeremiah appears to accuse God directly of deceiving the people, and this is disputed and unresolved rather than a settled interpretive point.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish a settled resolution to the tension between this verse and passages elsewhere affirming God's truthfulness; the verse's difficulty remains a live crux rather than a solved problem.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The exclamation may be hyperbolic personal lament rather than a formal theological accusation against God.
- The 'deception' may target false prophets who proclaimed 'ye shall have peace,' with God only implicated indirectly for permitting it, since the chapter never names who made that promise.
- Some commentators read this as God allowing a people already inclined toward false security to be confirmed in their own self-deception, rather than God actively deceiving them.
