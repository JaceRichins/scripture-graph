# Textual Criticism in Romans 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The closing doxology has a well-documented history of manuscript placement variation: in different ancient witnesses it appears after Romans 14, after Romans 15, at the end of Romans 16 as here, in more than one of these locations, or is missing altogether, making its original position (and in some scholars' view, its authorship) a genuine text-critical question distinct from the doctrinal content of the passage itself.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that the doxology is non-Pauline; the manuscript instability is compatible with either authentic-but-relocated or later-addition explanations.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The variation may reflect later liturgical rearrangement of an authentically Pauline doxology rather than non-Pauline composition
- Some scholars argue the doxology's vocabulary and style differ enough from the rest of Romans to suggest a post-Pauline liturgical addition
