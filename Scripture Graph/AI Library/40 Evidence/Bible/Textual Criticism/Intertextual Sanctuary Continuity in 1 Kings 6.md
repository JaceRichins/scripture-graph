# Intertextual Sanctuary Continuity in 1 Kings 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The most holy place, ark, cherubim, sanctuary boundary, and divine-dwelling promise provide strong literary evidence that the narrator presents Solomon's temple as continuing major functions and symbols of the Mosaic tabernacle.

**Does not establish:** These correspondences do not establish that the two structures were architecturally identical, nor do they independently establish the historical dates or manufacture of either sanctuary.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The parallels could partly result from later literary shaping that aligned temple and tabernacle traditions.
- Some similarities may reflect general ancient Israelite sanctuary conventions rather than direct architectural dependence on the Exodus design.
