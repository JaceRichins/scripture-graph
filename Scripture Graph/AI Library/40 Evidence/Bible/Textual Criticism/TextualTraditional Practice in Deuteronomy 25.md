# TextualTraditional Practice in Deuteronomy 25

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Paul's statement that he received 'forty stripes save one' (2 Corinthians 11:24) reflects a first-century Jewish practice of stopping just short of the Deuteronomy 25:3 cap of forty stripes, though the Old Testament text itself does not specify thirty-nine.

**Does not establish:** That 'thirty-nine' was already the fixed practice when Deuteronomy was composed; the text itself only forbids exceeding forty.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Paul's 'forty stripes save one' may reflect standard first-century synagogue disciplinary practice rather than a direct exegesis of Deuteronomy 25:3's wording.
- The specific 39-lash convention is attested mainly in later rabbinic sources, which postdate Paul, so the direct causal chain from Deuteronomy 25 to that number cannot be verified from the Deuteronomy text alone.
