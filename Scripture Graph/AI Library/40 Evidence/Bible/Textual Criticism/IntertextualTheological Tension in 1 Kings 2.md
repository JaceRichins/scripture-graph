# IntertextualTheological Tension in 1 Kings 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
David's statement of the dynastic promise as conditional — 'If thy children take heed to their way, to walk before me in truth with all their heart and with all their soul, there shall not fail thee a man on the throne of Israel' (v.4) — stands in tension with the promise to David reported in 2 Samuel 7:12-16, where the throne of David's house is said to be established 'for ever' and unfaithfulness in his offspring is met with chastening ('the rod of men') rather than forfeiture of the dynasty itself. Read together, the two texts show the Davidic covenant is not narrated with a single, uniform logic of unconditional grant versus conditional expectation.

**Does not establish:** That the Deuteronomistic Historian holds a single settled doctrine of covenant conditionality; it shows only that both conditional and unconditional language are applied to the same promise within the corpus.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The two statements can be harmonized as operating on different levels: the dynasty (house of David) is unconditionally secured while any given king's personal continuance on the throne is conditioned on obedience — a reading some scholars find supported by Psalm 89:30-37.
- The conditional wording in 1 Kings 2:4 may simply be David's own paraphrase/application of the promise rather than a fresh, independently authoritative restatement of its terms.
