# Internal Textual Evidence in Galatians 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Verses 12–13 provide strong internal evidence that pressure to accept circumcision formed part of the conflict addressed by the letter. They also report the writer's accusation that the advocates sought to avoid persecution and glory in the converts' flesh, but those alleged motives are polemical claims rather than independently verified facts.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently establish the opponents' actual motives, their precise identity, or the complete historical circumstances of the Galatian dispute.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passage may target a particular group of circumcision advocates rather than every Jewish Christian or every person who practiced circumcision.
- The stated desire to avoid persecution may be a rhetorically sharpened characterization of opponents whose motives were more complex.
