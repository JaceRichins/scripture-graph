# Intertextual Dependence in Deuteronomy 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close verbal correspondence between Matthew 4:4 and Deuteronomy 8:3 is strong evidence that Matthew's temptation narrative deliberately invokes Deuteronomy's wilderness theology, portraying Jesus's response to hunger through Israel's scriptural lesson.

**Does not establish:** This intertext does not establish the historical details, date, or authorship of Deuteronomy's wilderness account.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Matthew may reproduce a familiar scriptural or liturgical form mediated through a Greek textual tradition rather than translate directly from the Hebrew form of Deuteronomy.
- The verbal form alone does not determine every aspect of Matthew's intended Israel-Jesus typology.

**Scripture:** [[Deuteronomy 8#^deut-8-3|Deuteronomy 8:3]], [[Matthew 4#^matt-4-4|Matthew 4:4]]
