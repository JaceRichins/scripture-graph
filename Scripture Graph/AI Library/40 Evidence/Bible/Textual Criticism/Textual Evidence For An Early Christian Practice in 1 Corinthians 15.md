# Textual Evidence For An Early Christian Practice in 1 Corinthians 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Verse 29 is strong textual evidence that Paul expected his audience to recognize a practice described as baptism for the dead. It is weak evidence for reconstructing the practice's precise form, practitioners, theology, or Paul's degree of approval.

**Does not establish:** The verse alone does not establish the rite's procedure, saving effect, apostolic origin, universality, or exact relationship to later practices.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The phrase may describe a proxy ordinance performed for deceased persons.
- It may refer to baptism motivated by, associated with, or performed because of the dead rather than substitution for them.
- Paul may be citing a practice followed by only some Corinthians without endorsing every belief attached to it.
- The expression may have had a local meaning no longer recoverable from the surviving text.

**Scripture:** [[1 Corinthians 15#^1cor-15-29|1 Corinthians 15:29]]
