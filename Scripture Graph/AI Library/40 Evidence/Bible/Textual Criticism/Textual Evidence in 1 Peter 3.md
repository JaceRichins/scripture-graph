# Textual Evidence in 1 Peter 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Verses 18–20 explicitly connect Christ, the Spirit, preaching, imprisoned spirits, and disobedience in Noah's day. This is strong textual evidence that the author envisages a proclamation involving spirits associated with Noah's generation, but the compressed syntax leaves the timing, audience, and nature of the proclamation disputed.

**Does not establish:** By itself, the passage does not establish proxy ordinances, universal postmortem evangelization, the hearers' opportunity to repent, or a complete doctrine of the spirit world.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Christ may have preached through Noah by the Spirit to people who were alive then but are now spirits in prison.
- The spirits may be rebellious supernatural beings rather than deceased human beings.
- The preaching may be a proclamation of victory or judgment rather than an offer of repentance.
- The passage may describe a postmortem proclamation without specifying its full scope or consequences.
