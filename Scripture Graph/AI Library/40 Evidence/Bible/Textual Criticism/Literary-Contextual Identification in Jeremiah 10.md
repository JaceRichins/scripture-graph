# Literary-Contextual Identification in Jeremiah 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The immediate literary context strongly identifies the decorated wood of verses 3–5 as material used for an idol, not as a description directed at a modern Christmas tree. The passage proceeds to call such objects the work of craftsmen, graven or molten images, falsehood, and breathless things.

**Does not establish:** This contextual reading does not by itself settle the religious legitimacy of Christmas trees or every later custom involving decorated trees.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A reader may associate cutting and decorating a tree with later seasonal customs, but that resemblance does not account for the passage's repeated image-making vocabulary.
- The description may be stylized satire rather than a technical account of one precise idol-manufacturing method.
