# Contextual Interpretation in Ezekiel 37

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The movement from exposed bones to living, standing persons and the language of opened graves provide substantial resurrection imagery. Within this chapter, however, those images are explicitly used to promise Israel renewed life and restoration to its land, so they do not by themselves establish that the passage is a direct prediction of universal bodily resurrection.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that bodily resurrection is absent from the passage, nor that every detail refers only to political events.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The grave language may intentionally carry both national-restoration and bodily-resurrection meanings.
- Later readers may legitimately apply the vision typologically to resurrection even if national restoration is its immediate referent.
