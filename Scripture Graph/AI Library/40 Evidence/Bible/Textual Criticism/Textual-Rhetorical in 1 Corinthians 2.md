# Textual-Rhetorical in 1 Corinthians 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The linked contrasts in verses 1-5 provide strong textual evidence that Paul portrays his manner of preaching as a deliberate attempt to direct confidence away from the preacher's rhetorical prestige and toward God. This is evidence for Paul's stated communicative purpose, not proof that his preaching contained no rhetorical skill.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that eloquence, education, reasoning, or careful argument is inherently opposed to faith.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Paul's self-description may employ deliberate rhetorical contrast and need not be a literal denial that he used any trained persuasive technique.
- The phrase 'demonstration of the Spirit and of power' could refer to miracles, spiritual conviction, transformed hearers, or the perceived efficacy of the proclamation; the chapter does not specify which.
