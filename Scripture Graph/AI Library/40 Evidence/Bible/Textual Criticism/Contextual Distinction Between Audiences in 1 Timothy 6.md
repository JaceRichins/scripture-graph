# Contextual Distinction Between Audiences in 1 Timothy 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The contrast between verses 9–10 and 17–19 strongly supports reading desire, trust, pride, and use—not possession alone—as the chapter's explicit ethical concerns regarding wealth.

**Does not establish:** The distinction does not define an acceptable level of wealth, excuse economic injustice, or show that greed is the only possible moral problem associated with riches.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The warnings may be read as implying that wealth itself remains exceptionally dangerous, with verses 17–19 functioning as a pastoral concession rather than approval.
- The instructions assume that rich believers possess resources long enough to distribute them, but they do not endorse continued accumulation.
