# Textual Parallelism in Luke 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Close parallels between multiple Luke 11 units and material in Matthew 6, Matthew 7, Matthew 12, and Matthew 23 strongly support a shared literary or traditional relationship. The parallels alone do not identify the direction of dependence or settle a particular Synoptic-source theory.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not by themselves establish direct copying, which Gospel is earlier, a single common source, or the historical setting in which each saying originated.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Luke and Matthew could independently preserve common oral traditions.
- Both authors could have used one or more shared written sources.
- One Gospel could depend directly or indirectly on the other.
- Some wording could reflect later transmission or harmonization.
