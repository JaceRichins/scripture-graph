# Canonical Intertextual Reception in Zechariah 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
John 19:37 uses Zechariah's looking-and-piercing language in the account of Jesus's crucifixion, changing the wording represented in the KJV from looking upon "me" to looking on "him." Revelation 1:7 also combines the pierced figure with widespread mourning. These passages provide strong evidence for an early Christian christological reception of Zechariah 12:10.

**Does not establish:** This later canonical reception does not by itself prove that Jesus's crucifixion exhausts Zechariah's meaning, that the original audience understood the passage christologically, or that every detail belongs to one historical event.

**Alternative explanations:**
- John's wording may reflect a textual form, translation, or interpretive adaptation different from the KJV rendering of Zechariah.
- Revelation 1:7 combines imagery from more than one prophetic passage rather than merely quoting Zechariah.
