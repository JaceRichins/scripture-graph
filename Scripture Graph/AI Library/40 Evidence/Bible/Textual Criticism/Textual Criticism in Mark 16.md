# Textual Criticism in Mark 16

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Mainstream New Testament textual scholarship holds that Mark 16:9-20 (the 'Longer Ending') is absent from the two oldest and most reliable complete Greek manuscripts of Mark (Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus, 4th century), is missing or marked with asterisks/obeli in a number of other early witnesses, and differs from Mark 1:1-16:8 in vocabulary and style; most modern critical Greek editions (e.g., Nestle-Aland/UBS) print it with a note flagging it as a probable later addition rather than part of the earliest recoverable text.

**Does not establish:** This textual-critical judgment does not by itself determine Mark's authorial intent, nor does it settle the doctrinal status of vv.9-20 within LDS or other Christian canons, where the passage is received as scripture regardless of its manuscript history.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A minority of scholars (and some in the 'majority text'/Byzantine-priority camp) argue the longer ending is original or very early, noting it is attested in the vast majority of later Greek manuscripts and by some 2nd-century patristic writers.
- Some propose the original ending of Mark was lost (e.g., damaged final leaf) rather than that Mark intentionally ended at v.8, which would make vv.9-20 a later but well-intentioned attempt to supply a fitting close.
