# Close TextualLogical Reading in Deuteronomy 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The nonfulfillment test in Deuteronomy 18:22 functions logically as a way to disqualify a prophecy that fails, not as a sufficient proof that a fulfilled prophecy is authentically divine; the verse states only what unfulfilled speech shows, and does not claim that fulfillment alone certifies a prophet.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish a complete or self-sufficient test for true prophecy; the chapter itself supplies no positive fulfillment-based proof, and other passages (e.g. Deuteronomy 13) show fulfillment can accompany false, idolatrous speech.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some readers take v.22 as offering a general (if imperfect) practical criterion for the community to apply either direction, not merely a one-way disqualifier.
- The verse may simply be describing typical retrospective recognition of false prophecy rather than proposing a formal verification procedure at all.
