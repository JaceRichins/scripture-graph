# Textual Distinction in Leviticus 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Because the chapter classifies ordinary bodily processes such as semen emission, marital intercourse, and menstruation as unclean, its terminology provides strong evidence that ritual uncleanness here is not simply a synonym for personal moral guilt.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every act mentioned is morally neutral in every circumstance, nor that Israel's purity laws have no ethical or theological meaning.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The presence of sin offerings and atonement language shows that ritual impurity still has theological significance, even when no deliberate wrongdoing is stated.
- Elsewhere in the law, ritual status and moral obligation can intersect, so the distinction should not be converted into an absolute separation in every case.

**Scripture:** [[Leviticus 15#^lev-15-16|Leviticus 15:16-24]]
