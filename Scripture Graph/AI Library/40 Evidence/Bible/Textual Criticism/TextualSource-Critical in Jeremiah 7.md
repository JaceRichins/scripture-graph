# TextualSource-Critical in Jeremiah 7

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Verse 22's claim that God did not command burnt offerings or sacrifices 'in the day' the fathers came out of Egypt stands in apparent tension with sacrificial legislation elsewhere attributed to the Sinai/wilderness period (e.g., Leviticus 1; Exodus 12's Passover instruction given at the very time of the Exodus); this tension has long been used in biblical scholarship as evidence for a layered or developing history of Israel's cultic law, while more harmonizing readings treat the statement as a rhetorical relative negation emphasizing obedience over ritual rather than a historical denial that any sacrificial command existed.

**Does not establish:** This does not establish which specific redactional history of the Pentateuch is correct, nor does it resolve whether Jeremiah is denying, relativizing, or simply reordering the priority of sacrifice versus obedience.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Hebrew idiom sometimes uses absolute negation to express relative priority ('not X but Y' meaning 'Y matters far more than X'), so verse 22 may be rhetorical emphasis rather than a historical claim.
- Source-critical scholars who see Priestly sacrificial law as a later development read verse 22 as reflecting genuine awareness that detailed sacrificial legislation postdates the earliest Exodus-era covenant demand of obedience.
- Some read 'in the day that I brought them out' narrowly, referring only to the moment of the Exodus itself (before Sinai), which would not deny later Sinai-given sacrificial law.
