# TextualSource-Critical in Matthew 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The near-verbatim overlap between Matthew 5 and 3 Nephi 12 is a significant data point in debates over the composition and translation of the Book of Mormon, since it shows large blocks of King James Version wording embedded in a text purporting to render an independent ancient discourse delivered on another continent.

**Does not establish:** The overlap itself only establishes textual dependency between the English texts; it does not by itself establish either the historicity of the Nephite sermon or the specific mechanics (word-for-word vs. conceptual) of the Book of Mormon translation process.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Believing view: the resurrected Christ delivered a substantively similar discourse to Nephites, and Joseph Smith rendered it in the familiar biblical idiom he already knew, consistent with a revelatory (rather than mechanically literal) translation process.
- Critical view: the overlap reflects direct literary dependence on the printed King James Bible during dictation, indicating the Nephite sermon as narrated is a nineteenth-century composition rather than an independent ancient record.
- Mixed view: an authentic underlying event or teaching tradition existed, but its specific wording in the English text is substantially shaped by (not merely translated through) the KJV.
