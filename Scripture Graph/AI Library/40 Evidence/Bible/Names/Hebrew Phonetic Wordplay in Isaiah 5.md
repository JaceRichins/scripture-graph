# Hebrew Phonetic Wordplay in Isaiah 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Hebrew conclusion of Isaiah 5:7 uses two conspicuous sound-pair contrasts: mishpat/mispach and tsedaqah/tseaqah. The KJV represents the semantic contrasts as judgment/oppression and righteousness/a cry; many modern translations render mispach as bloodshed. The phonetic resemblance sharpens the reversal between what the Lord expected and what he found.

**Does not establish:** The wordplay does not by itself establish the passage's date, compositional unity, authorship, or any modern application.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The sound pattern may chiefly make the accusation memorable rather than encode an additional theological doctrine.
- English renderings such as oppression and bloodshed reflect differing judgments about the precise sense of mispach.
