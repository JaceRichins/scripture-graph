# Chiasmus in Alma 36

> [!evidence] The observation
> Alma's account of his conversion in [[Alma 36]] can be outlined as an
> extended chiasm (inverted parallel structure, A-B-C…X…C'-B'-A'), with the
> turning point at the cry to Jesus Christ in [[Alma 36#^alma-36-18|v. 18]]:
> captivity of fathers → bonds of iniquity → **remembering "one Jesus
> Christ"** → joy → deliverance of fathers.

<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 36's mirrored structure around vv.17-18 is a real, well-documented observation, but whether it reflects intentional ancient Hebrew chiasmus, natural narrative symmetry, or KJV-absorbed patterning remains genuinely disputed, with element-selection flexibility and a small, self-selected sample of strong BoM instances limiting how much weight the pattern can bear. It is honest, moderate supporting evidence for ancient literary influence, not proof of it.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Many phrases/elements in Alma 36 mirror each other in inverted order around vv.17-18 (first proposed by Welch, 1969); element-by-element pairing is not universally agreed on (Wunderli 2005 disputes several pairings).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Whether this reflects deliberate ancient Hebrew chiastic craftsmanship, versus a natural descent-and-return narrative shape, versus selective post-hoc element selection, is genuinely disputed among Welch, Edwards & Edwards, and critics such as Wunderli.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Chiasmus is well attested in ancient Near Eastern and biblical literature but also occurs, more loosely, in other literatures including English prose; mere presence does not strongly discriminate between an ancient Hebrew-influenced source and later composition unless the instance is unusually elaborate and thematically load-bearing.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Alma 36 is one of the more elaborate and thematically coherent proposed Book of Mormon chiasms, turning on 'remember' language and Christ-centered conversion at the center, which modestly supports the possibility of ancient Hebrew literary influence under a translation model; it does not establish that influence over natural symmetry or KJV-absorbed patterning.

**Inspiration:** Literary structure, if present, says nothing about whether the content is divinely inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish ancient Book of Mormon authorship, does not establish that Joseph Smith could not have produced or absorbed such patterning, and does not rule out that the outline is partly an artifact of the analyst's chosen units.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient Hebrew literary source (tight or loose translation)** — predicts: Elaborate, thematically-centered chiasmus at semantically important turning points, similar to attested biblical chiasms. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Natural descent-and-return narrative shape (unintentional)** — predicts: Rough beginning/end symmetry without fine-grained, densely nested pairing. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
- **19th-century composition with KJV-absorbed rhetorical patterning** — predicts: Occasional inverted parallelism around conversion narratives without an ancient Semitic source. Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Analyst-imposed selection of pairings and boundaries. — *plausible*
- Natural symmetry of a descent-and-return conversion narrative. — *independently supported*
- Unconscious rhetorical patterning absorbed from lifelong KJV exposure. — *plausible*
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A comparably elaborate but disputed chiastic reading proposed for, say, a passage in Chronicles or a New Testament epistle would be treated the same way: accepted observation, contested intentionality, modest not decisive evidence for technique — this note applies that standard rather than crowning or dismissing Alma 36.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

Base rate / look-elsewhere: No matched-control corpus of comparable conversion narratives has been scored blind; the natural-reversal model predicts the pattern about as well as an ancient-source model, so weight sits at the none/weak boundary rather than 'none' outright given the pattern's unusual thematic density.

What would move this: A preregistered blind analysis finding comparably dense, thematically-centered inversion at a low rate in matched 19th-century KJV-influenced prose would raise the weight; finding comparable rates in that control corpus, or substantial disagreement among blinded analysts on Alma 36's own pairs, would lower it further.
