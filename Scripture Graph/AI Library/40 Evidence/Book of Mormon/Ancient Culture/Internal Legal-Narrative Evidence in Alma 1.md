# Internal Legal-Narrative Evidence in Alma 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter provides strong internal evidence that its narrator understands Nehor's execution as punishment for killing Gideon and attempting to enforce priestcraft by the sword, not merely for holding or preaching an unpopular belief. The same account expressly says that the law could have no power over a person for belief.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the Nephite system was equivalent to modern constitutional religious liberty, nor does it supply enough procedural detail to evaluate the trial by modern legal standards.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The narrator may be presenting an idealized or selective description of Nephite law rather than a complete account of its operation.
- The protection stated for belief may not have extended to every kind of public religious conduct.
- A retrospective narrative can distinguish belief from crime without demonstrating that actual proceedings consistently maintained that distinction.
