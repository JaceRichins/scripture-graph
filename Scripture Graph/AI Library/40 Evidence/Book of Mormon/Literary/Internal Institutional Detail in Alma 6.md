# Internal Institutional Detail in Alma 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 6 portrays the Zarahemla church as a regulated community: priests and elders are ordained by laying on of hands, converts enter through repentance and baptism, and persistently unrepentant members are excluded. The language of names being blotted out and no longer numbered among the righteous suggests recognized communal standing, possibly maintained through a list or comparable accounting. **Scripture:** [[Alma 6#^alma-6-1|Alma 6:1]], [[Alma 6#^alma-6-2|Alma 6:2]], [[Alma 6#^alma-6-3|Alma 6:3]], [[Alma 6#^alma-6-4|Alma 6:4]]
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 6:1–4 depicts priests and elders ordained by the laying on of hands to preside over and watch over the church. Repentant nonmembers are baptized and received, while persistently unrepentant members are rejected and their names blotted out so they are no longer numbered among the righteous.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage presents the Zarahemla church as a regulated community with designated officers, an ordination procedure, conditions for admission and continued membership, and formal disciplinary exclusion. The language about names and numbering suggests recognized membership standing or communal accounting, although the verses do not explicitly identify a physical register.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage illuminates the narrative's conception of religious administration: ritual admission, authorized oversight, behavioral expectations, and formally maintained communal boundaries operate together.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
A plausible institutional reconstruction is that local priests and elders supervised an organized congregation whose members entered through repentance and baptism and could lose recognized standing through persistent misconduct. On this reconstruction, being numbered among the righteous denotes acknowledged membership within the community, and "the order of the church" summarizes its system of oversight, admission, and discipline.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
