# Source Date And Proximity in Joseph Smith—History 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Joseph Smith—History identifies itself as an 1838 history while recounting experiences beginning decades earlier ([[Joseph Smith—History 1#^jsh-1-2|Joseph Smith—History 1:2]], [[Joseph Smith—History 1#^jsh-1-3|1:3]], [[Joseph Smith—History 1#^jsh-1-14|1:14]], [[Joseph Smith—History 1#^jsh-1-60|1:60]]). It is therefore a primary source for Joseph Smith's recollections and public self-presentation in 1838, while its earliest narrated experiences are known through retrospective narration. Read as autobiography, it invites comparison with other sources and attention to memory, selection, and later interpretive framing.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Joseph Smith—History 1:2 places the account in 1838, while the chapter narrates Joseph Smith's birth in 1805 and experiences beginning in 1820.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter is a retrospective first-person history rather than a contemporaneous record of its earliest narrated events. The 1838 date identifies the account's stated temporal standpoint; it does not imply that the complete account was published in 1838.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The account is a primary source for Joseph Smith's recollections, claims, and retrospective presentation in 1838. Historians accordingly consider the ordinary features of retrospective autobiography, including memory, selection, later interpretation, and rhetorical purpose.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a retrospective-autobiography reconstruction, genuine memories are selected and organized through the author's later vocabulary, concerns, and understanding. This makes the account valuable for Joseph Smith's mature presentation while explaining why historians compare it with earlier and later sources when reconstructing the development and earliest recoverable form of his claims.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
