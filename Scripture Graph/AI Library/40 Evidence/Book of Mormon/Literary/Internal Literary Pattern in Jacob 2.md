# Internal Literary Pattern in Jacob 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Wounded, pierced, and broken-heart language links Jacob 2's opening description of the audience (vv.7-10) to its closing account of injured wives and children (v.35). Because the chapter explicitly announces tender hearts and wounding/healing speech as its topic from the outset, ordinary thematic restatement predicts this recurrence nearly as well as deliberate literary bracketing does, so the pattern gives only weak support for reading it as an intentionally engineered inclusio rather than habitual, topic-driven vocabulary.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Jacob 2:7–10 describes tender hearers, wounded souls, healing, enlarged wounds, broken hearts, and God's piercing eye; verse 35 returns to broken hearts and the injuries suffered by wives and children.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The recurring imagery connects Jacob's opening concern about injuring vulnerable listeners with his concluding identification of the injuries caused by the conduct he condemns. This is meaningful thematic framing without requiring a technical inclusio or a claim about provenance.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The pattern clarifies the sermon's final-form pastoral rhetoric: necessary public rebuke risks worsening wounds already inflicted by the offenders, while divine speech is presented as capable of healing those wounds.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a final-form literary reading, the opening and conclusion form a pastoral frame. Jacob begins by acknowledging that public correction may enlarge innocent listeners' wounds and closes by identifying the husbands' conduct as the source of their broken hearts. The healing word, dangerous sermon, household injuries, and God's piercing awareness thus form one coherent account of harm and repair.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
