# Source Perspective in 2 Nephi 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 5 narrates the separation through Nephi's first-person perspective ([[2 Nephi 5#^2ne-5-1|2 Nephi 5:1]], [[2 Nephi 5#^2ne-5-4|5:4]], [[2 Nephi 5#^2ne-5-5|5:5]], [[2 Nephi 5#^2ne-5-6|5:6]]). Its reports of records, defenses, temple building, and kingship show how the narrator constructs the new community's identity ([[2 Nephi 5#^2ne-5-12|5:12]], [[2 Nephi 5#^2ne-5-14|5:14]], [[2 Nephi 5#^2ne-5-16|5:16]], [[2 Nephi 5#^2ne-5-18|5:18]], [[2 Nephi 5#^2ne-5-29|5:29]]). The brothers and the people later called Lamanites receive no independent voice, so their motives are represented through Nephi's account.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 5 presents Nephi as the first-person narrator and participant in the separation. He reports the conflict, departure, defensive preparations, temple construction, and political organization; no independent account from Laman, Lemuel, or the people subsequently called Lamanites is preserved.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The separation is narrated from Nephi's interested perspective. The text directly shows how its narrator represents and evaluates the conflict, while the other parties' motives and claims reach readers through his account.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Recognizing the chapter's viewpoint helps readers distinguish narrated events from the narrator's evaluations of motives, divine causation, collective identity, and political legitimacy. Participant testimony may be informative without being neutral.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a participant-memoir reconstruction, Nephi selects the threats, divine direction, migration, defenses, religious construction, and political organization that explain the new community's separation and identity. On an editorial or literary reconstruction, the same arrangement constructs Nephi as the community's authorized founder and explains the chapter's lack of an independent counter-narrative.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
