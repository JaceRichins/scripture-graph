# Explicit Scriptural Intertext in 3 Nephi 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The verse-for-verse correspondence between [[3 Nephi 22#^3ne-22-1|3 Nephi 22:1-17]] and [[Isaiah 54#^isa-54-1|Isaiah 54:1-17]], reinforced by the reference to what is written, is strong evidence of deliberate dependence on the Isaiah 54 textual tradition. Ancient quotation, KJV-mediated translation, and direct modern reuse all accommodate that relationship, so the correspondence alone does not identify its historical mechanism. This instance shares its evidentiary cause with other extended Book of Mormon Isaiah quotations and should not be counted independently toward authorship.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Third Nephi 22 corresponds verse for verse with Isaiah 54 across the chapter's sequence, imagery, and much of its English wording, and it begins by invoking what is written.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The sustained correspondence is best interpreted as deliberate literary dependence on the Isaiah 54 textual tradition. The observation alone does not identify whether the dependence occurred in an ancient source, during translation, or during modern composition.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The chapter is compatible with an ancient speaker or record quoting Isaiah, a translation rendering an ancient quotation in familiar biblical English, or a modern author directly reusing the King James Bible. Independent composition without access to the Isaiah tradition fits very poorly.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The correspondence strongly supports literary dependence on Isaiah, but it does not favor ancient Nephite quotation over KJV-mediated translation or 19th-century composition. It belongs to the same Isaiah-dependence issue as other extended Book of Mormon quotations and should not be counted as fully independent evidence each time the shared mechanism recurs.

**Inspiration:** Literary dependence on Isaiah, without evidence identifying its mechanism, implies nothing by itself about whether the Book of Mormon is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
It does not establish the discourse's date, the direction or historical stage of transmission, ancient Book of Mormon authorship, or a particular fulfillment of Isaiah 54.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient Nephite quotation** — predicts: A speaker with access to an Isaianic text may quote and apply Isaiah 54 at length.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **KJV-mediated translation** — predicts: An ancient quotation may appear in English language closely aligned with the familiar King James rendering.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **19th-century composition** — predicts: A modern composer with access to the King James Bible may reproduce and frame Isaiah 54 directly.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Independent coincidental composition** — predicts: The same extended wording, images, and order arise without literary or traditional dependence.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- A historical speaker quoted an Isaianic text available within the narrative's ancient textual tradition. — *possible*: This is permitted by the narrative, but the correspondence itself does not independently verify the ancient document or transmission path.
- A revelatory or loose translation rendered an ancient quotation in familiar King James Bible language. — *possible*: This explains the English resemblance, but no independently testable translation mechanism is established by this note.
- A 19th-century composer directly reused the King James Bible. — *plausible*: The King James Bible was available in the production environment and direct reuse straightforwardly predicts extended English correspondence.
- The correspondence arose independently by coincidence. — *ad hoc*: Coincidence does not credibly explain seventeen verses retaining the same progression of distinctive language and imagery.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

The chapter-length correspondence strongly supports deliberate dependence on the Isaiah 54 tradition, but it does not distinguish ancient quotation from KJV-mediated translation or direct 19th-century reuse.
