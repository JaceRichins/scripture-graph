# Verbal And Thematic Intertext in 3 Nephi 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The shared gathering language, hen-and-chickens image, wings, Israel/Jerusalem setting, and refusal strongly support deliberate engagement between 3 Nephi 10:4–6 and Matthew 23:37. Third Nephi adapts the distinctive core formula across past, rejected or unrealized, and future gathering rather than reproducing the entire Matthean saying three times. Historical repetition, KJV-shaped translation, revelatory harmonization, and direct modern adaptation remain viable explanations.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 10:4-6 repeats the hen-gathering-chickens image three times, retaining the distinctive combination of 'how oft,' the hen/chickens simile, and the refusal motif from Matthew 23:37, while expanding the saying into past, counterfactual, and future tenses and broadening the address from Jerusalem specifically to 'the house of Israel' generally.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The core simile is close enough in wording, not merely thematic, that a shared-idea-only explanation is weak; some form of direct textual relationship with the KJV Matthew wording best fits the phrase-level match. The threefold repetition and tense expansion is a genuine literary elaboration beyond Matthew, not a bare copy, and is itself worth noting as a deliberate compositional choice in the final text.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Consistent with the broader, already-registered pattern of Matthew/KJV mediation in 3 Nephi's sermon material and other discourse, extended here to a distinct verse (Matthew 23:37) outside the Sermon on the Mount block.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Under the text's own narrative premise, the resurrected Jesus addresses the Nephites using a saying he is remembered to have used of Jerusalem, extending its scope from a specific city to the whole house of Israel he identifies with -- a reconstruction consistent with a single teacher reusing his own memorable images for a new audience, rendered into familiar biblical English by an inspired translation process. Under a compositional reconstruction, the same three-part expansion is a deliberate literary choice by the text's author, adapting a well-known Matthean verse to a new theological argument about scattered and gathered Israel. Both are viable given the wording alone.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports a direct textual/literary relationship between 3 Nephi 10 and Matthew 23:37 in the final English form. It does not, by itself, indicate whether that relationship arose because the resurrected Christ historically repeated a characteristic saying to a new audience (rendered into familiar biblical English), because a translator harmonized a differently-worded underlying saying into known KJV phrasing, or because the passage was composed with direct literary reuse of Matthew.

**Inspiration:** Nothing independently: whether the resurrected Christ genuinely repeated this saying, or a translator/author supplied familiar KJV wording for it, is not decided by the closeness of the match alone.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the historicity of Christ's post-resurrection ministry among the Nephites, the direction of any textual dependence, translation mechanism, or ancient provenance; it is not independent of the general Matthew/KJV-mediation pattern already registered for 3 Nephi's sermon material, though it is a distinct verse-level instance worth its own entry.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient historical repetition, KJV-rendered** — predicts: a close match to Matthew 23:37 in translated form. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Translation-level harmonization** — predicts: the same close match regardless of the underlying saying's original wording. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **19th-century direct literary reuse** — predicts: a close match plus deliberate expansion suited to a new theological argument. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- The resurrected Jesus historically repeated a characteristic lament to a new audience, rendered by inspired translation into familiar KJV English — *plausible*: Consistent with the text's own historical claims, though unfalsifiable independently
- Direct 19th-century literary borrowing and expansion of Matthew 23:37 during composition — *plausible*: Economically explains both the close wording and the elaboration/adaptation to a new setting
- Independent oral-tradition preservation of the same saying transmitted separately into two languages and centuries — *ad hoc*: The specific English wording match, not merely the image, is hard to explain without common textual or translation mediation
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A purportedly independent ancient text reproducing a specific, well-known line from a widely circulated translation nearly verbatim, three times, with literary elaboration, would be read as evidence of direct textual relationship to that translation in any comparable case (e.g., an apocryphal Gospel echoing a Synoptic saying); the same standard is applied here.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->
