# Prophetic Literary Intertext in Jacob 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The master's question about what more could have been done, together with bad fruit and threatened destruction, closely echoes Isaiah's vineyard song. The echo strengthens the reading of Jacob 5 as participating in biblical prophetic vineyard tradition.

**Does not establish:** The echo does not establish that Isaiah borrowed from Zenos, that Zenos was historical, or that Jacob 5 must predate its biblical parallels.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The author may be independently reusing conventional vineyard imagery.
- The echo may reflect modern familiarity with the King James wording of Isaiah.
- Translation diction may heighten a resemblance that was looser in an underlying source.
