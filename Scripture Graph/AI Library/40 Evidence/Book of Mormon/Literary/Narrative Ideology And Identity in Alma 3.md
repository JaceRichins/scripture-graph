# Narrative Ideology And Identity in Alma 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Verses 6–10 are strong textual evidence that this narrator uses embodied, hereditary, religious, and social categories together to explain population boundaries. They should therefore be read candidly as racialized boundary-making language, while recognizing that the precise nature and historical referent of the reported skin difference remain unresolved.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a biological mechanism, the actual appearance of any historical population, or divine endorsement of modern racial hierarchies.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some readers interpret the skin language literally as pigmentation.
- Others propose symbolic, idiomatic, cultural, or identity-marking meanings.
- The passage may transmit an inherited Nephite explanation of group difference rather than a neutral ethnographic account.
- The modern analytical term "racialized" does not imply that the text employs modern racial taxonomies.
