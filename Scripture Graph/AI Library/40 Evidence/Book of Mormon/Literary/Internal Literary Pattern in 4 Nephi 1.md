# Internal Literary Pattern in 4 Nephi 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The placement and repetition of wealth language provide strong internal literary evidence that Mormon's abridgment treats economic inequality and pride as central markers of apostasy. The prosperous united society has no rich and poor, whereas the declining society ends common ownership, divides into classes, adorns churches, and accumulates gold and silver.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that wealth inevitably causes apostasy, that every form of private property is condemned, or that the narrated sequence supplies a complete economic history.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The sequence may reflect the narrator's theological compression rather than a complete social history.
- The cessation of common property might have had unstated practical causes; temporal sequence alone cannot prove economic causation.
- Wealth itself is not condemned uniformly, since the earlier community also prospers.
