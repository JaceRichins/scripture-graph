# Narratorial Epistemic Calibration in Alma 45

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The narrator's distinction among knowledge, report, supposition, and admitted ignorance is meaningful evidence of epistemic restraint in this passage. It supports reading the account as intentionally source-conscious, although it cannot verify that the disappearance occurred or determine the text's date of composition.

**Does not establish:** The narrator's restraint does not establish Alma's translation, the historical accuracy of the report, or ancient authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The distinctions may be a literary technique used to create verisimilitude.
- A later author could imitate the caution of historical narration.
- The wording may preserve genuine uncertainty without preserving an independently verifiable historical report.
