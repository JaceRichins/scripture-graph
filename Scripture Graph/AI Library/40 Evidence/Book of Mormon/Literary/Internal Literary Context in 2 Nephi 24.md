# Internal Literary Context in 2 Nephi 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The poem's own framing supplies strong internal evidence that its immediate literary target is a human imperial ruler: verse 4 addresses the king of Babylon, and observers in verse 16 call the fallen figure 'the man' who shook kingdoms. This does not exclude a secondary or typological application to Satan, but it weighs against treating Satan as the poem's only referent.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that the passage has no legitimate secondary application to Satan or premortal rebellion.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The historical king may function typologically as an image of Satan, allowing both human and cosmic applications.
- Readers may understand the passage as deliberately shifting from an earthly king to a premortal spiritual figure.
- Later theological reception can give the language an additional referent beyond its immediate poetic setting.
