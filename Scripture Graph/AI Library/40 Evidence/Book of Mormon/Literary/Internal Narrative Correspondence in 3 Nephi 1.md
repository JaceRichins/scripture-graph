# Internal Narrative Correspondence in 3 Nephi 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The detailed narrative correspondence between Samuel's announced signs and their reported fulfillment strongly supports the conclusion that the text intends the episode to vindicate prophetic testimony. Because both prediction and fulfillment occur within the same literary history, that correspondence alone is weak evidence for the event's external historicity.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently establish supernatural foreknowledge, the physical cause of the signs, or the historical occurrence of the reported events.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A narrator or editor could have shaped the fulfillment account to correspond with the earlier prophecy.
- The earlier prophecy could have been formulated retrospectively within the narrative tradition.
- The correspondence could reflect an actual prediction and fulfillment, but the literary correspondence alone cannot distinguish that explanation from compositional alternatives.
