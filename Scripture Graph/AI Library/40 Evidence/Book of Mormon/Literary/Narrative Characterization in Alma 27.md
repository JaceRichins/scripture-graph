# Narrative Characterization in Alma 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
In [[Alma 27#^alma-27-6|Alma 27:6]], [[Alma 27#^alma-27-8|Alma 27:8]], [[Alma 27#^alma-27-23|Alma 27:23]], and [[Alma 27#^alma-27-27|Alma 27:27]]–[[Alma 27#^alma-27-30|Alma 27:30]], former violence is set against restitution, honesty, faithfulness, refusal to kill, and courage before death. Together these details portray conversion as a costly transformation of conduct and communal identity, grounded in hope in Christ and the resurrection.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 27 juxtaposes the converts' acknowledged former murders and sins with their willingness to make restitution, refusal to shed blood, honesty, uprightness, religious zeal, steadfast faith, and lack of terror before death.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
These traits form a coherent narrative portrait of conversion as a comprehensive moral reorientation. The people move from violence toward restitution, nonviolence, communal trustworthiness, and resurrection-grounded courage.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The portrait illuminates the chapter's theology of communal identity and repentance. Conversion is represented not merely as changed belief but as costly conduct recognized by both the converts and the Nephite community.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the chapter's moral-transformation framework, acknowledgment of past violence explains the proposed restitution in servitude, abhorrence of bloodshed explains refusal to bear arms, and hope in Christ's resurrection explains willingness to face death. Nephite protection and the concluding description of the converts as beloved complete their transition from feared enemies to trusted members of a new community.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
