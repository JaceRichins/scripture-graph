# Internal Literary Structure in 2 Nephi 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 2:5-27 develops a connected chain from law and accountability through opposition, the Fall, and redemption to agency, and this internal dependency of ideas gives moderate-to-strong evidence that the passage functions as one sustained argument rather than a loose collection of aphorisms. The passage's own hedge is correct: coherence does not establish the truth of its premises, that it constitutes formal philosophy in a technical sense, or ancient authorship. The high precision implied by the original 0.93/0.96 sub-scores overstates what is really a qualitative, if well-supported, literary judgment.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Verses 5, 10-11, 13, 15-16, and 26-27 move through a connected chain: law creates accountability, opposition is necessary for moral categories to exist, opposition was built into Eden via the forbidden fruit, agency requires enticement by opposites, the Fall enables agency, and the Messiah's redemption completes the cycle by restoring free choice.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The sequence reads as a coherent developing argument in its final form, with each step supplying a premise the next depends on; this is compatible with deliberate philosophical construction but also with associative preaching that circles back to reinforce one controlling theme (agency) without a rigorously formal structure.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This is a claim about the coherence of Lehi's discourse as received; it does not bear on whether the underlying ideas are ancient Israelite thought, Joseph Smith-era theology, or some combination, and it does not establish the historicity of Lehi as a speaker.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Insofar as internal coherence across 23 verses is harder to produce by accident than short-range coherence, this moderately-to-strongly supports the passage functioning as one sustained argument; it says nothing about whether that argument's premises are true or ancient.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: a coherent theological argument can be produced by an inspired ancient prophet, an inspired modern revelator, or a skilled nineteenth-century writer working from Christian theological categories already in play.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the truth of the passage's metaphysical claims, that Lehi (or anyone) formally systematized these ideas as philosophy in a technical sense, or ancient authorship.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Associative/homiletic preaching that returns repeatedly to one theme (agency) without a tightly engineered formal structure. — *plausible*: Sermonic literature commonly circles a central theme through restatement rather than deductive chaining, and readers retrospectively perceive tighter logical structure than was originally intended.
- Later editorial or translational smoothing imposed logical connectives ('wherefore', 'and thus') onto material that was originally less tightly sequenced. — *possible*: The connective language could be supplied at any transmission stage without altering the underlying content.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
