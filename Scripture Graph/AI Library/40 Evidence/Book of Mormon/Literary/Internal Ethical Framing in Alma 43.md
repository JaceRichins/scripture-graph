# Internal Ethical Framing in Alma 43

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 43 provides strong internal textual evidence for a bounded defensive-war ethic in this narrative: defense is justified by protection of families, liberty, land, and worship; the Nephites are told not to initiate repeated offenses; and Moroni stops the killing when the encircled enemy is terrified.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every war labeled defensive is righteous, that all Nephite conduct was morally exemplary, or that the passage supplies a complete universal doctrine of warfare.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passage may describe a command for this particular covenant community and conflict rather than a universal theory of war.
- The account is written from the Nephite perspective and may idealize Nephite motives.
- Moroni's order to stop may be tactical or preparatory to demanding surrender, rather than a general rule of mercy.
