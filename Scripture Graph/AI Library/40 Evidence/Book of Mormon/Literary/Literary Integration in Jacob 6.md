# Literary Integration in Jacob 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Jacob 6:1-3 and 7 explicitly interpret and apply the vineyard imagery developed in Jacob 5:61, 65, 66, 75, and 77. Repeated references to servants, nourishment, pruning, fruit, casting out, burning, and the last labor make Jacob 6 the integrated interpretive conclusion to the allegory, moving naturally from symbolic narrative to prophecy and direct exhortation.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Jacob 6:1 explicitly identifies the preceding olive-tree allegory as Zenos's prophecy concerning Israel. Verses 2-3 and 7 reuse the allegory's vineyard, servants, nourishment, pruning, casting out, fire, last labor, and approaching-end language to apply Jacob 5's closing scenes to Jacob's audience.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Jacob 6 functions as an interpretive conclusion to Jacob 5, converting the allegory's vineyard actions into prophecy, exhortation, blessing, warning, and eschatological application.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The chapter transition shows how scriptural allegory can be followed by an explicit interpretive homily, preserving Jacob 5's symbolic vocabulary while shifting its function from narrated vineyard management to direct explanation and exhortation.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a final-form literary reading, Jacob 5 supplies the symbolic narrative and Jacob 6 supplies its interpretation and audience-facing application. The explicit reference to Zenos anchors the transition, while the repeated vineyard vocabulary lets Jacob restate the allegory's final labor and sorting as an imminent religious summons.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
