# Geographic Identification in 1 Nephi 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter supplies relative geographic information—travel near the Red Sea, three days in the wilderness, and a valley beside a river entering the sea—but no distances, coordinates, travel speed, or uniquely identifying landmarks sufficient by themselves to establish a modern campsite.

**Does not establish:** The textual underdetermination neither proves that a proposed campsite is wrong nor establishes that the journey was fictional.

**Alternative explanations:**
- External topographic, linguistic, archaeological, or travel-route evidence might narrow the range of proposed locations.
- The narrator may have expected an ancient audience to recognize geographic conventions or landmarks that are not recoverable from this chapter alone.
