# Historical And Chronological Specificity in 2 Nephi 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 18 is a near-verbatim KJV quotation of Isaiah 8, and its historically specific details (Damascus, Samaria, Assyria, Rezin, Remaliah's son) were available to any Bible-literate nineteenth-century writer without independent historical knowledge. This strongly supports textual dependence on the Isaiah/KJV tradition — reusing, not adding to, the registry's existing bom-isaiah-textual-dependence weight — while giving only minimal independent support to any claim about the Book of Mormon's own ancient grounding, and bearing mainly on the separate, unsettled question of Isaiah's own composition history.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Second Nephi 18 reproduces Isaiah 8's references to Damascus, Samaria, Assyria, Judah, Rezin, and Remaliah's son. Verses 3–4 connect the anticipated plunder of Damascus and Samaria with the brief period before a child can say "my father" and "my mother."
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The named rulers and kingdoms place the oracle in the Syro-Ephraimite crisis, when Damascus and the northern kingdom of Israel threatened Judah and Assyria was the dominant regional power. The child's early development functions as a near-term horizon for the announced reversal.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage reflects the political world of the late eighth century BCE: Judah faced pressure from the Damascus-Samaria coalition while Assyrian intervention threatened to overwhelm both Judah's enemies and Judah itself. Its specificity belongs to the quoted Isaiah tradition and helps readers locate that oracle within a concrete regional crisis.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->
## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a crisis-period reconstruction, the child's birth and development provide an immediate temporal marker while the names Rezin, Remaliah's son, Damascus, Samaria, Judah, and Assyria identify the actors and expected military sequence. Read in that setting, the chapter moves from a near-term announcement about Damascus and Samaria to the broader danger that Assyrian power will flood into Judah.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
