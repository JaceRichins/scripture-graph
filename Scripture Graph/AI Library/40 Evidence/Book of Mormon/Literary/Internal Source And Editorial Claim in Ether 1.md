# Internal Source And Editorial Claim in Ether 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[Ether 1#^ether-1-1|Ether 1:1]], [[Ether 1#^ether-1-2|Ether 1:2]], [[Ether 1#^ether-1-5|Ether 1:5]], and [[Ether 1#^ether-1-6|Ether 1:6]] frame the book as Moroni's selective account drawn from twenty-four plates and ultimately attributed to Ether. This layered source notice prepares readers to encounter an abridged narrative mediated through a self-conscious editor rather than a complete reproduction of Ether's record.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
In Ether 1:1–6, Moroni speaks in the first person, identifies twenty-four plates found by Limhi's people as his source, calls the underlying work the Book of Ether, attributes its record to Ether, and says that he will give only part of the account.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter presents its narrator as a selective editor working from a named intermediary source rather than as the original witness or author of the Jaredite history.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This framing establishes how the final text asks readers to understand its narrative mediation: an underlying record attributed to Ether is recovered by another community and selectively retold by Moroni. The layered presentation supplies the narrative context for subsequent compression, source notices, and changes of narrative level.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Within the book's stated transmission framework, Moroni has access to the twenty-four plates and selects material extending from the tower account to the civilization's destruction. Selective mediation rather than exhaustive transcription thus becomes the organizing editorial frame for the Book of Ether.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
