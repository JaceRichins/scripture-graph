# Internal Chronological Evidence in 3 Nephi 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
In 3 Nephi 2:4-8, several dating systems converge as the narrative adopts a new reckoning based on Christ's coming. The cluster functions as a chronological bridge, translating the new era into the older Mosiah- and Lehi-based frameworks so readers can follow the transition.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 2:4-8 coordinates years since Mosiah, years since Lehi left Jerusalem, and years since the sign of Christ's coming while describing a transition in Nephite calendrical reckoning.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage deliberately marks a calendrical pivot and helps readers locate the narrative simultaneously within several remembered eras.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The synchronized notices illuminate how the final narrative manages overlapping chronological systems at a major transition. Such cross-dating, translating a new era into older communal reference points, is a familiar device in historical and historically styled writing.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->
## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the reconstruction the text itself proposes, Nephite record-keepers tracked time from multiple reference points, and the editor here marks the point where the sign of Christ's coming becomes the primary civil era. Whether read as an inherited chronicle convention or as editorial bridging introduced at the moment of transition, the cluster's placement exactly at the calendar change is what it is there to do: let readers translate the new era into the older Mosiah- and Lehi-based frameworks.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
