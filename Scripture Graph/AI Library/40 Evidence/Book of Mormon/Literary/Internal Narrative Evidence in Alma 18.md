# Internal Narrative Evidence in Alma 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The gated progression from Great Spirit to God, creation, heaven, and revelation in Alma 18:24-33 is strong evidence that the final text deliberately portrays Ammon adapting instruction to Lamoni's existing vocabulary. This is a literary-design finding: it does not establish that the conversation happened as narrated, that Lamoni's prior belief matched the description, or which model of authorship produced it.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
In Alma 18:24–33, Lamoni says he does not understand the word "God" but affirms belief in a "Great Spirit." Ammon identifies the Great Spirit as God, asks about the creation of heaven and earth, explains what he means by heaven, and continues questioning after each response. The broader scriptural and redemptive exposition begins in verses 34–39.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The dialogue portrays Ammon beginning with terminology Lamoni recognizes and incrementally building a shared conceptual framework. His identification may translate, simplify, or revise Lamoni's prior concept rather than affirm that their original conceptions were identical.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage depicts instruction across a religious and conceptual boundary through familiar vocabulary, questions, and clarification. The sequence is intelligible as remembered teaching, didactic shaping of a tradition, or purposeful literary exposition.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a pedagogical reconstruction, Ammon tests Lamoni's vocabulary, adopts "Great Spirit" as a point of contact, and then reshapes that category through claims about God, creation, and heaven before proceeding to the fuller exposition in verses 34–39. On a narratorial reconstruction, the same sequence guides readers through Lamoni's changing understanding while preserving his moments of incomprehension.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
