# Literary Coherence in Moses 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The book of remembrance introduced in [[Moses 6#^moses-6-5|Moses 6:5]] and associated with Adam's genealogy in [[Moses 6#^moses-6-8|Moses 6:8]] returns in Enoch's explanation of how his people know Adam and the fathers ([[Moses 6#^moses-6-45|Moses 6:45]]; [[Moses 6#^moses-6-46|Moses 6:46]]). The recurrence gives the chapter a coherent record-transmission framework linking its primordial history to Enoch's community.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Moses 6:5 introduces a book of remembrance written in the language of Adam. Verse 8 associates the genealogy with the book of the generations of Adam, and verses 45–46 connect Enoch's knowledge of Adam and the fathers to a book of remembrance kept among his people.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The final-form narrative uses continuing written records to explain how Enoch's community possesses knowledge of primordial people and teachings.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage participates in the scriptural record-and-genealogy motif represented by the book of Adam's generations while expanding that motif into an account of inspired writing and communal transmission.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the chapter's own reconstruction, Adam's descendants preserve genealogical and religious knowledge through an inspired record copied or continued among later communities. The recurrence of the record in Enoch's speech therefore supplies the narrative mechanism connecting Adam's generation with Enoch's knowledge.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
