# Internal Epistemology in Alma 32

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The text supports reading Alma's experiment as a staged appeal to lived consequences: a hearer begins with desire, notices limited effects, continues cultivation, and awaits mature fruit. Because the participant is already motivated to believe and evaluates inward changes, the procedure is not equivalent to a controlled scientific test.

**Does not establish:** The reported inward effects alone do not establish a supernatural cause, eliminate psychological explanations, or verify every proposition a hearer might identify as the word.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Expectation and confirmation effects can produce perceived inward enlargement or enlightenment.
- Social belonging or persuasive rhetoric could contribute to the reported experience.
- General moral practice may produce beneficial fruit without verifying every associated theological proposition.
