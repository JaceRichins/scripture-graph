# Narrative Political Consequence in Alma 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Lamoni's actions provide direct narrative evidence that his religious transformation has public consequences, including changes to Ammon's status, communal institutions, political self-description, and freedom of worship.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a modern theory of church-state separation, universal rights beyond Lamoni's territory, or the historical implementation of these measures outside the narrative.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some measures may reflect Lamoni's political independence from his father as much as his religious conversion.
- Building synagogues may represent royal sponsorship of worship rather than a modern distinction between religious and civil authority.
