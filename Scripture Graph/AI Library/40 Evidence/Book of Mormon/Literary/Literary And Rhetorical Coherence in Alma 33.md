# Literary And Rhetorical Coherence in Alma 33

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma answers people excluded from their synagogues by citing Zenos's assurance that God heard him when he was likewise cast out. The correspondence makes the citation a direct pastoral response: exclusion from a human worship space does not prevent prayer from being heard. See [[Alma 33#^alma-33-1|Alma 33:1]], [[Alma 33#^alma-33-2|Alma 33:2]], and [[Alma 33#^alma-33-10|Alma 33:10]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
The audience's stated problem is that they have been cast out of their synagogues and believe this prevents worship. Alma responds that worship is not confined to the synagogue and quotes Zenos saying that God heard him when he too had been cast out and despised.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The selected portion of Zenos's prayer is rhetorically tailored to the audience's exclusion. It supplies a scriptural example that directly answers their concern: being cast out by other people does not place a worshipper beyond God's hearing.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The final narrative presents Alma as choosing a scriptural precedent suited to his hearers' circumstances. Such adaptation fits effective historical preaching, narratorial or editorial selection from a larger discourse, and purposeful literary composition.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a historical-sermon reconstruction, Alma selects the most pastorally relevant line from Zenos because his audience can recognize its situation as their own. On an editorial reconstruction, the narrator places the audience's exclusion beside Zenos's cast-out prayer to make the pastoral inference unmistakable. The resulting correspondence turns an abstract argument about worship into an example addressed to displaced worshippers.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
