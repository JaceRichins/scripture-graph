# Narrative And Audience Adaptation in 3 Nephi 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The coordinated changes in 3 Nephi 12:3, 6, 10, 14, 26, and 48, set against their Matthew 5 counterparts, adapt the sermon to this people, their religious vocabulary, their currency, and the risen Christ's personal presence. The passage reads as a contextualized sermon within 3 Nephi rather than an unchanged duplicate, a literary fact equally compatible with ancient re-delivery, translation-level harmonization, or modern reworking of a base text.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Compared with Matthew 5, 3 Nephi 12 repeatedly adds or substitutes audience-specific language: the poor in spirit are invited to 'come unto me' (v.3); those hungering and thirsting after righteousness 'shall be filled with the Holy Ghost' rather than simply 'filled' (v.6); persecution is specified as 'for my name's sake' rather than 'for righteousness' sake' (v.10); the hearers are named 'the light of this people' rather than 'the light of the world' (v.14); the debtor illustration's Roman farthing becomes the Nephite senine, with an added question about payment while imprisoned (v.26); and the closing call to perfection names the resurrected Jesus alongside the Father as the standard, 'even as I, or your Father' (v.48).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
These changes cluster around one theme: the sermon is fitted to a Nephite audience already visited by the resurrected Christ, already promised the Holy Ghost, persecuted specifically for confessing his name, using local currency, and hearing him teach in person rather than only by report.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The pattern shows that, whatever process produced this English text, the final-form sermon was reworked for its own narrated audience and moment rather than transplanted unchanged from Matthew 5. The separate question of whether and how the Sermon material as a whole depends on the Matthew/KJV textual tradition is already addressed in the registry (bom-3nephi-matthew-kjv-dependence, strong); this note is about the direction and content of the departures from that base text, not a second scoring of that dependence.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a historical-sermon reconstruction, the resurrected Jesus adapts related teaching himself for this new audience. On a translation or editorial reconstruction, a translator or record-keeper renders and harmonizes the discourse with its Nephite setting. On a modern-composition reconstruction, an author reworks a Matthew 5 base text to fit the surrounding narrative. All three reconstructions account equally well for the observed pattern: the sermon reads as audience-aware rather than mechanically copied, regardless of which process produced it.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
