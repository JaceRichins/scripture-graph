# Explicit Internal Intertext in Alma 36

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 36:22 deliberately recalls Lehi's throne vision, making the internal literary relationship nearly certain. That relationship provides none-band evidence for ancient origin, however, because ancient authorship or editing, translation harmonization, shared-source linkage, and modern composition all readily predict it.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 36:22 explicitly says "even as our father Lehi saw" and then closely reproduces the throne-and-angels sequence of 1 Nephi 1:8. Deliberate literary recall in the received text is therefore near-certain.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage intentionally links Alma's experience to Lehi's account. That literary observation is distinct from the contested inference that the linkage originated in antiquity.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Ancient recollection, ancient editing, translation harmonization, modern unified composition, collaborative composition, and shared-source editing can all produce this explicit callback. The datum establishes no meaningful preference between ancient and modern production models.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The echo contributes genuine internal cohesion and is compatible with the claimed ancient record. It does not independently support antiquity because modern authorship or editing predicts the same feature at least as readily.

**Inspiration:** Deliberate internal cohesion says nothing by itself about inspiration; inspired and non-inspired texts can both employ explicit callbacks.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The callback does not establish the historicity of either vision, ancient source-language wording, ancient authorship, a single controlling author, or divine inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient autobiographical or family-tradition recall** — predicts: Alma may explicitly compare his experience with an ancestral account.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Ancient compiler or editor** — predicts: A compiler may preserve or create the cross-reference to reinforce continuity.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Translation-level harmonization** — predicts: A translator may standardize intentionally related descriptions.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Single or collaborative modern composition** — predicts: Authors controlling both passages can deliberately create the callback.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Shared-source or later editorial linkage** — predicts: A common source or editor can supply the explicit relationship.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Alma recalled an ancestral record available in antiquity. — *possible*: This fits the narrative but lacks independent external verification.
- An ancient compiler created or standardized the link. — *possible*: Editorial linking is a general mechanism, but this intervention is not independently evidenced.
- The English translator harmonized two related source passages. — *plausible*: Translation can standardize recurring scenes, although no source-language witness tests this instance.
- A single or collaborative modern author deliberately constructed the callback. — *plausible*: Access to both passages straightforwardly predicts explicit literary cohesion.
- Generic throne-vision imagery alone produced the correspondence accidentally. — *ad hoc*: Generic imagery cannot explain the explicit reference to Lehi, though rejecting coincidence does not choose between ancient and modern deliberate production.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A biblical passage explicitly recalling an earlier named vision would be treated as clear literary linkage, not as independent corroboration of the visions or the antiquity of the linkage. The same distinction applies here.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->
