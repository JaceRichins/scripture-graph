# Internal Literary Evidence in Alma 41

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 41 develops "restoration" from bodily resurrection into a moral principle of correspondence: works and desires return to the agent, so restoration cannot transform wickedness into happiness. The chapter's concluding exhortation makes this definition practical by linking merciful, just, and righteous conduct with the recompense one receives.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 41:2–15 repeatedly develops the word "restoration": bodily restoration to proper order leads into judgment according to works and desires; restoration from sin to happiness is rejected as an unnatural reversal; and the discourse ends with paired forms of conduct and recompense.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
On a unified-discourse reading, the repeated term carries a developing argument rather than functioning merely as a slogan. Alma moves from restored embodiment to moral correspondence, corrects Corianton's apparent misunderstanding, and turns the principle into ethical counsel.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The chapter illustrates a method of theological instruction in which a key term is defined, extended, protected against a mistaken inference, and applied to conduct, integrating resurrection, judgment, moral nature, and recompense.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On this reading, "restoration" names both the return of the person to embodied order and the just return of moral consequences to the agent. Verses 10–13 reject the category mistake that restoration could transform wickedness into happiness, while verses 14–15 convert the corrected definition into counsel: what a person sends out returns to that person. The word therefore joins resurrection, desire, justice, and ethical responsibility in one sustained argument.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
