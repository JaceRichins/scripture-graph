# Internal Literary Evidence in Alma 41

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter provides strong internal evidence that its several uses of “restoration” belong to a coherent argument: restoration to proper bodily order introduces restoration according to works and desires; a proposed reversal from sin to happiness is rejected; and the discourse concludes with matched pairs of conduct and recompense.

**Does not establish:** Internal coherence does not establish ancient authorship, historical authenticity, or any particular account of the Book of Mormon’s production.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The coherence may result from effective nineteenth-century composition rather than an ancient source.
- The repeated vocabulary may be pedagogical elaboration without constituting a deliberately designed literary structure.
- A reader may understand bodily restoration and moral recompense as adjacent doctrines rather than two dimensions of one concept.
