# Explicit Source Attribution in 2 Nephi 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 2:17 explicitly presents Lehi's account as a supposition based on things he has read and on 'that which is written.' That observation is near-certain, but an actual ancient act of reading, rhetorical appeal, editorial framing, and modern characterization predict the wording about equally; the verse therefore provides none-band support for historical ancient source use. It neither identifies the source nor independently bears on historicity or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 2:17 has Lehi introduce his account of an angel's fall as a “supposition” drawn from “things which I have read” and “that which is written.” The verse neither names nor quotes the writing behind the supposition.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The discourse presents Lehi as a reader and interpreter of prior writings. His wording marks the angel account as an inference or synthesis from texts rather than as a new revelation explicitly received at that moment.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Appeals to written authority and interpretive deductions from inherited texts occur in ancient scriptural discourse and later religious writing. The verse illuminates the discourse's rhetorical and epistemic posture by portraying Lehi as reasoning from a textual tradition while acknowledging the inferential status of his conclusion.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->
## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
A useful discourse-level reconstruction reads the verse as an epistemic marker within Lehi's argument: he identifies both the source category—prior writings—and the degree of inference—supposition—before using the angel's fall to explain opposition, agency, and temptation. This casts Lehi as a scriptural interpreter assembling a theological argument from inherited texts rather than delivering an uninterrupted oracle.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
