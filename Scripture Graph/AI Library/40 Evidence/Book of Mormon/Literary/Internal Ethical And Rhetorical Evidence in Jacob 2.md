# Internal Ethical And Rhetorical Evidence in Jacob 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter's concrete references to sorrow, mourning, captivity, broken hearts, lost confidence, and children's exposure to bad examples strongly support a harm-centered dimension in Jacob's marital ethic.

**Does not establish:** This evidence does not make harm the chapter's only moral criterion or supply a complete modern theory of consent, marriage, or abuse.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The injuries may be consequences cited to intensify condemnation while the primary ethic remains obedience to divine command.
- Terms such as captivity may be rhetorical rather than descriptions of a formally defined social condition.
