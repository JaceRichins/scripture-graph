# Canonical Doctrinal Statement in Abraham 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Abraham 3:18-23 is direct, essentially uncontested textual evidence that LDS canon teaches premortal existence of spirits and describes Abraham as foreordained before birth. This is strong evidence of doctrinal content, not independent evidence that the events occurred, that the doctrine is metaphysically true, or that Abraham 3 derives from an ancient record; those separate questions are addressed by other evidence such as papyri correspondence and ANE parallels.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Abraham 3:18 states spirits/intelligences existed before and have no beginning or end; vv.22-23 describe intelligences organized before the world and Abraham chosen before birth.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Read straightforwardly, these verses assert a premortal state of conscious spirits/intelligences and foreordination of Abraham prior to birth; this is the standard reading and is not seriously disputed as a reading of the text itself.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Bears on the history of LDS doctrine (that premortal existence is explicitly taught in canonized scripture), not on whether the narrated premortal events occurred or whether Abraham 3 derives from an ancient document.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strong, essentially uncontested support for the narrow claim that LDS canon explicitly teaches premortal existence; it is not evidence, on its own, that the doctrine is metaphysically true, that Abraham 3 is ancient, or that its origin account is accurate.

**Inspiration:** Both inspired and non-inspired texts can explicitly assert a premortal-existence doctrine; the assertion alone says nothing about which is true here.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish premortal existence as metaphysical fact, ancient Abrahamic origin, or the precise ontology relating intelligence, spirit, and soul.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient patriarchal-source translation** — predicts: Doctrine, if present, traces to genuinely ancient Abrahamic tradition.. Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
- **19th-century revelatory/compositional expansion by Joseph Smith** — predicts: Doctrine reflects Joseph Smith's own developing theology placed into an Abrahamic narrative frame.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- 19th-century theological formulation rather than ancient Abrahamic discourse. — *plausible*
- Modern revelation rather than translation of an ancient record. — *possible*
- Intelligence and organized spirit denote distinguishable stages not sharply defined here. — *possible*
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A biblical text uniquely asserting a doctrine found nowhere else in the canon would likewise count as strong evidence of what the text/tradition teaches, not as independent proof the doctrine is true or historically enacted; this note applies that same restraint rather than treating LDS canon more generously.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**None** (supports, evidence_strength 0.08) for the proposition: *Abraham 3:18-23 provides evidence, beyond its own bare assertion, that the premortal-existence and foreordination doctrine it teaches derives from an ancient Abrahamic source rather than from Joseph Smith's own developing 19th-century theology.*

Abraham 3:18-23 is direct, essentially uncontested textual evidence that LDS canon teaches premortal existence of spirits and describes Abraham as foreordained before birth, but this gives almost no independent support for the doctrine's metaphysical truth or ancient origin since modern revelatory composition predicts the same explicit assertion equally well.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Explicit doctrinal assertions of this kind are common in both ancient and modern scripture; bare presence of an assertion does not discriminate between an ancient and a modern source without external dating evidence.

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the translation model LDS canon holds, an ancient patriarchal source could carry a premortal-existence teaching forward into Joseph Smith's rendering; on a compositional model, the same wording reflects his own developing 1835-42 theology placed into an Abrahamic frame. Both reconstructions fit the text equally well, so the verse's own wording cannot adjudicate between them without an external anchor such as an independently dated ancient parallel or a demonstrable textual source in Smith's own environment.
