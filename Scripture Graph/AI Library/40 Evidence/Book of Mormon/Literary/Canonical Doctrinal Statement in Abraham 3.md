# Canonical Doctrinal Statement in Abraham 3

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Abraham 3:18–23 explicitly teaches the premortal existence of spirits or intelligences and describes Abraham as chosen before birth. It is therefore a primary canonical source for understanding Latter-day Saint teachings about premortality and foreordination.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Abraham 3:18 states that spirits existed before and have no beginning or end; verses 22–23 describe intelligences organized before the world and Abraham chosen before birth.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Read straightforwardly, the passage teaches premortal existence and Abraham's foreordination. This reading of the canonical text is essentially uncontested.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage documents the explicit place of premortal existence and foreordination within canonized Latter-day Saint doctrine and helps distinguish those teachings from later commentary or inference.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->
## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Within Abraham 3, the teaching develops through an analogy between ordered heavenly bodies and ordered intelligences: as celestial bodies differ in relative greatness, intelligences differ in degree, with God above them all. The council scene then applies this framework to premortal persons, including Abraham, whose prior nobility provides the narrative setting for his foreordination.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
