# Internal Collective-Witness Claim in 3 Nephi 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 17:25 asserts that roughly 2,500 men, women, and children individually saw and heard the resurrected Christ, using precise-headcount and individualized-witness language that functions as an internal authentication of public, non-private revelation. That rhetorical claim is unmistakable but is not independent historical corroboration: the same specific-number, individualized-witness features are equally well produced by genuine mass-witnessed history, legendary elaboration, or literary invention aiming for narrative credibility. Weight is revised down from 0.34 to 0.15 (weak band), since the rivals predict the internal claim about equally well and no independent source exists to break the tie.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 17:25 states that 'the multitude...did see and hear and bear record,' specifying a headcount of about 2,500 men, women, and children, each individually witnessing the event ('every man for himself').
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The verse functions as an internal claim to broad, individually-verified public witness -- the text specifies plural, first-person, individualized perception and a headcount, reading as an authentication strategy within the story.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
A specific headcount and 'every man for himself' language are compatible with an authentic report of a real mass event, but the same features are also the standard toolkit ancient and modern authors use to rhetorically authenticate a claimed event; precise numbers and individualized-witness language appear across texts ranging from the mundane to the clearly legendary.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
As evidence a real historical mass event occurred, this is weak: the same textual features are predicted about equally well by an actual historical event, by later legendary elaboration of a smaller nucleus, or by literary invention aiming for narrative authenticity. The witnesses are reported within the same single narrative source rather than separately attestable accounts, so there is no independent corroboration to weigh.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: an internal witness-count claim cannot itself indicate whether the underlying event, if any, was real, embellished, or invented.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish that the event occurred historically, does not establish that 2,500 separate testimonies were ever actually collected, and does not distinguish a real mass witness from a rhetorically inflated or literary one.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Genuine historical mass-witnessed theophany** — predicts: a specific headcount and individualized-witness language. Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
- **Rhetorical/literary authentication device (ancient or modern)** — predicts: identical specific-headcount, individualized-witness language, since that is precisely the toolkit such authentication uses. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Redactional elaboration inflating an originally smaller/private account** — predicts: the same final surface text. Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Approximately 2,500 people historically witnessed the event as described. — *possible*: The narrative explicitly reports this, but no independently identifiable testimony or external record supplied here corroborates the event or number.
- A historical gathering was remembered with amplification of its size or unanimity. — *possible*: Collective-memory and editorial models permit this mechanism, but no earlier form or independent evidence establishes amplification in this instance.
- The language functions as literary authentication without preserving recoverable individual testimony. — *plausible*: The emphatic crowd estimate and individualized-perception language directly serve an authenticating function in the final narrative, although no matched corpus establishes how frequently this convention occurs.
- The inclusive reference to men, women, and children emphasizes the communal scope of the experience. — *plausible*: The wording explicitly stresses comprehensive participation, though this rhetorical function does not determine whether the event was historical.
- A later editor amplified an earlier, less specific witness tradition. — *possible*: Editorial amplification is a general transmission mechanism, but no textual variant or earlier form independently supports it here.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A comparable claim of a precise, large witness count in an extrabiblical text (e.g., legendary multiplication of witnesses to a miracle in later Christian hagiography) would be treated by historians as an authenticating narrative convention, not as evidence the event was witnessed by that many people -- the same standard is applied here rather than granting this number special credibility.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**None** (supports, evidence_strength 0.14) for the proposition: *The event described in 3 Nephi 17 was historically seen and heard by approximately 2,500 individual witnesses.*

The text makes an unmistakably strong internal claim to broad, individualized public witness, but because that same specific-headcount, individualized-witness language is exactly what a rhetorical authentication device would also produce, it offers only weak evidence that a real historical mass event occurred.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: No matched ancient or nineteenth-century control corpus has been supplied, so no quantitative inference should be drawn from claims that crowd estimates or collective-witness formulas are common. The secure evidentiary denominator is one narrative source reporting approximately 2,500 witnesses, not 2,500 separately preserved testimonies.

What would move this: Would move up: independent (non-BoM, non-later-LDS) attestation of a mass Mesoamerican theophany narrative predating or independent of the Book of Mormon. Would move down: identification of the '2,500' figure or 'every man for himself' phrasing as a stock convention lifted from a known biblical or contemporary 19th-century source.
