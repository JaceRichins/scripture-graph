# Official Doctrinal Reception in Abraham 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Current official Church teaching disavows theories that black skin signifies divine disfavor or a curse, that race reflects premortal unrighteousness, or that any race is inferior. This reception history governs responsible present-day use of Abraham 1:21–27 while preserving the separate historical question of how the passage itself and its earlier reception should be understood.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Abraham 1:21–27 associates Egypt with Ham and the Canaanites, describes a curse preserved in the land, and denies Pharaoh a claimed priesthood right while also calling him righteous, wise, and just. Current official Church materials disavow theories that black skin signifies divine disfavor or a curse, that race reflects premortal unrighteousness, or that any race is inferior.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The disavowal excludes using Abraham 1 to teach skin-color-based divine disfavor, premortal unworthiness, or racial hierarchy. It governs responsible present-day application without functioning as a verse-by-verse determination of the passage's original conceptual background.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The juxtaposition marks an important development in Latter-day Saint reception history: racial theories formerly advanced in connection with the priesthood restriction are not binding implications of the canonical passage under current official teaching.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
The text and its modern reception fit together by distinguishing canonical description from later racial application. Abraham 1's lineage, curse, and priesthood language remains available for historical and theological interpretation, while the Church's official disavowal establishes that it should not be extended into claims about black skin, premortal misconduct, or racial inferiority.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
