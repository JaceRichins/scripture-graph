# Explicit Narrative Causal Attribution in Alma 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 4 gives strong textual evidence for the narrator's view that misconduct by professed believers can damage a religious community's public witness: church wickedness is called a stumbling-block, and Alma sees its example as influencing unbelievers toward further iniquity.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish a universally measurable causal law or remove unbelievers' responsibility for their own choices.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The account presents the narrator's and Alma's causal assessment rather than independent testimony from the unbelievers.
- Other unstated social or political factors may also have affected church growth and public conduct.

**Scripture:** [[Alma 4#^alma-4-10|Alma 4:10]], [[Alma 4#^alma-4-11|Alma 4:11]]
