# Internal Geographic Coherence in Alma 22

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 22:27-34 presents an internally coherent relative geography linking boundaries, regions, a travel measurement, and defensive strategy. This moderately supports deliberate final-form spatial design; it gives much weaker support for any unique modern identification or ancient origin, questions tracked separately in the registry.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 22:27-34 relates named regions (Zarahemla, Nephi, Bountiful, Desolation), sea-to-sea boundaries, a narrow neck/strip, a day-and-a-half travel measurement, and a stated defensive rationale that fit together without internal contradiction.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The details cohere into one connected relative map rather than being contradictory or free-floating, which is itself a real literary/compositional achievement regardless of what, if anything, the map corresponds to outside the text.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Internal consistency of this kind is compatible with genuine geographic memory carried through a real record, with later editorial systematization of received traditions, or with careful literary worldbuilding; the passage alone does not distinguish these.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Ancient administrative and narrative geographies (e.g., the tribal-allotment lists in Joshua, or Herodotus's regional descriptions) regularly combine named regions, boundary markers, and travel distances into an internally coherent relative map without requiring the reader to know the absolute location. Alma 22's summary fits that same genre: a plausible reconstruction is that the Nephite record here preserves (through whatever transmission process) a relative geography meant to orient an internal audience already familiar with the terrain, in the same way ancient boundary lists did for their own readers -- which explains the coherence without settling, on this passage alone, whether the terrain being oriented to was ever real.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
This gives moderate support to the narrower claim that the final text presents a deliberately connected spatial model. It gives much weaker, largely non-discriminating support for any particular modern geographical identification or for an ancient-authorship claim, since all three models above predict an internally consistent map about equally well once an author (ancient or modern) is assumed to be careful.

**Inspiration:** Nothing about inspiration follows from internal geographic consistency.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish an ancient origin for the geography, identify a unique modern location, fix absolute scale, or guarantee every expression is correctly interpreted by any one reconstruction.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient geographic memory transmitted through the record** — predicts: internally consistent relative geography reflecting a real landscape once known to the source. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Editorial/narrative systematization** — predicts: the same coherence, produced by a later editor smoothing or organizing received material into a usable summary. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century literary worldbuilding** — predicts: the same coherence, since a careful author constructing a fictional geography would also aim for internal consistency. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- A connected spatial model can be produced through careful literary worldbuilding without describing a real landscape. — *plausible*
- The coherence may reflect later editorial systematization of scattered notices rather than firsthand geographic knowledge. — *plausible*
- Multiple, mutually incompatible modern reconstructions each claim consistency with this same passage. — *independently supported*
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A biblical boundary list with the same internal cross-referencing (Joshua 15-19) would be treated the same way: real evidence of a coherent internal model, weak evidence for a unique modern site absent external anchors.
