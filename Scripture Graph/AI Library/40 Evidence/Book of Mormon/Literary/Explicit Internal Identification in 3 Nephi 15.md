# Explicit Internal Identification in 3 Nephi 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the narrative, Jesus explicitly identifies the immediate hearers as the "other sheep." This makes the Book of Mormon's interpretation nearly certain, but it provides none-band independent evidence that the narrated encounter or identification occurred historically because the serious models predict the same explicit assertion.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Within the narrative, Jesus explicitly tells the immediate hearers that they are the other sheep and says they have heard and seen him. The textual identification is direct rather than inferred.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The text presents the immediate audience as at least one intended referent of the other-sheep saying. Treating that assertion as a historical report is a separate evidentiary step.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Historical, revelatory, legendary, and literary-composition models all readily predict an explicit identification in a scene constructed around this interpretation. Its explicitness therefore gives almost no independent discrimination for historicity.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The passage decisively identifies the Book of Mormon's own interpretation of the saying and contributes to internal theological coherence. That exegetical clarity is not independent historical corroboration.

**Inspiration:** The explicit identification neither establishes nor undermines inspiration; revealed truth, historical report, and literary theology require evidence beyond the assertion itself.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The assertion does not establish that the encounter occurred, the existence or ancestry of the audience, its location, or that this audience was the saying's exclusive referent.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Historical post-resurrection ministry** — predicts: A record of the encounter may preserve an explicit identification.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Ancient theological or legendary development** — predicts: A narrative interpreting the saying may place the identification directly in Jesus's speech.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century revelation** — predicts: A revelation may explicitly disclose a new referent for the biblical saying.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century literary composition** — predicts: A constructed New World ministry can explicitly connect its audience to John 10:16.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- The passage accurately reports an identification made by the risen Jesus. — *possible*: The text asserts it, but this datum supplies no independent witness to the event.
- The identification arose as an ancient theological or legendary development. — *possible*: The mechanism can produce the explicit claim, but no independent evidence for this specific development is supplied.
- The identification is a nineteenth-century revelatory disclosure. — *possible*: The wording is compatible with this model but cannot verify the claimed revelation.
- The identification is a nineteenth-century literary interpretation of John 10:16. — *possible*: This model also predicts explicitness, but this observation alone does not establish comparative plausibility.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
An explicit identification in a biblical narrative would securely establish what the narrative claims but would not independently verify the event. The same distinction is applied here.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->
