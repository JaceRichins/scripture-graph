# Literary Design in Alma 31

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 31 juxtaposes the Zoramites' repeated prayer of separation and assured election with Alma's prayer of sorrow, dependence, and preparation to serve the same people. The arrangement invites readers to compare worship that ratifies superiority with worship that acknowledges weakness and turns outward in ministry. See [[Alma 31#^alma-31-15|Alma 31:15]], [[Alma 31#^alma-31-16|Alma 31:16]], [[Alma 31#^alma-31-17|Alma 31:17]], [[Alma 31#^alma-31-18|Alma 31:18]], [[Alma 31#^alma-31-22|Alma 31:22]], [[Alma 31#^alma-31-26|Alma 31:26]], [[Alma 31#^alma-31-30|Alma 31:30]], [[Alma 31#^alma-31-31|Alma 31:31]], [[Alma 31#^alma-31-32|Alma 31:32]], and [[Alma 31#^alma-31-34|Alma 31:34]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 31 places the Zoramites' standardized prayer beside Alma's prayer. The Zoramite prayer emphasizes separation, election, superiority to surrounding people, certainty of salvation, and gratitude for not being led astray; Alma's prayer expresses sorrow over others' condition, acknowledges his own infirmity, seeks strength and comfort in Christ, and prepares for ministry among those for whom he prays.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The juxtaposition invites comparison between two models of worship: self-validating prayer that reinforces group superiority and dependent prayer that leads toward patient service of others.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The chapter's final form uses contrastive characterization to interpret the religious conflict, allowing each prayer to clarify the values expressed by the other. The opposition can preserve a remembered contrast while also reflecting narratorial selection, editorial sharpening, or translation-level wording.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a historical-memory reconstruction, the two communities' prayers preserve genuinely different religious postures that naturally invite comparison. On a narrative or editorial reconstruction, the prayers have been selected and arranged to sharpen the contrast between exclusionary assurance and compassionate dependence. The repeated Zoramite formula establishes communal conformity, while Alma's vulnerability and concern for others orient prayer toward transformation and ministry.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
