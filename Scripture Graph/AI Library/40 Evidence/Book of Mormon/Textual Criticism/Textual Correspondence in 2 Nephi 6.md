# Textual Correspondence in 2 Nephi 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The ordered correspondences between 2 Nephi 6:6–7, 16–18 and Isaiah 49:22–26 are strong evidence for deliberate reuse or transmission of the Isaiah textual tradition. Ancient quotation remains possible but faces the mainstream exilic dating of Isaiah 49; KJV-shaped translation, harmonization, and nineteenth-century adaptation also remain viable.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 6:6-7 and 6:16-18 reproduce Isaiah 49:22-26 with sustained verbatim and near-verbatim English wording in the same order, including King James Version supplied words not present in the Hebrew.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The English text of this passage stands in a direct literary relationship to the Isaiah 49/KJV tradition rather than an independently generated translation of the same underlying Hebrew.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The correspondence is compatible with KJV-shaped translation, translation-stage harmonization, or nineteenth-century adaptation. An ancient quotation from the brass plates is possible within the narrative, but mainstream critical scholarship commonly dates Isaiah 40–55, including Isaiah 49, to the exilic period, later than the narrated departure with the plates; earlier or unified authorship and later insertion remain contested responses.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On an ancient-source reconstruction accepting an early form or early authorship of Isaiah 49, Jacob quotes connected restoration promises and interprets them between the two quoted blocks. On a KJV-mediated reconstruction, translation or editing supplies the received biblical diction. On a nineteenth-century-composition reconstruction, Isaiah 49 supplies both the wording and sequence. Each explains the final-form relationship, while the exilic dating of Isaiah 49 creates a separate difficulty for the brass-plates reconstruction.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
This gives strong support to textual dependence on the Isaiah/KJV tradition specifically, but per the registered bom-isaiah-textual-dependence issue this is capped at strong (0.84) rather than near-certain, because the same evidence is equally well predicted by tight-control ancient translation and by direct nineteenth-century use of the KJV. It is not independent additional evidence beyond that registered issue.

**Inspiration:** Textual dependence on the KJV tradition says nothing by itself about whether the underlying record or its translation is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish which mechanism produced the dependence, the antiquity of a source manuscript, the historicity of Jacob's discourse, or divine inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient-source quotation** — predicts: Jacob uses Isaiah 49 from an inherited Israelite record, with later translation producing the English correspondence.. Fit with this evidence: *possible but historically contested by the mainstream dating of Isaiah 40–55*.
- **KJV-shaped translation of ancient material** — predicts: An underlying quotation is rendered in familiar KJV wording.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent, while inheriting the ancient-source dating difficulty*.
- **Translation-stage or editorial harmonization** — predicts: Underlying material is aligned with Isaiah's received English wording during production or editing.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century KJV-based composition** — predicts: Isaiah 49:22–26 is selected and incorporated into a newly composed discourse.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **No textual relationship** — predicts: Only incidental resemblance rather than two extended blocks retaining wording and order.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jacob quotes an ancient Israelite Isaiah text from the brass plates. — *possible*: The narrative permits this reconstruction, but the source event lacks external attestation and the mainstream exilic dating of Isaiah 49 creates a specific chronological difficulty.
- Ancient material was rendered or harmonized into KJV-shaped English. — *plausible*: Translation or harmonization can explain the English agreement, although an ancient Isaiah 49 substrate retains the dating difficulty.
- A nineteenth-century producer adapted Isaiah 49 from the KJV. — *plausible*: The KJV was available and this mechanism economically explains the ordered English correspondence, but availability alone does not independently identify the producer or mechanism.
- The two texts are unrelated and converged incidentally. — *ad hoc*: It does not adequately explain two substantial blocks from one passage retaining close wording and order.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
