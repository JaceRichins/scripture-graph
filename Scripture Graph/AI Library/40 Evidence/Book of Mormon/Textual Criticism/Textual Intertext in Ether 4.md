# Textual Intertext in Ether 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ether 4:18 closely tracks Mark 16:16-17 in both wording and three-part sequence (belief-and-baptism leading to salvation, unbelief to damnation, signs following believers), strongly supporting a direct English-textual relationship mediated by the KJV. This holds regardless of mechanism (ancient discourse rendered in translation, editorial harmonization, or nineteenth-century composition), but Mark 16:9-20 is itself a textually disputed 'longer ending' absent from the earliest manuscripts -- relevant to any claim about the antiquity of the underlying saying, though not to the English-text relationship itself. Scripture: [[Ether 4#^ether-4-18|Ether 4:18]], [[Mark 16#^mark-16-16|Mark 16:16]], [[Mark 16#^mark-16-17|Mark 16:17]]
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Ether 4:18 reproduces, in the same order, the belief-and-baptism/salvation, unbelief/damnation, and signs-follow-believers sequence found in Mark 16:16-17, with close verbal overlap: 'he that believeth and is baptized shall be saved; but he that believeth not shall be damned ... and signs shall follow them that believe.'
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The ordering and near-verbatim clauses make independent, coincidental formulation unlikely; this is a textual-level relationship between the English of Ether 4:18 and Mark 16:16-17 in the KJV.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Securely establishes an English-text intertext. Compatible with an ancient discourse rendered into familiar biblical English during translation, with direct literary borrowing from the KJV New Testament, or with modern composition drawing on the same passage.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the translation-mediation reconstruction, an ancient discourse (attributed to the brother of Jared receiving Christ's words, per Ether 3-4) is rendered by the translator into the register of the King James Bible he knew well, producing verbatim overlap with a passage as familiar as Mark 16:16-17 without implying the ancient original used those exact English words. On the direct-dependence reconstruction, the wording is drawn straight from Mark during either translation or composition. Both fits are live; the text supplies no way to choose between them, though the tightness of the match makes some form of KJV mediation the best-supported explanation for the wording itself.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports biblical/KJV mediation of this verse in the final English text, comparable to the corpus's other tight passage-level New Testament correspondences (e.g., bom-ether12-new-testament-intertext at 0.72, bom-3nephi26-john5-intertext at 0.83). It does not distinguish translation-level harmonization from direct nineteenth-century reuse, and it is not independent of the corpus's broader, already-registered KJV/New Testament-mediation pattern.

**Inspiration:** This textual relationship bears on translation or composition mechanism, not on whether the underlying doctrine or its revelation is inspired; the two questions are independent.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The direction of dependence, the date of the underlying discourse, the historical authenticity of either narrative's setting, or whether the echoed Markan wording was original to Mark rather than part of the longer ending (Mark 16:9-20), which is widely regarded by textual critics as absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts (e.g., Codex Sinaiticus, Codex Vaticanus) and likely a later addition.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient discourse translated into KJV idiom** — predicts: Close verbal match arising at the translation stage rather than in an ancient Vorlage.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Direct literary dependence on KJV Mark** — predicts: Exactly this kind of close sequential wording match.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Shared independent early-Christian commission formula** — predicts: Some thematic overlap (belief, baptism, salvation) but not this close a verbal/sequential match absent shared textual ancestry.. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
- **Nineteenth-century composition** — predicts: The identical close match, since an author could draw directly on a familiar KJV verse.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Translation intentionally rendering an ancient discourse in familiar biblical English. — *plausible*: Consistent with the corpus's well-established general pattern of KJV-shaped translation (bom-isaiah-textual-dependence at 0.84).
- Direct literary dependence on the KJV New Testament. — *plausible*: The simplest explanation for such close sequential wording, though it does not by itself distinguish ancient discourse rendered in translation from modern authorship.
- Both passages independently express a common early Christian commission formula, intensified by translation. — *possible*: The specific three-part sequence and exact phrasing is more distinctive than a generic shared formula would predict, which is why this is rated no higher than possible.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.8) for the proposition: *Ether 4:18 stands in a direct English-textual relationship with Mark 16:16-17 (KJV), reflecting deliberate biblical/KJV-shaped mediation rather than independent formulation.*

The close sequential wording of Ether 4:18 and Mark 16:16-17 strongly supports a direct English-textual relationship mediated by the KJV, without establishing whether that relationship arose in an ancient discourse rendered through translation, editorial harmonization, or nineteenth-century composition.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Exact three-part sequential matches of this length (believe+baptize+saved / believe-not+damned / signs-follow) are not a generic Christian commonplace but specific two-verse KJV wording; no matched-control search across comparable non-Markan early Christian sources was performed, so the look-elsewhere cost is unquantified, keeping this below the highest strong-band correspondences (e.g., bom-3nephi15-john10-intertext at 0.96).

What would move this: Evidence that an earlier textual layer lacked this exact wording and only acquired it through later KJV-influenced revision would support translation-stage or later harmonization over an ancient discourse already containing it; evidence of the same three-part sequence in a plausibly pre-1829 non-KJV source independent of Mark would weaken the direct-KJV-dependence reading in favor of a shared formula.
