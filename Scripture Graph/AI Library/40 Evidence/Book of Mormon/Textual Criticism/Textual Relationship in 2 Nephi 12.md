# Textual Relationship in 2 Nephi 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The Isaiah attribution and extensive correspondence between [[2 Nephi 12#^2ne-12-1|2 Nephi 12:1]], [[2 Nephi 12#^2ne-12-2|2 Nephi 12:2]], [[2 Nephi 12#^2ne-12-3|2 Nephi 12:3]], [[2 Nephi 12#^2ne-12-4|2 Nephi 12:4]] and [[Isaiah 2#^isa-2-1|Isaiah 2:1]], [[Isaiah 2#^isa-2-2|Isaiah 2:2]], [[Isaiah 2#^isa-2-3|Isaiah 2:3]], and [[Isaiah 2#^isa-2-4|Isaiah 2:4]] strongly support dependence on or mediation through the Isaiah 2 textual tradition. Ancient copying rendered in KJV-shaped English, translation or editorial harmonization, and direct modern adaptation all fit the relationship. The parallel securely identifies its source tradition but not its date or transmission route.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 12 explicitly attributes its material to Isaiah and follows Isaiah 2 across the chapter with extensive same-order verbal correspondence. The supplied first four verses closely match Isaiah 2:1–4, including the Judah-and-Jerusalem heading, mountain-of-the-Lord oracle, Zion and Jerusalem formulation, and swords-to-plowshares sequence.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter is best understood as a quotation, adaptation, or translation-mediated form of Isaiah 2 rather than as an independent passage linked only by common themes.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The final English chapter belongs directly to the Isaiah 2 textual tradition. The correspondence is compatible with Nephi copying Isaiah, a translator or editor using familiar biblical wording, or a modern composer adapting the KJV.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On the traditional ancient-record reconstruction, Nephi copies Isaiah from an available scriptural collection and the English translation adopts KJV diction. On a textual-harmonization reconstruction, an ancient or revealed quotation is aligned with the familiar Isaiah text during translation or editing. On a modern-origin reconstruction, Isaiah 2 is the direct literary source. The attribution and full sequence fit each dependence model, while excluding an unrelated-source account.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The attribution plus sustained verbal sequence strongly supports the named textual-dependence proposition and strongly challenges source independence. It does not significantly distinguish ancient copying from translation harmonization or nineteenth-century KJV reuse.

**Inspiration:** Dependence on an earlier scriptural text is compatible with both inspired and ordinary composition and therefore does not adjudicate inspiration.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
It does not establish the date or direction of transmission, the contents of the brass plates, the source language or precise Isaiah textual form, the translation method, Book of Mormon antiquity, or historical authenticity.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Nephi copied Isaiah from the brass plates and the translation rendered it in familiar KJV language. — *possible*: The narrative supplies this source model and ancient scriptural quotation is ordinary, but the brass-plate text cannot be independently compared.
- The translation or a later editorial process harmonized an underlying Isaiah quotation to Isaiah 2. — *plausible*: It explains both chapter-level continuity and close received English wording without requiring that every phrase reproduce the ancient record.
- A nineteenth-century author adapted Isaiah 2 directly from the KJV. — *plausible*: The source was accessible and straightforwardly predicts the observed order and language.
- Differences preserve a distinct ancient Isaiah textual form. — *possible*: Ancient Isaiah textual variation is independently known as a general phenomenon, but each claimed distinctive reading requires comparison with ancient witnesses and early Book of Mormon transmission.
- The similarities arose independently from shared prophetic subject matter. — *ad hoc*: Common themes cannot account for explicit attribution and extended same-order verbal correspondence.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
