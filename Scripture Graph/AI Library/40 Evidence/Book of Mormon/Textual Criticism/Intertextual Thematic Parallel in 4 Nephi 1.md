# Intertextual Thematic Parallel in 4 Nephi 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The combination of conversion, unity, common goods, and the absence of poverty closely parallels the summaries of the Jerusalem church in Acts. This strongly establishes a thematic and verbal-scriptural relationship, but competing explanations make it weak evidence for any particular authorship or historical-origin theory.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish direct borrowing, ancient authorship, modern authorship, or that the communities used identical economic arrangements.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Both accounts may independently apply a widespread religious ideal of communal sharing.
- The resemblance may arise from the Book of Mormon's English translation idiom.
- A modern composition could consciously draw on the King James text of Acts.
- An ancient Christian community could model itself on teachings or practices shared with the Jerusalem church without literary dependence.
