# Internal Textual Evidence in 1 Nephi 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
1 Nephi 2:20-24 is written in explicit 'inasmuch as / except' conditional clauses, making prosperity, leadership, separation from God, and power between the two lineages contingent on obedience or rebellion rather than fixed privileges of birth. This is an uncontested reading of this passage's own grammar; it is offered as background relevant to, but not itself an adjudication of, the separate and genuinely contested question of how later 'skin of blackness' passages (2 Nephi 5, Jacob 3, Alma 3) should be read.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
In 1 Nephi 2:20-24, each promised or threatened outcome is expressly conditioned on conduct: keeping the commandments precedes prosperity, a promised land, and leadership (vv.20, 22); rebellion precedes separation from the Lord's presence (v.21); the 'sore curse' and any power the cursed line gains over Nephi's seed are explicitly conditioned on continued rebellion, 'except they shall rebel against me also' (v.23); becoming a 'scourge' is likewise conditioned (v.24).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
On its own grammar, the passage presents status and power between the two descendant groups as conditional covenant consequences, revisable in either direction according to conduct, rather than as fixed privileges or disabilities permanently attached to one lineage.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This conditional structure is the theological logic Nephi uses to interpret his family's later fortunes, and it is the kind of textual feature current LDS discussion of the Book of Mormon's later 'curse' and 'skin of blackness' language (2 Nephi 5, Jacob 3, Alma 3) points back to when distinguishing a conditional-covenant reading from a fixed, hereditary one. This note is limited to what 1 Nephi 2:20-24 itself says; it does not analyze those later passages, which is where the historically racialized readings are actually anchored.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read on its own terms, 1 Nephi 2:20-24 fits an ordinary covenant-conditional pattern: blessing and curse are framed as responses to conduct rather than as birthright status. That gives readers a clear textual anchor for the conditional-covenant side of the later, genuinely disputed question of how the book's curse and mark language should be read — a question this note does not itself resolve, since it requires reading the later chapters together with this one.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
