# Internal Textual Evidence in 3 Nephi 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 27:13-21 pairs an explicit definition of 'the gospel' (Christ's mission and the judgment it establishes, vv.13-15) with an explicit 'commandment' to repent, be baptized, and endure to the end (v.20), presenting them as one continuous discourse in the same genre as other scriptural gospel-summary-plus-response texts (1 Corinthians 15:1-4; Mark 1:14-15). The passage's own wording distinguishes 'gospel' from 'commandment' even while uniting them, so the accurate reading is that the two are tightly integrated, not that the term 'gospel' grammatically includes the commandment.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Verses 13-15, introduced by 'this is the gospel which I have given unto you' (v.13), describe Christ's mission, His being lifted up on the cross, and the judgment of all people. Verses 16-19 describe the consequences of enduring or not enduring. Verse 20 separately introduces further material: 'Now this is the commandment: Repent, all ye ends of the earth, and come unto me and be baptized in my name...' Verse 18 affirms that Christ 'fulfilleth all his words.'
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The discourse names 'the gospel' (Christ's saving mission and its ordained outcome, vv.13-15) and 'the commandment' (the response required of hearers, v.20) as two distinct but tightly joined elements of one continuous teaching, rather than using 'gospel' as a single term that grammatically absorbs the commanded response.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This pairing places 3 Nephi 27 in the recognizable genre of scriptural gospel-summary-plus-response texts, comparable to Paul's gospel definition in 1 Corinthians 15:1-4 followed by its implications, and to Mark 1:14-15, where 'the gospel of the kingdom' is immediately paired with a call to repent and believe. As in those texts, the saving message and the required response are held together without being treated as strictly synonymous.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read against that genre, 3 Nephi 27:13-21 reads as an occasion-specific teaching to the twelve Nephite disciples, given as the resurrected Christ establishes doctrine and ordinances among them: he names the saving message as 'the gospel' and, within the same discourse, names the required response as 'the commandment,' joining the two into one coherent teaching while still using distinct terms for each.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
