# Literary Intertextuality in 3 Nephi 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Close verbal and thematic correspondences with biblical scenes—the Father's declaration about the Beloved Son, inspection of Jesus's wounds, the triadic baptismal formula, and the rock-versus-sand conclusion—are strong evidence of a textual relationship with biblical language. They do not by themselves determine whether the relationship arose through shared revelation, deliberate composition, translation vocabulary, editorial shaping, or direct literary dependence.

**Does not establish:** The correspondences do not establish the direction of dependence, the historical date of composition, or either ancient or modern authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A translator may have rendered conceptually similar source material in familiar King James biblical language.
- The author or editor may have consciously modeled the account on New Testament passages.
- Within a believing framework, the parallels may reflect the same divine speaker or shared doctrine in different settings.
- Some thematic similarities may arise naturally from narrating baptism, resurrection witnesses, and divine instruction.
