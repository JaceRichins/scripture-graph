# Intertextual-Parallel in 2 Nephi 26

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 26:16's imagery of a destroyed people whose speech is 'low out of the dust' and whose voice is 'as one that hath a familiar spirit' closely parallels the well-known KJV language of Isaiah 29:4, which describes a humbled Jerusalem/Ariel speaking 'low out of the dust' with a voice 'as of one that hath a familiar spirit, out of the ground.' Nephi elsewhere quotes Isaiah 29 at length (2 Nephi 27), making direct literary dependence between these passages highly plausible.

**Does not establish:** The parallel alone does not establish whether the borrowing occurred anciently (Nephi consciously alluding to Isaiah) or in the translation/composition process; it establishes only that clear literary dependence of some kind exists between the two passages.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Nephi, having Isaiah on the brass plates, deliberately alludes to Isaiah 29 within this same Isaiah-saturated sermon (chs. 25-27) as a conscious ancient literary device.
- Critics argue the near-verbatim match to the King James wording reflects dependence on the KJV text available in 1829 during the Book of Mormon's production, rather than an independent ancient composition later translated.
