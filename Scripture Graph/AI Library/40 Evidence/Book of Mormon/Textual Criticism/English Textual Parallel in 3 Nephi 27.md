# English Textual Parallel in 3 Nephi 27

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The ask-and-knock saying in [[3 Nephi 27#^3ne-27-29|3 Nephi 27:29]] and the two-gates saying in [[3 Nephi 27#^3ne-27-33|3 Nephi 27:33]] closely reprise [[3 Nephi 14#^3ne-14-7|3 Nephi 14:7–8]], [[3 Nephi 14#^3ne-14-13|3 Nephi 14:13–14]], and their parallels in [[Matthew 7#^matt-7-7|Matthew 7:7–8, 13–14]]. The paired clusters and preserved order strongly support intentional literary reuse rather than coincidence. They do not distinguish historical repetition by Jesus from translation harmonization, editorial reuse, or modern composition, and they are not independent of the earlier 3 Nephi 14 parallel.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 27:29 repeats the ask-and-knock language found in 3 Nephi 14:7–8 and Matthew 7:7–8, while 3 Nephi 27:33 repeats the contrast between the strait and wide gates found in 3 Nephi 14:13–14 and Matthew 7:13–14. The two teaching clusters occur in the same relative order, although 3 Nephi 27 abbreviates and adapts them.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The combination of close wording, two distinct saying clusters, preserved order, and an earlier occurrence within the same narrative strongly favors intentional reprise. The immediate textual source may be 3 Nephi 14, Matthew 7 as mediated through 3 Nephi 14, or a common translation template.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The reprise is compatible with Jesus intentionally repeating earlier teachings, an editor or translator harmonizing two discourses, or a modern author reusing a prior passage. It establishes literary cohesion in the received text more readily than it establishes the historical origin of the sayings.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The strongest supportive reading is that repeated teachings are natural within a sustained account of Jesus addressing the same community and can represent authentic pedagogical repetition. That fit is real, but literary repetition is equally available to editorial and modern-composition models, so the passage does not independently support ancient authorship.

**Inspiration:** The literary reprise by itself says nothing about whether the text is inspired; repetition and textual mediation occur in both inspired and non-inspired literature.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The reprise does not establish whether the repetition occurred in history, whether an ancient source used Matthew-like wording, whether 3 Nephi directly copied Matthew, or whether the book is ancient or modern.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Historical repetition by Jesus** — predicts: A teacher may intentionally repeat and adapt previously delivered teachings for a later setting.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Ancient source with translation harmonization** — predicts: A translator may render related sayings with the same KJV template, preserving or increasing their verbal similarity.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Later editorial reuse** — predicts: An editor familiar with the earlier discourse may deliberately reprise it in a concluding scene.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Nineteenth-century composition** — predicts: An author using Matthew 7 and the already composed 3 Nephi 14 can readily repeat both clusters in the same order.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent recurrence** — predicts: Traditional sayings may recur independently, but two clusters in matching order and close wording are less expected.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jesus intentionally repeated teachings previously given to the same audience. — *plausible*: Teachers commonly repeat central sayings, and the narrative presents both occurrences as teachings of Jesus.
- A translator used stable KJV wording for related sayings present in an ancient source. — *plausible*: Consistent translation templates can create close English correspondences without identical source-language wording.
- A later editor or modern author deliberately reused the earlier Book of Mormon passage. — *plausible*: Direct access to 3 Nephi 14 predicts the wording, paired clusters, and preserved order.
- Both passages independently preserve a shared oral teaching tradition. — *possible*: Shared tradition can explain recurring sayings, although the close English wording may still reflect common translation or editorial mediation.
- The two clusters recur together by chance. — *ad hoc*: It does not adequately explain the close wording and retained order of two distinguishable sayings.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
Comparable repeated saying clusters within a biblical corpus would be treated as strong evidence of shared tradition, literary reuse, or editorial harmonization, while historical repetition would remain possible. The original note was right about the relationship but its 0.98 score overstated what an internally available source can discriminate.

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.78) for the proposition: *The received wording of 3 Nephi 27 deliberately reprises the ask-and-knock and two-gates teachings already presented in 3 Nephi 14 rather than reproducing both ordered clusters by coincidence.*

The paired sayings, close wording, and preserved order strongly support an intentional reprise of 3 Nephi 14 in 3 Nephi 27, but they do not identify whether the reprise is historical, translational, editorial, or compositional.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Teachers and religious texts often repeat short maxims, so recurrence of either ask-and-knock or two-ways imagery alone has a meaningful base rate. The occurrence of both clusters in the same order and close wording is less common, but no matched-control frequency or accounting of all searchable sayings has been supplied. Because 3 Nephi 14 is already present in the same work, 3 Nephi 27 is not independent evidence for broader Matthew dependence.

What would move this: Assessment dated 2026-09-03. The weight would rise if textual analysis found further distinctive, ordered correspondences tying 3 Nephi 27 specifically to 3 Nephi 14 rather than to general gospel tradition; it would fall if matched ancient and nineteenth-century discourse corpora showed that paired recurrence of these formulas in this order is common without literary dependence.
