# Intratextual Narrative Fulfillment in Mosiah 11

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
As intratextual evidence of planned narrative correspondence, Mosiah 21 explicitly treats the people's bondage as fulfillment of the Lord's word and later reports that the Lord was slow to hear their cries because of their iniquities, matching Abinadi's warning in Mosiah 11.

**Does not establish:** The literary correspondence does not independently establish the historicity of the events, supernatural foreknowledge, or a particular theory of authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- An author or editor could have shaped the warning and later account to correspond.
- The correspondence may express a recurring theological pattern rather than preserve a uniquely predictive event.
- Retrospective composition could present an already-known disaster as earlier prophecy.
