# Direct Textual Parallel in 2 Nephi 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 15 matches Isaiah 5 almost word-for-word across all 30 verses, including KJV-specific translation choices, which is strong evidence that this chapter's English wording depends on the King James translation tradition. It does not establish the mechanism of that dependence, the antiquity of an underlying non-English source, or anything about the reliability of the translation elsewhere in the text.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 15 and Isaiah 5 correspond almost word-for-word across all 30 verses, including KJV-specific translation choices and archaic phrasing distinctive to the King James Version rather than to the Hebrew Masoretic Text directly.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
This level and specificity of correspondence, matching the KJV's particular English translation decisions and not merely the underlying Hebrew content, is standard evidence of direct literary dependence on the KJV text itself; what is disputed is the mechanism, not the fact of dependence.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Establishes that, whatever the translation process was, its output for this chapter tracks the specific 1611 KJV English tradition rather than an English rendering produced independently from Hebrew; this bears on 19th-century compositional/translation mechanics, not on the antiquity of the underlying record Nephi is said to be quoting.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strong evidence that this chapter's English wording depends on the KJV translation tradition; compatible with several LDS-accepted models (e.g., Joseph Smith consulting an open Bible during Isaiah blocks) and does not by itself undermine the claim that Nephi's plates contained Isaiah content, but it does rule out models claiming the Isaiah chapters were rendered wholly independently of the KJV English tradition.

**Inspiration:** Dependence on the Isaiah/KJV tradition says nothing about whether the translation or narrative process was divinely guided.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the date of any underlying non-English source, the precise mechanism of dependence, the historicity of the plates, or the reliability of the translation process for non-quoted material elsewhere in the text.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Tight-control ancient-source translation independent of the KJV English tradition** — predicts: Correspondence with Isaiah's Hebrew content but divergence from KJV-specific English translation choices. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
- **Translation/dictation incorporating the KJV text directly for quoted Isaiah material** — predicts: Near-verbatim match to KJV wording including its specific translation choices. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **19th-century composition drawing on the KJV as literary source** — predicts: Same near-verbatim KJV match. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Ancient Nephite quotation of an available Isaiah tradition. — *possible*
- Ancient source translated using familiar King James wording. — *plausible*
- 19th-century composition copying/adapting the KJV Isaiah text. — *plausible*
- Independent convergent composition without a shared source. — *ad hoc*
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
