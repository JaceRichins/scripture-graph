# Direct Textual Parallel in 2 Nephi 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[2 Nephi 15]] follows [[Isaiah 5]] through the full chapter in the same order and in near-verbatim KJV English, strongly supporting mediation through the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition. The strongest critical explanation is direct nineteenth-century use of the KJV, but an ancient quotation rendered or harmonized through familiar biblical language predicts the same English result. The parallel therefore establishes strong textual dependence while leaving its mechanism and the provenance of any underlying source unresolved.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 15 follows Isaiah 5 across the full thirty-verse chapter in the same order and with near-verbatim KJV English wording. The supplied note reports that the correspondence includes archaic phrasing and translation choices associated with the KJV, although it does not enumerate those examples.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The strongest critical reading is direct dependence on the available KJV text during nineteenth-century composition or dictation. The sustained wording and order make independent English generation implausible. The observation establishes textual mediation but does not by itself decide whether that mediation occurred through ordinary copying, consultation, memory, revelatory rendering, or harmonization of an ancient quotation.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The English form of 2 Nephi 15 belongs to the Isaiah 5/KJV textual stream rather than representing an English translation produced independently from Hebrew. This constrains models of the translation or composition process while leaving the proposed pre-English history of the material open.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The parallel strongly challenges a model in which 2 Nephi 15's English wording was revealed or translated independently of the KJV tradition. It strongly supports KJV mediation, but because both ancient-source translation using biblical wording and modern composition predict that mediation, it does not by itself discriminate between those broader origin models.

**Inspiration:** Use of the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition does not by itself determine whether the translation or compositional process was inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
It does not establish whether an underlying Nephite source existed, which human or revelatory mechanism introduced the KJV wording, or how non-Isaiah portions of the Book of Mormon were produced.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient Isaiah quotation translated independently of KJV English** — predicts: Agreement in underlying content but substantial independent English phrasing, especially where the KJV makes distinctive translation choices.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
- **Ancient source rendered or harmonized through KJV language** — predicts: Chapter-length agreement with KJV wording while permitting an ancient source behind the English output.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Revelatory translation incorporating received biblical wording** — predicts: Familiar KJV language for an extended Isaiah quotation.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Nineteenth-century composition or revision using the KJV** — predicts: The same order, wording, and KJV-specific renderings found in Isaiah 5.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent convergent composition** — predicts: General thematic similarity but not sustained near-verbatim agreement across an entire ordered chapter.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient Nephite Isaiah quotation was translated using familiar KJV wording. — *plausible*: This directly predicts the English parallel while locating dependence at the translation stage rather than the source stage.
- The KJV wording was supplied through revelatory harmonization rather than ordinary consultation. — *possible*: It is compatible with the textual result, but the parallel alone cannot independently identify a revelatory mechanism.
- A nineteenth-century composer copied, adapted, or recalled Isaiah 5 from the KJV. — *plausible*: The KJV was available in the composition environment, and direct use parsimoniously predicts the sustained English match.
- The two English chapters arose independently from the same underlying Hebrew content. — *ad hoc*: Independent translation does not plausibly predict near-verbatim agreement in wording and sequence across the entire chapter, especially at distinctive English renderings.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
Extended verbal parallels between Kings, Chronicles, Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Psalms are properly treated as strong evidence of literary dependence or a shared textual tradition, without assuming a particular direction or historical mechanism. The same standard warrants strong dependence here, while the Book of Mormon's nineteenth-century English production context makes KJV mediation especially relevant.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *2 Nephi 15's near-total 30-verse correspondence with Isaiah 5 reflects dependence on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition rather than independent generation of the same sequence.*

The sustained order and near-verbatim English wording strongly support dependence of 2 Nephi 15 on the Isaiah 5/KJV textual tradition, while leaving ancient-source translation, revelatory harmonization, and modern reuse unresolved.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Thirty verses of ordered, near-verbatim correspondence are far less expected under independent composition than under textual dependence. This chapter is nevertheless one dependent instance within the Book of Mormon's larger cluster of KJV Isaiah quotations, so it should not be counted as independent cumulative evidence in addition to sibling Isaiah chapters.

What would move this: A systematic inventory of readings unique or highly distinctive to the KJV would sharpen the case for specifically KJV-level mediation; substantial departures from the KJV that independently track earlier Hebrew or pre-KJV witnesses would increase the plausibility of an additional ancient textual layer, though they would not erase the demonstrated English dependence.

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a modern-composition reconstruction, Isaiah 5 served directly as the English literary base for 2 Nephi 15. On an ancient-source translation reconstruction, Nephi's Isaiah quotation supplied the underlying content while the translation process rendered or harmonized it through the familiar KJV text. Both reconstructions fit the English correspondence; a wholly KJV-independent English translation does not.
