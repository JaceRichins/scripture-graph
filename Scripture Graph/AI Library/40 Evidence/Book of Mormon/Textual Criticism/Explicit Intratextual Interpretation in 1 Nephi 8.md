# Explicit Intratextual Interpretation in 1 Nephi 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Later passages explicitly interpret the principal images in Lehi's dream, making symbolic interpretation in the final form near-certain. Unified planning and later interpretive development both readily explain those glosses, so the observation gives none-band discrimination for unified original composition and none for antiquity, historicity, or inspiration. It also does not require one exhaustive referent for every detail.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
1 Nephi 11–15 explicitly interpret principal images from 1 Nephi 8: the tree as the love of God, the iron rod as the word of God, the mist of darkness as the temptations of the devil, the great and spacious building as human pride and vain imaginations, and the filthy water and gulf as images of hell and separation from God.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The final text supplies its own interpretive framework for Lehi's dream. Nephi's later vision and dialogue function as internal commentary authorizing a symbolic reading of the principal images.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Interpretive guides are familiar features of biblical apocalyptic and visionary literature, including Daniel, Zechariah, and Revelation. First Nephi uses that practice to connect Lehi's family-centered dream with a broader account of covenant history.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->
## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
A useful literary reconstruction reads chapters 8 and 11–15 as a dream-and-interpretation sequence: Lehi supplies the symbolic landscape, while Nephi's guided vision and later dialogue progressively explain it. This structure connects the family's immediate choices—approaching or leaving the tree—to the book's larger covenant history and makes divine love, revelation, temptation, pride, and separation the controlling themes without requiring every incidental detail to have one exhaustive referent.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
