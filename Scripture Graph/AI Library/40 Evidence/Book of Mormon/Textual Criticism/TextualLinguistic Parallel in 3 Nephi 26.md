# TextualLinguistic Parallel in 3 Nephi 26

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 26:5's 'resurrection of everlasting life ... resurrection of damnation' contrast closely tracks John 5:29 KJV's matching good/evil, paired-resurrection, damnation-wording formula. This is the same evidentiary finding as the registered bom-3nephi26-john5-intertext issue (0.83), fully contained within v.5 itself, so this note reuses that canonical weight rather than scoring independently at 0.7 and must not be counted as separate corroborating evidence.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 26:5 pairs 'the resurrection of everlasting life' for the good against 'the resurrection of damnation' for the evil; John 5:29 KJV uses the closely matching 'resurrection of life ... resurrection of damnation' contrast for the same good/evil judgment. This good-and-evil, paired-outcome, distinctive-damnation-wording cluster is already fully present within v.5 itself, which is the substance the registered bom-3nephi26-john5-intertext issue (covering vv.4-5/28-29) rests its 0.83 weight on.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The specific, distinctive shared phrase 'resurrection of damnation' paired with the good/evil contrast is best read as a deliberate textual or translation-level relationship with John 5, not independent theological phrasing.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Reinforces, rather than adds to, the existing finding that 3 Nephi 26 is mediated through Johannine KJV language at this point.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
This verse sits within the well-attested pattern of 3 Nephi's discourse material rendered in recognizably Johannine KJV idiom, alongside the registered John 1, John 10, and John 17 correspondences in the same block of chapters.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Per the no-double-counting rule, this should adopt the registered issue's weight (0.83, strong) rather than carry its own independent 0.7 score, and should not be aggregated on top of it as if it were separate corroborating evidence.

**Inspiration:** Says nothing about inspiration; KJV-Johannine phrasing is equally predicted by translation-level harmonization, deliberate reuse, or modern composition.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not by itself establish literary dependence direction, translation mechanism, historical delivery of the saying, or the antiquity of the underlying record; establishes only a strong relationship between the two English texts as we have them.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Independent historical repetition of the same teaching** — predicts: Similar doctrinal content but not the same distinctive 'resurrection of damnation' phrase. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
- **Translation-level harmonization to familiar KJV Johannine phrasing** — predicts: Exactly this kind of shared distinctive wording without requiring direct literary reuse. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Direct literary/editorial reuse of John 5:29** — predicts: Close verbal match with preserved order, as observed. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- The passages independently express common Christian resurrection theology — *possible*: The doctrine is widespread, but the distinctive paired 'resurrection of damnation' wording and order make full independence less economical.
- Translation into familiar King James idiom produced this overlap regardless of literal dependence — *plausible*: Consistent with the broader, independently documented pattern of Johannine/KJV mediation elsewhere in 3 Nephi (John 1, John 10, John 17).
- A nineteenth-century author directly adapted John 5:29 — *plausible*: Parsimoniously predicts the close English wording.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
