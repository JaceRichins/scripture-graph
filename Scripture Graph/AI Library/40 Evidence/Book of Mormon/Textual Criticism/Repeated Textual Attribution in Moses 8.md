# Repeated Textual Attribution in Moses 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Moses 8:25 attributes grief to Noah, and verse 26 repeats that attribution within the Lord's speech while adding Noah's appeal and the threat against his life. The repetition makes Noah's grief integral to the chapter's portrayal of a sorrowing, endangered prophet whose appeal immediately precedes the announced judgment.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Moses 8:25 says that Noah repented and was grieved that the Lord had made humanity. Verse 26 repeats within the Lord's speech that it is Noah who repents, adding that Noah called upon the Lord and that the people sought Noah's life.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The second attribution makes Noah's grief an integrated feature of the chapter's present wording rather than a reading dependent on one isolated or ambiguous pronoun. The repetition connects Noah's sorrow and appeal with the announced destruction.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compared with Genesis 6:6, Moses 8 presents a sustained interpretive revision in which Noah bears the explicitly stated grief. The final-form effect is to place a sorrowing and endangered prophet immediately before the divine judgment.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a final-form theological reconstruction, Noah grieves over humanity, calls upon God, and faces threats against his life; the Lord's speech then answers that situation with the announcement of destruction. Repeating the attribution inside the speech anchors Noah's sorrow within the chapter's explanation of the flood judgment.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
