# Repeated Textual Attribution in Moses 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Because Moses 8 attributes Noah's grief both in narration and again within the Lord's speech, the chapter provides strong internal evidence that the Noah attribution is an integrated feature of its present form, not an isolated ambiguous pronoun.

**Does not establish:** The repetition does not establish that Moses preserves an earlier textual form than Genesis 6:6 or prove a particular theory of revelation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The difference from Genesis may reflect modern editorial or revelatory revision.
- It may preserve or recreate a theological interpretation intended to protect divine immutability.
- The repeated wording does not disclose whether the variant derives from an ancient source, inspired expansion, or modern composition.
