# Internal Textual Argument in 2 Nephi 31

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 31 builds an explicit a fortiori argument: Christ, being holy, had the least apparent need of baptism, yet submitted to it, so people who are unholy have a still greater need to be baptized and follow him. Verses 7-10 develop the act as humility, obedience, entry onto 'the strait path,' and a pattern for discipleship.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 31:5 states: 'if the Lamb of God, he being holy, should have need to be baptized...O then, how much more need have we, being unholy.' Verses 7, 9, and 10 build on this: Christ's baptism as humility and obedience 'according to the flesh' (v.7), as setting 'the straitness of the path' (v.9), and as a direct call to 'follow' him (v.10).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Nephi reasons a fortiori: Christ, being holy, had the least apparent need of baptism, yet submitted to it; his submission therefore establishes an even greater, more obvious need for baptism among people who are unholy. Verses 7-10 unpack that inference as humility, obedience, example, and invitation.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage connects Christology, ritual obligation, and discipleship: baptism is presented not merely as a command but as participation in a pattern Christ himself modeled, despite having the least apparent need of it.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
The explicit 'how much more' marker is the chapter's logical hinge: because even the sinless Christ submitted to baptism to 'fulfil all righteousness,' those without his holiness have a still stronger reason to do the same. The following verses develop that inference by tying baptism to humility before the Father, obedience, the narrow path, and the command to follow Jesus.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
