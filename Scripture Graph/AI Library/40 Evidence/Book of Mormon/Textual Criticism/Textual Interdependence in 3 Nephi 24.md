# Textual Interdependence in 3 Nephi 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 24 reproduces Malachi 3 at chapter length with an explicit internal claim of attribution to Malachi, giving strong support (raised from 0.75 to 0.83 for consistency with the comparable bom-1nephi22-malachi4-dependence registry entry) to a direct literary/textual relationship. It establishes neither ancient Book of Mormon authorship nor modern fabrication, does not identify the transmission mechanism, and bears on inspiration not at all.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 24 reproduces the wording and order of Malachi 3 at chapter length, and the surrounding narrative explicitly names the source: 'the words which the Father had given unto Malachi.'
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The final text presents this chapter as a deliberate, explicitly attributed citation of the Malachi tradition rather than an independently composed prophecy.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Establishes a direct literary/textual relationship between 3 Nephi 24 and Malachi 3 at the level of the received English text; the explicit internal attribution is itself a feature of the narrative frame, not independent external corroboration.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read within the visit narrative, 3 Nephi 24 fits as Jesus explicitly commanding that 'the words which the Father had given unto Malachi' be written and expounded to the Nephites; on the ancient-record model this is a literal transmission of Malachi's prophecy to a New World audience, and on the translation/composition models it is the same KJV chapter reproduced and re-contextualized as revelatory dialogue. Either way it situates 3 Nephi 24 as intentionally, explicitly Malachi-dependent rather than an independent prophecy.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Steelmanned: chapter-length verbatim correspondence combined with explicit internal naming of the source prophet is among the clearest, least ambiguous cases of textual dependence in the corpus -- essentially ruling out coincidental convergence. This is at least as strong a dependence signal as the already-registered bom-1nephi22-malachi4-dependence (0.84), which covers a smaller two-image parallel from the same book; scoring this larger, explicitly-attributed chapter-length case at only 0.75 undersells it relative to that sibling and creates an inconsistency the registry should not carry (rule 10).

**Inspiration:** Nothing: a confirmed textual relationship does not by itself indicate whether the passage's content or transmission was divinely guided.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish transmission mechanism, date, ancient authorship, or which text has priority; the in-narrative attribution to Malachi is part of what needs explaining, not independent proof that an ancient Malachi citation actually occurred.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient citation of a received Malachi text rendered via KJV-influenced translation** — predicts: Chapter-length close correspondence with explicit attribution, matching what is observed. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Translation-level or revelatory harmonization to the KJV Malachi text** — predicts: Same pattern. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Direct 19th-century adaptation of the KJV Malachi text** — predicts: Same pattern, including the explicit attribution as an authorial device. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- KJV-mediated translation of an authentic ancient Malachi citation — *plausible*: Consistent with the text's own claim, though not independently verified by it
- Direct 19th-century adaptation of the KJV Malachi chapter — *plausible*: Fully sufficient to produce the observed correspondence and attribution
- A shared intermediary textual tradition distinct from the KJV — *ad hoc*: No evidence of a pre-KJV shared intermediary; the wording tracks the KJV translation specifically
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A Qumran-era text explicitly citing and reproducing a canonical prophet by name with matching wording would be treated by textual critics as strong, uncontroversial evidence of literary dependence; the same treatment applies here.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.83) for the proposition: *3 Nephi 24 stands in a direct literary/textual dependence relationship with the Malachi 3 tradition, rather than being independent composition.*

The chapter-length correspondence with Malachi 3, reinforced by explicit internal attribution, strongly supports a direct literary relationship, in line with the comparable bom-1nephi22-malachi4-dependence issue, but the parallel alone cannot determine whether that relationship arose through ancient citation, translation-level harmonization, or direct modern reuse.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Chapter-length, explicitly attributed correspondence of this kind is rare even within the Book of Mormon's own Isaiah/Malachi quotation pattern, making it one of the least ambiguous instances of the broader quoted-block phenomenon rather than a novel independent data point.

What would move this: Evidence that pre-Christian Nephite culture had independent access to a Malachi-tradition text distinct from the later Masoretic/KJV wording would raise this weight toward a genuinely ancient citation; evidence that the specific translation choices match idiosyncrasies unique to the 1769 KJV edition (rather than any hypothetical earlier Hebrew Vorlage) would strengthen the direct-modern-reuse alternative.
