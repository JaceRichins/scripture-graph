# TextualLiterary in 3 Nephi 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The near-verbatim overlap between 3 Nephi 9:15-18 and Johannine/KJV phrasing ('I am in the Father, and the Father in me'; 'I came unto my own, and my own received me not'; 'I am ... the light ... of the world'; 'Alpha and Omega') is evidence bearing on the literary/textual relationship between the Book of Mormon's English text and the King James Bible, but the evidence is compatible with more than one explanation and does not by itself establish either ancient origin or modern authorship.

**Does not establish:** This overlap does not by itself establish either the historicity of the Nephite Christophany or a conclusion that the text is solely a 19th-century composition; it is a data point requiring interpretation within a larger framework of one's view of the translation process.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Believing view: the same divine speaker (Christ) uses similar self-revelatory language across dispensations, and/or Joseph Smith's translation rendered the underlying text into familiar KJV idiom he already knew, without implying literary dependence on the Gospel of John's authorship.
- Critical view: the close verbal match to a specific KJV New Testament verse (John 1:11) is more economically explained by direct borrowing during the Book of Mormon's 19th-century composition/translation than by two independent ancient/modern speakers using identical English phrasing.
- Intermediate view: the KJV New Testament phrasing may reflect translation choices layered onto an underlying (non-English) source text with different but thematically similar wording.
