# Textual Intertextuality in 2 Nephi 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 6:6-7 and 6:18 correspond almost verbatim to Isaiah 49:22-23 and 49:26, strongly supporting the chapter's own claim to be citing Isaiah, while v.17 contains genuinely expanded wording not found in Isaiah 49 — so the relationship should not be described as uniformly verbatim. This reuses the registry's canonical weight for Book of Mormon Isaiah dependence rather than scoring the chapter independently. It does not establish the direction, mechanism, or antiquity of the underlying text. Scripture: [[2 Nephi 6#^2ne-6-4|2 Nephi 6:4]], [[2 Nephi 6#^2ne-6-6|2 Nephi 6:6]], [[2 Nephi 6#^2ne-6-7|2 Nephi 6:7]], [[2 Nephi 6#^2ne-6-16|2 Nephi 6:16]], [[2 Nephi 6#^2ne-6-17|2 Nephi 6:17]], [[2 Nephi 6#^2ne-6-18|2 Nephi 6:18]], [[Isaiah 49#^isa-49-22|Isaiah 49:22-26]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 6:6-7 and 6:18 correspond almost verbatim to Isaiah 49:22-23 and 49:26. 2 Nephi 6:17 inserts material not present in Isaiah 49 ("the Mighty God shall deliver his covenant people"), distinguishing this instance from a mechanically verbatim quotation throughout.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter presents itself explicitly as citing Isaiah (v.4) while also visibly interweaving Jacob's own explanatory or expanded wording into the cited material, a pattern consistent with either an ancient practice of quotation-with-commentary or an author/translator elaborating on a base KJV text.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This is another instance of the general Isaiah/KJV dependence pattern (`bom-isaiah-textual-dependence`, strong, 0.84), with the added observation that not all of it is mechanically verbatim — a genuinely useful textual-critical detail, but one that modifies the description of the same issue rather than creating an independent one.

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On any of the live models, the chapter functions as an explicit Isaiah citation with an identifiable expansion woven in; the historically interesting question of whether that expansion reflects ancient prophetic commentary, translation-level elaboration, or modern doctrinal insertion is not settled by the wording alone.

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports textual dependence on Isaiah 49 for the quoted portions, while the v.17 expansion shows the final text does not present pure verbatim transmission throughout — a detail that is compatible with ancient quotation-with-commentary, translation-level elaboration, or modern editorial expansion, and does not favor one over the others.

**Inspiration:** Nothing — the presence or absence of verbatim correspondence in a quotation-with-commentary passage says nothing by itself about inspiration.

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **ancient record quoting Isaiah with an ancient editor's (Jacob's) own interwoven commentary** — predicts: verbatim core quotation plus visibly distinguishable non-Isaiah material. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **KJV-mediated translation with revelatory/editorial expansion** — predicts: the same near-verbatim-plus-expansion pattern. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **nineteenth-century composition expanding on a KJV base text** — predicts: the same pattern, with expansions serving the author's theological purposes. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Direct literary reuse of the King James Isaiah text with deliberate doctrinal expansion — *plausible*: consistent with the text's demonstrated pattern of expanding Isaiah quotations elsewhere (e.g., 2 Nephi 12/Isaiah 2)
- Translation of a genuine ancient Isaiah quotation with Jacob's own ancient commentary interwoven — *plausible*: matches the chapter's own narrative framing (v.4) but is not independently verifiable
- Editorial/redactional harmonization during compilation — *possible*: cannot be distinguished from the other mechanisms on textual grounds alone
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
