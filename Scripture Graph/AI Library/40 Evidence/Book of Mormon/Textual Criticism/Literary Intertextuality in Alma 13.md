# Literary Intertextuality in Alma 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The shared Melchizedek cluster is strong evidence that Alma 13 is literarily or conceptually related to biblical Melchizedek traditions. It is weak evidence, considered alone, for any particular account of the Book of Mormon's historical origin.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not establish independent ancient transmission, Hebrew authorship, or the historicity of Alma's additional account of Melchizedek's people.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The parallels may arise through direct or indirect use of the King James Bible.
- They may reflect broadly available Jewish and Christian interpretation of Melchizedek.
- Alma's additional narrative may be interpretive expansion rather than preservation of an independent ancient source.
- Believing readers may understand the additions as preservation of traditions absent from the surviving Genesis account.
