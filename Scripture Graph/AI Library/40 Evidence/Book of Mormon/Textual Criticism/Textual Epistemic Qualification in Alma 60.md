# Textual Epistemic Qualification in Alma 60

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 60:18–19 presents Moroni's allegations of ambition, treason, and negligent complacency as possible explanations for the government's failure to send aid. His repeated "we know not but what" signals that the gravest charges are suspicions rather than confirmed knowledge, while their placement still exerts strong rhetorical pressure. This distinction helps readers separate Moroni's urgent inference from established narrative fact. **Scripture:** [[Alma 60#^alma-60-18|Alma 60:18]], [[Alma 60#^alma-60-19|Alma 60:19]]
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
In Alma 60:18 Moroni twice introduces serious accusations with "we know not but what": the authorities may be seeking power and may be traitors. Verse 19 then proposes neglect arising from security at the political center as another question rather than reporting a settled explanation.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Moroni regards corruption, treason, or complacent neglect as possible explanations for the missing support, but his wording marks them as suspicions inferred from circumstances rather than confirmed knowledge. The qualification can simultaneously acknowledge uncertainty and intensify rhetorical pressure.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage illuminates Moroni's limited information and the accusatory rhetoric of a wartime letter. It distinguishes what the character fears or infers from what the narrative has established, an important distinction when reading his subsequent charges.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
A plausible epistolary reconstruction is that Moroni, facing severe shortages and lacking an explanation, enumerates increasingly culpable possibilities to force a response from the government. The repeated qualification preserves his incomplete knowledge even as the accusations communicate urgency, frustration, and distrust.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
