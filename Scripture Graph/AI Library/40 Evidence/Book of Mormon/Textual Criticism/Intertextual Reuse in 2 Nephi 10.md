# Intertextual Reuse in 2 Nephi 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[2 Nephi 10#^2ne-10-9|2 Nephi 10:9]] closely reuses the distinctive nursing-father and nursing-mother language quoted in [[2 Nephi 6#^2ne-6-7|2 Nephi 6:7]] from [[Isaiah 49#^isa-49-23|Isaiah 49:23]]. Its position within Jacob's continuing Isaiah exposition strongly supports deliberate final-form continuity, but this derivative callback must not be counted independently from the underlying Isaiah-quotation phenomenon.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Second Nephi 10:9 describes Gentile kings as nursing fathers and queens as nursing mothers. The same distinctive pair appears in 2 Nephi 6:7 as part of an extended quotation corresponding closely to Isaiah 49:23, and 2 Nephi 10:9 appeals to what the Lord has spoken.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The distinctive paired titles and their location within Jacob's continuing Isaiah exposition strongly support deliberate final-form reuse rather than an accidental thematic resemblance.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage shows that 2 Nephi 10 is presented as an interpretation and application of Isaiah material introduced earlier in Jacob's discourse. This continuity can arise through ancient exposition, later editing, translation harmonization, or modern composition.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On an ancient-exposition reconstruction, Jacob quotes Isaiah 49 and later reapplies its royal-nursing imagery to the Gentiles' future role. On translation or modern-composition reconstructions, the received English discourse deliberately recalls its earlier KJV-shaped quotation to bind exposition and application together. In each reconstruction, 2 Nephi 10:9 functions as a concise callback to 2 Nephi 6:7.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The reuse strongly supports coherent final-form scriptural exposition. It provides almost no separate leverage for ancient authorship because ancient discourse, translation-level standardization, and modern biblical composition all predict deliberate reuse of an immediately preceding quotation.

**Inspiration:** Deliberate scriptural reuse implies nothing by itself about whether the discourse is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The reuse does not establish Nephi's or Jacob's access to a particular Hebrew manuscript, determine the compositional date, authenticate the discourse historically, or establish fulfillment of the prophecy.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient prophetic exposition** — predicts: A speaker who has quoted Isaiah later reuses its distinctive imagery while interpreting the prophecy.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Editorial or translation harmonization** — predicts: A later editor or translator standardizes the callback to the wording of the quoted Isaiah passage.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Nineteenth-century biblical composition** — predicts: An author familiar with Isaiah deliberately develops and recalls the quotation within Jacob's discourse.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent thematic restatement** — predicts: Generic reference to royal aid but not necessarily the distinctive nursing-father and nursing-mother pair.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jacob deliberately reapplies a scripture he quoted earlier in the discourse. — *independently supported*: The earlier quotation is present in 2 Nephi 6:7, the distinctive wording recurs, and 2 Nephi 10:9 explicitly appeals to what the Lord has spoken.
- An English translator or editor standardized the later allusion to the wording of Isaiah 49:23. — *plausible*: Translation-level harmonization readily produces identical biblical terminology while preserving a source-level thematic connection.
- A nineteenth-century author deliberately reused the KJV passage. — *plausible*: The KJV wording was available, and sustained biblical exposition naturally encourages internal verbal callbacks.
- The later wording independently restates a general idea about royal patrons. — *ad hoc*: It does not adequately explain the distinctive paired nursing-parent titles or their placement within the same continuing discourse.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
