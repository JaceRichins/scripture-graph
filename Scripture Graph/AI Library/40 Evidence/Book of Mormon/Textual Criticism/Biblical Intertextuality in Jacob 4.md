# Biblical Intertextuality in Jacob 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[Jacob 4#^jacob-4-15|Jacob 4:15]], [[Jacob 4#^jacob-4-16|Jacob 4:16]], and [[Jacob 4#^jacob-4-17|Jacob 4:17]] combine the rejected stone, sure foundation, building, and head-of-the-corner images represented in [[Isaiah 28#^isa-28-16|Isaiah 28:16]], [[Psalm 118#^ps-118-22|Psalm 118:22]], and the Christian application in [[Matthew 21#^matt-21-42|Matthew 21:42]]. The concentrated combination and the phrase “according to the scriptures” strongly support deliberate intertextuality. Ancient scriptural synthesis, Christianizing translation or revelation, inherited devotional tradition, and nineteenth-century KJV use remain viable mechanisms.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Jacob 4:15–17 describes a stone rejected by the Jews, a safe and sure foundation upon which they may build, and the rejected foundation becoming the head of the corner. Isaiah 28:16 joins stone, foundation, cornerstone, and sure foundation; Psalm 118:22 joins rejected building stone and head of the corner; Matthew 21:42 quotes the latter tradition and applies it to rejection.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Jacob 4 draws the Isaiah foundation complex and the Psalm 118 rejected-cornerstone complex into a unified argument. The explicit phrase “according to the scriptures” further supports intentional scriptural engagement rather than an accidental accumulation of common metaphors.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The passage participates in a well-attested biblical stone tradition. Its final Christian application is compatible with an ancient Israelite allusion developed through revelation, Christianizing translation or redaction, later theological synthesis, or direct nineteenth-century use of the KJV.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Jacob's argument treats Israel's rejected foundation as the very stone that must become the head of the corner, thereby joining Isaiah's secure Zion foundation to Psalm 118's rejected-but-vindicated stone. On an ancient reconstruction, Israelite stone traditions are combined and later rendered through Christian biblical diction; on translation, revelatory, editorial, or modern-composition reconstructions, the established Christian synthesis supplies the same linkage. The result is a compact scriptural argument about rejection, restoration, and the only secure foundation.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The convergence strongly supports deliberate scriptural intertextuality in the final text and strongly challenges an accidental-metaphor account. It provides little independent leverage between an ancient-source model with later Christian or KJV mediation and a nineteenth-century-composition model, both of which predict the combined imagery.

**Inspiration:** The intertextual relationship itself implies nothing about inspiration, since inspired discourse and ordinary literary composition can both interpret and combine earlier scripture.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The correspondence does not establish Jacob's date, access to a particular manuscript, direct dependence on Matthew rather than Psalm 118, an ancient Book of Mormon source, or the mechanism by which the biblical traditions entered the final English text.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient Israelite scriptural synthesis** — predicts: An ancient writer combines foundation and rejected-stone traditions available through Israelite scripture or related tradition.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Christianizing translation or redaction** — predicts: An ancient source is rendered or expanded through the Christian cornerstone interpretation and familiar KJV wording.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Revelatory synthesis** — predicts: Revelation intentionally unites the Israelite stone texts around Christ without ordinary historical access to later Christian writings.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century KJV-informed composition** — predicts: A modern author familiar with Isaiah, Psalms, and Matthew combines their established Christian stone imagery.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent metaphor without biblical engagement** — predicts: Building, rejection, foundation, and cornerstone language converge without deliberate use of the scriptural tradition.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient author combines Israelite stone traditions represented by Isaiah 28 and Psalm 118. — *possible*: The underlying images belong to the Hebrew Bible, although the date and textual availability of the precise traditions to the narrative speaker are not independently established here.
- A translator or editor renders an ancient stone allusion through the established Christian and KJV cornerstone vocabulary. — *plausible*: This explains both the older scriptural imagery and its distinctly Christian convergence without requiring the English wording to reproduce an ancient source verbatim.
- A nineteenth-century composer synthesizes familiar KJV passages. — *plausible*: All cited texts were readily available together in the KJV, and their Christian association was already conventional.
- The language reflects a general Christian cornerstone tradition transmitted indirectly through preaching or devotional language. — *plausible*: The Isaiah, Psalm, and Gospel texts had long circulated as an interpretive complex, so direct consultation of every cited passage is unnecessary.
- The convergence is accidental because stone and foundation are common metaphors. — *possible*: The individual images are common, but the rejected stone, sure foundation, building, and head-of-corner sequence, accompanied by “according to the scriptures,” makes coincidence a weak account of the whole cluster.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
