# Biblical Intertextuality in Jacob 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The convergence of rejected-stone, sure-foundation, and cornerstone language is strong evidence of scriptural intertextuality. It supports intentional participation in a recognizable biblical tradition, while leaving the direction and historical mechanism of dependence unresolved.

**Does not establish:** The intertext does not establish ancient authorship, a particular source manuscript, or whether the wording originated with Jacob, a translator, or a modern composer.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The English wording may reflect direct use of the King James Bible during modern composition.
- A translator may have rendered an ancient scriptural allusion in familiar King James language.
- Several related biblical stone texts may have been blended through later Christian interpretive tradition.
