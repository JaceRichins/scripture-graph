# Textual Dependence Or Shared Source in Moses 2

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The extensive verbal and sequential correspondence among Moses 2, Genesis 1, and Abraham 4 is strong evidence of a direct literary relationship among these canonical creation accounts. That observation alone cannot identify the direction of dependence, reconstruct the complete transmission history, or establish an ancient date for any particular wording.

**Does not establish:** The correspondence does not by itself establish direction of dependence, historical provenance, translation method, or ancient authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Moses 2 may directly revise or expand the Genesis text available to Joseph Smith.
- The accounts may preserve or adapt a shared earlier creation tradition.
- From a believing perspective, revelation may restore or clarify material while using familiar biblical language.
- Some distinctive wording may reflect nineteenth-century English revision rather than an independent ancient textual witness.
