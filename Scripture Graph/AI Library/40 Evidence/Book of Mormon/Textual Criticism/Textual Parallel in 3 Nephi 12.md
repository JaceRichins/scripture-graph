# Textual Parallel in 3 Nephi 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sustained sequence and close wording shared by [[3 Nephi 12#^3ne-12-3|3 Nephi 12:3–48]] and [[Matthew 5#^matt-5-3|Matthew 5:3–48]] strongly support Matthew/KJV mediation in the final English sermon. Historical repetition rendered in biblical language, translation or revelatory harmonization, and nineteenth-century adaptation each fit the correspondence, while source-independent composition does not fit it well. The parallel therefore establishes a relationship much more securely than it identifies the relationship's historical mechanism.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
From 3 Nephi 12:3 through verse 48, the sermon follows the sequence of Matthew 5 and repeatedly shares its wording. The opening supplied example closely reproduces Matthew 5:3 while adding “who come unto me.”
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The sustained ordered correspondence is best explained by a textual, translational, or traditional relationship with Matthew's Sermon on the Mount rather than by incidental similarity.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The received sermon belongs to the Matthean textual and interpretive stream. Its adaptations show that it is not merely an unaltered duplicate, but the final English wording alone cannot locate the relationship in the historical ministry of Jesus, an ancient record, the translation process, or nineteenth-century composition.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a historical-repetition reconstruction, the same teacher delivered substantially related instruction in two settings, with translation supplying familiar biblical English. On a harmonization or revelatory reconstruction, the received Matthew text shaped the expression of corresponding teachings. On a nineteenth-century-composition reconstruction, Matthew 5 served as the literary base and was adapted to the Nephite setting. The additions and contextual changes fit adaptation under all three reconstructions.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The evidence strongly supports Matthew/KJV mediation in the final English text and challenges a model in which the sermon was composed independently of the Matthean tradition. It does not materially favor ancient repetition over revelatory harmonization or modern adaptation because each serious model predicts extensive correspondence.

**Inspiration:** Literary or translation-level dependence on Matthew neither establishes nor negates inspiration.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
It does not establish that Jesus historically delivered these exact English-corresponding words in the Americas, that an ancient writer possessed Matthew's Gospel, that the underlying record used New Testament language, or that direct nineteenth-century copying was the mechanism.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Historical repetition with stable translation wording** — predicts: The sermon should preserve the sequence and substance of Jesus's earlier teaching while adapting details to a new audience and receiving familiar English phrasing.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Translation-level or revelatory harmonization** — predicts: Conceptually related material should be expressed in close Matthean and KJV language, including long ordered stretches.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Direct nineteenth-century adaptation** — predicts: The order and wording of Matthew 5 should be extensively retained while selected clauses are altered for Book of Mormon theology and setting.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent composition without Matthean mediation** — predicts: Shared ethical themes may occur, but sustained agreement in sequence and wording should be unlikely.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jesus repeated and adapted substantially the same teachings in an American setting. — *possible*: Repeated teaching is historically ordinary and is the narrative's own presentation, but it does not alone explain the close English wording without a translation component.
- The translation rendered corresponding ancient teachings in familiar KJV language. — *plausible*: This directly explains why an ancient-language discourse could appear in Matthean English while allowing contextual adaptations.
- A revelatory or editorial process deliberately harmonized the sermon with Matthew. — *possible*: It fits the final text, although this particular mechanism is not independently recoverable from the correspondence.
- A nineteenth-century author adapted Matthew 5 directly. — *plausible*: Access to the KJV and the retained sequence make this an economical explanation.
- The similarities arose from universal ethical teaching without textual or traditional dependence. — *ad hoc*: General ethical overlap cannot explain the long same-order verbal correspondence.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *The final English sermon material in 3 Nephi is textually mediated through or deliberately related to Matthew and its KJV form.*

The sustained sequence and close wording strongly support Matthew/KJV mediation of 3 Nephi 12's final English form, but do not distinguish historical repetition rendered biblically from revelatory harmonization or nineteenth-century adaptation.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Individual beatitudes and ethical sayings have substantial parallels across Jewish and Christian literature, but a long series retaining Matthew 5's order and close wording is much less expected independently. No quantitative matched-sermon corpus or complete search denominator is supplied, and the chapter belongs to the broader Matthew-dependent sermon block, so it should not be multiplied into numerous independent pieces of evidence.

What would move this: Assessment dated 2026-09-04.
