# Internal Textual Anthropology in Abraham 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Abraham 5:7 portrays the human being as a dust-formed body into which the Gods place "the man's spirit," followed by the breath of life and the emergence of a living soul. The verse therefore gives Latter-day Saint anthropology an unusually explicit narrative of embodiment while leaving the spirit's origin and the precise metaphysical relation among spirit, breath, body, and soul unstated. Scripture: [[Abraham 5#^abr-5-7|Abraham 5:7]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Abraham 5:7 says that the Gods formed the man's body from dust, took "the man's spirit" and put it into him, breathed into him the breath of life, and thereby made him a living soul.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The verse distinguishes the man's spirit from his dust-formed body and portrays mortal life as their ordered conjunction. Its wording also distinguishes the placement of the spirit from the subsequent breathing of the breath of life.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The verse expands the creation anthropology of Genesis 2:7 by making the man's spirit an explicit participant in embodiment. It supplies an important canonical basis for Latter-day Saint readings that distinguish spirit, body, breath, and the living embodied soul.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a straightforward narrative reconstruction, a distinguishable spirit exists at the point when the Gods place it into the formed body, and embodied life follows. This makes the verse naturally compatible with premortal-existence doctrine while locating its direct contribution more precisely in the transition from disembodied spirit to living embodied person.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
