# Intratextual Fulfillment And Verbal Callback in Helaman 13

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The later narrative explicitly reports that hidden treasures became slippery and could not be retained, closely matching Samuel's language about the curse on treasures. This functions as a clear intratextual fulfillment or authorial callback.

**Does not establish:** The textual correspondence does not by itself establish the historicity, physical mechanism, or supernatural cause of the reported loss.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The later passage may be a literary callback intentionally created by the author or editor rather than a report of an independently verified event.
- Slipperiness may describe theft, social disorder, or inability to secure property rather than a supernatural physical phenomenon.
- The language may combine literal loss with symbolic judgment.
