# Extended Textual Parallel in 2 Nephi 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 17 reproduces Isaiah 7 with the same near-verbatim, verse-by-verse KJV correspondence found throughout the Book of Mormon's Isaiah chapters — an essentially certain observation, not a discovery unique to this chapter. Because this is the same evidentiary cause already weighed for the Book of Mormon's Isaiah-dependence issue overall (strong, 0.84), this note should carry that weight rather than the near-certainty (0.99) proper only to the observation itself. It does not decide whether the KJV wording entered through an ancient Isaiah source rendered into familiar English, translation choices, or direct modern reuse of the Bible during dictation.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Verse-by-verse, near word-for-word correspondence across the whole chapter, including proper names, syntax, and KJV-specific phrasing, with only minor orthographic/punctuation differences (e.g. 'Rezin, king of Syria' vs 'Rezin the king of Syria'; 'for the treading' vs 'and for the treading').
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Best read as a direct textual relationship to a text essentially identical to the KJV, not independent redaction of a shared oral tradition.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Consistent with (a) an ancient source containing Isaiah material later rendered using the KJV at the translator's disposal, (b) direct copying/adapting of a printed KJV during dictation, or (c) some mixture. Not consistent with a translation process producing wording independent of the KJV.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports direct literary/textual dependence on the KJV Isaiah text, but this is the same evidentiary cause already established for the Book of Mormon's Isaiah-dependence issue generally (registry weight 0.84, strong) — this note is a sibling instance and should not be scored independently higher.

**Inspiration:** Nothing directly — dependence on KJV wording bears on compositional/translation history, not on whether the underlying content or process was inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish whether the underlying Nephite record actually contained an Isaiah quotation versus a modern insertion during dictation, and does not decide between tight-control, loose, or non-inspired-composition models.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Tight-control translation of an ancient Isaiah-bearing source** — predicts: verse-level KJV-style correspondence, since translation defaults to familiar biblical English for recognized material. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Loose/expansion translation without a fixed underlying text** — predicts: corresponding themes but freer wording, not verbatim KJV phrasing. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
- **1829 composition/dictation directly incorporating an open or memorized KJV** — predicts: exactly this degree of verbatim correspondence. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient Nephite source quoted an available version of Isaiah, later rendered in KJV-style English. — *possible*: The narrative explicitly presents the chapter as a written quotation, but neither the claimed ancient exemplar nor the proposed translation mechanism is independently attested.
- A revelatory or loose translation used the KJV as its English vehicle. — *plausible*: This explains both the source claim and the close English wording, although this particular mechanism is not independently demonstrated by the parallel.
- Joseph Smith directly used or closely imitated Isaiah 7 in the KJV. — *plausible*: KJV availability and corpus-wide KJV correspondences independently support this as a general mechanism, but direct use during this particular dictation segment is undocumented.
- The correspondence arose independently by coincidence. — *ad hoc*: Coincidence does not plausibly explain the chapter-length agreement in characters, sequence, speeches, imagery, and wording.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
If an ancient manuscript reproduced a KJV-tradition-adjacent Isaiah text this closely, scholars would call it a copy or member of the same recension, not evidence of independent composition; this note applies the same standard. Compared to the Bible's own duplicate-text notes (2 Kings 19/Isaiah 37 at 0.94-0.95), the 'shared source' side here is more contested because Joseph Smith's KJV access is independently documented in a way biblical intertextual duplication is not.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *The English Book of Mormon Isaiah chapters depend literarily on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition.*

The verse-by-verse KJV-level correspondence is essentially certain as an observation and strongly supports literary dependence on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition, but this is the same evidentiary fact already captured for every quoted Isaiah chapter and does not by itself decide whether that dependence entered through an ancient source, translation choices, or direct modern reuse of the Bible.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Long, near-verbatim quotation of an entire chapter is not a chance event; the relevant comparison is 'does this text quote or closely paraphrase Isaiah,' and by that measure correspondence is essentially total, so no matched-control denominator is needed to rule out coincidence.

What would move this: Weight for specifically Isaiah/KJV mediation would rise if complete collation found a dense, coherent set of KJV-distinctive renderings; it would fall if the alleged ordered and distinctive correspondence disappeared under collation or proved common in matched independent texts.
