# Extended Textual Parallel in 2 Nephi 17

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Second Nephi 17 follows Isaiah 7 in the same sustained sequence and near-verbatim King James wording, strongly supporting dependence on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition. This is another manifestation of the corpus-wide Isaiah relationship, not an independent strong datum to add to it. The parallel leaves translation-level KJV harmonization and direct nineteenth-century reuse as viable mechanisms alongside an underlying ancient Isaiah quotation.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Second Nephi 17 and Isaiah 7 preserve the same chapter-length sequence of characters, speeches, signs, threats, and images in near-verbatim King James English. The correspondence includes syntax and distinctive wording as well as proper names; the differences are minor, such as “Rezin, king of Syria” versus “Rezin the king of Syria” and “for the treading” versus “and for the treading.”
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The strongest critical reading is direct use or close adaptation of KJV Isaiah 7, which straightforwardly predicts the English wording. More generally, the sustained ordered match strongly supports literary dependence on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition; an ancient Isaiah quotation rendered or harmonized through KJV language remains viable, whereas independent composition from merely similar traditions does not.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The observation identifies the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition as the immediate ancestry or mediating source of the extant English chapter. It is compatible with an ancient Isaiah-bearing source translated into familiar biblical English, translation-level harmonization, or direct nineteenth-century reuse, but creates difficulty for any model predicting English wording independent of the KJV tradition.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
This is strong evidence for Isaiah/KJV textual mediation, not independent evidence for ancient Nephite authorship. Because the same dependence is already established by the Book of Mormon's extended Isaiah block, this chapter should inherit the canonical issue weight rather than be counted as an additional independent strong datum.

**Inspiration:** This textual relationship implies nothing by itself about whether the Book of Mormon or its translation process was inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The parallel does not determine whether the dependence entered through an ancient quotation, translation or revelatory harmonization, or direct modern adaptation. It also does not establish the historical existence of Nephi's plates or the inspiration of the translation.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Direct nineteenth-century KJV adaptation** — predicts: Chapter-length preservation of KJV sequence and wording, with occasional small copying, dictation, or editorial variants.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Ancient Isaiah quotation rendered or harmonized through the KJV** — predicts: An ancient source relationship at the content level and KJV-level correspondence introduced during English translation or revelation.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Loose translation without KJV harmonization** — predicts: The same Isaiah content but substantially freer English wording.. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
- **Independent composition from general prophetic tradition** — predicts: Some shared themes but not the same ordered chapter, characters, speeches, and near-verbatim English.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient Nephite source quoted a form of Isaiah 7 that was later rendered through KJV language. — *possible*: The narrative presents the material as a written quotation, but neither the proposed ancient exemplar nor the KJV-producing translation mechanism is independently established by this comparison.
- A revelatory or translation process harmonized an ancient quotation to the KJV. — *plausible*: This accounts for both the claimed ancient content and the extant English wording, although the parallel cannot independently verify the mechanism.
- Joseph Smith directly used, remembered, or closely imitated KJV Isaiah 7. — *plausible*: The KJV was available and the Book of Mormon contains an extended block of closely corresponding Isaiah material; this particular production event is not separately documented.
- The chapter-length correspondence arose independently by coincidence. — *ad hoc*: Chance does not plausibly explain the preserved sequence, characters, speeches, images, syntax, and wording.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A biblical or extrabiblical text reproducing an earlier chapter this closely would likewise be classified as dependent on, translated from, or descended from the same textual tradition. The same allowance for transmission and translation is applied here, while Joseph Smith's access to the KJV makes direct modern reuse a particularly well-supported rival mechanism.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *The English Book of Mormon Isaiah chapters depend literarily on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition.*

The chapter-length sequence and near-verbatim English strongly support dependence on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition, while leaving unresolved whether that dependence entered through translation of an ancient quotation, revelatory harmonization, or direct nineteenth-century reuse.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: The relevant candidate is an explicitly aligned, chapter-length unit rather than a short phrase located by searching a large corpus. Near-verbatim ordered correspondence across the entire chapter has a negligible coincidence rate. The remaining denominator question concerns which specific readings are distinctively KJV rather than shared broadly across the Isaiah textual tradition, not whether a literary relationship exists.

What would move this: The weight for specifically KJV mediation would rise if full collation showed a dense and coherent concentration of readings distinctive to the KJV against earlier Isaiah witnesses; it would fall if the apparent KJV-specific agreements disappeared and the overlap reduced to readings broadly inherited throughout the Isaiah tradition. Evidence about the production mechanism could shift weight among origin models without changing the underlying dependence finding.

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
KJV-level agreement is readily explained under a modern-composition reconstruction in which Isaiah 7 was copied, recited, or closely adapted. Under an ancient-record reconstruction, Nephi may have quoted an Isaiah text while the English production process supplied the familiar KJV rendering. Both reconstructions fit the observed English dependence; they locate that dependence at different stages.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
