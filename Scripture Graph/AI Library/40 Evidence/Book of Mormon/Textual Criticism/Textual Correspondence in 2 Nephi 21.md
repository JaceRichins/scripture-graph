# Textual Correspondence in 2 Nephi 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The extensive correspondence between [[2 Nephi 21#^2ne-21-1|2 Nephi 21:1–16]] and [[Isaiah 11#^isa-11-1|Isaiah 11:1–16]] is strong evidence of dependence on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition. The sustained order and wording are much more specific than shared messianic or restoration imagery. Ancient-source translation, revelatory harmonization, and nineteenth-century adaptation remain viable mechanisms.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 21 corresponds extensively and continuously to Isaiah 11, including the distinctive opening ('there shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a branch shall grow out of his roots') carried through the full chapter with only minor capitalization-level differences.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The extent and continuity of the match indicate a direct textual relationship to the specific KJV rendering of Isaiah 11, not an independently arrived-at translation of the same Hebrew content.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Establishes a direct literary relationship to the KJV Isaiah tradition; it does not by itself determine whether that relationship arose through ancient-source translation rendered in Bible English, direct KJV dependence, or some combination.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
This fits the same broader, well-documented pattern as 2 Nephi 20: the Book of Mormon's quoted Isaiah material tracks KJV wording closely, including features (like italics) traceable to the printed KJV text. Under the leading reconstructions, 2 Nephi 21's correspondence to Isaiah 11 is one further instance of that pattern, best explained by either a Bible-English-saturated translation process or direct KJV consultation for the Isaiah blocks, rather than a separate, independently weighted data point.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports KJV-mediated textual dependence as a fact about the final English text. As with 2 Nephi 20, this is the same evidence class as the general Isaiah-dependence pattern already registered and should not be counted as separate corroborating weight.

**Inspiration:** Nothing -- the textual match to KJV Isaiah does not by itself bear on inspiration; that question depends on the unresolved translation mechanism.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the direction or mechanism of dependence, the composition date, the precise underlying Hebrew text, or the Book of Mormon's ancient authorship.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Direct copying from, or heavy reliance on, a physically present KJV Bible** — predicts: Near word-for-word correspondence including KJV-specific translation choices. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Tight ancient-source translation rendered in period Bible-English idiom** — predicts: Close but not necessarily identical correspondence. Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
- **Independent ancient transmission with no KJV contact** — predicts: Agreement with Isaiah's content but not with KJV's specific English phrasing. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient Isaiah text on the brass plates was rendered in period Bible English during translation — *plausible*: Consistent with the Book of Mormon's general reliance on KJV idiom outside the Isaiah chapters as well
- The translator or author directly incorporated a physical KJV Bible for this chapter — *independently supported*: Documented textual-critical evidence (e.g., KJV italics reproduced in Book of Mormon Isaiah blocks) supports direct textual contact in at least some Isaiah chapters
- Coincidental convergence without contact — *ad hoc*: Implausible given the chapter-length precision of the match
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
Treated the same as any two texts sharing extensive, precise wording in comparative literature: strong evidence of a literary relationship, silent on direction or mechanism.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *The Book of Mormon's Isaiah chapters depend on or descend from the Isaiah textual tradition represented in the King James Bible.*

The extensive, continuous correspondence between 2 Nephi 21 and Isaiah 11 strongly supports KJV-mediated textual dependence in the final English text, but as an instance of the same registered Isaiah-dependence evidence class it carries the canonical weight rather than an independently near-certain one.
