# Textual Dependence Or Allusion in 2 Nephi 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 9:50 is nearly verbatim to Isaiah 55:1, and v.51 loosely echoes 55:2 before shifting to distinctively Nephite phrasing, giving strong (0.68, not 0.9) support to a literary/translation-level relationship with the Isaiah 55 tradition. Because this belongs to the same general Isaiah/KJV-mediation phenomenon already scored elsewhere in the registry at comparable levels, it should not be counted as independent standout evidence. It does not establish translation mechanism, date, ancient authorship, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 9:50 is nearly verbatim to Isaiah 55:1 ('come...thirst...waters...no money...buy...wine and milk without money and without price'), and v.51 loosely parallels Isaiah 55:2 ('spend money for that which is of no worth'/'not bread'; 'labour/labor for that which cannot satisfy/satisfieth not'; 'hearken diligently unto me'), before diverging into distinctively Nephite phrasing ('Holy One of Israel', 'perisheth not, neither can be corrupted').
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The speaker closes a sermon with the same 'come, buy without price' invitation Isaiah used, adapting it partway through with Nephite theological vocabulary rather than continuing a strict quotation.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Establishes a literary/textual relationship between the received English text of 2 Nephi 9 and the Isaiah 55 tradition as mediated through the KJV; compatible with several models of how that relationship arose.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read against Isaiah 55, 2 Nephi 9:50-51 fits as Jacob closing his sermon with the same 'come, buy without price' invitation Isaiah gave, universalizing salvation ('every one') with an already-recognizable prophetic image; on the ancient-record model this is an authentic citation woven into a new sermon, on the translation/composition models it is the same rhetorical move performed at the level of the English text.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Steelmanned: v.50's near-verbatim match to Isaiah 55:1 is about as tight as a two-verse echo gets outside the large embedded Isaiah blocks, and the shift to looser paraphrase in v.51 shows the passage working from Isaiah 55 rather than simply reproducing it, which is what deliberate reuse (as opposed to accidental phrasing overlap) predicts. This is real support for dependence on the Isaiah 55 tradition specifically. But the current 0.9 rating overstates it: this is one more instance of the same general Isaiah/KJV-mediation phenomenon already carrying its own registry entries at 0.65-0.72 for comparably sized two/three-verse echoes (2 Nephi 1/Isaiah 52, 2 Nephi 26/Isaiah 29), and scoring this one at 0.9 double-counts the same underlying cause under rule 13.

**Inspiration:** Nothing: a literary/textual-dependence finding does not bear on whether the passage is divinely inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish translation mechanism, date of composition, whether an early-sixth-century-BCE figure had access to material later attributed to a Deutero-Isaiah tradition, or ancient authorship generally. Does not add independent weight beyond the broader, already-registered Isaiah/KJV-dependence pattern.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Tight-control ancient translation incorporating a known Isaiah citation** — predicts: Close but not necessarily verbatim English rendering of a real ancient quotation. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Loose/revelatory translation drawing on familiar KJV idiom** — predicts: Partial verbatim match blended with paraphrase and added theological vocabulary. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **19th-century composition using the KJV as literary source** — predicts: The same partial-verbatim-plus-paraphrase pattern from an author quoting and adapting familiar KJV language. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- KJV-mediated translation of an authentic ancient citation of Isaiah 55 — *plausible*: Consistent with the text's self-presentation and with translation practice elsewhere in the corpus
- Direct 19th-century literary reuse of the KJV Isaiah 55 text — *plausible*: Fully sufficient to produce the observed pattern without positing any ancient source
- Independent common source producing similar wording without dependence on the KJV translation specifically — *ad hoc*: The verbatim overlap tracks specific KJV translation choices ('without money and without price'), which an independent rendering would be unlikely to reproduce by chance
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A New Testament epistle closely echoing an earlier prophetic passage would be called ordinary intertextual dependence without doubting authenticity by default; the same non-alarmed treatment applies here.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.8) for the proposition: *The final English text of 2 Nephi 9:50-51 deliberately reuses or is textually mediated by Isaiah 55:1-2, rather than reflecting independent generic exhortation language.*

The near-verbatim overlap between 2 Nephi 9:50 and Isaiah 55:1, continuing more loosely into v.51, strongly supports a literary or translation-level relationship with the Isaiah 55 tradition, but this is one more instance of the broader, already-registered Isaiah/KJV-mediation pattern and does not itself discriminate among ancient citation, translation-level harmonization, or nineteenth-century reuse.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Near-verbatim two-verse matches at this density are uncommon outside the marked large-block Isaiah quotations, but dozens of shorter Isaianic echoes already exist through the Book of Mormon and are separately catalogued; this is one instance of a broader pattern, not an independent data point.

What would move this: A pre-1830, non-KJV-derived ancient witness sharing this exact 'money/buy/wine and milk' cluster would raise the weight toward a source-specific ancient claim; evidence that 'buy wine and milk without money and without price' was common stock idiom independent of Isaiah's influence would lower confidence in a targeted allusion.
