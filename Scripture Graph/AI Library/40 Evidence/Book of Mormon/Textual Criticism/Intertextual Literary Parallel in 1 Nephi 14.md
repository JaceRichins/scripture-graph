# Intertextual Literary Parallel in 1 Nephi 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[1 Nephi 14#^1ne-14-11|1 Nephi 14:11]] and [[Revelation 17#^rev-17-1|Revelation 17:1]] share the concentrated image of a whore sitting upon many waters, moderately supporting a deliberate Revelation-linked echo in the final text. Jeremiah's older Babylon-and-waters idiom and the Book of Mormon's broader KJV diction leave open whether this arose through direct adaptation, shared apocalyptic tradition, or translation-level harmonization.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
1 Nephi 14:11 describes "the whore of all the earth" who "sat upon many waters." Revelation 17:1 introduces "the great whore that sitteth upon many waters." Both passages use the unusual combination of a whore, enthronement or sitting, many waters, and worldwide political scope.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The combined wording and imagery make a deliberate final-form echo of Revelation 17 more likely than accidental verbal overlap. The wider narrative's association of Nephi's vision with a future writer named John reinforces a Revelation-oriented reading, although the cited verse alone does not specify the mechanism of relationship.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The parallel locates 1 Nephi 14 within the biblical-apocalyptic imagery used for corrupt imperial or religious power. Jeremiah 51:13 supplies an older Babylon-and-many-waters background, while Revelation combines that background with the personified whore.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Revelation 17 fuses the older biblical image of Babylon beside many waters with the personification of a corrupt woman exercising dominion. First Nephi 14 uses the same combined image for a worldwide corrupt power and later directs attention to John's account of related visionary material. The strongest reconstruction is therefore a deliberate final-form association with Johannine apocalypse; ancient shared imagery, translation harmonization, and modern KJV adaptation remain viable accounts of how that association entered the text.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The cluster moderately supports a specific Revelation-linked intertext in the final English text. It does not favor ancient provenance over loose translation or nineteenth-century composition, because each can produce the same Revelation-shaped wording.

**Inspiration:** The intertextual relationship itself implies nothing about inspiration; inspired translation, literary adaptation, and ordinary composition can all employ earlier scriptural language.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The parallel does not identify the direction or stage of mediation, demonstrate that an underlying ancient Nephite text quoted Revelation, identify the corrupt power historically, or independently support antiquity, foreknowledge, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Both passages independently inherit the Babylon-and-many-waters idiom represented in Jeremiah 51:13. — *plausible*: The waters motif predates Revelation, but Jeremiah lacks the complete whore-sitting-on-waters lexical cluster.
- A translator rendered related but nonverbal ancient imagery in familiar KJV Revelation diction. — *plausible*: KJV-like phrasing is widespread in the Book of Mormon and can account for close English wording without underlying-language quotation.
- A nineteenth-century composer deliberately adapted Revelation 17. — *plausible*: The KJV was available, and this model directly predicts both the wording and the surrounding Johannine associations.
- The complete lexical cluster arose accidentally. — *ad hoc*: Coincidence is less satisfactory than shared tradition or mediation given the several linked features.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
