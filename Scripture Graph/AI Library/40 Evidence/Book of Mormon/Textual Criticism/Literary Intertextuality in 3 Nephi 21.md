# Literary Intertextuality in 3 Nephi 21

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 21 combines Isaiah 52 restoration imagery with Micah 5 judgment imagery in matching sequence, which strongly supports deliberate textual dependence on both KJV chapters in the final English form. The Isaiah portion is a dependent instance of the corpus-wide Isaiah/KJV finding and should not be added to it separately, and neither the combination's specificity nor its popularity in 19th-century rhetoric decides ancient versus modern origin.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 21 reuses Isaiah 52 language concerning astonished kings and a marred servant in verses 8 and 10, then follows the sequence and imagery of Micah 5:8-14 in verses 12-18: Jacob's remnant among the Gentiles, the lion comparison, the lifted hand, and the removal of horses, chariots, cities, strongholds, witchcrafts, graven images, standing images, and groves.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The ordered clusters provide an intentional scriptural framework for the chapter's restoration and gathering scenario. Isaiah contributes servant and recognition imagery, while Micah supplies the remnant oracle and the sequence of judgment and purification.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The chapter illustrates the recombination of inherited prophetic texts into a new eschatological exposition. Its restoration scenario develops through recognizable scriptural sequences rather than as a free-standing prediction.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a final-form literary reconstruction, 3 Nephi 21 joins texts by theme while preserving enough of their sequence to guide recognition: Isaiah's servant language introduces divine action, and Micah's remnant oracle describes its consequences for Gentile power, warfare, divination, and idolatry. The resulting discourse coordinates servant, remnant, judgment, and purification imagery.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->
