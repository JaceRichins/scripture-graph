# Textual Interrelationship in Ether 12

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Ether 12:4 and 12:6 closely correspond to [[Hebrews 6#^heb-6-19|Hebrews 6:19]] and [[Hebrews 11#^heb-11-1|Hebrews 11:1]], while [[Ether 12#^ether-12-32|Ether 12:32]] and [[Ether 12#^ether-12-33|Ether 12:33]] gather language associated with [[John 14#^john-14-2|John 14:2]], [[John 14#^john-14-3|John 14:3]], [[John 10#^john-10-17|John 10:17]], and [[John 10#^john-10-18|John 10:18]], within Moroni's discourse on faith and hope rather than a single prayer. The concentration of distinctive wording strongly supports deliberate New Testament or KJV-shaped mediation in the final English text, at the lower end of the strong band given the lack of matched-corpus controls and the dispersed placement of the correspondences. Translation-level harmonization, revelatory restatement, indirect Christian reception, and nineteenth-century composition remain viable mechanisms, so the relationship does not independently determine date, historicity, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Ether 12 contains several concentrated verbal correspondences with New Testament passages within Moroni's discourse on faith and hope (not a single prayer; vv.4 and 6 are general exposition, while later verses including 32-33 address the Lord directly). Ether 12:4 joins hope, an anchor of the soul, and the paired adjectives 'sure' and 'steadfast,' closely paralleling Hebrews 6:19. Ether 12:6 defines faith through 'things which are hoped for and not seen,' closely paralleling Hebrews 11:1. Ether 12:32 uses the John 14 complex of a prepared place, the Father's mansions, and an inheritance there; verse 33 recalls John 10:17-18 through the ordered actions of laying down one's life and taking it again.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The density and specificity of the correspondences, especially the hope-anchor-sure-steadfast cluster and the paired definitions of faith, are better explained by deliberate New Testament or KJV-shaped mediation than by coincidence or generic Christian vocabulary alone. The observation concerns the final English form, dispersed across roughly thirty verses rather than one tight passage; it does not by itself identify when or how that mediation occurred.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Ether 12's English discourse belongs recognizably to the reception history of New Testament language. This is compatible with an ancient discourse translated or harmonized into familiar biblical English, a revelatory restatement in that idiom, or direct nineteenth-century literary use of the King James Bible.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Direct or indirect nineteenth-century reuse of the King James Bible — *plausible*: The KJV was readily available and its idiom permeated contemporary religious discourse; this is a demonstrated general mechanism elsewhere in the corpus, but is not independently established as THE mechanism for this specific cluster over the alternatives.
- KJV-shaped translation or translation-level harmonization of ancient material — *plausible*: Explains why the final English text resembles Hebrews and John without requiring the same English wording, or the same textual dependence, in an underlying ancient record; not independently evidenced beyond the stated translation framework.
- Revelatory restatement of related teachings through familiar biblical language — *possible*: Compatible with a revelatory model, though the verbal correspondences alone cannot independently verify that mechanism.
- Indirect reception through sermons and general Christian discourse rather than consultation of the biblical passages — *plausible*: Portable KJV expressions circulated beyond direct Bible reading and could account for some wording while still constituting New Testament reception.
- Independent expression of shared religious concepts — *possible*: Faith, hope, heavenly dwellings, and voluntary sacrifice are broad Christian themes, but independent expression explains the distinctive hope-anchor-sure-steadfast and hoped-for-not-seen wording less successfully.
- A lost pre-New Testament source independently used by both Ether 12 and the New Testament writings — *ad hoc*: No such source is identified, and positing one specifically to explain several correspondences across Hebrews and John adds unsupported assumptions.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.72) for the proposition: *Ether 12's final English wording deliberately participates in or is mediated through the New Testament textual tradition represented by Hebrews 6:19, Hebrews 11:1, John 10:17-18, and John 14:2-3.*

The concentration of distinctive Hebrews- and John-like wording strongly supports deliberate New Testament or KJV-shaped mediation in Ether 12's final English form, at the lower end of the strong band given the missing matched-corpus denominator and dispersed placement, while providing little independent leverage for choosing between translation-level harmonization, revelatory restatement, and nineteenth-century composition.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Religious concepts such as faith, hope, heavenly dwellings, and voluntary sacrifice are common in Christian literature, so conceptual resemblance alone has a high base rate. The hope-anchor-sure-steadfast cluster and the hoped-for/not-seen definition are substantially more distinctive, and their concentration with Johannine wording in one chapter lowers the chance-coincidence explanation. No matched nineteenth-century control corpus, pre-registered phrase criteria, or complete search denominator has been supplied, and the four correspondences are dispersed across roughly thirty verses rather than tightly clustered, which together prevent a higher assessment.

What would move this: The weight would increase if a pre-registered phrase-level comparison found these ordered, uncommon clusters far more often in Ether 12 than in matched non-biblical religious prose, especially if KJV-specific readings or syntax were identified. It would decrease if matched controls showed that equivalent clusters commonly arise without textual dependence, or if the relevant wording were documented as widespread independent English religious idiom rather than specifically associated with Hebrews and John.
