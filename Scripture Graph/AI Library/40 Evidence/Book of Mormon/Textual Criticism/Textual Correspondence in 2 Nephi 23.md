# Textual Correspondence in 2 Nephi 23

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter-wide correspondence between [[2 Nephi 23#^2ne-23-1|2 Nephi 23]] and [[Isaiah 13#^isa-13-1|Isaiah 13]] is strong evidence of dependence on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition. The shared attribution, sequence, and wording are much more specific than generic Babylon-judgment imagery, while the localized additions show adaptation rather than mechanical identity. The correspondence does not distinguish ancient-source translation, revelatory harmonization, or direct nineteenth-century reuse.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Second Nephi 23 corresponds throughout to Isaiah 13, beginning with the same attribution and continuing in the same oracle sequence with close English wording. Second Nephi 23:22 also contains additional concluding material not present in Isaiah 13:22.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
Second Nephi 23 presents and adapts the Isaiah 13 oracle. The chapter-wide order and verbal correspondence strongly indicate mediation through the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition rather than independent development from generic Babylon-judgment themes.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The correspondence places 2 Nephi 23 within the textual transmission and reception of Isaiah 13. It fits an ancient-source model rendered in KJV-shaped English, translation-level or revelatory harmonization, and direct nineteenth-century adaptation.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Under an ancient-source reconstruction, an Isaianic oracle corresponding to Isaiah 13 was preserved in the source record and translated through familiar biblical diction. Under revelatory or loose-translation reconstruction, the received Bible supplied the English vehicle while the Book of Mormon conclusion was adapted or expanded. Under nineteenth-century composition, direct reuse of KJV Isaiah naturally accounts for the sequence, wording, and localized additions.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The chapter strongly supports Isaiah/KJV textual dependence in the final English text but provides little discrimination among the principal mechanisms. It is one dependent instance of the registered Book of Mormon Isaiah phenomenon, not independent cumulative evidence.

**Inspiration:** Literary dependence alone says nothing about inspiration because inspired translation, revelatory reuse, and ordinary literary adaptation can each employ an earlier text.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The correspondence does not determine the historical direction or mechanism of dependence, identify the precise underlying-language recension, date Isaiah 13 or the Book of Mormon framing material, or establish ancient authorship or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Ancient Isaianic source with KJV-shaped translation** — predicts: Preservation of Isaiah 13's sequence and content with close KJV English and localized variants.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Loose translation or revelatory harmonization** — predicts: Isaianic material expressed or standardized through familiar biblical English, with possible expansion.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century KJV-based composition** — predicts: Close chapter-wide wording and order accompanied by selective adaptation.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent composition without Isaiah dependence** — predicts: Broad thematic similarity without sustained sequential and verbal correspondence.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- An ancient copy of the Isaiah 13 oracle was transmitted on the plates and rendered in KJV-like English. — *possible*: This explains the shared content while locating the close English wording at translation, but the correspondence does not independently attest the source record.
- A loose or revelatory translation harmonized the material with the familiar KJV. — *plausible*: This mechanism readily predicts extensive English agreement together with localized additions.
- The chapter directly adapts KJV Isaiah in a nineteenth-century compositional setting. — *plausible*: Direct literary access straightforwardly predicts the preserved wording, sequence, and selective expansion.
- The correspondence arose independently from generic Babylonian judgment traditions. — *ad hoc*: Generic traditions do not adequately explain the shared attribution and continuous agreement in sequence and wording.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->
