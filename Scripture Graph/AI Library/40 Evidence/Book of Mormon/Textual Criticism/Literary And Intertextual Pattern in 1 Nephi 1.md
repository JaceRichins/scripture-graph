# Literary And Intertextual Pattern in 1 Nephi 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Lehi's account has several features of biblical prophetic-call and commission narratives: divine glory, an opened-heaven throne scene, a written heavenly message, proclamation of judgment, and resistance from the audience. Isaiah 6 and Ezekiel 1-3 provide particularly relevant comparisons. These correspondences strongly support reading the episode as deliberately framed in biblical prophetic terms, but they provide little leverage for determining when or how the account was composed.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not establish direct borrowing, a particular ancient source, historical occurrence, or ancient authorship of the Book of Mormon.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The similarities may arise from a broadly shared prophetic type-scene rather than dependence on any one biblical passage.
- A modern author familiar with the Bible could deliberately imitate the same forms.
- Some elements, such as visions, books, judgment, and rejection, are common enough that individual correspondences have limited diagnostic value.
