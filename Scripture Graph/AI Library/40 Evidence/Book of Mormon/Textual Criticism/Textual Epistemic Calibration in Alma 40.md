# Textual Epistemic Calibration in Alma 40

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 40 strongly evidences a deliberately constructed, epistemically careful prophetic persona distinguishing revelation, mystery, immaterial detail, and opinion (strong, 0.78), matching the registry's editorial-persona pattern; this construction gives only none-band support for the angelic disclosure's actual occurrence or the doctrine's historical truth.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Alma 40 reports diligent inquiry and angelic disclosure about the state of souls between death and resurrection (v.11), explicitly reserves the timing and number of resurrections to God's exclusive knowledge (vv.3-5,8), treats that number as immaterial to his point, and expressly labels his view on when the righteous are resurrected as his own opinion rather than revealed fact (vv.20-21).
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The discourse differentiates what its speaker claims to know by revelation, what he concedes is unknown, what he considers unimportant, and what he offers only as inference, constructing a persona of careful, non-overclaiming religious authority.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This illuminates Alma's teaching style and the text's approach to distinguishing revealed doctrine from speculation; it documents a rhetorical/theological feature of the discourse rather than corroborating any external historical event.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Read as a piece of reflective religious teaching, Alma's repeated hedging functions as a familiar didactic device: a teacher flags which claims rest on reported revelation, which remain genuinely unknown, and which are the teacher's own reasoned opinion, so hearers can weigh each claim appropriately. This kind of graded epistemic language appears in other reflective religious teaching that separates revealed content from human inference, and Alma 40 reads naturally as an instance of that pattern.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Comparable to the registry's editorial-persona findings: a sophisticated, self-aware narrative voice is sometimes cited as evidence of authentic ancient authorship and sometimes as exactly what a skilled writer of any era constructs; this note credits the persona construction as well-evidenced without letting it discriminate between those origins.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish that the angelic disclosure occurred, that Alma existed, that the discourse is ancient, or that the doctrine taught is true.
