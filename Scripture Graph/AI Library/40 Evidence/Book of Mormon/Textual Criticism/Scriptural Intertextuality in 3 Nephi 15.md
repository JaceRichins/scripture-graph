# Scriptural Intertextuality in 3 Nephi 15

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
[[3 Nephi 15#^3ne-15-17|3 Nephi 15:17]] repeats [[John 10#^john-10-16|John 10:16]] in essentially the same wording, and [[3 Nephi 15#^3ne-15-21|3 Nephi 15:21–24]] then identifies the other sheep and explains the earlier audience's misunderstanding. This provides exceptionally strong evidence that the final text deliberately engages John 10:16 as an interpretive problem. Historical repetition, translation harmonization, modern revelation, and direct literary adaptation remain viable mechanisms, and the textual relationship is separate from the much weaker historical claim that the Nephites were actually John's referent.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
John 10:16 and 3 Nephi 15:17 share the other-sheep saying in essentially the same English wording. In 3 Nephi 15:21–24, Jesus repeats the saying, identifies the immediate hearers as those other sheep, and explains why the Jerusalem audience did not understand the earlier statement. The relationship is explicit and interpretive rather than an inference from scattered motifs.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The final text unmistakably treats John 10:16 as an interpretive problem and supplies an identification of the other sheep. This is stronger than a merely verbal echo because the surrounding discourse quotes, identifies, and explains the saying.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
The explicit engagement fits a historical-repetition model, an ancient discourse translated or harmonized through John, modern revelation expanding John, and direct nineteenth-century interpretation. It securely establishes final-form engagement with the received Johannine saying but does not determine when or how the explanatory discourse originated.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
John 10:16 leaves the other sheep unidentified within the saying itself. Third Nephi 15 builds a sustained interpretation around that opening: it repeats the wording, names the Nephite hearers as its referent, and explains the Jerusalem disciples' misunderstanding. On a historical-repetition reconstruction, this is Jesus clarifying his earlier teaching in another setting; on translation, revelatory-expansion, or modern-composition reconstructions, it is an explicit reception and interpretation of the canonical Johannine text.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
This is exceptionally strong evidence for deliberate John-related intertextuality, not for the historical truth of the Nephite identification. The separate registry issue concerning whether the Nephites were historically Jesus's other sheep remains none-band because an internal identification is not independent corroboration.

**Inspiration:** The deliberate interpretation of John 10:16 implies nothing by itself about whether 3 Nephi's explanation is inspired or historically revealed.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The textual engagement does not establish that Jesus historically delivered the reported explanation, that the Nephites were the historical referent of John 10:16, that John preserves the prior saying verbatim, or that the Book of Mormon is ancient.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Historical repetition by Jesus** — predicts: Jesus may repeat the same saying in another setting and identify its intended referents.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Ancient discourse with Johannine translation** — predicts: An ancient explanation may be rendered through the exact familiar wording of John 10:16.. Fit with this evidence: *not ruled out*.
- **Translation-level harmonization** — predicts: A translator or revelatory process will quote the KJV form to make the cross-reference explicit.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Modern revelation interpreting John** — predicts: A revelation may explicitly quote a canonical saying and disclose an additional interpretation.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Nineteenth-century literary interpretation** — predicts: A modern writer familiar with John could quote the saying and construct an explanatory discourse around it.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Coincidental verbal resemblance** — predicts: No explicit quotation, attribution, identification, and explanation sequence.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Jesus historically repeated and explained his earlier saying to another audience. — *possible*: The narrative explicitly presents this mechanism, although the narrative claim is not independently corroborated.
- An ancient discourse was translated or harmonized using the received English wording of John 10:16. — *possible*: A loose or revelatory translation model permits this, but the passage does not independently locate the wording at the translation stage.
- The passage is modern revelation that deliberately interprets John 10:16. — *possible*: This directly predicts the quotation and explanatory expansion without treating the text as ordinary composition.
- A nineteenth-century author directly adapted and interpreted John 10:16. — *plausible*: The exact KJV saying was available and the explanatory discourse addresses an evident interpretive opening.
- The correspondence is coincidental or merely reflects generic shepherd imagery. — *ad hoc*: It cannot adequately explain the essentially verbatim quotation and explicit explanation of what the prior audience understood.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.96) for the proposition: *The final text of 3 Nephi 15 deliberately quotes and interprets the other-sheep saying preserved in John 10:16.*

The verbatim saying, explicit identification, and explanation of its earlier misunderstanding provide exceptionally strong evidence that 3 Nephi 15 deliberately engages John 10:16, while supplying almost no independent evidence that the reported explanation occurred historically.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Sheep and shepherd imagery is common in Christian literature, but essentially verbatim quotation combined with explicit retrospective attribution, referent identification, and explanation of misunderstanding is rare outside deliberate engagement. The original note did not report a formal search denominator, but here the text itself identifies the saying, greatly reducing the look-elsewhere concern.

What would move this: Assessment dated 2026-09-04. The intertextual weight is already near its practical ceiling and would rise only marginally with early manuscript evidence explicitly marking John as the cited text; it would fall if the earliest text lacked the quotation-and-explanation sequence or if a demonstrably independent source predating John contained the same wording and interpretive structure.
