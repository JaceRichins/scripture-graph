# Textual Interrelationship in 3 Nephi 9

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
3 Nephi 9:16-17 reproduces the clause structure and most of the vocabulary of John 1:11-12, recast from third to first person at the moment the risen Christ applies a rejection-and-sonship formula to himself. This strongly supports a direct textual relationship between the two passages in their transmitted English form, but does not by itself determine whether that relationship arose through KJV-mediated translation, revelatory harmonization, or nineteenth-century composition, nor does it bear on historicity. Scripture: [[3 Nephi 9#^3ne-9-16|3 Nephi 9:16]], [[3 Nephi 9#^3ne-9-17|3 Nephi 9:17]], [[John 1#^john-1-11|John 1:11]], [[John 1#^john-1-12|John 1:12]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
3 Nephi 9:16, "I came unto my own, and my own received me not," differs from John 1:11, "He came unto his own, and his own received him not," only in grammatical person. 3 Nephi 9:17, "as many as have received me, to them have I given to become the sons of God ... believe on my name," shares clause order and most vocabulary with John 1:12, "as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name," again recast from third to first person.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The passage takes John's narratorial summary of the Word's rejection and adapts it into the risen Christ's own first-person self-declaration, delivered immediately after the heavenly voice and self-identification in the surrounding verses of 3 Nephi 9 — a dramatically apt moment for Christ to apply a well-known scriptural formula to himself.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This is one of the closer phrase-level correspondences in the corpus, differing from its Johannine source mainly by pronoun, and sits alongside other registered Johannine intertexts of comparable or lesser closeness (3 Nephi 15/John 10 at 0.96, 3 Nephi 26/John 5 at 0.83, 1 Nephi 12's Christian intertext at 0.78).
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Both a tight-control-translation model and a nineteenth-century-composition model can produce a first-person recasting of a well-known New Testament formula; what distinguishes them is not available from this passage alone. On either reconstruction, the passage functions in its final form as Christ's self-application of a rejection-and-adoption formula recognizable to any English-Bible-literate reader.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports a deliberate textual relationship between 3 Nephi 9 and John 1 in the final English form. Under a translation model this could reflect genuine underlying content rendered in familiar KJV cadence, or revelatory/editorial harmonization; under a composition model, deliberate reuse of a known Johannine formula recast in the first person. The correspondence itself does not distinguish these.

**Inspiration:** Nothing on its own — a close textual relationship in the English wording does not by itself indicate whether the underlying event or record is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the direction of dependence, the date of composition, an underlying ancient-language correspondence, the historicity of the reported discourse, or inspiration.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **ancient record translated with KJV-influenced English** — predicts: a distinct underlying ancient theological statement rendered in familiar biblical cadence; a first-person recasting of a known formula is plausible if Christ is portrayed as consciously fulfilling scripture. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **19th-century composition using the known KJV New Testament text** — predicts: close phrase reuse recast into first person, matching what is observed. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **shared Christian theological tradition without direct textual borrowing** — predicts: similar theological content (rejection, sonship) but not this precise a wording match. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- KJV-mediated translation of a distinct ancient text — *plausible*: the Book of Mormon demonstrably uses KJV language elsewhere (the Isaiah blocks), so the mechanism is independently attested in the corpus even though not for this specific verse
- Independent shared Christian theological formula (rejection-then-adoption) arising without direct borrowing — *possible*: the theological idea is common in Christian teaching, but this does not explain the near-identical clause-level wording
- Nineteenth-century composition or redaction deliberately echoing John 1 — *plausible*: requires no unattested mechanism and is a straightforward reading of a KJV-literate author's output
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A patristic or intertestamental text that echoed a canonical verse this closely, recast into first person, would be read as directly textually dependent without hesitation; the same standard is applied here rather than a more indulgent one.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.78) for the proposition: *3 Nephi 9:16-17 deliberately engages the textual or traditional form represented by John 1:11-12 in the transmitted English text.*

The near-verbatim rejection-and-sonship sequence in 3 Nephi 9:16-17 strongly supports a direct textual relationship with John 1:11-12 in the transmitted English text, without establishing which direction or mechanism produced it.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: This is not a large-search-space list match but a two-clause sequence (rejection formula followed by a grant-of-sonship formula) that is specific enough that coincidental convergence on this wording is unlikely; no matched-control corpus of comparable inversions was surveyed.

What would move this: A demonstrated case in 3 Nephi 9 or its immediate context where the text uses genuinely independent, non-KJV wording for comparable theological content would weaken confidence in direct dependence; corroborating evidence that the translation process deliberately varied wording for distinct source content elsewhere would raise it.
