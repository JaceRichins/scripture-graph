# Extended Textual Parallel in 2 Nephi 19

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
2 Nephi 19 matches Isaiah 9 with the same sustained, near-verbatim KJV correspondence seen throughout the Book of Mormon's Isaiah chapters, with a couple of minor wording variants that are too small to indicate independent composition. This is the same evidentiary cause as the corpus-wide Isaiah-dependence issue and should be scored at that issue's weight (0.84, strong) rather than as an independent 0.85-0.99 data point. It leaves open whether the dependence and its small variants arose from an ancient source, translation choices, or direct modern reuse of the KJV.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Sustained verse-level correspondence (e.g. verses 1 and 21 quoted above), essentially identical to the KJV apart from small variants: 2 Nephi 19:1 inserts 'Red' before 'Sea' and drops 'her,' and verse 21 shows only punctuation/pronoun differences.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The same direct textual relationship documented for 2 Nephi 17/Isaiah 7, with a handful of minor wording divergences that are themselves too small to indicate an independently-composed text.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Consistent with an ancient Isaiah source rendered via KJV idiom, a loose translation drawing on KJV language, or direct modern reuse/light editing of the KJV; the small variants (e.g. 'Red Sea' vs 'sea') do not by themselves favor any one of these.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports literary/textual dependence on the KJV Isaiah text — the same evidentiary cause as every other Isaiah chapter in the corpus — and should carry the same registry weight rather than being scored as a separate, independently strong data point.

**Inspiration:** Nothing directly — the wording's relationship to the KJV speaks to compositional history, not to whether the content is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish whether the minor wording variants reflect a genuine ancient-source variant, a deliberate translation emendation, or ordinary dictation/editorial change; and, like all Isaiah chapters, does not decide ancient versus modern origin of the dependence itself.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Tight-control translation of an ancient Isaiah-bearing source** — predicts: close correspondence with occasional translator-introduced variants. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Loose/expansion translation without a fixed underlying text** — predicts: looser wording than what is observed. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
- **1829 composition/light editing directly from an open KJV** — predicts: near-verbatim text with occasional small deliberate or accidental changes. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Nephi quoted or adapted an ancient Isaiah text later rendered in familiar biblical English. — *possible*: The narrative permits this model, but the claimed ancient exemplar and the mechanism producing KJV-level wording are not independently attested.
- A loose or revelatory translation harmonized an ancient source to Isaiah 9 in the KJV. — *plausible*: It accounts for the close wording and localized variations but is not uniquely supported by this datum.
- The chapter was composed or revised in the nineteenth century using the KJV. — *plausible*: KJV availability and corpus-wide parallels support this general model, but direct use during this particular passage is undocumented.
- Both passages independently developed the same sequence without textual contact. — *ad hoc*: The extended ordered agreement and repeated refrain make coincidence an inadequate explanation.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
Small wording variants between duplicate biblical passages (e.g. Isaiah 37 vs 2 Kings 19) are routinely treated by textual critics as ordinary scribal or transmission variation, not evidence against a shared source; the same allowance is extended here rather than treating 'Red Sea' vs 'sea' as suspicious.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.84) for the proposition: *The English Book of Mormon Isaiah chapters depend literarily on the Isaiah/KJV textual tradition.*

The sustained match to KJV Isaiah 9, including a couple of small wording departures, is strong but not independently novel evidence of literary dependence on the Isaiah/KJV tradition, and should carry the same weight as every other Isaiah-dependence note rather than being treated as its own data point.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: As with 2 Nephi 17, correspondence of this length and specificity is not a chance event; no control corpus is required to exclude coincidence.

What would move this: Weight for specifically Isaiah/KJV mediation would rise if complete collation identified numerous coherent KJV-distinctive renderings; it would fall if the apparent correspondence reduced to generic prophetic language or independently recurring material without sustained ordered verbal agreement.
