# Literary Intertextuality in Alma 30

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 30 and Jacob 7 exhibit a substantial narrative pattern: an opponent challenges prophecy about Christ, demands a sign, is struck by divine power, confesses deception, and dies. This is strong evidence of literary relationship or deliberate typological patterning, but it does not determine whether the similarity reflects repeated history, editorial shaping, a conventional cautionary narrative, or direct literary reuse.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish historicity or fictionality, identify the direction of literary dependence, or prove a particular theory of Book of Mormon authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The accounts may describe separate historical events that followed similar trajectories.
- A compiler may have shaped distinct traditions into a recurring anti-Christ pattern.
- Both accounts may employ a conventional religious cautionary-story structure.
- One account may intentionally allude to the other, but the parallels alone do not establish the direction of dependence.
