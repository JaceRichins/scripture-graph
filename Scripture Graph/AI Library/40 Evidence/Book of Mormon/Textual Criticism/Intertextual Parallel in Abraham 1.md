# Intertextual Parallel in Abraham 1

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Abraham 1:16 and Genesis 12:1 share a conspicuous cluster of ideas and language: leaving country or kindred and the father's house for a land not yet known or shown. Abraham 1:19 and Genesis 12:2–3 then associate Abraham's calling with a worldwide or multinational blessing. This strongly supports a deliberate literary or traditional relationship between the passages, but the textual parallel alone cannot determine the direction or historical origin of that relationship.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish independent ancient authorship, historical priority, or a particular theory of translation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Both passages may draw on a shared Abraham tradition.
- The Book of Abraham may deliberately expand or reinterpret the Genesis account.
- The parallel may reflect direct dependence on the King James wording of Genesis.
- A believing interpretation may understand the shared wording as restored narrative context for the biblical call.
