# Literary Intertextuality in 2 Nephi 25

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Second Nephi 25 integrates recognizable language and motifs from Numbers 21, Isaiah 11 and 29, Malachi 4, and Acts 4, strongly supporting deliberate biblical and KJV-shaped mediation in the final English discourse. The Numbers and Isaiah material fits its overt scriptural exposition, while the Malachi- and Acts-like wording creates a genuine chronological difficulty for strict pre-Christian-verbatim models. Loose translation, revelatory harmonization, and nineteenth-century composition remain viable mechanisms, and the cluster must not be double-counted as independent of the broader KJV-mediation evidence. See [[2 Nephi 25#^2ne-25-13|2 Nephi 25:13]], [[2 Nephi 25#^2ne-25-17|2 Nephi 25:17]], [[2 Nephi 25#^2ne-25-20|2 Nephi 25:20]], [[Numbers 21#^num-21-8|Numbers 21:8]], [[Numbers 21#^num-21-9|Numbers 21:9]], [[Isaiah 11#^isa-11-11|Isaiah 11:11]], [[Isaiah 29#^isa-29-14|Isaiah 29:14]], [[Malachi 4#^mal-4-2|Malachi 4:2]], and [[Acts 4#^acts-4-12|Acts 4:12]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Second Nephi 25 combines the raised serpent from Numbers 21, the Lord setting his hand a second time from Isaiah 11:11, a marvelous work and wonder from Isaiah 29:14, healing in the Messiah's wings corresponding to Malachi 4:2, and the no-other-name, given-under-heaven, whereby-one-may-be-saved formulation corresponding closely to Acts 4:12. Several correspondences preserve distinctive KJV wording or compact phrase sequences.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The discourse deliberately operates within a dense, KJV-shaped intertextual field spanning Torah narrative and two separate prophetic books.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Compatible with ancient prophetic reuse of shared scriptural tradition, translation/editorial harmonization to KJV idiom, or modern compositional reuse of well-known biblical phrases.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Ancient and early Christian messianic exposition regularly wove together proof-texts from the Torah and the Prophets (the lifted serpent of Numbers 21, restoration language from Isaiah, healing imagery from Malachi) to argue for a coming deliverer. One reconstruction: Nephi's discourse in 2 Nephi 25 reflects that same associative, prooftext-weaving mode of scriptural argument, rendered here in the King James idiom already used elsewhere in the text for its Isaiah material.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
The cluster strongly supports biblical and KJV-shaped mediation of the final text. The Acts-like wording and conventionally later Malachi parallel create real difficulty for a tight-control model in which the precise English correspondences directly reproduce pre-Christian source wording; they discriminate much less between loose or harmonizing translation, modern revelation, and nineteenth-century composition. These observations are dependent on the broader KJV-mediation phenomenon and must not be counted again as wholly independent evidence.

**Inspiration:** Nothing independently; intertextual density bears on composition, not on inspiration.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
The correspondences do not determine whether the underlying discourse was ancient, whether later biblical wording was supplied during translation or revelation, or whether a modern author composed the synthesis. They do not establish prophetic foreknowledge, direction of dependence for every motif, historicity, or inspiration, and they must not be added independently to the already-recognized Isaiah/KJV-dependence evidence.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Sustained KJV/biblical textual mediation** — predicts: near-verbatim overlap for the Isaiah and Malachi components. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Ancient prophetic use of shared scriptural memory** — predicts: thematic and some verbal overlap without requiring KJV-specific wording. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Modern compositional assembly of well-known biblical proof-texts** — predicts: the same clustering of familiar messianic prooftexts. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Shared biblical vocabulary rather than a distinct intended allusion for each component. — *plausible*: More plausible for the diffuse Numbers 21 echo than for the near-verbatim Isaiah/Malachi phrases.
- KJV/translation-stage mediation. — *plausible*: Consistent with the Book of Mormon's broader, already-registered Isaiah-dependence pattern.
- Direct modern compositional reuse. — *plausible*

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
In any corpus, multiple distinctive correspondences with earlier and later texts would support literary or translation-level mediation, while chronological implications would depend on the proposed date and transmission model. Treating the later parallels as a difficulty for tight verbatim preservation without treating them as excluding every ancient-source model gives this text the same translation, redaction, and transmission allowances used in biblical textual criticism.
