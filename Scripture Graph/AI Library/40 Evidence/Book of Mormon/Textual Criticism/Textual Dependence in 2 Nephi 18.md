# Textual Dependence in 2 Nephi 18

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sequential, chapter-length correspondence between 2 Nephi 18 and Isaiah 8 is strong evidence for dependence on or transmission of the Isaiah textual tradition. Ancient-record transmission, KJV-shaped translation or harmonization, and direct modern reuse remain viable mechanisms, and this chapter is one dependent instance of the broader registered Isaiah pattern.
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
2 Nephi 18 corresponds to Isaiah 8 across the full chapter in sequence and close English wording.
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The chapter is in direct literary relationship with the Isaiah 8/KJV tradition; as with the other Isaiah-block chapters, the relationship's existence is not seriously contested, while the mechanism is.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
Establishes dependence upon or transmission of the Isaiah passage; does not by itself determine when, where, or by what translation process that relationship arose.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
On a translation model, an Isaiah text carried on the brass plates would be rendered by a 19th-century translator into the only full English Bible register available, producing chapter-length KJV-level correspondence as a byproduct of mediation rather than of copying. On a composition model, the same correspondence follows directly from consulting an open Bible. The chapter's wording alone cannot distinguish these reconstructions.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
This is strong support for textual dependence, but the registered bom-isaiah-textual-dependence issue caps the mechanism-discrimination weight at 0.84: this instance, occurring within the main Isaiah block (2 Nephi 12-24) already covered by that issue, is not independent additional evidence and should not be scored separately at 0.99.

**Inspiration:** Says nothing by itself about whether the translation or underlying text is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the Book of Mormon's antiquity, Nephi's historical possession of Isaiah, or a particular translation mechanism.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **Tight-control ancient translation** — predicts: Full-chapter correspondence in available biblical English.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Loose/revelatory translation** — predicts: Substantial overlap with some divergence.. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **Nineteenth-century composition using the KJV** — predicts: Near-total sequential verbatim overlap.. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **Independent ancient transmission** — predicts: Partial, non-KJV-specific overlap.. Fit with this evidence: *strongly challenged*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Isaiah material preserved on the brass plates and translated by divine means. — *possible*: A believing account consistent with the narrative but not independently attested.
- Direct or mediated use of the KJV Isaiah text. — *plausible*: Fully accounts for the chapter-length verbatim overlap.
- Later editorial or translation-stage harmonization increasing English-level similarity. — *plausible*: Compatible with an underlying source plus KJV-level smoothing.
- Coincidental independent transmission. — *ad hoc*: Offers no mechanism for matching KJV-specific wording this closely without contact.
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check

Chapter-length verbatim biblical quotation elsewhere (Bible-to-Bible parallel passages, or D&C quoting scripture) is scored as strong dependence without resolving mechanism; this note is held to the same standard, not elevated because the match happens to be unusually clean.
