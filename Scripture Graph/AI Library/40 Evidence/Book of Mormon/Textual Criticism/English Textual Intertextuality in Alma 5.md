# English Textual Intertextuality in Alma 5

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Alma 5 contains a dense constellation of close English-language biblical parallels: pure heart and clean hands, the shepherd's voice and fold, the ax at the tree's root, wages of death, separation from unclean things, and names in the book of life. The parallels strongly support literary intertextuality in the received English text.

**Does not establish:** These English parallels do not establish the direction of dependence, deliberate quotation in every instance, the wording of an underlying source language, or ancient or modern authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The English text may directly depend on familiar biblical or King James phrasing.
- A translation process may have rendered older concepts through conventional biblical English.
- Some images may derive independently from broadly shared Jewish and Christian traditions rather than one specific passage.
- Individual verbal similarities may be conventional, although their cumulative density remains noteworthy.
