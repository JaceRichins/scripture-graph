# Textual Intertextuality in Moroni 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Moroni 10:8-17 closely parallels the opening frame and the wisdom/knowledge pairing of the 1 Corinthians 12:4-11 spiritual-gifts catalog, including a near-verbatim clause structure, which strongly supports a direct English-textual relationship rather than mere shared genre. The later portion of the catalog is reordered and omits "discerning of spirits," showing the relationship is not a mechanical copy throughout. This does not establish the direction of dependence, the underlying source language, or ancient versus modern authorship. Scripture: [[Moroni 10#^moro-10-8|Moroni 10:8]], [[Moroni 10#^moro-10-9|Moroni 10:9]], [[Moroni 10#^moro-10-10|Moroni 10:10]], [[Moroni 10#^moro-10-11|Moroni 10:11]], [[Moroni 10#^moro-10-12|Moroni 10:12]], [[Moroni 10#^moro-10-13|Moroni 10:13]], [[Moroni 10#^moro-10-15|Moroni 10:15]], [[Moroni 10#^moro-10-16|Moroni 10:16]], [[1 Corinthians 12#^1cor-12-4|1 Corinthians 12:4]].
<!-- SG:END summary -->

## Observation
<!-- SG:BEGIN observation -->
Moroni 10:8 ("they are many; and they come from the same God ... different ways that these gifts are administered ... the same God who worketh all in all ... by the manifestations of the Spirit") closely parallels 1 Corinthians 12:4-6,11 ("diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit ... diversities of operations, but it is the same God which worketh all in all ... the manifestation of the Spirit"). Moroni 10:9-10 ("to one is given by the Spirit ... the word of wisdom; And to another ... the word of knowledge by the same Spirit") closely parallels 1 Corinthians 12:8 ("to one is given by the Spirit the word of wisdom; to another the word of knowledge by the same Spirit"), differing mainly by the added verb "teach." The subsequent catalog items (faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, tongues, interpretation) substantially overlap in content though not in exact order, and Moroni's list omits 1 Corinthians 12's "discerning of spirits."
<!-- SG:END observation -->

## Interpretation
<!-- SG:BEGIN interpretation -->
The opening frame and the wisdom/knowledge pairing are close enough in structure and wording to indicate a direct textual relationship rather than mere shared subject matter, while the reordering and omission in the remainder of the catalog show the relationship is not a mechanical, item-for-item copy.
<!-- SG:END interpretation -->

## Historical Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN historical-significance -->
This is a distinctive frame-and-pairing correspondence (not just a shared theme of "spiritual gifts," which is common across Christian literature broadly), closely comparable in tightness to other catalog-type correspondences in the corpus, though less rigid in ordering than a full verbatim block.
<!-- SG:END historical-significance -->

## How This Fits
<!-- SG:BEGIN how-it-fits -->
Whether by KJV-mediated translation of a genuine ancient teaching or by nineteenth-century composition, the passage functions in its final form as an adaptation of the Corinthians gift catalog with its own reordering and one significant omission — a pattern consistent with an author or translator drawing on a well-known text while shaping it for a new context.
<!-- SG:END how-it-fits -->

## Apologetic Significance
<!-- SG:BEGIN apologetic-significance -->
Strongly supports a direct English-textual relationship between Moroni 10 and 1 Corinthians 12 for at least the opening frame and the wisdom/knowledge pairing. It does not establish which model produced the reuse, and the reordering and omitted item in the remainder of the catalog moderate the strength somewhat below a case of sustained verbatim correspondence.

**Inspiration:** Nothing — a close textual relationship to a known KJV passage says nothing by itself about whether the underlying teaching is inspired.
<!-- SG:END apologetic-significance -->

## What It Does Not Establish
<!-- SG:BEGIN does-not-establish -->
Does not establish the direction of dependence, the language of an underlying source text, or ancient versus modern authorship.
<!-- SG:END does-not-establish -->

## Models On The Table
<!-- SG:BEGIN models -->
- **ancient record whose spiritual-gifts teaching is rendered through KJV-influenced translation** — predicts: conceptual overlap with possible reordering, since translation intervenes between a distinct underlying text and English wording. Fit with this evidence: *consistent*.
- **translation directly mediated through the King James 1 Corinthians 12 text** — predicts: the close frame-and-pairing correspondence actually observed, including the near-verbatim wisdom/knowledge clause. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
- **shared Christian catechetical genre without direct textual borrowing** — predicts: general overlap in gift categories but not this close a match in the opening frame and the wisdom/knowledge clause specifically. Fit with this evidence: *some difficulty*.
- **nineteenth-century composition drawing on the familiar KJV 1 Corinthians 12 text** — predicts: the same close correspondence, adapted and reordered for the author's purposes. Fit with this evidence: *supports*.
<!-- SG:END models -->

## Alternative Explanations
<!-- SG:BEGIN alternatives -->
- Moroni and Paul independently describe a shared religious understanding of spiritual gifts without direct literary dependence — *possible*: gift-catalog teaching is a recognizable feature of early Christian catechesis generally, but this does not account for the close wording of the opening frame and the wisdom/knowledge clause
- Translator/author familiarity with 1 Corinthians 12 shaping the English rendering of a distinct underlying gifts teaching — *plausible*: consistent with the demonstrated pattern of KJV-mediated rendering elsewhere in the corpus
- Deliberate modeling on the King James 1 Corinthians 12 text during composition — *plausible*: requires no unattested mechanism and is the simplest account of the close frame match
<!-- SG:END alternatives -->

## Comparative Check
<!-- SG:BEGIN symmetry -->
A patristic catechetical text that reproduced Paul's gift-list opening this closely, while reordering and omitting one item, would be treated as directly dependent on 1 Corinthians with adaptation, not as merely evoking a shared genre; the same standard applies here.
<!-- SG:END symmetry -->

## Evidentiary Weight
<!-- SG:BEGIN weight -->
**Strong** (supports, evidence_strength 0.75) for the proposition: *Moroni 10:8-16 deliberately reuses or is textually dependent on the 1 Corinthians 12:4-8 spiritual-gifts catalog in the transmitted English text.*

The close correspondence between Moroni 10's opening gifts frame and wisdom/knowledge pairing and 1 Corinthians 12:4-8,11 strongly supports a direct English-textual relationship, moderated by the reordering and one omitted item later in the catalog, without establishing dependence direction or authorship date.

Base rate / look-elsewhere: Spiritual-gift catalogs are not rare in Christian literature generally, so the bare presence of a gifts list carries little weight on its own; what raises this above a generic-genre match is the near-verbatim frame ("same God... worketh all in all... manifestation of the Spirit") and the wisdom/knowledge clause specifically, which are more distinctive than the list-of-items feature alone. No matched-control corpus of other gift catalogs was surveyed to quantify how often such close frame matches occur by chance.

What would move this: A demonstrated case where the Book of Mormon's other gift-related teaching (e.g., D&C 46) diverges sharply in structure from 1 Corinthians 12 would weaken the case for a single recurring dependence pattern; conversely, finding the same close frame-and-pairing structure recurring in other Restoration-era gift teachings would strengthen the case that this is a deliberate, recognizable textual borrowing rather than coincidence.
