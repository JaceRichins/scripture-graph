# Personal Revelatory Authentication in D&C 6

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the revelation's own argument, knowledge of Oliver's unspoken prayer functions as a personalized authentication claim. For a later investigator, however, the episode has limited independent evidentiary force because the chapter itself is the principal account of both the private event and its interpretation.

**Does not establish:** By itself, the internal claim does not independently establish divine authorship, the exclusivity of this mode of revelation, or a general rule that every feeling of peace is revelation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The disclosure may be understood by believers as divine knowledge of a genuinely private prayer.
- Joseph may have learned relevant details through an earlier conversation not preserved in the text.
- The account may reflect retrospective narration or interpretation.
- A broadly phrased spiritual experience may have seemed specifically applicable to Oliver.
