# Institutional-Practice Evidence in D&C 127

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The requirements for an eyewitness recorder, orderly records, and temple archives provide strong textual evidence that baptism for the dead was being prescribed as a formally documented communal ordinance rather than merely described as a private devotional idea.

**Does not establish:** The procedures do not establish the ordinance's divine origin, metaphysical validity, or historical implementation in every case.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A prescriptive directive shows intended procedure but does not by itself prove how consistently the procedure was implemented.
- The administrative requirements could reflect a developing response to practical recordkeeping problems.
