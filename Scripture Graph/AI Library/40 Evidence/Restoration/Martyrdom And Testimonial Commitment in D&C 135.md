# Martyrdom And Testimonial Commitment in D&C 135

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Being killed in connection with a religious mission can support the conclusion that the victims bore severe costs associated with that mission, but their deaths do not by themselves demonstrate that all of their religious claims were true.

**Does not establish:** Martyrdom alone does not establish prophetic authority, the divine origin of a scripture, or the truth of every doctrine associated with the martyrs.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A person may sincerely hold a belief that is nevertheless mistaken.
- The killings may reflect political and social conflict in addition to religious hostility.
- Because the victims were attacked while confined, their deaths were not a controlled opportunity to retract specific propositions.
- The chapter's description of the deaths as proof is theological and commemorative rhetoric rather than a deductive argument.
