# Scriptural Intertext And Historical Inference in D&C 128

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
D&C 128 appeals to 1 Corinthians 15:29 as biblical precedent for baptism for the dead. The Pauline wording is strong evidence that an activity described as being 'baptized for the dead' was intelligible to its original audience, but the isolated verse does not by itself determine who practiced it, its exact form, or whether Paul endorsed it.

**Does not establish:** The verse alone does not establish the modern ordinance's procedure, priesthood administration, recordkeeping system, or Paul's endorsement of proxy baptism.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Paul may be referring rhetorically to a practice performed by people outside his own group.
- The expression may describe a practice different from modern Latter-day Saint proxy baptism.
- Paul may use the practice only as an argument for resurrection without approving every feature of it.

**Scripture:** [[D&C 128#^dc-128-16|D&C 128:16]], [[1 Corinthians 15#^1cor-15-29|1 Corinthians 15:29]]
