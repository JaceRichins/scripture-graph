# Biblical Verbal Dependence in D&C 81

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The clustered wording in D&C 81:5 corresponds closely to Isaiah 35:3 and Hebrews 12:12. Isaiah supplies weak hands and feeble knees, while Hebrews supplies hands that hang down; D&C 81 combines these expressions and adds “succor the weak.” This strongly supports literary dependence on, or conscious reuse of, the English Bible's language.

**Does not establish:** The verbal correspondence does not establish ancient authorship, a uniquely miraculous source, or that every element of Isaiah 35 and Hebrews 12 is being applied here.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The wording may come immediately from Hebrews 12:12, whose author already reworked Isaiah 35:3, rather than from separate use of both passages.
- The phrases had become familiar Christian exhortatory language, so their reuse need not reflect a carefully constructed composite quotation.
- Some overlap could result from Joseph Smith's immersion in King James Bible diction rather than a deliberate allusion to a specific context.
