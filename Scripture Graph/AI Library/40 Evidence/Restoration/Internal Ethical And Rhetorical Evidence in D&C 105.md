# Internal Ethical And Rhetorical Evidence in D&C 105

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of commands to seek favor, pursue legal redress, avoid boastful or inflammatory speech, and offer peace even to attackers is strong evidence against reading the chapter as an authorization for immediate, unrestricted religious violence.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish absolute pacifism, erase the chapter's violent imagery, or resolve how its conditional military language should be applied outside its immediate setting.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The peace policy may be temporary and strategic rather than an absolute rejection of later force.
- Verses 30-32 retain coercive and military expectations that prevent a fully pacifist reading.

**Scripture:** [[D&C 105#^dc-105-23|D&C 105:23-26]], [[D&C 105#^dc-105-38|D&C 105:38-40]]
