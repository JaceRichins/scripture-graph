# Institutional Conflict Narrative in D&C 28

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The treatment of Hiram Page provides strong evidence for the chapter's own institutional preference: extraordinary spiritual claims are subordinated to recognized appointment, existing covenants, orderly procedure, and communal consent.

**Does not establish:** It does not independently prove that Page was deceived by Satan or that the authorized leadership's revelations came from God.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A skeptic may read the rules as an institutional mechanism for protecting centralized leadership rather than as evidence of divine order.
- The text reports the divine judgment against Page but does not independently demonstrate the supernatural origin or falsity of either party's revelations.
