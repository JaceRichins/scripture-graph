# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 34

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The clustering of recognizable Johannine, Isaianic, Joel-like, and Matthean language is strong evidence that D&C 34 is composed in sustained conversation with biblical scripture. This observation is highly secure, while conclusions about why that language appears require separate argument.

**Does not establish:** The verbal correspondences alone do not establish divine revelation, supernatural prediction, or a uniquely correct explanation of authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The wording may reflect conscious imitation of the King James Bible by a modern author.
- The parallels may arise from deeply internalized biblical religious vocabulary rather than deliberate citation of each passage.
- A believing interpretation may understand the echoes as revelation deliberately reusing earlier scripture.
