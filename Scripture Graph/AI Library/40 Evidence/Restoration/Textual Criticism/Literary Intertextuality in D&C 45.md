# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 45

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sustained convergence of temple destruction, wars, false security, cosmic signs, the fig tree, watchfulness, clouds, angels, and gathering provides strong evidence that D&C 45:16–59 is literarily related to the New Testament Olivet discourse and associated judgment parables.

**Does not establish:** Literary correspondence alone does not determine whether the expansion came through revelation, composition, remembered biblical language, or a combination of these.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A believer may understand the relationship as Jesus restoring or expanding his earlier discourse by revelation.
- A historical-critical account may attribute the relationship to Joseph Smith's close engagement with biblical language while revising the New Testament.
- Some shared images derive from older Jewish prophetic and apocalyptic traditions rather than solely from one Gospel text.
