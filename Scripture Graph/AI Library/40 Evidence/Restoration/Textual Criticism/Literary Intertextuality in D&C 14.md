# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 14

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close verbal correspondences between D&C 14:2 and Hebrews 4:12, between D&C 14:3 and John 4:35, and between D&C 14:5 and Matthew 7:7 strongly support the limited conclusion that the section is framed in recognizable King James biblical diction.

**Does not establish:** These correspondences do not by themselves establish divine origin, ancient authorship, historical authenticity, or a particular mechanism of composition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language may have been consciously adapted from remembered biblical passages.
- The phrases may have entered the revelation through common nineteenth-century Christian preaching and idiom.
- The language may reflect revelatory accommodation to the scriptural vocabulary familiar to its recipients.
