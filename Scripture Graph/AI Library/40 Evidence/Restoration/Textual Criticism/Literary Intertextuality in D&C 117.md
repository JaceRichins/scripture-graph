# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 117

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of close biblical parallels—creation ownership, the solitary place blossoming, weighty matters, faithfulness over few and many, Nicolaitans, and temple moneychangers—provides strong evidence that D&C 117 is deliberately framed in King James biblical language.

**Does not establish:** The intertextual pattern does not by itself establish divine origin, supernatural knowledge, ancient authorship, or the precise intent behind every verbal parallel.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some expressions may have functioned as ordinary English religious idioms in a Bible-saturated nineteenth-century environment.
- Individual echoes could be unconscious reuse rather than deliberately constructed allusions.
- The weaker parallels may be coincidental even if the cluster as a whole is meaningful.
