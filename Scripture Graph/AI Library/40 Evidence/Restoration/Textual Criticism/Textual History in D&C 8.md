# Textual History in D&C 8

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Early textual witnesses of the revelation describe Oliver's second gift with sprout or rod terminology, whereas the current canonical text calls it the gift of Aaron. This textual development helps explain the otherwise unusual references to holding the gift in one's hands and asking by that means. It also makes a connection with early nineteenth-century divining practices historically plausible, although the precise intended operation and the purpose of each editorial change remain uncertain.

**Does not establish:** The textual history does not establish that the claimed gift actually produced supernatural knowledge, that every revision had one recoverable motive, or that the section has ancient authorship.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The revisions may have clarified the same claimed gift in biblical language rather than changing its intended substance.
- Aaron and his rod may supply a scriptural analogy without identifying Oliver's practice completely with ordinary divination.
- The hand-held language may have acquired a more symbolic meaning in the revised canonical form.
- Editorial refinement or standardization can account for textual change without implying deceptive intent.
