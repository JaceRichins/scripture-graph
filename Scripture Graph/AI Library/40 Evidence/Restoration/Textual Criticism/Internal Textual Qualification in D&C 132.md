# Internal Textual Qualification in D&C 132

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Within the chapter's own theological framework, its repeated appeal to commandment, appointment, and what is 'given' constitutes strong textual evidence against reading it as an unrestricted authorization of plural marriage.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish the revelation's divine origin, the ethical adequacy of its rules, or whether particular historical marriages complied with the stated conditions.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language could still permit broad practice whenever an authorized keyholder declares that a union was commanded or given.
- A critic may regard the claimed divine authorization as circular and therefore insufficient to establish that any historical union was actually authorized.
- Some readers understand the governing 'law' primarily as priesthood sealing rather than as a stable code devoted exclusively to plural marriage.
