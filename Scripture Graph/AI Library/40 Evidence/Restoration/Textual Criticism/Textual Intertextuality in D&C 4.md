# Textual Intertextuality in D&C 4

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The unusually extensive correspondence between D&C 4:6 and 2 Peter 1:5–7 is strong evidence that the wording of D&C 4 participates in, and probably consciously reuses, biblical language. It is not evidence that uniquely determines the revelation's mode of composition or historical origin.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish ancient authorship, modern fabrication, the precise mechanism of revelation, or why the sequence was modified.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language could have entered the revelation through Joseph Smith's familiarity with the King James Bible.
- The correspondence could reflect divinely adapted biblical wording rather than a conventional process of literary composition.
- Some individual virtues were common Christian vocabulary, although the extended sequence makes purely coincidental resemblance unlikely.
