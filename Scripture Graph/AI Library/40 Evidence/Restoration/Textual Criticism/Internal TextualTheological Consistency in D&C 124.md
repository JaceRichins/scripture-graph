# Internal TextualTheological Consistency in D&C 124

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Verses 49-54 articulate a general principle that God requires a commanded work 'no more' and 'accepts of their offerings' when faithful people are 'hindered' by enemies, and the text applies this principle by name to those 'commanded to build up a city and a house unto my name, in Jackson county, Missouri, and were hindered by their enemies.'

**Does not establish:** This does not establish a fixed, generally applicable legal test for when unfulfilled commandments are excused; the chapter states the principle and one historical application but does not define its limits or how it would be adjudicated in other cases.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The principle may function partly as a retrospective theological accommodation explaining why the earlier Missouri/Jackson County temple commandment (D&C 84, 97) was not fulfilled, rather than a pre-existing doctrinal rule.
- The 'hindered by enemies' exception could be read narrowly (limited to the specific 1833 Missouri expulsion) rather than as a general, repeatable doctrine applicable to any obstructed commandment.
