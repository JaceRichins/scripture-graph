# IntertextualLiterary Parallel in D&C 110

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The description of Christ in D&C 110:3-4 (white hair, eyes like flame, a countenance brighter than the sun, a voice like many waters, self-identification as 'the first and the last' and as one who 'liveth' and 'was slain') closely parallels John's description of the risen Christ in Revelation 1:14-18. This literary parallel is strong evidence of shared or dependent theophanic vocabulary, but it does not by itself establish whether the account directly drew on the text of Revelation, describes a genuinely independent experience expressed through familiar biblical idiom, or some combination.

**Does not establish:** This parallel does not establish literary dependence versus independent convergence, nor does it establish or disprove that a literal visionary event occurred.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The narrators independently experienced a theophany and naturally described it using familiar biblical vocabulary for divine glory.
- The written account was deliberately composed to echo Revelation 1 as a literary/theological signal of Christ's identity, independent of exact experiential detail.
- Both texts draw on a shared older Jewish/Christian stock of theophany imagery (e.g., Daniel 7, Revelation) rather than one directly borrowing from the other.
