# Textual Intertextuality in D&C 133

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The clustering and sequencing of language from Isaiah, Revelation, Malachi, and other biblical passages provide strong evidence that D&C 133 consciously incorporates and recontextualizes biblical apocalyptic and prophetic language.

**Does not establish:** The intertextuality by itself does not establish divine origin, fulfillment of the prophecies, a particular model of revelation, or the literal character of the predicted geography.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The biblical language may reflect Joseph Smith's extensive familiarity with the King James Bible rather than an independent ancient source.
- A believing interpretation may understand the reuse as revelatory expansion or divine harmonization of earlier prophecy.
- Some shorter verbal parallels can arise from the shared religious vocabulary of nineteenth-century English, though this does not adequately explain the longer ordered sequences.
