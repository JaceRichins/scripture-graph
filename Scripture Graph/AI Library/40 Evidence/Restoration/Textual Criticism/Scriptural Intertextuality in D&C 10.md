# Scriptural Intertextuality in D&C 10

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
D&C 10:57-59 closely reproduces or adapts the language of John 1:5, John 1:11, and John 10:16. The verbal proximity is strong evidence of deliberate Johannine intertextuality in the English text.

**Does not establish:** The verbal parallels establish intertextual reuse far more securely than they establish a particular revelatory mechanism, speaker identity, or historical origin.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language may be understood as explicit revelatory reuse of Jesus's biblical sayings.
- It may reflect Joseph Smith's familiarity with the King James Bible and composition in its scriptural idiom.
- The two explanations are not mutually exclusive within a believing model.
