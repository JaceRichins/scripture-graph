# Internal Textual Evidence in D&C 102

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The designated advocates for the accused, examination of evidence, direct participation by both parties, impartial review, rehearing for detected error, alteration when additional light appears, written transmission of proceedings, and appellate opportunity strongly support the limited conclusion that fairness, correction, and accountable review are explicit ideals of this prescribed system.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish that every hearing was impartial, that outcomes were always just, or that this procedure is equivalent to a modern judicial system.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Formal safeguards can express institutional ideals without proving that historical councils consistently followed them.
- The procedure belongs to an ecclesiastical forum and should not be equated uncritically with modern civil due process.
- Assigned advocacy may manage deliberation and decorum as well as protect the accused.
