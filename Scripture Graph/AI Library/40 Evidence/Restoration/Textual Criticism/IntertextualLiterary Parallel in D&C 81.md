# IntertextualLiterary Parallel in D&C 81

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The near-verbatim overlap between D&C 81:5 and Hebrews 12:12/Isaiah 35:3 is evidence of literary dependence on KJV phrasing, but does not by itself establish that the connection was a deliberate theological allusion (e.g., to the endurance-under-chastening context of Hebrews 12) rather than incidental reuse of familiar biblical idiom.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that the author/revelator intended readers to recall the specific context of Hebrews 12 (endurance under divine chastening) as opposed to reusing a stock scriptural phrase for exhortation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The phrase may simply reflect common 19th-century biblical idiom absorbed into revelatory dictation rather than a targeted literary allusion to Hebrews 12.
- Joseph Smith's revelatory language regularly incorporates KJV phrasing as its natural linguistic register, so wording overlap alone does not prove intentional citation of a specific biblical passage or its surrounding context.
