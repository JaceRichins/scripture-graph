# Intertextual Synthesis in D&C 138

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The chapter integrates language and themes attributed to Peter, Isaiah, Ezekiel, Daniel, Malachi, Nephite prophets, and latter-day temple teaching into one redemption narrative. This strongly supports the limited conclusion that the text is deliberately intertextual and theologically synthetic.

**Does not establish:** The synthesis does not independently establish the historical reality of the assembled figures, divine authorship, or the truth of every doctrine inferred from the cited passages.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The synthesis may arise from the author's extensive familiarity with scripture rather than from supernatural disclosure.
- Some connections are made explicitly by the narrator and therefore do not independently corroborate one another.
- Intertextual density demonstrates literary construction but does not by itself determine historical or metaphysical truth.
