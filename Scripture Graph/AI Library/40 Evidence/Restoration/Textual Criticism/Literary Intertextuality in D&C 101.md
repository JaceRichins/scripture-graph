# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 101

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of recognizable biblical materials—including Abraham's test, the call to be still, salt imagery, wheat and tares, the unjust judge, and Isaianic renewal language—is strong evidence that biblical language deliberately frames the chapter's response to the Zion crisis.

**Does not establish:** These parallels do not by themselves establish a particular theory of revelation, compositional process, historical authenticity, or divine origin.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The biblical reuse may reflect revelatory adaptation of earlier scripture.
- It may reflect Joseph Smith's deep immersion in the language and narrative patterns of the King James Bible.
- Some verbal shaping may be editorial, and more than one process may have contributed.
