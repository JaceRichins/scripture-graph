# Scriptural Intertextuality in D&C 42

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Close parallels to New Testament teachings provide strong evidence that the chapter is deliberately framed in biblical language and patterns: lust is treated as inward unfaithfulness, service to the least as service to Christ, wisdom is obtained by asking God, and private correction precedes church adjudication.

**Does not establish:** These parallels do not establish a unique mechanism of composition, historical antiquity, or the truth of the chapter's revelatory claims.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The correspondences can arise from Joseph Smith's familiarity with the King James Bible and need not imply direct quotation from a single passage.
- A believing interpretation may regard the language as divine restatement of earlier revelation.
- Some shared wording may reflect the general biblical register of early nineteenth-century religious discourse.
