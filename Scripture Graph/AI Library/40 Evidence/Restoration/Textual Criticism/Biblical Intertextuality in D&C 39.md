# Biblical Intertextuality in D&C 39

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
D&C 39:2-4 closely follows the sequence and distinctive wording of John 1:5, 11-12, providing strong evidence of deliberate Johannine intertextuality. The overlap does not determine whether it arose through quotation, revelatory reuse, or composition in familiar biblical language.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not establish ancient authorship, independent transmission, a particular translation process, or the historical truth of the speaker's claims.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The passage may intentionally quote or adapt the King James Bible.
- The wording may reflect Joseph Smith's familiarity with biblical idiom.
- A believing interpretation may regard the wording as revelatory restatement of an earlier divine teaching.
