# Scriptural Intertextuality in D&C 132

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The close use of biblical language and narratives is strong evidence of sustained scriptural intertextuality in the chapter.

**Does not establish:** Intertextuality alone does not establish divine revelation, ancient authorship, historical accuracy, or the moral validity of the chapter's marital rules.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A believer may understand the parallels as revelation deliberately interpreting earlier scripture.
- A historical-critical account may attribute them to composition within a nineteenth-century culture saturated in the King James Bible.
- Some verbal parallels may arise from a shared scriptural register rather than deliberate quotation of one particular passage.
