# Biblical Intertextuality in D&C 109

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Several passages closely echo biblical language or imagery: Pentecostal wind, fire, and tongues; the kingdom becoming a mountain that fills the earth; the moon-sun-army description; and being caught up to meet the Lord. Together these provide strong evidence that the prayer is densely scriptural in expression.

**Does not establish:** These parallels do not by themselves prove supernatural origin, ancient authorship, literary independence from the Bible, or any single model of composition.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The language may result from deliberate literary composition using the Bible.
- It may reflect memorized scriptural phrasing employed naturally in prayer.
- A believer may understand the reuse as part of revelation; textual resemblance alone cannot test that theological explanation.
- Some similarities may arise from participation in a broader biblical religious register.
