# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 98

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of close biblical phrases is strong evidence that D&C 98 deliberately speaks in a scriptural register and invites interpretation through biblical texts. This observation has little power by itself to determine whether the revelation's ultimate origin is divine, human, or some combination understood through a theology of revelation.

**Does not establish:** The parallels do not establish divine origin, fraud, ancient authorship, historical accuracy, or a specific mechanical process of revelation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Joseph Smith's familiarity with the King James Bible could account for the language through memory and composition.
- The wording could be understood by believers as revelation deliberately adapting earlier scripture.
- Some overlap may arise from the broader religious vocabulary of nineteenth-century English rather than a single intended source text.
