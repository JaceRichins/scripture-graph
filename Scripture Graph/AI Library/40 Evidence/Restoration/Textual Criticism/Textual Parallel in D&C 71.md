# Textual Parallel in D&C 71

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
D&C 71:9's assurance, "there is no weapon that is formed against you shall prosper," closely parallels Isaiah 54:17 KJV, "No weapon that is formed against thee shall prosper." This is a strong verbal parallel and indicates the revelation draws on (or echoes) this specific Isaianic covenant-assurance language, though the text does not explicitly flag it as a quotation.

**Does not establish:** Does not establish that the overlap is a deliberate literary allusion (versus coincidental use of shared biblical idiom), nor does it establish anything about the historical circumstances the verse addresses.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The phrase may reflect common, widely-circulated biblical idiom already embedded in early-19th-century religious speech rather than a deliberate, marked allusion to Isaiah specifically.
- Joseph Smith's revelatory language elsewhere frequently echoes KJV cadence generally, so isolated phrase overlap does not by itself demonstrate intentional literary allusion to this particular verse.
