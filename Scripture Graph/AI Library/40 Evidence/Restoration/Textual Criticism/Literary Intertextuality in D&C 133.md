# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 133

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The sustained reuse and recombination of Isaiah, Revelation, Malachi, Matthew, Deuteronomy, and Acts provide strong textual evidence that the chapter deliberately frames its message through biblical prophetic and apocalyptic language.

**Does not establish:** The intertextual density does not by itself establish divine origin, fulfillment, a particular compositional process, or the literal manner in which the prophecies will occur.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The biblical language may reflect Joseph Smith's familiarity with the King James Bible and need not imply a source beyond his nineteenth-century environment.
- Believing readers may understand the correspondences as divine reapplication of earlier prophecy.
- Some repeated phrases belong to a broad biblical idiom, although the extended clusters are more specific than isolated verbal resemblance.
