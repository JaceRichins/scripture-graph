# Literary Intertextuality in D&C 112

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The concentration of close parallels to Acts, Isaiah, the Gospels, 1 Peter, and Revelation is strong evidence that the chapter is composed in a deeply King James biblical register rather than merely containing an isolated biblical allusion.

**Does not establish:** The verbal parallels do not by themselves establish divine origin, ancient authorship, plagiarism, or a particular mechanism by which the wording arose.

**Alternative explanations:**
- A revelation could be expressed through scriptural language already familiar to its recipient or recorder.
- A nineteenth-century author immersed in the King James Bible could consciously or unconsciously reproduce its diction.
- Some wording could reflect editorial or scribal shaping rather than a single-stage composition process.
