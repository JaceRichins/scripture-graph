# Verbal And Thematic Intertextuality in D&C 121

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Multiple phrases in the chapter demonstrably participate in King James Bible diction and imagery, including Job, the called-and-chosen saying, the millstone warning, the generation-of-vipers denunciation, and "kick against the pricks." This is strong evidence of biblical intertextuality and scriptural register.

**Does not establish:** Biblical intertextuality does not establish divine origin, independent antiquity, or the historical fulfillment of the chapter's promises and judgments.

**Alternative explanations:**
- Some phrases may reflect the ordinary biblical idiom of nineteenth-century English-speaking religious discourse rather than deliberate quotation at every point.
- Shared language can result from remembered scripture, conscious allusion, or editorial phrasing; verbal similarity alone cannot distinguish among these mechanisms.
