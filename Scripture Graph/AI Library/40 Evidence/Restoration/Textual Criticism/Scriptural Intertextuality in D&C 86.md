# Scriptural Intertextuality in D&C 86

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The shared parable, field imagery, mixed growth, angelic reapers, harvest, bundling, and burning constitute strong evidence of a direct literary or revelatory relationship between D&C 86:1-7 and Matthew 13's wheat-and-tares discourse.

**Does not establish:** The parallel does not by itself determine the mechanism of composition, prove a particular historical interpretation of the parable, or establish the revelation's divine origin.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The relationship may be understood as revelation restating and expanding Matthew rather than ordinary literary composition.
- Joseph Smith's familiarity with the King James Bible could account for the shared language and imagery.
- Some individual motifs could derive from broader Christian apocalyptic vocabulary, although their combined pattern strongly favors a relationship with Matthew 13.
