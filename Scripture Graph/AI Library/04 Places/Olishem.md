# Olishem

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Olishem is a geographical name found once in the canon. Abraham 1:10 associates “the plain of Olishem” with [[Potiphar's Hill]] and an altar used by a priest of Pharaoh. Abraham 1:20 later places that hill in the land of [[Ur]] of Chaldea. Within [[Abraham 1]], this geography forms part of the setting for the attempted sacrifice and deliverance of [[Abraham (patriarch)]]; Olishem has no independent narrative history. Some Latter-day Saint scholarship compares the name with the ancient toponym Ulisum or Ulišum. That proposal is investigable but disputed and does not establish a real-world identification, the narrative's historicity, or its antiquity.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
Olishem's canonical profile is confined to one episode. Abraham 1:7-13 describes human offerings performed by a priest identified with both Elkenah and [[Pharaoh]], and Abraham 1:10 associates the altar, [[Potiphar's Hill]], and the plain of Olishem. Abraham is delivered in Abraham 1:15. Abraham 1:20 retrospectively states that Potiphar's Hill was in the land of [[Ur]] of Chaldea. Olishem therefore helps localize the narrative crisis, but the text gives the place no separate action, population, political identity, or later history. It calls the feature a plain without saying whether Olishem also named a settlement, district, or people.
<!-- SG:END scripture-profile -->

## Geography & Identification
<!-- SG:BEGIN geography -->
The canon supplies relational rather than modern geography. Abraham 1:10 associates the altar and [[Potiphar's Hill]] with the head of the plain of Olishem; Abraham 1:20 places the hill in the land of [[Ur]] of Chaldea. The text gives no coordinates, distances, modern equivalent, or explicit statement that Olishem was a city.

John Gee's 2013 article “Has Olishem Been Discovered?” compares Olishem with an ancient Near Eastern toponym rendered Ulisum or Ulišum. [[Abraham, Book of (Gospel Topics)]] directs readers to that study, and [[Translation and Historicity of the Book of Abraham (Essay)]] presents the comparison as plausible while acknowledging disagreement about Ulisum's location. The proposal contains several distinct questions: whether ancient Ulišum existed, whether its location can be reconstructed, and whether it is identical with the Olishem intended by Abraham 1:10. Evidence for the first does not establish the second or third.

Many versions of the correlation also depend on locating Abraham's Ur in the north rather than at the traditional southern Mesopotamian site, adding another contested premise. No source supplied here independently connects Ulisum with Abraham, Pharaoh's priest, the altar, or Potiphar's Hill. Phonetic resemblance, uncertain vocalization, disputed geography, and the possibility of coincidence leave the identification interesting but inconclusive. Olishem's real-world location remains unknown.
<!-- SG:END geography -->
