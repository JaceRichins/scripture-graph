# Land of Bountiful

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
The [[Land of Bountiful]] is a New World Book of Mormon land associated with a city of the same name. It lies on the land-southward side of the boundary with [[Desolation]] and near passage into the land northward (Alma 22:31-33; Alma 52:9). Across the narrative it serves as a settled border region, fortified military position, shipbuilding vicinity, refuge during territorial collapse, and finally the setting of the risen Christ's ministry at a temple (Alma 53:3-5; Alma 63:5; Helaman 4:5-7; 3 Nephi 11:1-17). It must be distinguished from [[Bountiful (Old World)]], the coastal place named by Lehi's party for its fruit and wild honey (1 Nephi 17:5-7).
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
[[Alma 22]] places Bountiful south of [[Desolation]], near the small-neck geography connecting the lands southward and northward (Alma 22:31-33). [[Jershon]] is east by the sea, adjoining and south of Bountiful (Alma 27:22). These relationships give Bountiful recurring strategic importance: Amalickiah advances toward it and the land northward before [[Teancum]] stops him (Alma 51:28-35), and [[Captain Moroni]] later orders Bountiful fortified and the narrow pass secured (Alma 52:9).

After Mulek is retaken, captured Lamanites bury the dead and are compelled to construct a ditch, timber breastwork, and earthen wall around the city of Bountiful; they are then confined in the stronghold (Alma 53:1-5). The text reports this coerced labor without directly defending or condemning its justice. Other prisoners are later transferred there under strong guard (Alma 55:26). Hagoth builds and launches a ship near the Bountiful-Desolation border, into the west sea by the narrow neck (Alma 63:5). Coriantumr subsequently drives from [[Zarahemla]] toward Bountiful, but Lehi blocks him while Moronihah closes from behind (Helaman 1:23-32). During the later territorial collapse, Nephite armies retreat to Bountiful and fortify a line to preserve the north country (Helaman 4:5-7). The AD 17 defensive gathering against the Gadianton threat extends from Zarahemla toward the Bountiful-Desolation boundary (3 Nephi 3:22-24).

Bountiful's culminating canonical role is the temple setting of Christ's ministry. [[3 Nephi 11]] opens with a multitude gathered around the temple and discussing the recent transformation of the land (3 Nephi 11:1-2). The risen [[Jesus Christ]] descends, identifies himself, and invites the people to examine his wounds individually (3 Nephi 11:8-17). He then gives Nephi and eleven others authority to baptize, prescribes the ordinance, and rejects disputation about it (3 Nephi 11:18-28). The movement from defended frontier to temple gathering is a meaningful narrative connection, but the text does not explicitly declare the progression symbolic.

## Geography & Identification
<!-- SG:BEGIN geography -->
What is textually secure is relative geography, not a modern location. Bountiful is on the land-southward side of [[Desolation]] and is associated with the boundary and route into the land northward (Alma 22:31-33; Alma 52:9). [[Jershon]] adjoins it on the south (Alma 27:22), while Hagoth's launch site places part of its border near Desolation, the west sea, and the narrow neck (Alma 63:5). The text uses both land and city terminology for Bountiful, although their precise spatial or administrative relationship is not mapped (Alma 53:3-5).

Alma 22:32 describes the line between Bountiful and Desolation as a day-and-a-half journey for a Nephite 'from the east to the west sea.' It does not explicitly say 'from the east sea.' Helaman 4:7 later describes a fortified line from the west sea toward the east as a day's journey. The passages differ in wording, measurement, and context; they should not be silently harmonized or converted into fixed modern mileage without assumptions about routes, terrain, travelers, and endpoints.

Modern identification remains disputed. [[A Compendium of the Doctrines of the Gospel]] records an 1884 association between the fortified setting and the Isthmus of Panama; this is reception history, not canonical or independent geographic evidence. Hemispheric or Panama-based models, limited-geography models, and internal-only reconstructions arrange the same features differently. The text provides real constraints, but no coordinate, independently identifiable inscription, or unique set of distances that selects one modern site. See [[Book of Mormon Geography]] and [[Where did the Book of Mormon take place]] for the broader question.

The New World land must also be kept distinct from [[Bountiful (Old World)]] in [[1 Nephi 17]]. Modern Arabian-site proposals concern that earlier place, not the land adjoining Desolation. References to Bountiful, Utah, the Bountiful Utah Temple, or the adjective 'bountiful' are unrelated name matches.
