# Nauvoo

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Nauvoo was the principal Latter-day Saint gathering city in Illinois during the years 1839–1846, developed in the area previously known as Commerce on the east bank of the Mississippi River. Historical sources establish the Commerce-to-Nauvoo transition, although municipal, plat, and postal usage did not necessarily change all at once. In the canon, Nauvoo is named directly in [[D&C 124]] and [[D&C 125]]. These revelations present it as an appointed city, one settlement within a broader gathering network, and the setting for two distinct projects: a holy house associated with ordinances and a boarding house called the Nauvoo House. Nauvoo's history also includes major institutional development, [[Baptism for the Dead]], the deaths of [[Joseph Smith Jr]] and Hyrum Smith at [[Carthage]], and the 1846 exodus. These historical conclusions should be distinguished from what the canonical verses themselves explicitly state.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
[[D&C 124]] supplies Nauvoo's fullest canonical profile through commissions rather than a continuous history of the city. D&C 124:109 calls it "the city which I have appointed unto you" while directing a particular individual not to seek refuge elsewhere; this should not be expanded into a universal promise of safety. The chapter distinguishes two building projects. The holy house is associated with divine presence, priesthood ordinances, revelation, and baptism for the dead (D&C 124:27–31, 39–42). The separate boarding house is named the Nauvoo House and is intended to receive travelers, provide lodging, health, safety, and rest, and direct attention to the Lord's word and Zion (D&C 124:22–24, 60). Its investment, consent, restricted-fund, restitution, ownership, and labor provisions present the enterprise as accountable administration (D&C 124:63–72), though they may also reflect ordinary corporate practice.

[[D&C 125]] places Nauvoo within a wider gathering pattern. A settlement called Zarahemla is to be built on land opposite Nauvoo (D&C 125:3), and Nauvoo appears with Nashville and other appointed stakes as a place where prospective residents may receive an inheritance (D&C 125:4). [[D&C 127]] and [[D&C 128]] subsequently develop record keeping and theological reasoning for [[Baptism for the Dead]]. D&C 135:3 later credits Joseph Smith with gathering thousands and founding an unnamed "great city"; identifying that city as Nauvoo is historically persuasive but remains an inference rather than an explicit occurrence of the name.

## Geography & Identification
<!-- SG:BEGIN geography -->
The canonical geography is relative rather than cartographically complete. D&C 125:3 places land called Zarahemla "opposite" Nauvoo, but the verse itself names neither the Mississippi River nor Illinois and Iowa. Historical accounts identify Nauvoo with the developing Commerce-area settlement on the east bank of the Mississippi in Illinois and place Montrose and other Latter-day Saint settlements across the river in Iowa Territory; see [[Life of Heber C. Kimball]], [[‘Take Special Care of Your Family’]], and [[Organizing the Church in Nauvoo]].

The Zarahemla named in D&C 125 belongs to this nineteenth-century setting and must not be confused with the ancient [[Zarahemla]] of the Book of Mormon. The reused name supplies no evidence for locating the ancient city near Nauvoo. It may reflect devotional or typological reuse, but the revelation does not explain the naming motive. Likewise, "Commerce was renamed Nauvoo" is reliable shorthand for the settlement's history, not proof that every older plat, jurisdictional term, and postal designation changed simultaneously. The traditional association of "Nauvoo" with beauty should be treated as historical naming tradition unless supported by a verified contemporary statement and careful Hebrew lexical analysis.
