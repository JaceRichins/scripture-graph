# Sacred Grove

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
The Sacred Grove is the traditional Latter-day Saint name for woods in the Palmyra–Manchester area of New York where Joseph Smith reported praying and receiving the First Vision in early spring 1820. The canonical narrative says that he “retired to the woods” but never calls the location “Sacred Grove” (Joseph Smith—History 1:14–20). The proper name and the place’s sacred status belong to later Latter-day Saint historical and devotional interpretation: believers regard the location as sacred because of the divine appearance reported there. The narrative establishes what Joseph reported; it does not independently verify the vision or identify the precise point where he knelt. See [[First Vision]], [[First Vision (Gospel Topics)]], and [[The Life and Ministry of Joseph Smith]].
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
Joseph’s journey into the woods follows his religious uncertainty and his reading of James 1:5 (Joseph Smith—History 1:5–13). He reports selecting a secluded place, confirming that he was alone, kneeling, and attempting vocal prayer (1:14–15). The narrative then presents three turning points: hostile opposition as prayer begins (1:15–16), deliverance accompanied by a pillar of light (1:16), and the appearance of two Personages, one introducing the other as His Beloved Son (1:17). Joseph asks which religious body he should join and reports being told to join none (1:18–20). The chapter records no separate consecration of the woods; the location’s significance derives from the encounter narrated there. This canonical account is not the only firsthand account Joseph Smith wrote or dictated, so it should be read with [[Why are there multiple First Vision accounts]].

## Geography & Identification
<!-- SG:BEGIN geography -->
The broad regional setting is substantially clearer than the precise location. Joseph Smith—History records the family’s move to Palmyra and later Manchester and places the prayer in accessible woods within that family setting (Joseph Smith—History 1:3–5, 14–15). [[The Life and Ministry of Joseph Smith]] describes the traditional grove as near the home, while [[All Nations, Kindreds, and Tongues (Elder Gerrit W. Gong, October 2020)]] describes it as a few hundred yards from the Smith home. “Palmyra” is common regional shorthand, but [[Manchester (New York)]] is also important for geographic precision. The canonical narrative provides no coordinates, distinctive tree, or exact kneeling point. [[The Eternal Gift of Testimony (Elder Kevin G. Brown, October 2025)]] records Brown’s personal acknowledgment that he does not know the exact spot. That statement supports caution but is not, by itself, a comprehensive institutional or historical finding. The present grove is therefore best described as the traditionally identified broader setting; uncertainty about an exact point neither proves nor disproves the reported vision. The Missouri grove mentioned in [[26 A Holy and Consecrated Land]] belongs to a different 1838 event.
<!-- SG:END geography -->
