# Kirtland

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Kirtland, Ohio, was an early Latter-day Saint gathering and administrative center and the location of the community's first temple. The revelations describe it as a consecrated stake to Zion while distinguishing its institutions from those in the City of Zion (D&C 82:12–13; 104:47–48). Kirtland's canonical importance joins ordinary institution-building—bishopric work, farms, stores, printing, stewardships, debts, and city lots—with the promised endowment, temple dedication, and visionary claims associated with [[D&C 105]], [[D&C 109]], and [[D&C 110]]. The record also preserves moral rebuke, financial strain, relocation, and a later promise to build up Kirtland coupled with a warning of scourging (D&C 124:83). It therefore supports neither an exclusively triumphal portrait nor a reduction of Kirtland to institutional failure.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
The canonical record first presents Kirtland as a working gathering center. D&C 63:38–45 and D&C 64:21 regulate farms, a store, financial support for Zion, and a five-year Kirtland stronghold. In its Kirtland setting, [[D&C 72]] calls [[Newel K. Whitney]] as bishop for “this part of the Lord's vineyard”; D&C 82:12–13 later coordinates bishopric affairs in Kirtland and Zion and calls Kirtland a stake to Zion. D&C 89:1 locates the council that received the Word of Wisdom in Kirtland. [[D&C 94]] commands preparation of a planned stake-city “beginning at my house,” with lots for leadership, printing, and inheritances. D&C 98:19–21 then rebukes many Kirtland members for unforsaken sin, pride, covetousness, and failure to observe earlier counsel.

[[D&C 104]] gives named participants distinct Kirtland stewardships and separates the Kirtland and Zion organizations while permitting approved loans between them (D&C 104:47–52). This reorganization shows that the chapter's “everlasting order” language did not prevent alteration of a particular administrative configuration, though that conclusion is an interpretation rather than an explicit definition supplied by the chapter.

The temple material forms the record's central development. [[D&C 105]] makes empowerment part of the elders' required preparation and locates their anticipated endowment in the house commanded in Kirtland (D&C 105:11–12, 33). [[D&C 109]] records the dedicatory prayer. [[D&C 110]] then presents [[Joseph Smith Jr]] and [[Oliver Cowdery]] reporting appearances of the Lord, [[Moses (prophet)]], Elias, and [[Elijah]], connecting the temple with divine acceptance, the [[Gathering of Israel]], Abrahamic blessing, and committed keys. This is strong documentary evidence for what the canonical record claims, but not independent proof that supernatural appearances occurred. The chapter itself does not use the expressions eternal marriage, marriage sealing, or work for the dead; those applications arise through later Latter-day Saint interpretation and teaching.

Finally, [[D&C 117]] commands William Marks and Whitney to settle business, apply Kirtland properties to debts, and leave before winter, while charging servants who remained to preserve the Lord's house as holy. D&C 124:83 later combines a promise to build up Kirtland with a warning of scourging. The resulting rise, development, and contraction form a useful retrospective outline, not a timetable announced in advance by the revelations.

## Geography & Identification
<!-- SG:BEGIN geography -->
The Restoration-era Kirtland is securely identified with Kirtland in northeastern Ohio. A contemporary 1836–1838 elders-quorum record identifies Kirtland in Geauga County; the area is now in Lake County. Contemporary Church periodicals also use the dateline “Kirtland, Ohio.” A much later editorial note in History of the Church, Volume 1 associates “Kirtland Mills” with Kirtland and explains the name by reference to local mills. That note is useful secondary evidence, not a contemporary publication, and precise historical usage may distinguish the wider township from the mill-centered settlement.

The revelations speak of the land of Kirtland (D&C 82:13), the city of Kirtland (D&C 104:48), and the Kirtland stake of Zion (D&C 94:1). These are overlapping geographic and institutional descriptions rather than evidence for competing locations. D&C 109:2 includes “[Kirtland]” in the present canonical text; the brackets indicate editorially supplied clarification, while D&C 94:1 and 105:33 independently connect Kirtland with the commanded house. Exact modern boundaries for the farms, lots, businesses, and institutional properties require specialized historical mapping.

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
For Kirtland's beginnings, read [[A Mission to the Lamanites]], then [[D&C 72]], [[D&C 82]], and [[D&C 94]] with [[Newel K. Whitney]] and [[Consecration]]. Together they show how conversion, bishopric work, care for the poor, city planning, printing, commerce, and stewardships interacted.

For the temple sequence, follow [[D&C 105]], [[D&C 109]], and [[D&C 110]] with [[Kirtland Temple dedication]], [[Priesthood keys restored in the Kirtland Temple]], [[Gathering of Israel]], and [[Elijah]]. [[Joseph Smith Jr]] and [[Oliver Cowdery]] are essential because D&C 110 is presented as their joint visionary report.

For the difficult end of the period, read [[23 Every Snare]], [[Why did the Kirtland Safety Society fail]], [[D&C 117]], and [[27 We Proclaim Ourselves Free]]. These sources place the scriptural departure instructions beside the financial crisis, dissent, and organized migration without treating any one factor as a complete explanation.
