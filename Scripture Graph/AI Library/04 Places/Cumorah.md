# Cumorah

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Cumorah is the Book of Mormon land and hill where Mormon gathers the remaining Nephite forces for their last great battle and conceals the larger sacred-record archive ([[Mormon 6]]). [[Ether 15]] calls the corresponding Jaredite hill Ramah and identifies it as the place where Mormon would later hide the records. The internal Ramah–Cumorah identification is strong; identification of that ancient site with the hill near [[Manchester (New York)]] where Joseph Smith reported recovering the plates is a separate, historically important but disputed proposition. Biblical places named Ramah are unrelated Israelite locations and must not be folded into this subject.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
In [[Mormon 6]], Cumorah is chosen rather than stumbled upon: Mormon requests permission from the Lamanite king to gather there (Mormon 6:2-3). The people encamp around the hill in a land of “many waters, rivers, and fountains,” hoping for an advantage (Mormon 6:4), and the gathering is complete in the record's 384th year (Mormon 6:5). Before battle, [[Mormon (prophet-historian)]] hides the inherited archive in the hill but entrusts a few plates to [[Moroni (son of Mormon)]] (Mormon 6:6), making Cumorah a crucial site in [[Records and Sacred Writings]].

The battle is catastrophic, but the text is more exact than the shorthand “every Nephite died at Cumorah.” Mormon and twenty-three others survive the immediate battle; a few escape southward and others desert to the Lamanites (Mormon 6:11, 15). [[Mormon 8]] reports the subsequent hunting of fugitives (Mormon 8:2). Mormon's lament names sons, daughters, fathers, mothers, husbands, and wives (Mormon 6:17-20), presenting the loss as the ruin of [[Family]] and peoplehood. It ends by looking beyond decomposing bodies to [[Resurrection]] and [[Final Judgment]] (Mormon 6:21).

[[Ether 15]] supplies the earlier layer. Coriantumr's army camps at the hill Ramah, which the editor identifies as the hill where Mormon later hid the sacred records (Ether 15:11). Read with Mormon 6:6, this internally identifies Ramah with Cumorah and creates a sobering parallel between the Jaredite and Nephite endings. The parallel is textually grounded, but claims about its historical or archaeological significance require evidence beyond the narrative.
<!-- SG:END scripture-profile -->

## Geography & Identification
<!-- SG:BEGIN geography -->
The secure internal facts are limited but meaningful: Cumorah is both a land and a hill; the surrounding land has many waters, rivers, and fountains; armies can gather and camp there; and the hill can serve as a record repository (Mormon 6:2, 4, 6). Ether 15:11 establishes the hill's internal Jaredite name, Ramah. These details constrain models but do not yield modern coordinates.

Joseph Smith—History describes the plate-recovery site as a hill near Manchester, New York, without naming it Cumorah in that description (Joseph Smith—History 1:51-52). D&C 128:20 later associates “Cumorah” with Moroni and the Book of Mormon. Early and later Latter-day Saint usage consequently applied Cumorah to the New York hill; examples of that reception appear in [[Autobiography of Parley P. Pratt]], [[Key to the Science of Theology]], and [[Conference Report, April 1928 (General Conference (full report), April 1928)]]. This tradition should be reported plainly, but it should not be treated as if Mormon 6 itself named New York.

Two broad readings remain in discussion within [[Book of Mormon Geography]]. A one-hill reading identifies the ancient battlefield/archive hill and the New York recovery hill. A two-hill reading holds that Mormon deposited the large archive at an ancient Cumorah elsewhere, gave Moroni a smaller record, and that Moroni eventually deposited that smaller record in New York. The latter is textually possible because Mormon distinguishes the archive from the plates given to Moroni (Mormon 6:6), but the canon does not narrate Moroni's complete itinerary or explicitly announce two hills. Conversely, traditional nomenclature and D&C 128:20 favor an association with New York but do not independently resolve the ancient geography. The water-rich description fits western New York, yet it also fits many other regions and is therefore weak as a unique identifier. See [[Where did the Book of Mormon take place]] for the wider dispute.
<!-- SG:END geography -->
