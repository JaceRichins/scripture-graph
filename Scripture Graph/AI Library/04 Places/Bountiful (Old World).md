# Bountiful (Old World)

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Bountiful (Old World) is the fertile stretch of Arabian coastline where [[Lehi (father of Nephi)]]'s family arrived after roughly eight years of wilderness travel following their departure from Jerusalem, and where [[Nephi (son of Lehi)]] built the ship that carried the company across the ocean to the promised land (1 Nephi 17:4-8; 1 Nephi 18:1-8). The travelers named it Bountiful for its abundant fruit and wild honey, and named the adjoining sea Irreantum (1 Nephi 17:5-6). It is also the setting of the family's sharpest recorded dispute over Nephi's ability to build a seaworthy vessel, and of Nephi's response invoking the pattern of Israel's exodus from Egypt (1 Nephi 17:17-22, 23-55).

This Old World Bountiful must not be confused with the similarly named New World [[Land of Bountiful]], the site of [[Christ's appearance at the temple in Bountiful]] and of Nephite military action in Alma — an unrelated place that happens to share the same English name. Several library search results surfaced under "Bountiful" for this study — including [[The Things of Which I Know (President Gordon B. Hinckley, April 2007)]] and [[A Compendium of the Doctrines of the Gospel]] — in fact refer to that New World place or to the modern city of Bountiful, Utah, not to this location.

Where a real-world Arabian site corresponds to this Bountiful remains a matter of ongoing, disputed research among believing scholars, not a settled identification.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
The account occupies 1 Nephi 17-18. After sojourning in the wilderness for eight years following the burial of Ishmael at [[Nahom]], the company turns "nearly eastward" and reaches a fruitful seashore they call Bountiful, "because of its much fruit and also wild honey," pitching their tents beside a sea they name Irreantum (1 Nephi 16:34; 17:1, 4-6).

There Nephi is called by the Lord to go into a mountain and is commanded, "Thou shalt construct a ship, after the manner which I shall show thee" (1 Nephi 17:7-8). He is directed to a source of ore, makes a bellows from animal skins, and fashions tools (1 Nephi 17:9-11, 16) — the text does not say these materials all occurred at the immediate campsite. His brothers, named in the text only as his "brethren," mock his ability to build a ship and argue that suffering in the wilderness, or even dying in Jerusalem, would have been preferable (1 Nephi 17:17-22). Nephi answers at length, recounting the Exodus and the Lord's deliverance of Israel through Moses as evidence of God's power to sustain and to judge (1 Nephi 17:23-47), then warns them, "Touch me not, for I am filled with the power of God" (1 Nephi 17:48). They are shocked into submission; the Lord speaks to Nephi, who in turn commands his brothers, until they no longer murmur (1 Nephi 17:53-55).

Construction resumes: "we did work timbers of curious workmanship," with Nephi ascending "the mount" repeatedly, and the ship is built "not after the manner of men" (1 Nephi 18:1-3). The company loads it with fruit, meat, and abundant honey and "put[s] forth into the sea," driven by the wind toward the promised land (1 Nephi 18:6, 8). The traditional date of 589 BC sometimes attached to this crossing is a modern chronological estimate; the text itself gives only relative durations — eight years in the wilderness, an unspecified number of days at sea — not an absolute year (1 Nephi 17:4; 18:9, 23).

## Geography & Identification
<!-- SG:BEGIN geography -->
The text establishes a limited but definite profile: Bountiful lies on a seacoast reached by traveling generally eastward after leaving the vicinity of [[Nahom]]; it has much fruit, wild honey, a nearby mountain, ore accessible with divine direction, timber that can be worked into a ship, and a shoreline from which an oceangoing vessel launches (1 Nephi 16:34; 17:1, 5-11; 18:1-8). The text never names Arabia, Oman, Yemen, Dhofar, or any modern place; identifying the setting with the Arabian peninsula is a reasoned inference from the route anchored by [[Jerusalem]], the [[Red Sea]], and Nahom, not a textual statement.

Among researchers who treat the Book of Mormon as a historical record, two locations on Oman's Dhofar coast are the leading proposed candidates: Khor Kharfot, at the mouth of Wadi Sayq, and Khor Rori, farther east. Both are fertile inlets with features broadly consistent with the text's description, and researchers such as Warren P. Aston have argued for Khor Kharfot over Khor Rori — though the comparison remains a live, disputed question even within that research community, and no inscription, artifact, or excavation independently ties either site to the events of 1 Nephi 17-18. The fit is best treated as consistent with, rather than confirming, a historical journey.

Critics note that fertile coastal enclaves exist at more than one point along southern Arabia — a region anciently known as "Arabia Felix," in classical geography, in contrast to the desert interior — so a workable environmental match is not by itself surprising or specific, and some proposed correlations risk selecting criteria after the fact to fit a favored site. See [[Book of Mormon Geography]] and [[Where did the Book of Mormon take place]] for the wider methodological debate, and [[Is Nahom evidence for Lehi's journey]] for the analogous case of the preceding waypoint on which reconstructions of this route depend.

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
Read [[1 Nephi 17]] and [[1 Nephi 18]] together — they carry the entire episode from arrival to departure. [[Nahom]] is the preceding named waypoint that anchors the eastward route to Bountiful, and [[Is Nahom evidence for Lehi's journey]] shows the same kind of geographic-correlation argument applied to that better-known case. [[Voyage to the promised land]] continues directly from Bountiful's shore, and [[Lehi's departure from Jerusalem]] marks the start of the wilderness sojourn that ends there. [[Nephi (son of Lehi)]] and [[Lehi (father of Nephi)]] are the central figures at Bountiful; Nephi's response to his brothers draws on the pattern of [[Exodus and Deliverance]]. [[Book of Mormon Geography]] and [[Where did the Book of Mormon take place]] frame the broader debate over turning the text's internal descriptions into real-world identifications. Finally, note [[Land of Bountiful]] and [[Christ's appearance at the temple in Bountiful]] — an unrelated New World place and event that share this location's name and are easy to conflate with it.
