# Zarahemla

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Zarahemla is both a person-name and the name of a people, land, and city in the Book of Mormon. [[Omni 1]] presents its people as descendants of migrants from [[Jerusalem]] who had lost inherited records and whose language had changed; Mosiah I's refugee community teaches them its language, joins with them, and establishes a united kingdom (Omni 1:12–19). Zarahemla thereafter becomes a principal demographic, religious, governmental, and military center of the Nephite narrative. These are claims made within the Book of Mormon; they establish the text's internal portrait but do not by themselves identify an archaeological site.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## In Scripture
<!-- SG:BEGIN scripture-profile -->
Zarahemla first appears as the unexpected destination of Mosiah I's departure from the [[Land of Nephi]]. Its inhabitants rejoice over the newcomers' brass plates, report an origin at Jerusalem, and preserve a genealogy from memory despite their ancestors having brought no records (Omni 1:12–18). After language instruction, the peoples unite and Mosiah becomes king (Omni 1:18–19). The union is not portrayed as a simple demographic takeover: [[Mosiah 25]] says the descendants of Nephi were fewer than the people of Zarahemla, that the groups assembled in two bodies, and that Zarahemla's people were afterward numbered with the Nephites (Mosiah 25:2–4, 13).

The land becomes a gathering place and major religious and governmental center. [[King Benjamin]] summons the population to the temple (Mosiah 1:18). Limhi's people and [[Alma the Elder]]'s people later reach Zarahemla after separate deliverances from bondage (Mosiah 22:13–14; Mosiah 24:23–25). Mosiah has their histories read publicly, and Alma organizes multiple congregations because the population cannot be taught in one assembly (Mosiah 25:5–6, 19–23). [[Alma the Younger]] later calls the Zarahemla church to repentance and regulates it before continuing to [[Gideon (land)]] (Alma 5:1–14; Alma 6:1–7).

Centrality does not make the city secure. Zarahemla supplies troops and provisions during the wars and receives prisoners (Alma 57:6, 11, 15–16; Alma 58:3–4). King-men seize it before Moroni and Pahoran restore control (Alma 61:3–4; Alma 62:6–11). Political contention later leaves it inadequately guarded, allowing Coriantumr's army to capture the city—the 'strongest hold in all the land'—before its advance into the interior exposes the army to encirclement (Helaman 1:17–31). Another dissenter-Lamanite coalition subsequently captures Zarahemla and drives Nephite forces toward Bountiful (Helaman 4:5–8).

The city also frames prophetic confrontation. [[Samuel the Lamanite]], expelled and refused entry, preaches from its wall (Helaman 13:2–4). Zarahemla burns during the destruction at Christ's death and is later rebuilt (3 Nephi 8:8; 4 Nephi 1:8). Mormon still places Zarahemla and the waters of Sidon in the final-era landscape (Mormon 1:6, 10). Its textual arc is therefore one of migration, union, gathering, institutional centrality, internal fracture, conquest, warning, destruction, and rebuilding.
<!-- SG:END scripture-profile -->

## Geography & Identification
<!-- SG:BEGIN geography -->
The canon supplies relative relationships, not coordinates or a unique modern identification. The river [[Sidon]] runs by the land of Zarahemla (Alma 2:15). Minon is 'above' Zarahemla in the direction of the land of Nephi, and movements from Gideon toward the city help place it within that regional network (Alma 2:24–26). A narrow strip of wilderness separates the [[Land of Nephi]] from Zarahemla, while wilderness populations also live west of Zarahemla near the seashore (Alma 22:27–28). Zarahemla belongs to the land southward and is related within the larger description to [[Desolation]], Bountiful, and the land northward (Alma 22:30–32).

These observations constrain an internal map but do not determine a modern site. Alma 22:32's day-and-a-half journey describes the line between Bountiful and Desolation, not the width of Zarahemla. Likewise, language about traveling 'up' toward Nephi or 'down' toward Zarahemla may reflect elevation, conventional travel idiom, direction, or some combination; it should not automatically be converted into latitude or compass bearings.

Modern identifications remain disputed. A nineteenth-century sermon preserved in Journal of Discourses, Volume 15 proposed a location near the Magdalena River in northern South America. That is evidence for the history of interpretation, not a canonical identification or archaeological confirmation. Other modern models likewise must be judged by how well they fit the internal topology and external evidence; internal consistency alone cannot establish a Mesoamerican, South American, Heartland, or other location. See [[Book of Mormon Geography]] and [[Where did the Book of Mormon take place]].

Nineteenth-century Latter-day Saint records also mention a separate settlement called Zarahemla near Nauvoo. That namesake must not be merged with the Book of Mormon land or used as evidence for its ancient location.
<!-- SG:END geography -->

## Related
<!-- SG:BEGIN related -->
Begin with [[Omni 1]] for Mosiah I's migration, the condition and origins of Zarahemla's people, language instruction, union, and the engraved-stone account. Continue with [[Mosiah 25]] for demographic integration, public reading of records, and church organization. [[King Benjamin]], [[Alma the Elder]], and [[Alma the Younger]] show the city as a temple, covenant, and ecclesiastical center.

For movement between regions, read [[Land of Nephi]], [[Zeniff]], [[Limhi]], and [[Mosiah 7]]. For geography, compare [[Sidon]], [[Gideon (land)]], [[Desolation]], [[Book of Mormon Geography]], and [[Where did the Book of Mormon take place]]. For political and spiritual vulnerability, read [[Helaman 1]], [[Government and Laws]], [[War and Peace]], and [[Samuel the Lamanite]]. [[Destruction at Christ's death in America]] completes the city's destruction-and-rebuilding arc.
