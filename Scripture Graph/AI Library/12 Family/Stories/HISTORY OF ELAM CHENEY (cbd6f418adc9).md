# HISTORY OF ELAM CHENEY

_A story of [[Mehitable Wells (KN6W-2R1)|Mehitable Wells]], [[Aaron Cheney (KNX4-J2Z)|Aaron Cheney]]._

## The story as it was written

HISTORY OF ELAM CHENEY - 16 May 1825 - 22 March 1912
 "My Grandfather" By Aaron Linden Cheney
(Information in this text is taken from records of some of the other children of Grandfather that I have collected, and from my own records and scraps of newspaper clippings, etc.; also from stories told to me by him.)
He was born the son of Aaron Cheney and Mehitable Wells. Aaron Cheney, the father of Elam, was born in Berwich, Providence Co., Maine, July 14, 1787. When but a small boy his father moved to Cranby, Vermont. When he was twenty-six or seven, he met and married Mehitable Wells, and was a prosperous farmer and later the owner of a foundry where a number of men were employed.
 His mother Mehitable Wells, was born in Bolton, Laneshire, England, January 1, 1787 (Family group sheet has January 31 and place of birth as Amenia, Dtchas, New York.) Although a very frail woman, she was a capable and industrious housekeeper and a faithful wife and mother.
Both his father and mother were religiously inclined and were faithful members of the Methodist Church until they were visited by the Prophet Joseph Smith and Orson Pratt, who taught them the gospel of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints. They were baptized members of this church in the year 1831. In 1835, they moved to Kirtland, Ohio where their son Elam was baptized in the Shegran River by Elder D. Carter.
They purchased a farm which. was located about two miles southeast of where the Kirtland Temple was built. There the father put his boys to work on the farm. Elam was too young to do much heavy work, but learned not only how to manage the farm but also a great deal in how to handle iron and work at the blacksmith forge in the foundry which his father built. Circumstances that occurred in this place made a deep impression on the young boy's mind.
 As a boy he removed from Freedom, New York to Ohio; learned the cooper's trade and studied medicine and surgery. He also helped as a boy with some of the work on the Kirtland Temple. At this time, notice was given that there was a meeting held in the temple. All the family attended this meeting. The Prophet spoke there and Elam noticed that while he was speaking, he looked up as if he was looking at someone, so Elam looked in that direction and saw six personages .... three on each side of the room. Their appearance attracted his attention as they were dressed so differently to what he was accustomed to seeing. They had white moccasins on their feet and white caps on their heads. He asked his mother who they were and she said she could see no one. He told her they were dressed in white. He always remembered how they looked and how they were dressed. Later on when he went to the temple to get his own endowments, he discovered that the clothes these personages wore were like the temple clothes. This experience was a testimony which stayed with him throughout his life.
The Cheney family left Kirtland in July 1838 with the company known as "Kirtland Camp" intending to join the saints at Nauvoo. But owing to sickness, they were not able to continue the journey, so stopped temporarily at Decatur, Macon Co., Illinois. While detained at this place, they were to work and fence a piece of ground, build a small house, and raise a crop. It was while they were living at Decatur that Joseph the Prophet started his memorable trip to Washington for the purpose of laying grievances of the Saints before the President of the United States. As Joseph was passing through Decatur, he stopped and spent the night at the Cheney home. Elam who was 14 at the time was very much impressed by the conversation of the Prophet. As soon afterward as they could, the Cheney family continued their journey to Nauvoo. While in Nauvoo, Elam played in a game of baseball with Joseph Smith.
Elam was present when the cornerstone of the Nauvoo Temple was laid on April 6, 1841. While the temple was being built the young men were given an opportunity to work out their tithing by working on the temple so Elam worked every tenth day and sometimes oftener on the temple.
Later, Elam joined the Nauvoo Legion under General Joseph Smith and Captain Stout. He was ordained a Seventy and belonged to the Nineteenth Quorum. He took an active part in all the various organizations of the church. He often related how at many times his life was spared by the power of the Lord. At one time, he was employed to help move a barn some distance up the Missouri River by means of a large flat boat. A rope was tied to the boat and a horse on the bank pulled the boat up the river. In some way Elam was thrown overboard and was almost drowned before they could get him out of the water. He caught hold of the boat and some of them seeing his hands, drew him in. He was almost frozen before he could be taken to a house. He said, "It was through the power of the Lord I was saved."
Elam also related how he nearly lost his life trying to cross the Mississippi River on the ice. As he neared the middle of the river, he found the ice had thawed and broken in great blocks. At first, the blocks were close together but as he went on, the spaces between them became wider. All at once he found himself on a block of ice which had moved with his weight so he could neither step back nor forward. He thought he would run and jump as far as he could, as that would be his only chance but as he leaped, his foot slipped and he fell with only the upper part of his body on the ice. His foot and legs were hanging down in the swift running river. How he ever got upon the block of ice he never knew, but it was only through the power of the Lord that he did not go down amid the ice.
While in Nauvoo he received his Patriarchal blessing under the hands of Patriarch John Smith. In this blessing he was told he should soon find a companion and they should receive their endowments in the Nauvoo Temple. This was literally fulfilled. He made the acquaintance of Hannah Compton, born in Worchestershire, England March 27, 1804, (and died at Fairview, Sanpete County, Utah, July 21, 1884). She was left with a boy from a former marriage, and had no way to care for herself and her child. Even though she was 20 years older than grandfather, (he was just past 19 and she was 40 years old) he took her as his wife upon the request of Brigham Young and cared for her and her child. They were married on October 15, 1844 and received their endowments in the Nauvoo Temple.
As mob violence increased and the saints were driven from their homes in Nauvoo, Brother Cheney shared all the hardships endured by the saints. He was at Nauvoo and saw the Prophet leave for Carthage and heard him make the remark, "I am going like a lamb to the slaughter". He was at the meeting in Nauvoo when Governor Boggs was present while the tragedy was being enacted at Carthage and was among those who left Nauvoo to accompany the bodies of the Prophet and his brother Hyrum to the city. He was present at the funeral of the martyred brothers. He also attended the meeting when President Brigham Young took the stand and made manifest to the people that he was the legal successor of the Prophet Joseph. He saw the mantle of the Prophet appear upon Brigham Young at the time he addressed the congregation telling them of his succession to the Presidency.
Being a blacksmith and carpenter, Elam made his own wagon for the journey westward, coming in the second company of pioneers. This company was organized at Far West, Missouri on May 16, 1847. Jedediah M. Grant was captain. The company was sub-divided into companies of fifty and companies of ten. Each company was under a captain. Brother Cheney was in the company under John Vance. This company endured many hardships. The men took turns standing guard.
One morning after a night guard, Brother Cheney came into camp very tired and hungry, so he thought he would eat breakfast before yoking up his cattle. After breakfast he found one of his oxen missing. He tried to find it in camp, but failed. He supposed it had strayed away and he went in search of it. Not finding it, he returned to camp. The company had gone on and his wife was left alone on the prairie. He was afraid to linger behind on account of the hostile Indians, so he yoked up his cow and two oxen and took the place of the missing oxen himself. They journeyed on, overtaking the company as they were leaving the place they had stopped at noon. He was informed that one of the captains had his ox and was working him. He got his ox and they continued their journey. At Green River, he was stricken with mountain fever and was sick the remainder of the journey.
The company arrived in Salt lake Valley, October 5, 1847. Here Elder Cheney lived in the old fort until he built a log house outside the fort. As they arrived too late to raise any crops, they had only what provisions they had left from the store provided for the journey westward. Food became scarce and the people were compelled to dig up roots and boil rawhide for food.
Elam was a shoemaker and many times he traded a pair of shoes for a pint of corn. On one occasion while making shoes, a group of men came in for grandfather to read their hands. When looking at one of the men's hand, he looked up and said, "you are a murderer". The man was going to kill grandfather, but the other men held him and took him away. A few days after this happened, officers were sent, from the East and picked up those men and took them back for murder. Brigham Young then told grandfather he had a wonderful gift and he thought it was all right to use it among the people but not to tell them anything he thought would cause trouble.
His first and only child by Hannah Compton Cheney was a daughter, Matilda Cheney born February 06, 1849, 16 months after they arrived in Salt lake City. They moved from Salt Lake to Centerville in the spring of 1849 (Davis County). Here, he secured a farm, built a home and helped to build up the place. Then he moved to Kaysville, Davis County, Utah in 1860, or about that time the child was 12 years old.
They stayed with Hannah's parents a short time, then moved to Springville, Utah the same year. While in Springville, Elam helped build a fort as a protection against the Indians and took an active part in defense of the colony.
Elam married Margaret Wimmer Johnson, a second wife in May 1851. She was the widow of Hugh George Johnson and she was born at Quincy, Illinois, May 24, 1829. She had two sons, Rufus and George Johnson. There were eleven children born to Elam and Margaret. Elizabeth Ellen Cheney Sanders (mother of Lee W. Sanders) was their third child. Elam's wife Margaret died at Kimberly, Idaho, May 19, 1908.
His third wife was Talitha Avery Garlick, February 13, 1855. She was born September 22, 1824 and had a son, William by a previous marriage. She died April 17, 1902.
He, married a fourth wife, Martha Taylor, endowed June 23, 1866. She was born November 27, 1828 and died August 13, 1908.
His fifth wife was Harriet Hedgehill married July 7, 1866. She was born at Burnington, England on October 5, 1847. His last four marriages were solemnized in the endowment house at Salt Lake City. Brigham Young was instrumental in all five marriages.
In 1857 Elam moved to a place which later became known as Cheney's Ranch and went into the stock business. He became a friend to the Indians. This protected his wives and children in the Indian raids. He and his five wives lived in the same log house. All ate at the same table and the women made their dresses from the same bolt of cloth.
Will W. Brady writes in the history of his grandmother, Martha Taylor that grandfather Elam was asked by President Young to go to Fairview and build a grist mill. He rode to Fairview on horseback in 1867 in search of a suitable place for the mill. With the purchase of 80 acres along Sanpitch River, his search came to an end and he made preparations for a home and the building of the mill. During that summer, he brought stones from Cottonwood Canyon for the butts which he built himself east of what is now Murray, Utah. In 1868 he moved to North Bond, now known as Fairview in Sanpete County, Utah. He had a good farm and owned the first grist mill which he ran himself. (unsifted flour) Soon thereafter he added roller sifters, which made it possible to separate the clear white flour from the bran. The profits from his operations were taken as a toll from the wheat brought to be ground into flour and his portion traded to the settlers for cash or store script or other needed commodities.
At one time while the mill was in operation, the people were having a hard time because of crop failure. Elam had a barrel of meal on his porch so the people could come and help themselves without cost whenever they were in need, and, for some reason, this barrel never went empty.
William, the son of Talitha, decided at the age of 17 or 18 to leave the farm and seek employment elsewhere in order to make more money. He went to San Bernardino, California and got in with a bunch of ruffians and a murder was committed. The blame was put onto William. Grandfather being notified of his being in jail, didn't believe the accusation saying he was too good a boy, so he saddled his horse and rode to San Bernardino. Disguised as a ruffian, he found out who committed the murder. He went to the law about it and the man confessed. He was able to bring William home with him.
In 1877 Elam sold his mill to the partnership of Bishop Petersen, Ed Terry, Willis Howell and Peter Sundwall. The stones Elam used in the mill have been made into a monument relic on the main street in Fairview, Utah.
The law would not leave grandfather alone (regarding the plurality of wives) so that is why he moved Martha Taylor on the corner where Aaron Cheney now lives (at that writing) and she took care of Hannah, his first wife until she passed away. Harriet lived a block and a half south of Aaron Cheney's by Cottonwood Creek. Margaret stayed with him at the mill until he moved.
Elam moved to Payson, Utah long enough to build a brick home, then he moved to Salt River, Arizona, then to Tonto Creek, Arizona where he remained one year. In 1884 he located at Pinedale, Apache County, Arizona, then known as the Black Forest. Margaret and Harriet journeyed south and met grandfather in Pinedale. A few years later, Margaret went to Idaho to meet Elam Jr., Peter, Frank and Theodore, her children and died at Kimberly, Idaho, May 19, 1908. After ten years, Elam moved to Fruitland, New Mexico (1894) and later returned to his home at Pinedale where he lived until death summoned him.
In 1897 Elam attended the Pioneer Jubilee in Salt Lake City and received a gold medal as a pioneer of 1847. Five months before his death, he attended the Cheney Family Reunion at Burley, Idaho (1910). Here grandfather gathered his large family around him and bore a strong testimony to the divinity of the mission of the Prophet Joseph Smith, having been personally acquainted with the prophet from the year 1831 to the time of his martyrdom.
Uncle Frank Cheney played the violin and grandfather step-danced for us and he also read some of his grandchildren's hands, which some I will relate: He told Tina (Clementine Cheney), Theodore's daughter that she would get a letter of proposal. She got it the next day and showed it to the rest of us. He told Lillian Cheney Wadsworth that she was going to walk through the forest and pick up the crookedest stick she could find. He (the stick) would be dark complexioned and they would have five or six children. She said he wasn't the crookedest stick, but he does have a dark complexion and she does (at this writing) have five living children and another one she lost at birth. He told me (Aaron Cheney) that I would get a nice little plump girl, just a little darker than myself. He also said I would have to go north to get her and I did, six miles.
Grandfather returned to his home in Arizona where he died as stated above, 22 March 1912, at the age of 86 years and 10 months. His living children at the time were Matilda Anderson, Louisa Sanders (wife of David Sanders, Lee W. brother), Elam, Olive Humble, David, Elizabeth Sanders (Lee W. Sanders' mother), Sealer, William, Peter, Frank, Theodore, Ezekiel, Margaret Brewer, Hyrum and Charles. He was the father of 40 Children.
Elam Cheney held a number of positions in the Church. He loved to wrestle and never was outrun in a foot race. He had a fine personality and many friends wherever he went. His whole life was that of a pioneer, enduring hardships and meeting difficulties. In the practice of medicine and surgery, he was a helper to many, especially to one George Bryant who had been given up as a hopeless case, but whose broken and disjointed neck Elam set and saved his life.
Elam and his wives lived the Gospel of Jesus Christ as they understood it and taught their children to follow its teachings.
