When Caroline was six-years-old, her father decided to move his family to America to better their economic prospects. They sailed on the vessel, “Niord,” from Gothenburg in Sweden and arrived in New York harbor on August 15, 1837.
They traveled first to Chicago, Illinois, for a short time, then on to a farming section near Ottowa, LaSalle County, Illinois. Sometime after 1840, they moved to Lee County, Iowa, where an Elder of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints brought them the gospel in the year of 1843.
After joining the Church, they moved to Keokuk, Illinois. In 1846, the family sold their farm in Keokuk in order to leave the Nauvoo Area for Garden Grove, Iowa, where a resting camp had been built. They proceeded to Council Bluffs in 1847. They went to a place called Plumb Hollow where they lived for a year and a half. The Hogan family was part of Brigham Young’s Second division as they traveled to the mountains of the west under Zera Pulsipher. They arrived in Salt Lake Valley on September 21, 1848, settling in South Bountiful which became Woods Cross, Utah.
Caroline married Christian Larsen Hyer and they settled in a little log house near her parents. While Christian farmed and hauled wood from the canyon for fuel or building, Caroline cared for the house, spun their yarn, made clothing, gardened and raised the children. Caroline accepted the hard role of sharing her husband with her sister, Lovina.
In 1859, they were sent to Cache Valley to see if it was suitable for settlers. Caroline was an excellent cook and kept her home neat and orderly. She had a talent for fine handwork. She did beautiful embroidery work. She braided staw, made hats, and trimmed them. She also made caps of jean material for her family. The cloth for the children’s dresses and suits was spun and woven by her. She raised trees with delicious tasting apples.
Her husband passed away in September of 1901. She was a widow for sixteen years. She was generous in having others come to live with her. When she could no longer care for herself, she went to live with her daughter, Ellen Titensor in Cove.
