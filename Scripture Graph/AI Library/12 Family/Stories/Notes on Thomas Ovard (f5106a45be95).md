# Notes on Thomas Ovard

_A story of [[Thomas Ovard (KWJD-SCG)|Thomas Ovard]], [[Hannah Stowe (KWJD-SCL)|Hannah Stowe]]._

## The story as it was written

Compiled by Marguerite Smith Burbidge from conversation with Mary Ann (Polly) Smith, her aunt.
Thomas and Hannah Ovard had seven children: Joe, George, John, William, Jake, Esther, and Mary Ann.
Thomas Ovard was a hostler on a farm in England. He was the envy of everyone for the fine horses he cared for. He stole beans at night to feed his horses.
He had a large tumor on the front of his leg. It was never operated on. He had a sling made to carry it in.
Father (Enoch Smith) lived with his Grandma Ovard in American Fork about three years when he was between eight and eleven years old (approximately). He tookcare of the team and helped with the cows. Then quite early he and the neighbor children went trudging through the snow for about two miles to the school house. They pushed upon the window and got the ax and chopped cedat wood and made a big fire in the stove and the school house was nice and warm when the teacher came.
His grandpa [Thomas Ovard] was a wonderful farmer and had gooseberries, red currants, etc.
Enoch later came to Salt Lake to live with his mother. He cried when he left his grandparents in American Fork because his grandpa let him drive the horses.
Thomas and Hannah Stowe Ovard came to Utah on the 5 September, 1860, in the Samuel D. White Company. Their son, William Ovard, was born in Warwickshire England 23 Dec 1850. Thomas Ovard's parents were William Ovard and Mary Fletcher Ovard.
