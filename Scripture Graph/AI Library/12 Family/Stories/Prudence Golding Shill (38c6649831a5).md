# Prudence Golding Shill

_A story of [[Robert Chapel Shill (KWJ7-586)|Robert Chapel Shill]], [[Prudence Golding (LZPH-MQM)|Prudence Golding]]._

## The story as it was written

Robert Chaple was born 7th June 1789 at Colesborne, Gloucestershire, England. He was the natural son of Mary Shill and Robert Chaple. He was christened 14 June 1789 at Colesborne, Gloucestershire, England.
Robert had one sister, Ann Shill. She was christened 17 September 1786 at Colesborne, Gloucestershire, England.
Little is know of Robert Chaple, father of the two children. It is believed he was a seaman on a ship out of London. but of this we do not know for certain.
Mary's children took the name of Shill. Robert was but three years of age when his mother Mary, married Thomas Lawrence. They married on the 30th September 1792 at Colesborne; they were sealed in marriage on 12th October 1894 in the Logan Temple. Both children, Ann and Robert, were sealed to their mother and Mr. Lawrence on 21st January 1954 in the Salt Lake Temple.
Robert as a young man with a winning smile met and fell in love with Prudence Golding. She was also of Colesborne. After a period of courtship, Robert, age 20, and Prudence, about 19, were married on the 30th of May 1809 at Syde, Gloucestershire, England. Prudence was one of eight children and was born about 1787 at Shipton, Gloucestershire, England. She was christened on 14th of October 1787, the daughter of Robert John and Joan Mary Perry Golding.
Robert and Prudence lived in a small farming community all of their lives and raised their family of 12 children on a farm as stated by their son, Charles Golding. The 1851 Colesborne census records listed Robert as an agriculture-laborer, age 62, and Prudence his wife age 61 (could also have been 64).
When the missionaries of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints preached the gospel, the family being much impressed and convinced of the truthfulness of this new religion, their two sons, George and Charles were baptized on 13 May 1846. Prudence entered the waters of baptism on 11 July 1846, and Robert was baptized 7 months later, 2 February 1847. Both parents were baptized by their son, Charles, who later baptized other members of the family.
The Shill family were very active in the Church and desirous of sharing the joys and blessings of the gospel with others. Their home was open at all times to the missionaries and converts and many meetings were held there.
Grandmother Prudence's greatest concern was always for her family. A letter written to her son, Charles Golding, in London about 1850 were he had gone with his wife and child to seek employment, showed great concern for his welfare and that of his family. Thanking him for the love and concern of his parents, his kindness and thoughtfulness to other members of his family. She also mentions Drew Bird and Charles Richins who later married her two daughters. Prudence died on 13 September 1851 soon after writing this letter.
After the death of his wife, Grandfather Robert was welcomed into the home of his son, Charles and family, where he was cared for and had a good home. It had always been his desire to journey to America to join his sons and daughters and the Saints who were already in Zion. So, when Charles and family sailed from England, Grandfather Robert went with them.
They set sail on the ship Chimboraizo, April 1855, landing in Philadelphia in May. They traveled by railroad to Pittsburgh then down the Ohio and up the Mississippi River to St. Louis. Their dream of joining the Saints in Zion had to be postponed due to lack of funds. In St. Louis, Charles found work as a carpenter and provided for his little family. His work took him away from home and he returned only on weekends. One Monday morning as Charles was leaving for work, Grandfather bade him good-bye saying, "I shall never see thee again my son." When Charles returned home that weekend, Robert had passed away, 6 October 1855. Just six months after leaving his beloved England and four years after his wife and sweetheart of 42 years and mother of his 12 children had been laid to rest.
His dream of joining the Saints in Zion was finally realized. He was buried in St. Louis, Missouri.
