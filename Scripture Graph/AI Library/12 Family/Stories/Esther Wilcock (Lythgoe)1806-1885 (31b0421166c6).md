# Esther Wilcock (Lythgoe)1806-1885

_A story of [[Esther Wilcock (LB89-VQR)|Esther Wilcock]]._

## The story as it was written

ESTHER WILCOCK (LYTHGOE)
1806 ---------- 1885
Written by Esther Lythgoe Robinson
Grand-daughter
Esther Wilcock was born in April, 1806 at Hindley Lancashire, England, a daughter of James Wilcock (born 1775 at Wigan, Lancashire, England; died 1851, England) and Ann Ramsden Wilcock (born 1772 at Wigan, Lancashire, England; died 1849, England).
Esther was a small, dainty woman. She was a loving wife and mother and very devoted to her children. She married Thomas Lythgoe, the son of Joseph and Betty Ainsworth Lythgoe (both born about 1753 at Leigh, Lancashire, England). Thomas died about 1803 and Esther died in 1838.
Esther W. and Thomas Lythgoe were converted to the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints in the Pendlebury Branch in the year 1840 by the preaching of Apostle Parley P. Pratt. They also attended meetings in Carpenters Hall, Manchester, England. They always kept an open house for the elders on missions and were faithful and devout members of the Church. They were acquainted with Apostles Brigham Young, John Taylor, Orson Hyde and many others.
Esther was the mother of a large family, six daughters and three sons: Lucy, Joseph, Betty, John, Ann, Ellen, James, Elizabeth and Rachel. Betty died when only four years old. Elizabeth was always called Betty. Thomas Lythgoe did not come to America as he was afraid to travel on the ocean. He was born July 12th, 1804 at Leigh, Lancashire, England and died September 30, 1887 in England. Their son James wrote in his diary: “My Father came to see me a few days before I set sail for America and I accompanied him to the Bolton Station and gave him half sovereign 10 shillings as I parted with him. My Mother held means for his emigration until the last but he would not go”.
Esther Wilcock Lythgoe set sail for America on the sailing vessel, “Monarch of the Sea”, in the spring of 1864. Her daughter Ann Lythgoe Booth and Ann’s two little girls Lucy and Ellen came with her. Ann’s husband John Booth was already in America awaiting the arrival of his wife and children. Esther probably felt she needed to help Ann with the two little girls in crossing the ocean and the plains to Utah. After their sailing vessel landed they camped for three weeks near the Missouri River waiting for still more relatives to come on another ship so they could cross the plains together. Among those they were waiting for was James Lythgoe (Esther’s son) and his first wife Martha Hellis, who came on the sailing vessel “General McClellan”. During the course of their journey to Utah they crossed the Black Hills and then came through what is now Evanston, Wyoming. They then traveled down Chalk Creek into Coalville, Utah where Ann’s husband John Booth and Uncle John Lythgoe were waiting for them.
Five of Esther’s children emigrated to Utah. Her son Joseph was suffocated in a mine explosion at Lydsley Banks, England, where he was employed in the coal mines. This happened December 8, 1858 and he was buried in Street Gate Church yard, England. John Lythgoe worked in the coal mines in Coalville where he was killed September 23, 1867. This left her son James as the only surviving son. He lived most of his life in Henefer, Utah, as did two sisters Rachel Lythgoe Fowler and Elizabeth (Betty) Lythgoe Dearden. They all had large families. Esther Wilcock Lythgoe lived most of the time with one or the other of her children and is buried in Henefer, Utah. Ann settled in Coalville, Utah with her husband John Booth and they also had several children.
Very often Esther W. (grandmother), who was always active, would walk the nine or ten miles to visit with her daughter Ann Booth in Coalville. She would follow the Weber River through the river bottoms. As she was nearing eighty years of age it must have been quite a journey for her. In July of 1885, her daughters and son James became very anxious and worried about her. She was neither at Coalville nor had she returned to Henefer. They all began searching and praying that she would be found. In those days there were still many wild animals in that area and the river would also be very dangerous to an elderly woman walking alone. Finally she was found lying beneath some trees beside the river. She probably just laid down to rest and then died. She is buried in the Henefer Cemetery overlooking the Weber River and the beautiful valley of Henefer. Her son James shaped a flat sand rock into a headstone and carved upon it: “Esther, Beloved Mother of James Lythgoe”. This headstone is still there marking the grave of an early pioneer. She died July 23, 1885, and left a large number of descendants.
