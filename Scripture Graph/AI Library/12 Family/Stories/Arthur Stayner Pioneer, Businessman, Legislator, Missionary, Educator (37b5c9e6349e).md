# Arthur Stayner Pioneer, Businessman, Legislator, Missionary, Educator

_A story of [[Emma Irene Turner (KWJ6-XX3)|Emma Irene Turner]], [[Arthur Stayner Sr. (KWJD-CW9)|Arthur Stayner Sr.]]._

## The story as it was written

Arthur Stayner Pioneer, Businessman, Educator, Legislator, Missionary, Educator.
Arthur Stayner was born in 1835 and raised on the family estate on Guernsey Island England. He was the son of Thomas Colley and Elizabeth Pill Stayner. Arthur was educated in English boarding schools from age 5 to 17. He was by-lingual English and French. He also learned Greek and Latin.
In 1850 at age 15 Arthur joined the Mormon Church with his family and at age 17 was called on a special mission to Paris by Apostle and later Church President John Taylor where he helped translate The Book of Mormon into French.
In 1855 Arthur immigrated with his family to Utah.
In 1857 Arthur married Emma Louise Turner and settled in Farmington where they had nine children.
Arthur built a home in Farmington, the first with indoor plumbing. The home is now on the National Historical Register. Arthur hosted many visiting dignitaries for the LDS Church. He helped build the Farmington Ward Meeting House, home of the First Primary, where he served in the Bishopric for 21 years. With his brother Charles, Arthur organized and played in a territorial band.
Arthur was an Entrepreneur/Businessman and Secretary/Treasurer of the Railroad. He kept Railroad Company cash in a vault in his Farmington home. Arthur is also recognized as a Horticulturalist and Father of the Utah Sugar Industry. He formed Utah Idaho Sugar Company with the support of the LDS Church and 20 shareholders.
Arthur was a Territory Legislator representing Davis and Morgan Counties and Chief Clerk of the Territorial Government. He helped draft the Charter for Utah Statehood.
Arthur was an educator and Davis County’s first school superintendent.
Arthur died at age 64 in 1899 of blood poisoning. He is buried alongside his wife Emma Turner in the Salt Lake City Cemetery.
