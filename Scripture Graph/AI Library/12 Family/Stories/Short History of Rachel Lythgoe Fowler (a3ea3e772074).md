# Short History of Rachel Lythgoe Fowler

_A story of [[Rachel Lythgoe (KWNL-J72)|Rachel Lythgoe]]._

## The story as it was written

History of Rachel Lythgoe Fowler
This story of Grandma Fowler was given to daughter Thelma Nichols by her Mother Mary Ann (Polly), daughter of Grandma Fowler on her 69th birthday, April 1. Original handwritten document in possession of Shanna Schaefermeyer, great-great granddaughter.
Rachel Lythgoe, born 1847, 29th of May in Pendlebury, Lancashire, England. Daughter of Thomas and Esther Wilcox Lythgoe. In the family was James (Uncle James) Lythgoe—only boy we knew, who came to America. The daughters: Lucy, Ellen, Betty, Rachel, Ann (Booth). The boys were John and Joe. Joe never left England; killed in mine leaving two children. John, the oldest, came to America first and was here sometime before the rest came; but lived in Missouri then came to Utah. From time to time, he sent money to the folks to come to America. He had some money and somehow he found out he was going to be robbed (while he was working in Virginia). They (the robbers) searched everywhere, stirring ashes and went so far as to strip him of his clothing. He rolled his money up so tight and put it in his crotch. Then he came back to Utah.
My Grandmother Rachel, Ann, Betty came the first day of May 1864 and her mother (Wilcox), also. They were on the sea five weeks. A fever broke out at sea, Grandma's sister Ann had two girls; one of the girls was Lucy Richins (Harvey Richins' Mother) and the fever settled in her arm causing it to be stiff, for which I remember.
On the ship with Grandma (Rachel Lythgoe) was Pap's Mother, Eleanor Maycefield Fowler, and husband George Fowler. Visited in the usual manner and well acquainted, and Mrs. Fowler spoke so much of her son James (my Grandpa) Grandma Rachel got so tired of hearing of the story of "my son James"—by the way, he was already here in Utah. She tried to ignore and get away from the conversation, but the ship was so small. The ship name was The Monarch of the Sea. Mrs. Fowler taught Grandma Rachel how to knit on the ship (story of stockings don't forget). Grandma worked in England as a seamstress and worked in a Lady's Shop for pay.
Their father, Thomas Lythgoe, never came to the America. He had a dread of the sea. His two daughters, Ellen and Lucy, stayed in England with him and never came from England. He was our great grandfather, was a green grocer peddler and mad a good living—enough to say they never went hungry. They loved him dearly and was sorry to their dying day he never did get to America.
Grandma Rachel and her mother and two sisters landed in New York. They traveled to Wyoming on the Missouri River. There, they waited for Uncle James Lythgoe and wife for three weeks, as they were three weeks later leaving England. James said it was dark when they came to the camp, but could hear his Mother's voice calling him. There they traveled together and camped at Upton, Utah on the Church Creek; from there to Coalville.
The first one Grandma Rachel met was "our son James" (James Fowler) and was around September of 1864.
Grandma (Rachel Lythgoe) worked for some people by the name of Hoyet in Hoytsville, as a maid, and they were very stingy people and she stole bread for the children and took it to their beds at night. She never saw too much of (Pap) Grandpa (James Fowler) during that time, but later they were married, January 2 or 20, 1867, Coalville. They, they came to Henefer. They had 11 children. Esther Ellen, my Mother; James Henry, and Aunt Polly were born in a dugout on the hill where the Brewer home now stands. When Polly was six weeks old, they moved to the log home
