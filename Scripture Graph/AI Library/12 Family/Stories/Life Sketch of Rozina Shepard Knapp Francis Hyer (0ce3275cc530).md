## The story as it was written

Life Sketch of Rozina Shepard Knapp Francis Hyer
Rozina Shepard was born January 21, 1829, in Denmark, Lewis Co., New York. She died 23 Oct 1882 in Richmond, Cache Co., Utah.
While in her teens she left the East with her father, Isaac Shepard, and family who spent three or four years working their way out to Utah, arriving in the autumn of 1848, one year after the first pioneers. According to her father’s statement they “left Rodman, New York by team to Sackett’s Harbor; took boat on Lake Ontario through Welling Canal, Lake Erie to Cleveland, Ohio; remained there about a year, left by team for Nauvoo, Illinois; remained there about one month, then left for Lee County, Iowa; remained there for about two and one-half years to get means to continue our journey to Salt Lake City; left by team Lee County for Council Bluffs where we wintered at Davis Camp. At this time the Saints were called on for volunteers for the battalion. The following spring we went to Linden, Missouri, where mother died. The following spring we left by team for Winter Quarters, the same spring with Lorenzo Snow’s Company for Salt Lake City; this was in the year 1848.” Shepard Canyon north of Farmington was named for these pioneers.
In April, 1895, Lydia M. Funk said: “The children of Isaac Shepard are: Lavina, married and in Bishop Creek, California; Rozina (subject of this sketch): Justin, married Eliza Allred, Richmond; Lucina (d. in 1891): Alzina (D. in Cal.); one who died in the East; Sarah, married Fountain Welsh, Providence; Judson, married Elizabeth Barnett; and Survina who married John Cole, Santa Cruz, California.”
Rozina Shepard married Albert Knapp, January 7, 1849 in Salt Lake City, after he had been mustered out in San Diego and worked his way to Utah. His name is engraved on the Mormon Battalion Plaque on Utah State Capitol grounds in honor of those volunteers who served our country in the Mexican War.
Shortly after their marriage they moved to Farmington where they lived for about twelve years and had the following children: Azilka Retina (d. 3 Mo. Old); Lydia Malinda, married Hershel Bullen and later Christopher Funk; Sarah Armina, married W.L. Skidmore (d. 1891); Silas Albert, married Harriet Skidmore (dau. Of Henry B. Skidmore) Rachel’s mother; Justin Abraham who married Miss Lemon from Smithfield; and Morgan, married Mary Flamm and later “Aunt Clara”. From her second husband, Frederick Nelson Francis, who, like her first strayed away from her and the church, was born: Rozina Adelaide (d. 10 Mo. Old) and Maria Melvina who married and raised a large family by August Severene Schow.
Later and finally Rozina Shepard Knapp Francis married Christian Hyer, whom she did not out live. Their first child, Ezra Taft, died at the age of 10 months, but the second, Esther Jane, married Samuel W. Hendricks by whom she became the mother of a large family. Christian Hyer lived faithfully in the church until his death which occurred 20 September 1901.
Rozina Shepard thus lived to be a great woman, which is evidenced by the success of her posterity. Her early experiences in working her way out to Utah, her faithfulness in following the counsel of the leaders of her church and her abiding love and care for her children and grandchildren were seldom, if ever, excelled by any mother. The spot where Rozina Shepard Hyer was buried in the Richmond Cemetery is a sacred spot because of her outstanding life’s work. It should be well marked and well kept and frequently visited by her kindred who come after her, who are in search of truth and righteousness.
