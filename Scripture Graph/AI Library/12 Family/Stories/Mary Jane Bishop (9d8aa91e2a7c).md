# Mary Jane Bishop

_A story of [[Edward Jeremiah Price (KWV9-ZK2)|Edward Jeremiah Price]], [[Mary Jane Bishop (LRRX-9SM)|Mary Jane Bishop]]._

## The story as it was written

The following was compiled by Delmar O. Price and posted here at his request by his son Robert.
Great-grandmother Mary Jane Bishop was the first person on the Price side of the family to join the Church and make the long trip to Utah. Following is her life story as written by my aunt Nettie:
Mary Ann Bishop Price was born on December 17, 1819, at Shrewsbury, Shorpshire, England. She was the daughter of John Bishop and Catherine Evans. There were nine children in the family, seven sons and two daughters. Mary was the third child, the oldest girl. We find in reading her story one of the most outstanding characteristics was her devotion to her family and this may have come as a result of the many tragedies that came in her father’s family. Her brother, Evan, was killed in a war in India. Thomas while working in a gas factory was caught in the machine and literally torn to pieces, and Samuel the youngest was downed in the River Severn. The youngest girl died when a small child.
The grief of the parents over the loss of their children undermined their health, the father especially as he held himself accountable for the loss of Samuel, his favorite. He died a short time afterward, age 57 years. Ten years later the mother died, leaving Mary to mother and care for her four brothers, Richard, John, William and James.
Not only did Mary have the full responsibility of the family, but also she had to go to work to secure means to feed and clothe them. She found employment in an English laundry. The first article to press when trying out for the job was a skirt with thirteen ruffles. She did it so well she was hired.
While they were still living in the family home, the Mormon missionaries came to them with the gospel message. In a short time Mary, William and James were baptized and immediately began preparations to come to Zion. While crossing the ocean, Mary became acquainted with one of the sailors, Edward Price. We have no proven facts or figures but our supposition leads us to think she fell in love with him, explained the gospel as she understood it and converted him, for when they landed he remained with her and when they got as far as Missouri they were married.
They made the trip across the continent by ox team. William and James came with them. Richard came to America later, but did not join the Church. He married a French girl and returned to France. Grandmother never heard of him afterwards. Edward Price and Mary Bishop were married by Elder Thomas Wrigley in the city of St. Louis, Missouri, on March 30, 1852.
When they arrived in Utah they made their home in Provo. The two brothers lived with them until they were married. She felt a strong responsibility toward her brothers having cared for them since childhood and even after they were married she felt they needed her advice, which their wives sometimes resented. The result being a little friction at times.
I would like to mention the love that existed between these three. I have been told that the love and devotion of these two brothers for their sister Mary amounted almost to worship.
While in Provo five children were born to Edward and Mary Price, two girls and three boys. One girl and two boys died there. While the children were small the family moved to Franklin Idaho in the north end of Cache Valley in northern Utah and helped to pioneer that settlement. Their names are on a monument erected to the memory of Franklin pioneers. Later they moved to Old Paradise and then down to Paradise and built two-room dirt roofed home. Three sons were born here but all died while they were small.
Although Grandmother was a strong minded dominant woman, the loss of so many of her children made her exceptionally tender and lenient with the two she did raise and with all children she came in contact with. Grandmother was a strong ambitious woman and her efforts were not centered on her family alone. She was a friend to everyone, always willing to help the sick and needy.
Wherever there was a sickness or death or any other trouble you would always find Mary Price doing all in her power to alleviate pain and sorrow.
For many years she made burial clothes, being exceptionally fine as a seamstress. Who believed while she was a member of the Church she owed it service. She was an efficient leader and her social prestige found a place for her in all the religious and social affairs of the ward. She worked in the presidency of the Relief Society for 25 years.
In 1893 a charitable and benevolent association was organized and she was chosen as one of the board members.
In the meeting where Grandmother was first sustained as a member of the Relief Society presidency and which after the business was transacted, the meeting was turned over to testimony bearing, Sister Elizabeth Law spoke in tongues. Later, in another similar meeting, Grandmother spoke in tongues and it was interpreted by Sister Law and still on another occasion Grandmother interpreted for Sister Law.
As I mentioned, Grandmother was always on hand to aid the sick and needy. Doctors were seldom called in. Although she was never granted legal license to practices in obstetrics she probably had what meant more to people in those days. She had the time, the desire, the ability to help and the spirit of the Lord to assist her, and she assisted whenever she was needed.
Her home was never more than two rooms but she willingly shared this with her daughter when she married and again later allowed her first, grandchild, Samuel Oldham and his wife Lucy Schow, to occupy one room.
Grandmother and Grandfather were farmers and my father, John Lloyd, being the only son to help with the farm work. Grandmother, besides taking care of her family and doing so much for others in the community, helped with the harvesting until father grew up and was able to take over the bulk of the work.
As I remember Grandfather, he was a happy, good-natured, and submissive little man and as I have mentioned, Grandmother was of a more dominant nature and seemed to be the manager and moving force in the family. She taught the gospel by precept and example. Her children were given a deep religious training. Her Motto was, “Teach the need of a clean active life by living one”.
She passed on to her reward on March 12, 1904, and was buried at the Paradise Cemetery. She had not been feeling well for a short time and passed peacefully away in her sleep.
When her daughter Janie passed away at the birth of her 10th child, Jane Oldham Pierce, she grieved so over the loss of her daughter and the fact that she left such a big family, it undermined her health – the straw that broke the camel’s back. At the time of her death she had one living child and 19 grandchildren.
Note: Aunt Nettie’s references to Mary Jane’s brothers, James and William, traveling to America and living with her in Provo, doesn’t agree with their personal histories as published in the “History of Paradise, Cache County, Utah” written by Elizabeth Allen. In their personal histories it states that James sailed to America in 1853 a year later than Mary Jane, and William came in 1855, four years later. James lived in Salt Lake City for two years and then moved to Draper where he lived for six years prior to moving to Paradise. William arrived in Salt Lake City in 1855 and moved to Provo for a year and a half. I assume he lived with Mary Jane during this time. In 1856 he moved to Draper, probably to be close to his brother James. He didn’t move to Paradise until 1864, three years after James had moved there. So it appears that James was the first to move to Paradise in 1861, followed by Mary Jane in 1862, and William in 1864. In the early 1870’s, James moved to Salt Lake City where he did plumbing work for the Tabernacle. While in Paradise, he was the superintendent for the building of the Paradise canal. William and Mary Jane remained in Paradise for the remainder of their lives.
