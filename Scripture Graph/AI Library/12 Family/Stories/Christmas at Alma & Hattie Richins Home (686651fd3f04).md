# Christmas at Alma & Hattie Richins Home

_A story of [[Emeline Hattie Richins (KWCF-1GV)|Emeline Hattie Richins]], [[Alma Ether Richins (LLC5-YZB)|Alma Ether Richins]]._

## The story as it was written

This story reportably about going to Alma and Hattie's home is titled "My Most Memorable Christmas" and was written by Thora Sorensen.
Mama, why don't I have a grandmother like all the other kids? Everyone has relatives but me."
Thora, dear, come here. Dry your tears. You have a grandmother. She and Grandfather, with all your aunts, uncles and cousins, live many miles from here across the ocean in a beautiful country called England, where we used to live. One day the Mormon missionaries came to our house telling us about the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Your daddy and I joined the church. Taking you and your brother, we crossed this huge ocean to America, leaving Grandmother, Grandpa, aunts, uncles and cousins in England. They didn't like this church.
How we children yearned for relatives, especially during the holidays. My imaginative sister even told her classmates that she was going to spend Christmas with her Grandmother in Riverton. Why Riverton? Oh, she said. I thought that sounded like a place a grandmother would live.
Later my father was working at Devil's Slide. Mother became friendly with a neighbor who had relatives in Henefer, Utah. Knowing how we children longed for relatives, we were invited to celebrate Christmas with them. We were really excited. At last we had some relatives. Christmas morning the big bob sleigh pulled up to our house. The bottom was covered with hay and there were plenty of quilts to keep us warm. Pracing horses with jingle bells on their harness pulled the sleigh. Traveling through the narrows, high mountains and winding roads was very scary, but oh so exciting! Father's lovely tenor voice rang lound and clear as he sang Christmas carols while we all joined in. Time passed quickly, and we soon reached Henefer, stopping in front of a large two-story house.
Timidly we entered the large kitchen, with a hugh black stove. Such activity! Pies cooling on the kitchen table, delicious aromas in the air. Best of all, a real live Grandmother sitting in a rocking chair chocheting. So many uncles, aunts and cousins! Laughingly, they welcomed us as part of the family. The cousins my age invited me to go upstairs to their bedroom. It was so cold we could see our breath. But sitting on the bed with blankets around us we talked and talked and talked. My heart was so warj, I didn't feel the cold. Later we were called to dinner. There before our very eyes in a huge dining room were two long tables loaded with food. There was so much joking and laughter, I thought this must be heaven on earth.
After dinner, my adopted cousins took me outside to see the cow. I even stroked it on the side. We went sleigh riding down a long hill ending at a frozen pond where people were ice skating. When our fingers and toes were numb with cold, we hurried back into the house and sat around the fireplace getting toasty warm, listening to the folks tell about the good old days.
All too soon my magic day ended; time to go home. When we were all tucked snugly in the sleigh, our friends stood on the porch waving happy good-byes, telling us to be sure and come again soon as we were their adopted relatives.
Even though I have had many beautiful Christmases since, and our family numbers 185, that Christmas Day 65 years ago stands out as my most memorable Christmas.
- Thora Sorensen
