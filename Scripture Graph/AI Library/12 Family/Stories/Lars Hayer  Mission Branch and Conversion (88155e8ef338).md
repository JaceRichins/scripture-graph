# Lars Hayer: Mission Branch and Conversion

_A story of [[Lars Andersen Heyer (LKK2-XKN)|Lars Andersen Heyer]]._

## The story as it was written

Goodman Hougas an elder, embarked on a missionary trip for the church in company with another elder, Ole Hayer. In traveling in Rock County, Southern Wiscnsin, they visited the home of a Norse relative of Ole Hayer, one Lars Hayer and his wife, Ann Marie Olson Hayer, who had come to America in 1842 from Norway. A minister of another faith, alarmed at the success of the elders in converting people of the community, especially newcomers, had warned the Lars Hayers, "Don't invite them in." Ann Marie hid upstairs when the elders arrived and were invited in, and listened through an opening in the floor. She soon said to herself, "What a fool I am. I shall go down and visit with them too."
 All evidence points that the father, Lars, his wife and family of eight children, joined the Latter Day Saints church while living in Wisconsin. Upon learning that rich prairie land was available in LaSalle County, Illinois, and that there was a large branch of the new church organization to which they had recently attached themselves they moved at various times to LaSalle County, Illinois during the next three years. All came except Ann Marie Hayer, the mother, who died in Wisconsin in 1843. Here they became members of the old Mission Branch. Lars Hayer died in April of 1863.
Lars Hayer, baptized in June 1861, undoubtedly a re-baptism, lived a little less than two years as a member of the Reorganized Church, passing away in 1863. But the great influence of this good brother remains with us for nearly every family in the present Mission Branch of 1961 trace their lineage to him directly or thru intermarriage with descendants of the Lars Hayer children. For once Lars Hayer was converted to the gospel message of the Restored Church, he brought his family to Illinois so as to be members of Old Mission Church. His conviction bore good fruit when four sons became members of the Mission Branch priesthood after uniting with the Reorganized church. The Hayer family was the most populous group in the Mission Church history for several generations, very prominent in good works and deeds - a great influence for better living and righteous conduct. While the male line of the family after one hundred years is not so numerous at Mission today (1961) and not all are engaged in farming, the sole occupation of the early community, that state of affairs is to be expected. During the past century, many of the Hayer men have left to make their livelihood in other states, becoming members of other branches of the Reorganization, contributing much to the cause of the church.
(Account taken from The History of Mission Branch of the Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints, April 1961, written by Charles A Reedy)
