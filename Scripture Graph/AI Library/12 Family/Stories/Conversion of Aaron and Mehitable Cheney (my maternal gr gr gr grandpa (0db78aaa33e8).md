# Conversion of Aaron and Mehitable Cheney (my maternal gr gr gr grandparents) to the Gospel of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

_A story of [[Mehitable Wells (KN6W-2R1)|Mehitable Wells]], [[Aaron Cheney (KNX4-J2Z)|Aaron Cheney]]._

## The story as it was written

Both Aaron (1787-1862) and Mehitable ( 1787-1869) were religiously inclined and were faithful members of the Methodist Church. Aaron was a trustee of the First Methodist Episcopal Church of Sandusky, a village just west of Freedom. This Methodist Church met in the local schoolhouse, because they did not yet have their own building. Freedom and the surrounding villages made up a tight-knit religious community of people who were searching for religious answers. Meanwhile in Palmyra, New York, a young Prophet named Joseph Smith began translating some Gold Plates given to him by an Angel of God. A young school teacher by the name of Oliver Cowdery became convinced that this was God's work. He became the Prophet's scribe and was very involved in the restoration of the Gospel. Oliver shared the good news of the Gospel with his family, including his brother, Warren Cowdery who lived in Freedom, NY and was the town physician and postmaster. He was introduced to Mormonism by Oliver, who kept him abreast of the development of the Church. In turn, Warren shared what he learned with his neighbors. William Hyde, whose family lived next to the Cowderys, wrote that Warren "obtained from his brother Oliver at an early date some of the proof sheets to the Book of Mormon, some of which we had the privilege of perusing, and we did not peruse any faster than we believed."
 The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (or Mormon as it was called) was officially organized on the 6th of April 1830, with Joseph Smith as the Presiding Elder and Oliver Cowdery as its second. This exciting news certainly made its way to Oliver's brother in Freedom. In late October 1830, Oliver Cowdery, Peter Whitmer Jr., Ziba (Richard) Peterson, and Parley P. Pratt preached to a group of Seneca Indians living on the Cattaraugus Reservation, while passing through western New York en route to Missouri on the Lamanite mission. At this time they left 2 books which found their way to Freedom in the care of Wealtha Hatch. Family records indicate that Aaron and Mehitable were baptized into this church in 1831. This is certainly possible. Others of the community had been baptized by this point. However we have no record of the exact date of the baptism in 1831. Some histories are specific in saying that Aaron was taught by Joseph Smith and Parley P Pratt. We have no record of Joseph Smith being there in 1831, though he was there in 1834 when many more Saints were baptized.
 Meanwhile, the Gospel message was shared freely around Freedom NY. Early converts, like Warren Cowdery and Wealtha Hatch, shared their religious convictions with friends and family, allowing for the gospel message to grow in the community's consciousness. On 9 March 1834, the Prophet Joseph Smith, and Parley P. Pratt stopped in Freedom to enlist volunteers to march to Missouri to aid distressed Saints. His camp would be called Zion's Camp. While they were in Freedom, they not only visited their fellow Saints, but preached the Restored Gospel to all. Joseph Smith and Parley P Pratt moved on after only a few days, but more missionaries arrived to continue the preaching. The County history printed in 1879 reads, "At the time mentioned, the quiet precincts of Fish Lake neighborhood (a community near Freedom) were invaded by Joe Smith, Sidney Rigdon, John Gould, and Parley Pratt. They made their headquarters at Rufuf Metcalf's and Dr. Warren Cowdery's. Meetings were held, daily and nightly, in barns and dwelling houses, and a prodigious excitement pervaded the minds of many people in that immediate vicinity. Among those residents who were conspicuous as believers were Dr. Cowdery, Rufus Metcalf, Nehemiah Sparks, Orrin Cheney Aaron's son), Hurd Cheney (Aaron's brother), Capt. Heman Hyde, Heman T. Hyde and wife, and Barber Lawson. As a result, 30 men and women were induced to join the Mormons, and emigrated with them to Kirtland, Ohio. Some came back and renounced their faith in Mormonism, while others continued with them to the end of their lives. Steele says that in the space of a few months in 1834, 35 over 40 people were baptized into the Mormon Church. Because these members left other Churches, the other churches were negatively affected. The Methodist Church especially was decimated and was never the same. At that time it was not uncommon for members of the Church to be re-baptized when they wanted to renew their covenants.
 The exact date of Aaron and Mehitable's original baptism in 1831 have been lost, but the Ancestral file lists the following dates, which are probably re-baptism dates Aaron on 5 April 1834 and Mehitable on 9 April 1834. Furthermore, Orson Pratt recorded that on 2 April 1834 they preached at the Fish Lake Schoolhouse and afterwards baptized four persons including Amasa and Olive Cheney, the children of Aaron Cheney.
 At the Freedom (Mormon) Conference in January 1835, where representatives from eleven western New York branches attended. Presiding Elder Warren Cowdery reported that the Freedom Branch had seventy-four members in good standing. Meanwhile the members continued to share the Gospel and more people were baptized including more Cheney family members. Because so many community members joined the Church, the members were committed to the Gospel together They helped and supported each other and were less likely to be persecuted for their faith When the Saints chose to gather in Mormon centers like Kirtland and Nauvoo, these community members often moved together in groups The Aaron Cheney family continued to live in Freedom, New York until 1835.
