# Daniel Miley Smith home at 420 East 300 South, Pleasant Grove, Utah

_A story of [[Daniel Miley Smith Sr (KWJC-G74)|Daniel Miley Smith Sr]]._

## The story as it was written

Historic Smith home open house planned Saturday
By Beth Olsen
 In a day when Utah is losing so many of its valuable historic buildings because of a "bulldozer" mindset, i.e., tear down and rebuild, it was a refreshing change to tour the work of art created by JoAnn Crease Benson and her husband, Donald
 They have a "preserve our heritage" attitude. And it was with this attitude that they renewed JoAnn's great grandfather's house at 420 East 300 South, Pleasant Grove, Utah.
 Love for the stable, older comfortable house shone from JoAnn's eyes as she talked about her forbearer, his house and the careful renovation she and her husband did on it.
 Daniel Miley Smith, Sr. built the house about 1869-70. He brought his bride of one year, Emma H. Sinefield, to Pleasant Grove in 1864. They bought 12 acres of farming ground about 1868 and built a two-room house of field-stone in a traditional hall-parlor type.
 There were few field stone houses built in Pleasant Grove because of the easier to lay soft rock deposits in the east hills. Through the years the rock portion of the house was stuccoed and scored into simulated blocks and several additions were built to the east. This changed the house front from west to north.
 JoAnn painstakingly removed coats of stucco around the north porch exposing the earthen-grey natural beauty of the stone. The restored rock was so appealing that her contractor urged her to uncover the entire stone portion of the house. This she did--creating a striking contract with the frame exterior of the east wing, one of the outstanding features of the house.
 Although she worked under the advisement of her contractor, Lynn Thacker, who milled and shaped special woodwork to match interior older baseboards and door and window casements, she and her husband did much of the work and made decisions themselves. They removed original wood, stripped, sanded, repaired, painted, and reattached it keeping much originality to the house.
 A new kitchen was added to the back of the house and use of the rooms are rearranged to make a well-organized, comfortable and useful house. A small business office, used by Daniel Miley Smith and his partner, John Whittaker, who operated an implement company in 1883 from this property that sold reapers and self-binders, is now the Benson's office.
 Smith also did other business; he experimented with and grew different varieties of trees, berries, and seeds, and sold them from a nursery located on his property.
 When the present Pleasant Grove City park was planned, he donated one-half and planted all the trees in it, with the help of his son, Melvin. Among the yet existing White Ash trees, they interspersed fast growing poplars for the quick shade.
 In 1900, the poplars were cut and corded by 20 town's men to provide a two-year supply of widow's firewood. The park still provides the town with much shade and pleasure as it did before the century's turn.
 JoAnn feels right at home surrounded by constant reminders of family members who preceded her as residents, and of her own life having been born here, a daughter of Lawrence and Retta Smith Crease who were former owners.
The front porch JoAnn carefully worked over is the same porch where her husband courted her. A second cousin, Robert Bezzant from California, stopped by to tour the house and said his grandfather, Earl Conrad Smith, son of Daniel Miles and Emma, was baptized in the ditch that runs west of the house.
The large 106 year old walnut trees standing next to the road were saplings from her great grandfather's nursery. Even the flowers, berries, fruit and vegetables surrounding the house are much like those her great grandmother Smith tended, and bearing grapevines were the originals planted by Daniel Miley.
 Although the house renovations has not been inexpensive, the Bensons feel fortunate to get this family house in pristine condition. JoAnn thinks there is a little bit of luck involved; if her parents hadn't been content to live in the house, unchanged, she wouldn't have the original house to work with.
The Pleasant Grove Historical Preservation Commission congratulates JoAnn and Donald for preserving not only their own heritage but a part of the town's history as well. This house has an appeal combined with nostalgia that newer house can never have and yet it mingles interestingly with newer neighborhood houses.
 The Bensons are holding an open house Saturday, Sept. 5, 1992, from 1 to 3 p.m. for relatives and friends, and they welcome all interested citizens. If you don't go you are missing something special.
This is a good example of what can be done with older buildings to preserve them when owners realize their aesthetic value and seek to extend the lives of their houses for future generations that they too might fee ties that bind us all to the past.
