# Autobiography of Robert Jones

_A story of [[Robert Jones (KWJ7-5Q6)|Robert Jones]]._

## The story as it was written

Robert Jones
Henefer, Utah, Jan, 1892 (Original spelling and punctuation from copy.)
I, Robert Jones was born July 9, 1829 at Caudle Green Glo. England. My Father's name was William Jones, son of William and Elizabeth Jones. My Mother's name was Mary Shill, daughter of Robert and Prudence Shill of the Parish of Syde Glo. England.
When I was one year old my parents moved to a place called Brimpsfield, Common. There my father built a house and raised a family of eight children. I was the only son. I had seven sisters. Ann, Susan Randall, Lucy, Rhoda Bennion, Mary Stephens, Elizabeth Ure, Prudence Edgeworth.
At the age of 9 years, I went out to work at a farm house that was called Storry
Hill for one Joseph Wenning for four pence per day. I stayed with him for three years. Then I left and went to work for one Edward Statman at Brimpsfield Park. I did not get any schooling and went to Sunday School when 16 years of age. I went to live with Joseph Wenning and his son John at Storry Hill. Stayed three years, during this time Joseph and his wife Elizabeth died, 1848.
1848, I lived with his son Robert Wenning at Brimpsfield.
In the year 1849, I worked for Edward Statman again and during the month of
Sept. 1848, I heard the Latter-day Saints preach at the place called Hal d Break in the house of one William Nicholas and their testimonies was so strong that I could not resist it, and on the 18th of November, 1849 I made up my mind to go and be babtised. I thought if I did not find it as they said that I could leave it again. So I went and found my grandfather Robert Shill and told him that I had come to be babtised, but as he was living by Brimpsfield I thought it could not be done—but—he said, "I can soon find some one to do it”. So he took me to the house of William Edgeworth. There the Saints had met together to have a party as some of them were about to go to America. My Uncle George Shill went to Babtise me. A Brother Charles Day went with us as a witness, after which I was confirmed by Elder George Humphries.
That evening as I went to bed my mind was lit up so that I could see the things of God so plain that my heart was made glad. I was satisfied. I had done right.
That winter I went to work with one Samuel Painter at a farm called High Gate
for one William Rayus thrasing oats and wheat by hand with the Flial.
Samuel Painter came into the church and quite a number of others. In the spring of 1850 I went to work for Edward Statman a little while again.
In March I attended a conference at Cheltenham and Brother by the name of
William Booth presided. Sometime in the same month I was ordained to the office of a teacher by William Booth. In the year 1851 I worked at different places. Sometimes for one and then another.
In May 1852 I was ordained to the office of a Priest by John Hyde. At Caudle
Green on Nov. 18, 1852 I married Harriet Tipper, daughter of William and Jane Tipper of Birdlip of Cowley by the church minister. Charles Shill my mother's Brother and Charlett Wiggal was witness.
In the summer of 1853 I worked with one Henry Jackson, also helped to cut grain with the sickel for one William Penman at Birdlip. In the winter 1853 sometime about Christmas I was called to preside over the Sheepcombe Branch and in Jan. I was ordained an Elder by Joseph France at Cheltenham.
On Feb. 19, 1854 at 20 Minutes after 4 O'clock in the morning. Sunday my wife
gave birth to a son. We had him named Robert Allen.
For several years I worked with Henry Jackson for one Andrew Calderwood of
Syde. I babtised Henry and his wife, sometime in Nov. 1853. A very thick fog all around the place at Birdlip, but when we get to the water the fog dispersed and the moon shone so bright so that I could see Marie under the water.
On Saturday at 8 p.m. Mar. 8, 1856 my wife gave birth to a son. We had him
named William.
Sometime in 1856-57 with my little family and my Father-in-law moved from
Birdlip to a place called Nettleton.
During this time I was released from the Sheepcombe Branch and appointed to
preside over the Caudle Green Brance.
In 1858 I was released and appointed to preside over the compton Branch. It was about 10 miles to go on Sundays. The Saints there was not up to the times, as they ought to be but by fasting and prayer the Almighty moved upon them by His Spirit and then to feel good I went there about 10 months. Then I was released and appointed to preside over the Cheltenham Branch.
5 June in 1858 my wife gave birth to a daughter. We had her named Mary Jane.
Sometime in 1859 I was released from the Cheltenham Branch and again appointed to compton. I stayed for a while and then was released to preside over the Caudle Green Branch again.
On May 7, 1863 my wife gave birth to a son. We had him named George William. Willard G. Smith blessed him at Nettleton. He died Jan, 29, 1865. Buried at Cowley.
My wife Harriet was born 2 May 1824 at Birdlip. Died at Nettleton 12, Nov. 1865.
In April the 28, 1866 my home was left behind and I started with my family
bringing my youngest sister Prudence with me to Liverpool.
Joseph Edgeworth, William Richins, his wife Charlot and daughter Miriam Ann,
Arthur Richins, Ann Cook and 4 children came with them. We left Cheltenham on a morning train.
Arrived 3 O'clock p.m. in Liverpool. Stayed overnight at a legion place. Next day
got on board the Ship John Bright. Edwin Sheewells wife and two children were with us. We were 6 weeks on the sea. Had a pleasant voyage. Came by railroad through the states to St. Josephs. Then came up the river to Wyoming. Stayed there until 11 July. While there my daughter Jane was babtised by Stephan Allen, and confirmed by William W. Riter.
We came over the plains with Captain Chipman's train. We landed at Echo about
the middle of September. I stayed with Edward Richins one night. Left my children with him and he went with me on the train to Salt Lake City. Was absent a week. Then came back and helped Edward Richins to get his hay and grain up. Stayed with him that fall and winter. On Dec, 2, 1866 I was married to Miriam Ann Richins, daughter of William and Charlott Richins from Sheepcombe Glo. Englan.
The wedding took place at Croyden. My Uncle Charles and wife, Harriet made a
feast. Bishop James Walker performed the ceremony. William Butler and his wife were witnesses.
My step-daughter Caroline Ellen gave birth to a fine son the same date. When the child was a month old she went to a dance and caught cold, which caused her death. I stayed with Edward Richins the next summer, took his place on shares. My wife Miriam was house keeper for us both. On Sept,18, 1867 she gave birth to a fine boy. We had had him named Alfred R. and he was blessed by me.
We stayed with Edward Richins until the fall of 1869. Then came to Henefer.
Built a log house and on Nov. 9. 1869 my wife gave birth to a daughter. We had her named Emma, blessed by me.
In the year 1870, Sept. the 12, my wife Miriam and me went to Salt Lake City and had our Endowments. Apostle Wilfred Woodruff married us.
On Dec. 25, 1870 I met in council with the Priesthood of the Ward and Bishop
Charles Richins appointed me to Superintendent of the Sunday School. Also was
appointed to babtise all the new comers into the ward.
On Feb. 18, 1871 my wife gave birth to a fine boy. We had him named John. He
was blessed by Bishop Charles Richins.
Nov. 26, 1872 my wife gave birth to a daughter. We had her named Amelia. She was blessed by me Jan. 12, l873.
May 23, 1875 gave birth to a son, He was named Charles R. blessed by William
Brewer.
My wife Miriam Ann was born Dec. 24, 1837 at Sheepcombe, Glo. England.
I was ordained to the office of High Priest by Franklin D. Richards, at Coalville,
9 July 1877 and was appointed 1st counselor to Bishop Charles Richins which position I held Until Feb, 1890. Bishop Richins resigned.
11 Dec. 1884 my wife Miriam Ann died and left me with six children.
Alfred R. my son was married to Elizabeth Edgeworth at the Logan Temple, 28
Nov. 1888.
Emma was married to James Salmon at Logan Temple 19 June 1889.
Clara Ann was married to Charles Toone at the Salt Lake City Temple April 24
1895.

March 19 I married Caroline Agnes Peterson of the 21st ward. Salt Lake City at
the Logan Temple in the year 1890, by W.M.Merrill.
My wife gave birth to a boy 18 Sept, 1893, was blessed by me, named Clarence
Ray. 12 Nov. 1893.
On Sept. 9, 1896 I went to the Temple in Salt Lake City and had my first family
sealed to me and first wife Harriet Tipper. There were three boys and one girl. There names are Robert Allen, William Seth, Mary Jane, and George Willard who died Jan. 29, 1865 at Nettleton, Glo. England.
Nov. 1896 my wife Agnes was taken very sick for five weeks. On Sunday Jan. 3,
1897 She went to Salt Lake City and was babtised for her health, in the Temple. Jan 12 1897 came home much improved in health for which blessing we are thankful. She was appointed to preside over the Young Ladies Mutual Improvement Association Nov. 15, 1895. Set apart by Bishop John Paskett.
Jan. 12, 1897 she was babtised for her health in the Salt Lake Temple.
Feb. 6, 1897 we went to Coalville to Conference. The weather was stormy and a
little cold.
Monday, Feb. 15 I went to Coalville on business, brought a load of coal home.
Thursday 18 Feb, I was sick in bed. 19,20,21 I was still sick. Monday 22 a little
better. Tuesday 23 abed again. Brother James Salmon of coalville came down to see me, stayed overnight.
Wed.24, my daughter Amelia came to see me. She stayed overnight.
Fri.26, was very sick. Cold and sharp frost at night.
Sat.27, my daughter Emma came to see me, also my son, John and his wife Clara and her Sister, also Bishop John Clark of Upton. Andrew Patterson of Wanship, President of the High Priest Quorum, stayed overnight.
Sun. 28, I was much better. A fine Day. My wife went to a meeting. I stayed
home.
March 1. 1897 at home. Second snow storm last night but warm today. I went out doors today. My wife went to Relief Society meeting this afternoon.
Brother James Bromly of Echo died today 11 March 1897.
Sunday May 16 1897, Today I went to meeting. Parley P. Pratt was there. He
spoke at the meeting. Had a good time.
Jan. 24, 1898, went to the funeral of Susanna Stevens which was held in the
meeting house. She died at the age of nearly 92 years. I knew her in England many years ago. President W.W. Cluff preached her funeral sermon. She Died 22 Jan. 1898.
June 1898. Monday June 6, went to Salt Lake City from Bountiful. Arrived there
at 8 O1clock a.m. Went to the temple, gave in some names to be babtised for. Stayed with my wife Agnes' sister, 21st ward.
Tuesday June 7 was babtised for my health also for Thomas Jones my uncle. His
son, George, William Harper, Thomas Harper, the brothers that I had lived and played with in my youth.
My son, John S. Jones was babtised for Andrew Bird. He had died at New York.
Had left his home with his family to come home to these mountains.
This evening stayed with Brother Joseph Morgan, 15th ward. My son Alfred was
with me. June 8, taken Endowment for Samuel Painter who died. Had him and his wife sealed. (Hannah Edgeworth)
I with my sister Prudence was proxy for them. Also had my son Alfred and
daughter Emma sealed to me and their mother Miriam Ann, my wife. She died Dec. 11, 1884.
Also was sealed with my sisters to my father and mother, William Jones and Mary Shill. Names are Ann, Susan, Rhoda, Lucy, Mary, and Prudence Elizabeth.
My father was sealed to his father and mother, William and Elizabeth. Their
father and mother Robert and JoAnn Jones, Great Grandfather and mother.
