# Richard Shepherd - Zion Pioneer

_A story of [[Richard Shepherd (KWJ5-L47)|Richard Shepherd]]._

## The story as it was written

Richard Shepherd - Zion Pioneer
Written by Oral Sutton Mower
Great-granddaughter
Richard Shepherd was born June 21, 1804, Tynemouth, Northumberland County, England, to Richard Shepherd and Mary Younghusband. He was an only child.
His father's occupation was a joiner (ship's carpenter). Richard was a Captain Master Marine, otherwise known as a sea captain. He seemed rather wealthy. At one time he owned three ships, one ship was called the “Tintic or Tantic”, another "The Comfort", and another the “Pearcy”. The Comfort was skippered by a professional seaman. Richard skippered the "Empress" in the service of his country, this service extended over a period of thirty years. Another ship in connection with Richard Shepherd was the "Content" whose home pier was called "Shepherd's Quay".
The Richard Shepherd family lived at #8 Wlllington Street in North Shields. According to the County Directory of about 1844, listed this address for his shipping enterprise also. Records show that the Shepherd family were educated people, prosperous, cultured, religious, and highly respected in the community.
Here in this wind-swept country, which lies high above the waters of the North Sea on a rugged, rock-bound promontory, this loving and wise couple reared their family in comfortable and rather pretentious circumstances. Nine children were born of this union. Their father, Richard Shepherd, was a master mariner in the service of his King, for forty years. He owned his own ship "The Comfort", which was skippered by a professional seaman, while Richard, himself sailed "The Empress", in the service of his country, as Captain.
He married Ann Eltringham Henderson, the daughter of Thomas Henderson and Sarah Eltringham. To this marriage nine children were born, George, Sarah, Ann Henderson, Thomas Eltringham, Mary Park, Margaret Hannah, Richard S., another Richard. He also died as an infant. The eldest son, George was accidentally killed when he was struck by the mast on the ship Pearcy, January 13, 1843.
The children all grew up loving the sea, and often sailed upon the ocean having memorable adventures, with the adored skipper, Captain Shepherd. For any of them to suffer the pangs of seasickness was unheard of. A Shepherd was immune. It was altogether, a comfortable, colorful, satisfying life they lived.
Captain Shepherd made long voyages to the Orient, or other distant countries, of several months duration He loved to return to his home a little sooner than he was expected, to surprise his Ann and the children. Pushing the front door open he would toss his cap into the vestibule and call out "Ahoy! Ahoy, there! All hands aboard." All hands would come to engulf him with open arms and there would be a joyous reunion. Out of his bulging pockets would come money bags full of gold coin, the reward for his voyage. Then, from his luggage would come presents for everyone, all having been selected with great care.
One day Ann Shepherd was at home alone. Answering a knock at the door she faced two well-mannered gentlemen who asked if they might go in and talk with her. Ann was content with her own religion but when Latter-day Saint missionaries explained a few of the rudiments of the Gospel to her that first day, she was enthralled with their message and urged them to return so that she might hear all that they had to say regarding this new religion. It sounded feasible from the beginning the doctrine of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. She called all the children in to hear the missionaries teach the Gospel. As they all listened, believed and were baptized, and when Richard returned froth his voyage, dear, precious Ann his wife, prevailed upon him to join the Church. She immediately began urging her family to join others who were going to Zion. Richard could not share her enthusiasm to sell out, make all the necessary sacrifices, leave family and friends and even their country, above all, their Beloved England forever. He hesitated, knowing that this "Zion" in America seemed to far removed and too unpromising to be attractive. However, as a result if ambitions. He would take the family to Zion. When his decision was once made, he wrote his letter of resignation to His Majesty, the King, and sacrificed his cherished commission.
Ann Henderson Shepherd was baptized June 10, 1848. Richard Shepherd was baptized September 11, 1848. He was ordained a priest May 13, 1848 by Jes A. Ivins, and an Elder June 16, 1850 by Richard Robinson, North Shield LDS Branch President.
The preparations to leave England took considerable arranging and effort, but as time went on, Ann became increasingly anxious to leave. Word had gone out that the Shepherds, the Strawperrs, and the Wallaces had joined "that Mormon Church", and were going to America. The vicar and members of the Church of England would have no communication with these families of long years staunch membership. Members of their families cut them off from the family records. Business associates of Richard Shepherd, friends and neighbors, turned their backs on them. They were quite alone except for the Saints and the missionaries.
Richard with his wife, their daughters Ann, Mary, Margaret along with a son-in-law Robert Wallace, husband of Ann, and another son-in-law, George Shrewpert, husband of Sarah, crossed the Atlantic Ocean in the ship. "Old England" en route of the Great Salt Lake Valley. Richard Shepherd was forty-nine years of age; his wife, Ann was forty-eight, their daughter Sarah, twenty-four, Ann was twenty-two, Mary, age seventeen, and Margaret, age fifteen.
The ship, "Old England" sailed from Liverpool en route to New Orleans, U.S.A. Match 5, 1854. Elder John 0. Angus was president of the company. There were forty-nine saints aboard. The ship, S.S.
From New Orleans, the company proceeded by steamboat to St. Louis. At his time the dreaded disease of cholera or black fever raging in St. Louis. Soon after the saints arrived, Ann Henderson, the wife of Richard Shepherd was stricken with this disease, and was taken from the boat without her husband and children even knowing it. It was thought that she died at Quarantine Island on May 6, 1854. The lack of information left them all fearful and doubtful. "Who were these heartless Americans who could dispose of a mother without a word of explanation?"
Richard Shepherd and his two daughters, Mary Park and Margaret Hannah, still gained enough courage to continue the journey to Utah by team of wagon.
The long trek across the plains was made under the leadership of Job Smith. The train consisted of one hundred wagons. Many hardships were endured, broken wagons to be repaired at times, crippled horses and oxen. Disheartened members of the company fording rivers, the fear of Indians and many other hardships were encountered by these people.
On September 23, 1854, the Job Smith company arrived in Salt Lake City and pitched their tents near the Jordan River Bridge.
Richard Shepherd settled in the Sixteenth Ward of the Old Salt Lake Stake. On November, 25, 1861 he married a second wife, Hannah Thwaites. They were married by Frederick Kessler.
Richard Shepherd, a man who had spent the greater part of his life on the sea, was never again to sail or even see the ocean during his lifetime. He never saw England, his homeland, again. His daughter, Mary Park Shepherd Home, returned to England for a visit in 1904. She was 68 years old. She was able to visit her birthplace, Taynermouth on Tyne, Northumberland, England. She spent much of her time while there searching the Genealogical records of her people. Richard Shepherd died June 1866 in Salt Lake City, Utah and was buried in the Salt Lake City cemetery on June 24, 1866, five years after he married his second wife.
Hannah Thwaites Shepherd, Richard's second wife, spent her remaining years living with her stepdaughter and son-in-law, Margaret Hannah and John A. Sutton in Paris, Bear Lake County, Idaho.
In 1898, Henry Edward Sutton, a grandson of Richard Shepherd, went to England on a mission.
He visited the Shepherd homeland. While there he met an old retired sea captain and he had a talk with him about Richard Shepherd and family, the man said he remembered them well.
