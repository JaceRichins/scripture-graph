# Grandma Inez Shill Eakins Life Story

_A story of [[Rachel Lythgoe (KWNL-J72)|Rachel Lythgoe]]._

## The story as it was written

An interview with Shanna Spendlove Schaefermeyer, a granddaughter on February 16, 1991.
My ancestors came to America in 1863, that would be Rachel Lythgoe and James Fowler. They came from England. Rachel walked across the plains with a handcart company. The Lythgoes' settled in Henefer, Utah and the Fowlers' settled in Goshen, Utah. James and Rachel met on the ship and James came back to Henefer for her.
Rachel was a seamstress and James was a farmer. In total, they had 14 children.
FAMILY
My mother was Lucy Lythgoe Fowler and my father was Leslie George Shill. I was born September 26, 1906 and was the second child. Our brother Willard was first, but died just three days after he was born.

I was born in my parents home in the bedroom in Henefer, Summit, Utah. I was blessed on 4 November 1906 by Elder James Lythgoe, my uncle, in Henefer. I was baptized on 8 August 1915 by Hosia Richins. At that time, they baptized us in the Weber River down by the river bridge (near where Dearl presently lives). I was confirmed 8 August 1915 by William Brewer.
Dad and mom demanded that we respect them. They didn't tolerate slang words and they expected us to work.
SPECIAL EVENTS
When I was five, the railroad carried the Liberty Bell through Henefer. I remember seeing the crack. We used to go down all the time and watch the trains go through. I was very young when Woodrow Wilson was campaigning for the presidency and he made a whistle stop in Henefer. The back car was all draped with bunting and he stood and waved. Warren G. Harding's death train went through and it was draped all in black.
On June 5, 1918, I was 12, Elvira was 10, and Dearl was about 5, mom and dad went to be sealed in the Salt Lake Temple. We drove the Studebaker to Salt Lake. Of course we had a flat tire and got stuck in the sand at the Sandridge. We stayed with Aunt Lizzie and Uncle Charlie (Shill) dad's half-brother. I remember we went into the Temple and knelt at the alter; Dearl cried the whole time.
SCHOOL
I started school when I was six and attended the Henefer District School; I went through to the 8th grade (about 1920). Dad gave me a wristwatch for graduation that he sent for from Montgomery Ward. Henefer Elementary was a big brick building with four rooms, a hall, and an office. The first and second grades met together as did the third and fourth, fifth and sixth, and seventh and eighth.
I learned how to crochet in the 4th grade. Aliene Eskelson, our teacher, wouldn't let us graduate until we could crochet.
I went to high school at North Summit in Coalville, Summit, Utah. Our colors were purple and white. We had a junior prom and went to basketball games. This was the first time North Summit went to the state tournament; it was also the year they built the new gym (about 1924). We had to ride the bus to Coalville and many times it would rain and snow and we had to get out and push the bus up Walker Hill. I graduated from high school in May 1926. We sat and listened to a speaker. I sat by Lyonel Vernon. There were about 26-27 that graduated.
HOME, WORK, AND PLAY
I was also about six when I learned to milk a cow. I had to be the boy and milk and ride the horse to pull the hay in the barn. I would also mow peas from 4 in the morning to night—drove a team that followed the harvester to prevent the vines from getting tangled. I carried bundles of grain for dad to put in the barn. If I wanted money for things like the 4th of July, I worked all day pulling mustard out of the grain and earned twenty-five cents. At the 4th of July celebration a quarter would buy you a square of pink popcorn, a whistle balloon, an ice cream cone, and a bottle of strawberry pop.
I went to church every Sunday and went to Primary and Mutual. I didn't like Primary, but I did like religion class.
Every Fall, we would go up Echo Canyon and pick hops so mom could make rootbeer in the winter.
My favorite meals were bread and milk; molasses, bacon grease (hot) and fresh bread (dipped your bread in the molasses and grease); potatoes (mashed) and gravy; and eggs.
My dad raised chickens and we put in the chicken coops about when Dearl was 12.
For five cents, we could go to the silent movies. I liked Rebecca of Sonnybrook Farm and Charlie Chaplin. We would go almost every week. Our favorite music was Irish Wash Woman Waltz, Cadrells, and When the Moon comes Over the Mountain. When we were teenagers, mom and dad bought a Victrola and we would listen to the records. (This is in the possession of Shanna and Theron Schaefermeyer). They would also listen to the radio. (One of the old radio's is in the possession of Dawain and Jana Spendlove.)
HOLIDAYS
Mom made all our clothes, our underwear was out of Big J flour sacks. We would get a new pair of shoes for Christmas and slippers for the first of July.
We really didn't have family activities; mostly, we were just kids. Wherever I was, Elvira was right there. We would play run-sheepy-run and kick-the-can. At our first Christmas that I can remember, dad didn't think we needed a tree. He never would get us one. Eilene Stephens who lived next door had always had one; so one year, they cut off a big stump of theirs and we drug it home. We didn't have any popcorn so Mary Alice Stephens popped some corn and showed us how to thread it. We decorated our tree with popcorn and cranberries. From then on, we always had a Christmas tree. One year, we got pretty china-head dolls. Usually, we got one present. There wasn't a lot of money in those days and sometimes we were just lucky to eat.
At Easter, we would boil eggs and roll them down the hill into the flat and take a lunch and eat it. Out Easter baskets were a shoe box.
The circus train would go through every year and dad would promise us a trip to the circus. One year, I was about 7 or 8, we went to the circus. We stayed all night in a hotel, ate at a restaurant, and went down and back on the train.
The best trip I remember was going with dad to Morgan to get the horses shod and eating at Stewart's Cafe.
For our birthdays, mom would make us a cake and put whipped cream frosting on it. This was a real treat.
GROWN-UP YEARS
In 1927, after graduating from high school, I went to work fro Theodore Henefer for $30.00 month. I worked 12 hours a day at the Henefer Store. I mostly worked in the soda fountain and made nut sundaes and sodas, washed dishes, made the fire, and cleaned the ashes. While I was working here, Lean Henefer took me down to Boyles furniture and I bought my cedar chest. I worked here until I got married.
