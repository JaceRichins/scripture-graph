# Short History- taken from a document entitled "The Great Greats!" prepared for a family reunion

_A story of [[Charles Wager Richins (KVP1-SVD)|Charles Wager Richins]]._

## The story as it was written

Charles Wagner Richins (*Judith Richins Grand Father)
Born Aug. 17, 1828 Sheepscombe, Gloucestershire, England
His mother (Charlotte Priscilla Wagner) died when he was 6 years old, and his father died 5 years later. His Aunt “Hannah” took in the Richins children 5 boys and 1 girl. Charles had very little schooling, but taught himself to read and write and do mathematics. He had left school to work on a nearby farm as a chore boy to help the family. At age 21 (1849) he accepted the doctrine and converted to the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. His missionary was the brother of the woman he later married, Louisa Shill. They were married in Syde, England in 1851. Feb. 1853 Charles came to America,, and a year later sent for his wife, and their baby daughter Hannah. Unfortunately, Hannah died in the journey. The reunited couple made their home in SLC for 6 years while he worked as a Laborer. In 1860 Charles and Louisa were called to help settle Henefer, Utah; Their first home was a “dugout” home, but eventually he built a brick home over top of it; which came to be known as “the big house”. It was always open to emigrants, and anyone who needed a home or a bed. In 1861, Charles took a 2nd wife Ester Ovard. He also served as Bishop and Presiding Elder of the Henefer Valley for many years. In 1878, Charles married his 3rd wife, Agnes Mary Willmott. For a short time, all three families lived together i n “the big house”. One son stated, “there were no half-brothers or half-sisters, we were just members of the same family, and this relationship has remained the same all through our lives.” Charles was a successful stockman and farmer, he had considerable real estate and he served also as County Commissioner in Summit County, Ut. He was also noted to be a friend to the Native Americans. In 1882, pressure from US Government caused the family to make a difficult decision. Louisa would move in with their married son, and move to the Mesa, AZ area. However, with more pressure by 1888, Charles and his other two families moved to Colonial Diaz, Mexico. Here he set up successful dairy and cattle operations. 2nd wife Ester, did not like Mexico very much, so she went back and finished raising her family in Hennefer. Agnes stayed in Mexico with Charles. Louisa would go down for visits by train, but she never lived in the colonies.
He died in 1903 in Colonial Diaz. In all he was the father of,30 children, 10 from each wife, however not all reached adulthood.
