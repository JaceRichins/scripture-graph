# Notes on the life of Goudy Hogan, Christian Hyer, Mariner W. Merrill, and Edwin Pace

_A story of [[Christian Larsen Hyer (KWJH-WBR)|Christian Larsen Hyer]]._

## The story as it was written

1854: Mariner Merrill met Goudy Hogan who had 100 acres. Mariner offered to buy some but Goudy refused and appealed to Bishop John Stokes for help but received no support. There was no water for the land so it was "worthless to me". Mariner had not learned the importance of water on the land. Mariner took it to Brigham Young who was sympathetic with Merrill. Brigham Young helped him get a surveyor's certificate for some of the land. Goudy was angry but parted "surrendered."
Fall of 1859: Considerable talk of Cache Valley and settlement. E. Benson counseled Merrill to sell his place and "move up to Cache Valley." So in companionship with Edwin Pace they went to see. The snow was deep.
11 March 1860: Started for Cache Valley with wife and 4 month old child. "Neighbors concluded to move to Cache Valley." (Christian Hyer, Goudy Hogan, Edwin Pace) At Hampton Divide/Cache Hill they bet a bad snow storm. They had no fire but did the best they could till morning. They made a sage brush fire for breakfast. Stock found feed on the side of a hill. The snow was deep and the road unbroken. They stopped and camped again in "Cottonwood Hollow" and remained there several days.
They saw into Cache Valley. It was covered with snow. The teams were very tired. They arrived in Logan and camped where later the site of BYU College was (later Utah State University).
Mariner Merrill had visited Cache Valley the year before. They stopped at "High Creek" and camped for a time to explore to the north, to Coveville. Merrill, Christian Hyer, Goudy Hogan and Edwin Pace teams headed south to Richmond. Local brethren saw they were determined and "probably some land could be found for us." They waited for the snow to melt and then found some land to locate.
Jesse Fox, Territorial Surveyor," came. 20 acre lots, dugouts/cellar, rock wall, covered with brush and dirt. The plowed, harrowed, planted wheat, oats, potatoes, etc. They made canals, cut logs, and tended crops. Indians were troublesome. Edwin Pace concluded to go back to Bountiful.
That summer Brigham Young came to visit and advised a fort 60-100 rods. 2 rods front, 60-100 rods depth and built houses, corrals, willow sheeting, log shingles, sheets at windows, good fire place, sheep and cows (no sheep= no clothes).
There was a log cabin school house/meeting house. They found a teacher to come to Richmond. Christian Hyer served as a teacher for a time.
The first winter in Richmond was "quite pleasant." "Good meetings with good spirit of the Lord." No apostates.
1861: Chosen as 2nd bishop in Richmond. Served for 18 years till called to stake presidency 1st counselor. He was meticulous with money/script/investments.
1866: Goudy, Christian Hyer, and Mariner Merrill built a grist mill 2 miles north east of Richmond.
1866: Established Richmond Cooperative Mercantile Company, one of the 1st in Utah Territory. "Old record book" in possession of Richmond Mercantile Company: Richmond Cooperative was incorporated in the United Order established in 1875. The old brick building of the Co-op had large letters on the upper front wall that read "Holiness to the Lord."
1872: Part of the Union Pacific system extended north from Ogden. Bishop Merrill made superintendent. This was a "financial blessing" to Cache Valley. Later Merrill contracted to extend the railroad to Butte, Montana.
June 9, 1877: Christian Hyer ordained and set apart as 1st counselor to Bishop Merrill.
1879: William L. Skidmore set apart as Bishop if Richmond Ward. Christian Hyer set apart as 1st counselor. They served in these positions in Richmond Ward until 1900.
[notes made by Paul Van Hyer, my father. No date is given.]
