# History of Robert Chapel Shill

_A story of [[Robert Chapel Shill (KWJ7-586)|Robert Chapel Shill]]._

## The story as it was written

Robert Chapel Shill was born on 7 June 1789 in Colesbourne, Gloustershire, England. Robert was likely one of two children born to Robert Chapel and Mary Shill. Robert’s father was a seaman on a ship out of London. His mother, Mary, lived in the village of Colesbourne in the Cirencester district of Gloucestershire, England. The village and parish of Colesbourne lies within the Cotswolds, a designated “Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty”. The village is also near the highest source of the River Thames and is 3 miles east from the Roman road of "Ermine-street", actually Ermin Way (today's A417). The village is ~100 miles west of London and just 46 miles northeast of Bristol.
When Robert was 3 years old, his mother, Mary Shill, married Thomas Lawrence and they had 3 children together as well as raising Robert and his older sister, Ann Shill. Thomas Larwence was likely the only father that Robert knew as he was growing up in Colesbourne, England.
Robert Shill was a young man with a winning smile when he met and fell in love with Prudence Golding. At the time, both Robert and Prudence were living in Syde, England – just 3 miles west of Colesbourne, where Robert was born. After a period of courtship, Robert age 20, and Prudence age 23, were married on the 30th of May 1809 at the parish church in the village of Syde. Prudence is the 7th of ten children born to Robert John Golding and Joan Mary Perry.
Together Robert and Prudence had 12 children:
• Richard Golding Shill – born 12 August 1810 in Shipton Oliffe, Gloucestershire, England. Richard married Elizabeth Preston and together raised their 5 children in Gloucestershire, England. Richard died aboard ship in the Atlantic Ocean at the age of 57.
• Robert Shill – born 11 December 1811 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. Robert married Lucy Wicks in 1834. Robert and Lucy had no children. Robert then married Mary Ann Adcock in 1844 and they had 3 children together – with only the youngest son living to adulthood. Robert died in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil – Robert was a seaman.
• Mary Shill – born 12 December 1812 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. Mary married William Jones and together raised their 8 children in Caudle Green and later Brimpsfield – both neighboring villages to Syde. The family became members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in Caudle Green and most immigrated to the US and settled in Henefer, Utah.
• Prudence Elizabeth Shill – born 31 July 1814 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. Prudence married Joseph Williams and together raised their 3 children in Randcomb, Gloucestershire, England - just ~10 miles east of Syde.
• Susanna Shill – born 12 May 1816 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. Susanna married John Osbourne and together raised their 4 children in Cirencester and later Cheltenham, England – not far from Syde. Susanna’s husband, John, died 7 years after their marriage and she then married Frederick Lapper and together raised their 4 children – along with Susanna’s previous 4 children in Cirencester, Gloucestershire, England – 8 miles south of Syde.
• Rachel Shill – born 12 May 1817 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. Rachel married Henry Ballinger and together raised their 6 children in Cheadle, Staffordshire, England – 100 miles north of Syde.
• John Shill – born 18 December 1818 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. John married Ann Carpenter Hall and together raised their 5 children mainly in Staffordshire, England. John and his son, Charles, joined the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and immigrated to Utah. His wife and other children remained in England. John later married Dinah Tamisin Videan in Ogden, Utah and together raised their 4 children in Henefer, Utah. John was a carpenter and designed and supervised the construction of a Victorian Gothic Church Building in Echo, Utah (near Henefer). The church was used for Protestant church services on the weekends, and as a school for local children during the week. At the time, Echo was largely non-Mormon with the citizens coming from all over – mostly working on the railroad or for the mines. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints later bought the building and used it for their Sunday services and later for youth meetings.
• Charles Golding Shill – born 12 February 1821 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. Charles married Harriet Webb and together had one daughter. Charles and his family were baptized as members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and Charles baptized his parents and siblings later. They immigrated to the US and settled in Henefer. Charles later married Harriet Stronach Faulkes Painter and they raised 11 children together in Croyden, Utah and later in Lehi, Arizona (now considered to be part of Mesa, Arizona).
• William Shill – born 10 April 1822 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. William married Mary Owen and together raised their 5 children in London. After William’s wife, Mary, died, he married Harriet Harrison and together raised their 8 children mainly in Limehouse, Middlesex, England.
• Ann Shill – born 21 January 1823 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. Ann married Andrew Bird and together had 8 children with 3 living to adulthood. Ann became a member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints 2 years before her marriage. Ann’s husband joined several years after their marriage. The family immigrated to the US and settled in Henefer, Utah. Ann’s husband died in New York City while immigrating to Utah. Ann Shill Bird is my 3rd great-grandmother.
• George William Shill – born 1 June 1826 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. George was baptized a member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in 1846 at the age of 19. Four years later he immigrated to the US and moved to Utah. He married Jemima Howard and together they had 2 children. He then married Harriet Mitton who was also from England and who had 5 children from a previous marriage. Only one son moved to the US with her. George and Harriet had one son together. They settled in Croyden, Utah.
• Louisa Shill – born 22 June 1829 in Syde, Gloucestershire, England. Louisa was baptized a member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints at age 17. She later married Charles Wager Richins whom she had helped to convert to the church. Together they had 10 children that they raised mainly in Henefer, Utah.
After their marriage, Robert and Prudence continued to live in the village of Syde. Robert was a farmer and he worked hard to support his large family.
Missionaries from the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints were in the Gloucestershire area and taught members of the Shill family. Robert’s daughter, Ann, was the first in the family to join the church on 6 February 1846. Five other members of the family were baptized that same year – including Robert’s wife, Prudence. Robert was baptized on February 2, 1847. Both Prudence and Robert were baptized by their son, Charles Golding, who became an amazing missionary. The family attended the Caudle Green Branch of the church.
Prudence later wrote a letter to her son, Charles Golding, who was at the time serving as a missionary in London. She reported to him that she continued to attend Church meetings in the village of Sheepscombe (about 5 miles west of Syde) and that they had a full house every Sunday.
Robert and Prudence began to make plans to immigrate to the US and to join with the saints in Utah. Sadly, Robert’s wife, Prudence, died on September 30, 1851, in Syde, England – before they could make the journey. Prudence was 65 at the time of her death, and she and Robert had been married for 42 years.
Robert did immigrate to the United States four years later with his son, Charles Golding, and his family. On 17 April 1855 they sailed on the ship, “Chimborazo” from Liverpool and landed in Philadelphia on 21 May 1855. They then traveled by rail and by boat to St. Louis. The family stayed in St. Louis, Missouri, while preparing for the journey to Salt Lake City. Robert’s son, Charles Golding, worked in St. Louis as a carpenter to earn money for the family’s trip across the plains.
While in St. Louis, Robert’s son, Charles Golding, worked away from the family all week. Before leaving for work on one occasion, Robert said to his son, “Goodbye Charles, I shall never see thee again.” When Charles returned at the end of the week, he found that his father had passed away.
Robert died on October 6, 1856 at the age of 66 – never making it to Utah, but knowing that many of his and Prudence’s children were faithful members of the church.
We are grateful to our ancestors for their examples of faith, devotion and determination.
