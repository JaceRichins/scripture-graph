# Rosa Emily Stayner Call

_A story of [[Arthur Stayner Sr. (KWJD-CW9)|Arthur Stayner Sr.]]._

## The story as it was written

ROSA EMILY STAYNER CALL
When I was eleven months old my father, Thomas John Stayner, was called to the Echo Canyon War. While he was there my mother, Rosa Ann Orrell, was taken by
death. When he came home and was told my mother was dead and buried, it was such a blow to him he tumbled over. My grandmother’s sister took me and raised me ‘tilI was eleven years of age. Four years after my mother died, my father married Rebecca Clark. She was often ill, so at age eleven he sent for me to go and stay with them. With much reluctance of the good lady that raised me,she had to let me go. I stayed with them ‘til I was of age. Then I went back to the one I called mother.
Through Uncle Arthur Stayner, my father’s brother, I attended the University in Salt Lake City two years. I went as a normal student. After that I had papers to show that I could teach school anywhere. So I taught school and gave music lessons. Sometime later I got married going into polygamy.
I always believed in prayer, always prayed night and morning. I have seven
children. I raised all but one who died at eighteen months. The others are all married—all good citizens and true Latter-day Saints. They are raising their children the same way. None of them use tea, coffee or tobacco or and kind of strong drink. All are good workers.
While my children were small I had them pay a little tithing each year to teach them the principle and they have kept it up only paying a full tithing now. I spent most of my married life in Afton, Wyoming. I gave music lessons, played for dancing parties for a number of years. My eldest son, who lives in Afton, plays the cornet and violin. As we followed the music, we attended the best parties. The piano was my instrument.
When I got my new home I took lodgers and boarders, which is what I did the latter part of the time I lived in Afton. I always worked at something. Finally I had to give up the positions I held in the Church for I lost my hearing, which was a sad blow to me. I was one of the aids in the Stake Relief Society, and was a teacher in the ward.
After the children got homes of their own, I left Afton and lived with my sisters in
Bountiful, Utah. With one of them, Ida, I stayed two years, then went to Cache Valley with my married daughters Vienna and Rose. Then Rueben and Caroline Burton wished me to care for their home while they worked away. I was with them one winter in Salt Lake City and one winter in Ogden. I had a good home with them. I then came and took care of my little home in Logan.
Before I left Bountiful to live in Afton, I played for the choir for two years. With
Bountiful’s best singers, we got up a concert for the benefit of the choir. I taught them seventeen single songs and three heavy pieces, had it two nights, got a nice bookcase for the choir. (I took part in concerts when I was a girl.)
My children, whom I love very much, have been kind to me, did for me what their means could allow. I am proud of them. Since living in Logan, I have raised a garden each year. I have always paid tithing.
Source of Information:
Rosa Emily Stayner Call, written by herself
More information about Emily
Emily had one brother, Thomas Orrell who died when he was sixteen years of age of small pox. Mary Marrett, (sister of her grandmother Stayner) who had no children of her own, took the baby Emily and raised her as her own child until she was about eleven years old when she went back to live with her father. He had married Rebecca Clark, and Emily was needed to help in the home and care for the children. She lived with them for about six years then returned to live with Mary Marrett. Also, she lived part of the time with her Uncles Arthur and Charles Stayner, brothers of her father. During this time she received most of her education. She was a gifted musician and was trained in this profession.
She became a capable music teacher, giving lessons as a means of earning an income. She was very energetic and used to practice three to four hours a day. She sang beautifully and was in great demand as an entertainer. She played for choirs wherever she lived, and was organist in Relief Society meetings, and pianist for a dance orchestra. She also taught school for a short time.
Emily married Anson Vasco Call II, as a plural wife. His first wife, Alice J. Call, was her second cousin. They both lived with Mary Marrett. Emily said Anson wanted her for his second wife, but she said, “I would just as soon be tenth as second.” She did not find anyone she liked better, so at the age of twenty-six, she married him as his third wife. She was the mother of seven children, the youngest, Mary Edith died at nineteen months old. Their son, Thomas J., was but six months old when Anson Vasco Call II went to England on a mission. She continued to make her home with Grandmother Marrett and to support herself and her child with means obtained from teaching music. She enjoyed her music much more than
housework, however she managed both. She was the first music teacher in Star Valley.
She very reluctantly gave up a life of social recognition to be a pioneer in the isolated Star Valley in 1888. Her trip into the valley was most hazardous as the wagon tipped over and rolled down the hill which, aside from giving her many bruises, gave her bad impressions of her future home. Anson had built her a one-room frame house at Afton, where she lived several years when a larger house was built. The beautiful eight-room home was built in 1898. Emily’s sons, Thomas and Charles, did much of the work. After the marriage of most of her children,
this home was sold and a smaller one was built for her.
Rosa lived in Afton, enduring many of the hardships of pioneer life. She raised her six children to be honest, industrious, and self-supporting, and to be married in the temple. She then sold her Afton home and moved to Logan, Utah, where she bought a small one. She was unhappy living alone so rented her home and lived with her sister Ida Evans in Ogden, where she helped with the housework. She also assisted her niece Carry C. Burton in the same way.
During her last years she lived in her home in Logan raising vegetables and flowers every summer. She made quilts, braided mats, tatted and crocheted, giving hand made items to her children and friends at Christmas time.
Emily took care of herself until she was eighty-seven, when she made her home with her daughter Charlotte. She passed away in her ninety-fourth year on 12 June, 1950, at the home of her daughter Rosa in Lewiston, Utah.
