# Life Sketch of Edward Richins Gathered & Compiled by Margaret Richins

_A story of [[Edward Richins (KW8L-N6R)|Edward Richins]]._

## The story as it was written

Edward Richins was born at Sheerscomb(?), Gloucester, England April 24, 1835. He was the youngest son of Richard Richins and Priscilla Wager. He had an older sister, Hannah, who died from Tuberculosis about 1837. An older brother, John Edward Richins, ran away from home and was not heard from again. Ed’s other brothers were George Thomas, Thomas, Charles. and John.
His mother passed away when he was 8 years old. The family lived under humble circumstances and employment was scarce. But as the children grew old enough they would work to help support the family.
As a young man, he was visited by the Elders of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints and became interested in their message. He attended their meetings regularly and when he was about 18 years of age he was convinced that the gospel was true, He joined the church in 1852. Three year later in March 1855 he was ready to come to America. He sailed from England on the ship “Charles Banks" After landing in America, he started the journey westward with the "William H. Hooper Co." getting to Utah November 7, 1855He located in Salt Lake City where he remained about three years. This samel year he reached here he was one of the many who an option and went back and brought others that were immigrating across the plains.
After living in and around Salt lake a short time he decided to come to Henefer to visit his brother Charles. He purchased some land just north of Echo, near what is known as the "Witch Rocks". This land proved to be good and very productive so he decided to build his home there. The home was built of loge as most homes were then. It had a dirt roof and consisted
of four rooms which were in a straight row length wise. (Mother says there were no inside doors but one outside door to each room. Also there were windows in each room.)
Sometime later he met a fine young woman whose name was Ellen Tipper. She was the daughter of Harry Tipper of Hedlesome, Gloucester, England. Ellen was born 20 February 1846. She had come to Utah and was living with the William Kimball family at Parley Park now known as Kimball's junction. Their courtship probably began soon after their meeting and Edward brought her to Henefer, on December 25, 1862 they were married and lived comfortably in the home he built. A year later a baby boy was born. They gave him the new of Albert Edward. This little son lived with them 1 year when he passed away. There is no date of his death. Two years later on December 3, 1866, another baby boy came to live at their home named Alma Ether. When Alma was two months old, death came to their home, but this time this time to the mother. Ellen had passed away leaving him with a small baby. She died, February 15 1867 and was buried in a small grave near the banks of the Weber River in Henefer. After her death, Esther, the wife of Charles took care of the baby for a short time. Then Robert Jones and his wife Mary, who lived in a small two roomed log cabin cared for the baby. As time passed on, Robert and his wife it necessary to move back to Henefer. They had started work on a home for themselves. This left Edward with full responsibility of his small son.
Before this time he had met a young woman in Coalville whose name was Sarah Beard. She had come from England about a year before and was living with her older brother Thomas. She would to out to work whenever there was an opportunity. Edward went to ask her if she would come and work for him and care for his son. After being there for a short time and doing such good housekeeping, Edward thought she would make a good wife. They were married June 16, 1867. She was about 20 years of age and he was 34. It seems he fell in love, with younger women as was the case in his other marriages.
Edward and Sarah lived comfortable in this home on the farm. They were both from large families and had experienced hardships before they came to America. And at this time of year there was plenty of work on the farm, which wasn't easy. The women helped as much as possible.
In the fall of the following year a baby girl was born to them named Sarah Ellen. Sarah after her mother; Ellen after his first wife. From this time on a child was born to them about every two years until 12 precious souls had come to them. They also had Alma from the first wife. All of the children grew to maturity except twin boys who died early in childhood.
Uncle George tells a story on his father. It was about the early part of his life on the farm when they were still living in the log house. The family had a nice vegetable garden not far from the house. Very often they would find that some small animal had been in the garden. One morning when Edward looked out on the garden, he was surprised to see a bear digging up the carrots. Of course this frightened them all, especially Edward who hurried to get his gun. As soon as possible he climbed to the top of the house to shoot at the bear forgetting the rest of the family who were still on the ground.
Edward would often go to the river and fish. He enjoyed this very much. He had had very little schooling, but he was very good on managing a farm so it would ray off. Each year they clear another piece of ground from bushes and trees and make it ready for planting. Of course, the plowing was done by oxen the first years. Moroni can remember the oxen well. He says that they’d work till they'd get thirsty and then they would take off for the water regard¬less of what work you were doing. He can remember one day the oxen took off for the river and got caught by a tree. The yolk of the oxen separated them, and they didn’t have enough sense to back up and free themselves.
Later on they used horses for their work. The soil was very productive and grew good crops. The first two years the hay was cut by hand with a scythe. Few men could handle a scythe as well as Edward or do the job and well as he did. Even in later years he would cut his lawn with it and do an excellent job. He was very clever with a seythe. We are told that he harvested the beat potatoes around and had a lot of them. He had a neighbor Joseph Bunot, who grew potatoes also. One year those two families shipped three railroad cars full of potatoes to market. There was plenty of water for irrigation on the farm, but the drinking water was hauled or carried from a spring one forth of a mile away. It was near Leonard Richins' home.
Before the fourth child was born, they could see the need of a larger home so part of the old log house was torn down and a nice new red brick home was built which is still standing in good condition. A few changes have been made in the windows and the porch,
As the children got old enough to help with work or chores on the farm, they were each given their assignment and would be cautioned to do their work well. He always told them that anything worth doing was worth doing well." They were told to be careful and never waste anything.
The children would have their fun along with their work. In the summer time there was the usual sports, such as ball games, swimming, calf roping, fishing and other sports, Edward spent much of his time reading. His favorite book was the "Book of Mormon." Six of his sons were named from the Book of Mormon and the Bible.
As the boys grew to manhood they were well able to manage the farm work. Their father had taught them to take good care of animals, machinery, tools, etc. And they were taught to keep everything clean and neat and in its place. There were several boxes of tools, and they were a1ways kept in place when not in use. We must remember that Sarah was doing her part in
the home too. She managed housework well, taught her girls how to cook, wash, churn butter and many other things.
After their llth child was born and was about 1 year old, Edward received a call to go on a mission. His call was to go to England, his native country. He made arrangements with Sarah and the boys to do farm work and keep things going while he was away. This was a great responsibility to the mother to keep things running successfully, but she was ready and willing to do her part so he could do the Lord’s work. He left home April 10, 1289 and arrived in Liverpool in 1889. After he had been there about a month, he met some of his relatives and visited with them a short time. He was very happy to see them.
After he had been there about 9 months or about Christmas time the weather was bad and he caught cold. He couldn't stand cold weather very well. In a short time, he was better again and was able to go about preaching. Later he developed stomach trouble and his health was poor the remainder of his mission. The family was greatly blessed while he was away, but they were happy when he returned home.
About three years after his mission in 1894, he was made Presiding Elder of the Echo Branch and later made Bishop of the Echo Ward. About 1901 Edward and Sarah were ready to retire from farm work and mowed to Coalville where they had bought a home. They were active in the Coalville Ward. Sometime later he was chosen as high councilman in the stake, with Moses W.Taylor, as Stake President.
After living in Coalville, they had a desire to go to Salt Lake and live so they bought a home there. After a few years there (4 or 5) Sarah became ill and passed away. About a year later, Edward married Sarah's sister, Elizabeth Stone. They lived together about one year when she became ill and passed away.
Edward proved to be a good business manager and a true and faithful Latter Day Saints. He was 80 years of age when he passed away and was buried in a Henefer cemetery. He left a lot of good memories and a host of wonderful children.
