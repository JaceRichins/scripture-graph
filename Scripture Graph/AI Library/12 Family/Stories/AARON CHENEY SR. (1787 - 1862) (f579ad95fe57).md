## The story as it was written

AARON CHENEY SR. (1787-1862)
Aaron Cheney was born on 14 July 1787 in Berwick, York, Maine to Benjamin Cheney and Eunice Hubbard. He married Mehitable Wells on 14 August 1807 in Marsh Field, Essex, Vermont. She was born on 31 January 1786 in Amenia, Dutches, New York, to Selah Wells and Mehitable Tuthill. Selah is a descendant of King Edward I.
Aaron was a trustee of the first Methodist Church at Sandusky, New York, at the time of his conversion to the church in 1831. He was taught by Joseph smith and Parley P. Pratt. Aaron and his sons, Elam and Orin Cheney, and his brother, Hurd Cheney, were among the thirty who joined the church at that time. Elijah Cheney. A Descendant of William Cheney, a brother of John Cheney (born 1605) also joined the church at that time.
Aaron and his family moved to Kirtland, Ohio in 1835. He operated an iron foundry and helped in the construction of the Kirtland temple. They left Kirtland in 1838 as part of the Kirtland Camp. The group, 529 strong, began their 900-mile journey on 6 July 1838. The Cheney family left the group on 11 September 1838 when Aaron became too ill to travel further. They rented a piece of land near Decater, Macon, Illinois, and built a cabin. They raised a crop before moving to Nauvoo, Illinois late in 1839 or early 1840. The prophet, Joseph Smith, stayed overnight in the Cheney cabin in late October 1839 on route to Washington D.C. to see President Martin Van Buren.
Aaron served in the Nauvoo Legion under Captain Hosea Stout. He also helped in the construction of the Nauvoo temple. Shortly after the prophet was killed, the Mormon population was forced to leave Nauvoo in the winter of 1845-46. They crossed the frozen Mississippi River into Iowa and spent the winter of 1847 in a camp on the bank of the Missouri River near Council Bluffs, Iowa.
Aaron, Mehitable, their children, Selah, Olive, Elam, and Ezekiel, and their families went on west to Utah in the spring of 1847. Aaron died in Ogden, Weber, Utah, in September 1862. Mehitable died on 28 Nov 1869 in Gransville, Tooele, Utah.
