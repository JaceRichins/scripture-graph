## The story as it was written

"Lars Hayer was a man small in stature, but straight in form and very well proportioned. He stood not to exceed five and a half feet in height, and weighed about 150 pounds. He was not as noted a character as his brother, Christian, but was a man of very frugal habits. We can imagine it was not small undertaking for him to sell his holding in Norway, leave the land of his nativity, his many relatives, to say nothing of his scores of friends where he had lived about sixty years - all his life - and start anew in a foreign land under strange and adverse environments. We can but admire the noble impulses of this man in thus sacrificing his own feelings for the good of his growing family. His noble wife, Ann Marie Olson, acquiesced in his decision and shared in the privations incident to such an undertaking. About two years after her death, in the spring of 1845, Lars Hayer left Wisconsin, with his youngest child, now about fourteen years, and thereafter made his home with his son, Hans. He died in April, 1863."
Hayer, Oliver. What We Know About the Hayer Family, page10. Logan, UT: Published 1954.
