# Grandfather Oldham (Samuel Heap Oldham) by Granddaughter Alice Olsen Norman

_A story of [[Mary Jane Price (KWNK-N6H)|Mary Jane Price]], [[Samuel Oldham (KWZ1-FBS)|Samuel Oldham]]._

## The story as it was written

Grandfather Oldham-
Samuel Heap Oldham
By Granddaughter, Alice Olsen Norman
My first recollections of him—He had retired from school teaching and also as Bishop of the Paradise Ward, but was a very active member of the church.
He was tall and slender and had white hair, never going bald. He was always very kind to me, and my brothers and sister especially after my mother died, she being his eldest daughter.
For years his children’s families would gather at the big seven bed roomed home on Christmas Eve to have fun around the big tree and see Santa Claus and receive some gifts. One year I received a muff and fur set made by my mother and I was so happy with it.
Grandfather owned a farm in Avon and he sometimes took us up there for a few days, he owned some acres around Paradise and in the north field. He had to be thrifty and conservative to make ends meet to send so many missionaries in the mission field and educate his family as he did. Even with so many to feed, the out of town schoolteachers boarded there many years.
We had many good times at the old home and my step-grandmother was very good to me, after my mother died she curled until I was able to do it myself. She put soap on it to make it stay in curl, this I didn’t like.
When I was six years old I was staying there while my mother was to M.I.A. Conference in Salt Lake City as she was the president of Paradise Ward Y.W.M.I.A. The house started to shake until it slammed the bedroom door shut. The next morning we learned it was an earthquake.
Grandfather Oldham had heart aches too losing his first wife when her tenth baby was born, Erma a cute little three year old girl fell down stairs and broke her back, his son Royal drown while in the mission field in the Southern States. My mother died when her fifth baby was born, his son Raymond died with the flu while serving in the first World War, and his second wife Mary Brown died soon after her son Raymond did.
As we grew up without a father he never once interfered with our home life or corrected us in any way, not even advice on every corner like we received from some folks. He was respected and loved for his refinement and his worth as a teacher.
He married Isabell Obray and made their home in Paradise but for only two years when he passed away 24 July 1923.
