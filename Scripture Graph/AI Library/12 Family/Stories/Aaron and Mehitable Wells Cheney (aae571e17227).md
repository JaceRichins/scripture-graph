# Aaron and Mehitable Wells Cheney

_A story of [[Mehitable Wells (KN6W-2R1)|Mehitable Wells]], [[Aaron Cheney (KNX4-J2Z)|Aaron Cheney]]._

## The story as it was written

Aaron Cheney was born July 14, 1787in Berwick, Maine. His parents were Benjamin Cheney and Eunice Hubbard. When Aaron was a small boy, his father moved to Granby, Vermont. When he was twenty-six or twenty-seven he met and married Mehitable Well. Mehitable Wells was born January 31, 1787. She was among the refugees who came from Long Island, New York to Guilford, Maine with her parents during the Revolutionary War. She was frail but an industrious woman.\
Aaron was a prosperous farmer, a factory owner, and a good business manager. About the time of his marriage he moved to the town of Marshfield, Vermont, and in 1812 he sold some land in Granby, Vermont to his younger brother Hurd. Twelve years later he sold two remaining pieces of property in Granby. He moved to Freedom, Cattaragus County, New York in 1817, where his father's family was located, where he gained possession of several lots of land. While in Freedom, Aaron built and operated an iron foundry with a large crew of men. He produced great quantities of cast iron.
Aaron and his wife were religious people, being faithful members of the Methodist Church of that town. He was chosen trustee in 1827 of the First Methodist Episcopal Church of Sandusky. In 1831 they were visited by the Prophet Joseph Smith and Elder Orson Pratt. They received the Gospel and were baptized member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.
Aaron L Cheney records have the baptism of Aaron April 5, 1834, which is consistent with other family members baptisms in April 1834. The Wells Cheney Family record (Rexburg, Idaho, 1957) states, "Aaron Cheney moved to Freedom, N.Y. and he and his family belonged to the Methodist Church until they were visited by the Prophet Joseph Smith and Orson Pratt. They received the gospel and was baptized in the Mormon Church in 1831." Orson Pratt's records reveal that it was 1834, not 1831 (Orson Pratt Journals, Watson, Comp. p. 38-39, Pioneer Heritage Library, L.D.S.
