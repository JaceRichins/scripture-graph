# Lars Andersen Hejer History

_A story of [[Lars Andersen Heyer (LKK2-XKN)|Lars Andersen Heyer]]._

## The story as it was written

Born Aug. 19, 1784, to Anders and Karen Malene Marcusen Hejer, Lars Andersen Hejer was christened in Kirkevolden, Gransherred, Telemark, Norway. On Jan. 3, 1812, he married Anne Marie Olsen in Tinn, Telemark, Norway. In the spring of 1842 the Hejer family, including eight children from 29 years old to 10, left their home near Kongsberg, Buskerud, Norway, and went on foot to Larvik, where they boarded the Washington, old, loaded with iron, and bound for America. On June 3, 1842, they set sail, and the voyage lasted 14 weeks. They landed in New York on July 30, 1842, went up the Hudson River to the Erie Canal, and finally sailed to Milwaukee on the Great Lakes. In September 1842 the family arrived in Milwaukee and walked several miles southwest to the Norwegian settlement in Rock County, Wisconsin. They secured claims and built log cabins. The men worked for neighbors for $8 a month and often accepted produce or livestock for pay. They later went to Illinois to work in the hay fields for better wages.
 During the winter of 1842 the Hejers met Latter-day Saint missionaries from Illinois. One was their cousin Ole Olsen Hayer. They accepted the gospel. Between 1843 and 1845 the family gradually migrated to Fox River country in La Salle County, Illinois. There they became members of the Mission Branch. Their family constituted up to 95 percent of the branch, which grew to over 300 members. When the saints left Nauvoo to go west in February of 1846, only their son Christian joined the exodus. The rest of the family remained in Illinois. Some later moved to Missouri and Iowa. The Mission Branch dwindled between 1845 and 1858, and Lars died on April 18, 1863, in Lamoni, Decatur, Iowa.
