# A Brief Biography of Joseph William Bond

_A story of [[Charles Wager Richins (KVP1-SVD)|Charles Wager Richins]]._

## The story as it was written

A Brief Biography of Joseph William Bond
The Genealogical and Historical Magazine
January 1927
Joseph William Bond was the son of William Bond and Mary Ann Barker and was born at Fall River, Mass., on the 25th day of November 1852.
His parents were both natives of Manchester, England, where their parents had lived for generations’ before them. They had received the Gospel, through the preaching of the Mormon Elders and were baptized into the church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints. They were early imbued with the spirit of “gathering” and soon began making plans to migrate to the “Promised Land” in America.
Arriving in America, the located temporarily at Fall River, Mass., where they remained until 1854, when in company with other families of the saints they started on the “first lap of the journey” to the great west. Arriving at the Iowa Camp Grounds, they decided to remain here for a couple of seasons in order that they might layup provisions and outfit themselves for the greater journey across the plains, when the time arrived for the start. In the spring of 1855, Joseph’s parents were well equipped for the journey. They had a good covered wagon drawn by too yoke of oxen and loaded with provisions to last them to the valley.
They arrived in Provo, Utah that same fall and went first to Salt Lake City and later to Henefer, in Summit County, Utah, where they made their home. Here Joseph passed his boyhood days. At the age of eight years, he was baptized by Harrison Sparrey, and the same year he entered the grammar schools at Henefer. His father owned a fine farm and on it Joseph spent his young manhood. When twenty-four, he married Prudence Pricilla Richins, the daughter of Charles Richins and Louisa Shill, President John Taylor performing the ceremony in his office in Salt Lake City. On the third day of October, 1877, in company with his brother-in-law Wellington Richins and wife, they started for Arizona, driving three yoke of oxen. The fell in with the Pomeroy-Sirrine company near Johnson, Utah and although they generally made separate camps were often in sight of that company until they reached the Little Colorado River.
While camped at Navajo Springs, Mrs. Bond gave birth to their first child and was cared for by her mother, Louise Richins, who had accompanied them on the journey. The child, however, was feeble and only lived a few days. Its body was taken to Moan Copi by Wellington Richins and Francis M. Pomeroy, where it was place in the village cemetery. The two companies continued together until they reached the crossing of the Little Colorado River, when Joseph Bond, Wellington Richins and Redden Allred decided they would leave the main road and take a short cut across the level mesa towards the San Francisco Mountains. This they did with one saddle horse scouting ahead they picked a was with their slow moving ox teams in a southwesterly direction and finally striking the old Beal Road about where Flagstaff is now located, spending Charismas day in camp there.
On the 26th they continued their journey, passing through Prescott, Skull Valley to Thompson Valley where the decided to locate temporarily, engaging in farming and freighting until July 1878 when Joseph Bond decided to return to Utah. Leaving the company here, he returned to Henefer, Utah having traded his oxen for horses. He remained in Henefer, Utah three years, when he sold his property and took the train with his family, arriving in Maricopa, Arizona. He was met by Wellington Richins, who conveyed them to Mesa, Arizona arriving here on the third day of February 1881.
He purchased the north half of the northeast quarter of Section 21 from Charles Peterson and established his home on the northeast corner. The Mesa canal had been completed to the northwest corner of Mesa and by extending the ditch across the road and along his north line; he was able to get the water on his land the first year.
Joseph Bond played the violin and provided music for many of the dances and parties for many years to come.
