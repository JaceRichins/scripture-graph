# Story of Mary Jane Bishop Price

_A story of [[Mary Jane Bishop (LRRX-9SM)|Mary Jane Bishop]]._

## The story as it was written

written by Netti Price Obray
 I am happy and proud to give a small portion of the life story of my illustrious grandmother, Mary Jane Bishop Price.
 I remember Grandmother Price very well and I can remember that from what my parents and other relatives have told me and from reading of her activities in the community. I would say she put the Gospel into action during her entire life or at least after she became affiliated with the Church.
 A few years back while visiting with Mary Bishop Miles, her niece, she gave me some interesting data.
 The scripture which I think is especially applicable to Grandmother's life is from Proverbs Chapter 31: "Who can find a virtuous woman? For her price is far above rubies. The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her, and she shall have no lack of gain. She doeth him no evil all the day of her life. She seeketh wool and Flax and worketh willingly with her hands. She spreadeth out her hand to the poor. Yea, she reacheth out her hands to the needy. She looketh well to the way of her household and eateth not the bread of idleness. Her children rise up and call her blessed. Her husband also and he praiseth her. Give her the fruit of her hands and let her own works praise her at the gate.
 Mary Bishop Price was born 17 December 1819 in Shrewsbury, Shropshire, England. She was the daughter of John Bishop and Catherine Evans. There were nine children in this family, seven sons and two daughters. Mary was the fourth child, the oldest girl. We find in reading her story one of the most outstanding characteristics was her devotion to her family and this may have come as a result of the many tragedies that came in her father's family. Her brother even was killed in a war in India. Thomas, while working in a gas factory, was caught in the machinery and literally torn to pieces. Samuel, the youngest, was drowned in the River Severn. The youngest girl died when a small a child.
 The grief of the parents over the loss of their children undermined their health, the father especially as he held himself accountable for the loos of Samuel. He died a short time afterward at age fifty-seven. Then years later the mother died, leaving Mary to mother and care for her four brothers, Richard, John, William and James. not only did Mary have full responsibility of the family, but also she had to go to work to secure means to feed and clothe them. She found employment in an English laundry. Her first article to press when trying out ofr her job was a skirt with thirteen ruffles. She did it so well she was hired.
 While they were still living in the family home, the Mormon missionaries came to them with the Gospel message. In a short time, Mary, William and James were baptized and immediately began preparations to come to Zion. While crossing the ocean, Mary became acquainted with one of the sailors, Edward Price. We have no proven facts or figures, but our supposition leads up t think she fell in love with him, explained the gospel as she understood it, and converted him, for when they landed he remained with her and when they got as far as Missouri, they were married.
 They made the trip across the continent by ox team. William and James came with them. Richard came to America later, but did not join the Church. He married a French girl and returned to France. Grandmother never heard of him afterward.
 When they arrived in Utah they made their home in Provo. The two brothers lived with them until they married. She felt a strong responsibility toward her brothers, having cared for them since childhood and even after they were married she felt they needed her advice, which their wives sometimes resented. The result being a little friction at times. I would like to mention the love that existed between these three. I have been told that the love and devotion of these two brothers for their sister, Mary, amounted almost to worship.
 While in Provo five children were born to Edward and Mary Price, two girls and three boys. One girl and two boys died there. While the children were small the family moved to Franklin (now in Idaho) and helped to pioneer that settlement. Their names are on the monument erected to the memory of the Franklin Pioneers. Later they moved to Old Paradise and then down to Paradise and built a two-room dirt-roofed home. Incidentally, the great great grandson, Grant Norman, of this couple now lives in the home, now remodeled and with a new addition.
 Three more sons were born here, but all died while they were small.
 Although Grandmother was a strong minded dominant woman, the loss of so many of her children made her exceptionally tender and lenient with the two she did raise and with all children she came in contact with. Grandmother was a strong ambitious woman and her efforts were not centered on her family alone. She was a friend to everyone, always willing to help the sick and the needy. Whenever there was sickness of a death or any other trouble, you would always find Mary Price doing all in her power to alleviate pain and sorrow. For many years she made burial clothes, being exceptionally fine as a seamstress. She believed while she was a member of the Church she owed it service. She was an efficient leader and her social prestige found a place for her in all the religious and social affairs of the Ward. She worked in the presidency of the Relief Society for 25 years.
 In 1893 a charitable and benevolent association was organized and she was chosen as one of the board members.
 As I mentioned, Grandmother was always on hand to aid the sick and the needy. Doctors were seldom called in. Although she was never granted a legal license to practice in obstetrics, she probably had what meant more to people in those days. She had the time, the desire, and the ability to help, and the spirit of the Lord to assist her, and she assisted whenever she was needed. Her home was never more than two rooms but she willingly shared this with her daughter when she married and again later allowed her first grandchild, Samuel Oldham, and his wife, Lucy Schow, to occupy one room. To her, work was a song of triumph.
 Grandmother and grandfather were farmers and my father, John Lloyd, being the only son, helped with the farm work. Grandmother, besides taking care of her family and doing so much for others in the community, helped with the harvesting until father grew up and was able to take over the bulk of the world.
 As I remember Grandfather, he was a happy good-nature and submissive little man and as I have mentioned, Grandmother was a more dominant person and seemed to be the manger and moving force in the family. She taught the Gospel by precept and example. Her children were given a deep religious training. Her motto was "teach the need of a clean active life, by living one."
 She passed on to her reward 12 March 1904 and was buried in Paradise cemetery. She had not been feeling well for a short time and passed peacefully away in her sleep.
 When her daughter, Janie, passed away at the birth of her 10th child, Jane Oldham Pierce, she grieved so over the loss of her daughter and the fact that she left such a big family, it undermined her health. At the time of her death, she had one living child and 19 grandchildren.
