# My Mother Emma Turner Stayner

_A story of [[Emma Irene Turner (KWJ6-XX3)|Emma Irene Turner]]._

## The story as it was written

By Katherine Elizabeth Stayner (daughter)
“My father, Arthur Stayner, came to this country in 1853. He was educated in England to be a Minister for the Church of England. He also had some military training. When he was about 16 he went to France with John Taylor to help translate the Book of Mormon into French. He was well versed in French, Latin, and Greek.
My mother, Emma Turner, was born November 31, 1853 in Great Malvern, Worchester, England, being the eldest of ten children. She was the daughter of Henry and Anne Steed Turner. My grandfather was one of the 300 United Brethren who joined the Church all at one time under the supervision of Wilford Woodruff.
My Mother was sent to a girl’s school at a very early age. Here she was taught, aside from regular educational subjects, art, music, sewing, lovely needle work, and also charm and culture. I have a sampler which she worked when she was seven. It has a border of finely made stitches of flowers. In the corners there are pine trees, the alphabet, and sized letters and at the bottom these words:
“When I daily kneel to pray
 As I am taught to do
 God does not care for what I say,
 Unless I feel it too.”
 Emma Turner – 7 years
My Father was kept at boarding school from the time he was five years old until he was the age of seventeen years.
When my Mother came across the plains in 1856, she came a year before the rest of the family who came in 1857, with friends of the family. Grandma Dougall, and her two sons and others.
After my Mother was married, Grandma Dougall wanted one of her children to name one of her children after her, She named her youngest, me, Katherine after her. Elizabeth is after my Father’s Mother.
My Mother used to make the boy’s clothes out of my Father’s and Uncles so the boys were always well dressed. She used to do lots of fancy work and needlepoint. She generally had hired help, but even then she had too much to do attending to her family and her social and church duties. My Mother worshiped my Father and her family and was happy to work for them.
She was taken very ill with Mountain fever, and with my grief stricken Father and all her children around her bed, she died at the age of 41, almost in her prime. My sister, who was the oldest, took charge until she was married. This was when she was quite young. My grandmother wanted my Father to give me to her as I was a baby, but he would not let any of his children be separated, for which we were very thankful.”
