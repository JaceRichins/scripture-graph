# Pappa Thomas W. McBride's death by Alma M. Richie 2017

_A story of [[Thomas White McBride (KWJR-J45)|Thomas White McBride]]._

## The story as it was written

3/2/2017
Hi my family, here are true stories of your distant grandparents. [Thomas McBride was born in 1776. He calls himself a soldier because he was born at the same time a new nation came into being. It is apparent that he had some contact with the army even as a small boy. This was a common practice during this period of history. This may allude the the reason he always claimed to be a soldier in the Revolutionary War.]
The old man, trembling with age rather than with fear, surrendered his gun, saying, “Spare my life; I am a Revolutionary soldier.” This old gray-haired veteran of the American Revolution was your grandfather, five times removed. His name is grandfather Thomas White McBride. Miss Rebecca Judd saw from a hiding place what happened next. He was met by a number of the mob in front of Brother Haun’s house. Standing in front of them he raised his hands in his pathetic appeal. They shot him in the chest with his own gun. Then Mr. Rogers of Daviess County fell upon him and hacked him to pieces with an old corn cuter, leaving the veteran soldier of the Revolution covered with a score of unsightly wounds. His brains were oozing from his cracked skull and his white hair sprinkled with gore. Many dead and those left wounded lay crippled for life. The ladies were ravished and young boys 7 through 10 were caught and blown to pieces.
 Cathrine McBride Pope stood along the bank of the Mississippi River. She and her husband, William Monroe Pope looked back at their City, Nauvoo the beautiful. They were both young in their late 20s. It had now been ten years since the death of her father in October 1838. Her thought brought back their recent temple covenants of a celestial marriage performed in the Nauvoo Temple. There the prophet instructed all in the idea of eternal family organization, the eternal union which are the very foundation of everything worthy to be called happiness. She learned then that families may be bonded together forever. Without a doubt she knew that whatever happened in their journey, now they would have the strength and inspiration as they faced hardships along the way. O yes, mother Cathrine would give birth to baby Mary Isabelle Pope, our grandmother, four times removed, on December 10, 1851 at Pottawattamie, Iowa on their journey to the Rocky mountains.
My precious little family, we are after eternal exaltation in the Celestial Kingdom of God. And we want to feel that this is the main object of existence, that this is why we were born and that God has revealed Himself from the heavens, restored the Holy Priesthood. Let us go out and do His will. Our grandfather Thomas McBride is Scottish. We have this blood running through our veins. Yes, you and I are from the Scottish Cat Clan. You can be called “Brave Heart.” That is who you are.
This I know is true and we love you very much.
Grandpa Alma and Father
