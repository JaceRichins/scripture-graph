# Biography of Daniel Miley Smith by Alvira Olpin

_A story of [[Daniel Miley Smith Sr (KWJC-G74)|Daniel Miley Smith Sr]], [[Emma Hannah Sinfield (KWJC-G7W)|Emma Hannah Sinfield]]._

## The story as it was written

About 1770 a man and his family by the name of Schmidt landed in America from Germany. They were seeking a new home where they could worship God according to their belief. This man was the great grandfather of Daniel Miley Smith, the subject of this sketch. The Schmidts having lived in a country where there were mountains decided to locate in Franklin County, Pennsylvania because there were some mountains near by. There several generations of Schmidts were born and lived. The Schmidts after living in America for some time adopted the English way of spelling their name.
Daniel Miley Smith was born July 28th, 1838 in Quincy, Franklin County, Pennsylvania. He is the seventh child in a family of eleven. His parents names were Conrad Smith, born November 6th, 1802 and Ann Elizabeth Geesaman born March 1st, 1809. The children in this family were Charles J., Harriet, Judith, Conrad, Elizabeth, Hetty, Daniel Miley, Thomas G., Mary Catherine, James M. and Benjamin F.
In the year 1846 Daniel Miley's mother died leaving five children under eight years of age. The baby was only two weeks old. Harriet the oldest girl, then 18 years old, took the responsibility of caring for this family. Two years later the father died. Harriet had married Valentine Rock and the courts appointed him guardian over the five smaller children. The home of the Smiths still stands in a secluded spot close to the mountains. And not far away is the little school house with its fireplace and over the one door and two windows is a porch.
About 1848 the Mormon Elders visited the homes of the Smiths and Rocks and most of them joined the church. Charles, the oldest child, not being a Mormon became dissatisfied and asked to be appointed guardian over the younger children. He immediately apprenticed to a Tanner. This did not last long, the boys soon became dissatisfied and returned to their sister and she again took the responsibility of the children. Daniel Miley did team work for farmers and neighbors.
The Smiths and Rocks being poor farmers did not travel far from home but after the gospel found them the desire to come west overcame all obstacles and they gathered together all their belongings and started west. They traveled as far as Pottawattimie County, Iowa. There they stayed for four years. Daniel Miley came to Utah in 1859. Valentine Rock's mother whom they all called Grandma Rock, had a little money so she obtained a wagon and oxen. Being anxious to go to Utah with a company in 1859, Daniel Miley was given the job of driving her team to Utah.
The Rocks (Valentine and Harriet) with their small children and the rest of the Smith children did not come to Utah until 1860. I went to the Historian's Office in Salt Lake City to try and find out the exact date that father came to Utah. I found the following but nothing about father or who came with him. Valentine Rock, 34; Harriet Rock 30; Reuben Rock, 11; Mary Rock, 8; Jacob N. Rock, 6; Jenny Rock, 2; (Henry 25; Leannah, 19; Eliza, 11 months; were relatives of Valentine Rock) (Thomas Smith 20; Benjamin Smith 13; brothers of Harriet Rock) all crossed the plains in Captain James D. Ross' Company which arrived in Salt Lake City September 3, 1860.
Daniel Miley's schooling was very limited. He learned to read the printed page but could not write , but he was good at figures. After his marriage he learned to write his name. His uncle was the school teacher in their little home school. English and German were taught and all the people spoke German or as it was called, Pennsylvania Dutch.
We are told that James Smith, one of the signers of the Declaration of Independence was a relative of the Smith's.
The first winter after reaching Salt Lake CIty, Daniel Miley stayed at the home of Albert Carrington. In the Spring he went to Farmington to work for Bishop Hess. He later came back to Salt Lake City to work for Daniel H. Wells. There he met Emma H. Sinfield, who was working in the Wells home, whom he later married. In 1864 they moved to Pleasant Grove to farm for Daniel H. Wells who owned a large tract of land in Utah County.
The first year they lived in a little two roomed adobe house owned by John Baker located on the corner of First South and First West Streets. The next year they moved to the house, the adobe part, on First South between Center and First East. In 1868 or 1869 Daniel Miley bought a piece of land from William Sterrett located on Third South Street and began farming for himself. I have learned that my father bought a squaters right from William Sterrett, the land coming into the market in 1870. At that time John Brown, Mayor, Issued Mayor's Deeds to the land.
Out of this union eleven children were born, five boys and six girls. Four of the girls died before they were eight years of age. The children's names were Eliza Kate, Daniel Miley Jr., Florence Ella, Melvin, Earl C., Alvira, Harriet Dean, Burdett, Hetty Maud, Pearl, Elizabeth, Lawrence. Daniel Miley was a very successful farmer, he loved to cultivate the soil and often experimented in trying to develop better kinds of vegetables and fruits. At one time people from all over town came and bought their garden seeds from him. For many years he was a successful nursery man. Daniel Miley planned to plant and raise enough vegetables, grains and fruit to feed his family. He often said he wanted to have enough work to keep his family busy at home.
Daniel Miley brought the first reaper and self binder to Pleasant Grove, his oldest son drove it and demonstrated the cutting of grain. In connection with the farm work he was in the Implement Business. At one time he and Evan Gamett were successful business partners and ran an implement business near the Union Pacific Depot.
At one time he was water master of the Pleasant Grove Irrigation Company, and was very conscientious i the distribution of the water. He was an unassuming man. He seldom took any part in Church or politics but was an honest man both with God and man.
He died on April 26, 1905 in Pleasant Grove, Utah.
In the year 1870, father and a number of men, mostly young men, were in Battle Creek Canyon chopping down trees for wood. Among this group was his nephew Ruben Rock. As one of the men called to look out a tree was falling, Ruben ran up hill thinking the tree would fall down hill but it caught him killing him instantly. The men made a bed on his cart and brought him home. His mother was my father's oldest sister. She was like a mother to him because she mothered her five youngest brothers and sisters.
One of our neighbors told me that father worked in the mines in American Fork Canyon in the winter to earn a little money.
In those early days the men hauled all their firewood from the mountains. Coal was not thought of. One day a number of men including Joseph Olpin, Joseph Wadley, Father and others were getting wood. They discovered a bear in a hole at the roots of a large tree. They decided to catch it and while one of the men went for a rope, father teased it by poking it and throwing dirt in at it. This maddened it so, it came out at him, he grabbed his axe giving it a terrible blow killing it.
When the church in Pleasant Grove decided to plant trees around their meeting house which stood on the corner of Second South and State Street, my father being a nurseryman was asked to supply the trees and plant them. He donated one-half of the trees to the cause. There were two kinds of trees planted, White Ash, a slow growing kind and a Poplar, a quick growing kind. Later the Poplar trees were removed. The White Ash still stand. The park has been planted with grass, shrubs and flowers. Where this grove now stands were the old tithing barns and corrals and the northwest corner is where Bishop Brown's home stood for many years. This park was later sold to the Pleasant Grove city and they still preserve it as one of the beauty spots of the city.
The year the government took the church property from them, father decided not to pay tithing. That year his crops were almost a failure. He said from now on we pay our tithing. My brother often questioned the idea of tithing but always Father would say one-tenth belongs to the Lord.
(I found this typed biography in my grandfather's family history book. My grandfather is Dewitt Cross Smith, son of Melvin Smith. Alvira was his aunt. No date to give a clue when it was written.)
