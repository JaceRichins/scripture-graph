# Aaron and Mehitable Wells   (without pictures)

_A story of [[Mehitable Wells (KN6W-2R1)|Mehitable Wells]], [[Aaron Cheney (KNX4-J2Z)|Aaron Cheney]]._

## The story as it was written

Aaron Cheney and Mehitable Wells.
 by Nina D. Harris - Feb 2014

Chapter 1: Youth and Marriage
___________________________
Aaron Cheney was born in Berwick, Providence County, Maine on the 14th of July 1787. He was the fourth child of Benjamin Cheney and Eunice Hubbard. Aaron was the first boy, his older sisters were: Betsey who was born in 1784; Sarah who was born in 1786; and Olive was born on 20 Dec 1786.
Their father, Benjamin, had been a Revolutionary war soldier, who had served valiantly from a very young age to preserve the freedom of the country.
Berwick was a town situated on the Salmon Falls River which divides Maine from New Hampshire. The town’s main industry was the lumber trade, which was probably where Benjamin worked.
When Aaron was a small boy, his father, Benjamin, moved his family about 90 miles north and west to Granby, Vermont. Exactly when he arrived is not known, but he was certainly there by 1790 when Aaron was three years old.
On 14 June 1790, Benjamin Cheney and Joseph Herrick Sr were each granted land in Granby, Vermont as an inducement for settling the area. They were the first settlers in the area. Benjamin was given a 15 acre mill-site, where he would build and run a sawmill. Benjamin and Eunice moved into Granby in 1790 or 1791. Grandby was never more than a collection of homes around some lumber mills. It is supposed that when the two decided to settle in Granby they already had in mind that they would build and run lumber mills in the area, for later they obtained grants for below Granby, for that purpose. Benjamin also ran a farm to help provide for his family.
Soon the family had many more children. The first white child born in Granby was Hurd Cheney, Benjamin's son. He was born on 16 Sep 1791. Moses was born in 1793, Martha in 1795, Anna on 30 Jan 1796. Ephraim on 1 May 1796/98, Benjamin was born in 1798, Joseph in 1799 Enoch in 1802. They had one other child, Eunice, but her birthdate is unknown. (This brings the total number of Children born to Benjamin and Eunice Cheney up to 13.)
 Benjamin was a well-respected man. In a town meeting held in March l798, he was elected one of the town's "Listeners, and Selectman". He was probably an educated man who, with his wife, gave his children a good education. As a new settlement, Granby did not have a school to send the children to. The first school was not kept until Benjamin's daughter, Sarah, was old enough to teach it.
Meanwhile Aaron would have learned not only reading and writing, but how to farm and run a sawmill.
Meanwhile, Mehitable Wells was born in Amenia, Dutchess, New York on the 1st of January 1787. She was the 4th of seven children born to Selah Wells and Mehitable Tuthill. Her family moved to Marshfield, Washington, Vermont in 1780. It was there that she grew up. It was also here that she met and married Aaron Cheney. Marshfield is located along the Winooski River about 30 miles West and South of Granby (44 miles by today's roads (see map)).
Aaron Cheney and Mehitable wells were married in Marshfield, VT on 14 August 1807. They were both 20 years of age at this time. They settled down in Marshfield and Aaron ran a prosperous farm here.
 Although a very frail woman, Mehitable was a capable and industrious housekeeper. Soon children came to their family and Mehitable proved to be a faithful wife and mother. (Only eight of their eleven children would live to adulthood.)
Their first four children were born in Marshfield. Selah Cheney was born about 1808. Amasa F. Cheney (pictured here) was born on the 29th of December 1809. An infant was born in 1811, but died soon after and was not named. Their fourth child, Orrin Cheney was born on 10 August 1813. Orrin was either born in Marshfield or in Freedom (Fredonia) NY--we have listings for both.
At some point between 1813 and 1815 the family moved to Freedom, Cattaragus, New York. The first settlers arrived in the area in 1811 and the town was founded in 1820. Aaron's father, Benjamin Cheney, also moved there between 1813 and 1817.
"The first settlers of Freedom were enterprising and sturdy pioneers manly from New Hampshire and Vermont, who brought with them their habits of persevering industry and economy and their sterling principles of honor and integrity" (Adams, 674).
At this time "Cattaraugus County consisted of wooded rolling hills and narrow stream valleys. In the center of Freedom Township, a wide valley provided an area where trees could be cleared and farms started. The new settlers took on the backbreaking task of clearing forest, building roads and homes, and laying out farms. Freedom Township was created in 1820, and by its first census in 1825, over nine hundred people lived there…[The] leading early settlers and town leaders such as the Metcalfs, Sawyers, and Cheneys, lived near town" (Steele).
Aaron cleared land and began a prosperous farm. By 1822 Aaron is listed as living on lot 12 and his father Benjamin on lot 19. In the land records of 1835 Aaron is listed as owning 113 acres of which 20 w ere improved. (His father, Benjamin, owned 20 of which 8 were improved. Aaron's brother, Hurd, owned 50 acres, 6 improved) (Everts). His Uncle, Ephraim, lived in the nearby Fish Lake settlement and there were many other Cheney's in the area.
Aaron and Mehitable continued to raise their children and have more children as well. Their next child, Selar was born here in 1815. It is possible that their oldest child, Selah, had died by this point. It was quite common for names or variations of names to be reused if the children died. However one listing has him living until 1868.
 Their sixth child and only girl, Olive Mehitable Cheney was born on the 15th of May
1817. She was born in Bloomfield, Cattaraugus, New York. (Currently there is no
Bloomfield in Cattaraugus county, although there could have been at the time. There is a
Bloomfield 71 miles away near Canandiagua.(in Ontario County)). In 1819 another infant was
 born and sadly died. Another boy, Aaron was born 5th of March 1822 in Freedom.
 Elam Cheney (pictured on the right) was born in Freedom, Cattaraugus County, New York on the 16th of May 1825. He was their seventh surviving child. He had light brown hair and blue eyes. (He would not grow to be a tall man).
Ezekiel Wells (pictured on the left ), was born the 13th of June 1828 in Freedom. He was
their youngest surviving child. Another infant was born about 1830 but died soon after. Only eight of Aaron and Mehitable's 11 or 14 children survived to adulthood (different sources list more children who died on birth).

Chapter 2: Religion
_____________________________________________
Both Aaron and Mehitable were religiously inclined and were faithful members of the
Methodist Church. "Aaron was a trustee of the First Methodist Episcopal Church of Sandusky,
a village just west of Freedom" (Steele). This Methodist Church met in the local schoolhouse,
because they did not yet have their own building. "Freedom [and the surrounding villages
made up] a tight-knit religious community of people who were searching for religious answers"
(Steele).
Meanwhile in Palmyra, New York a young Prophet named, Joseph Smith, began
 translating some Gold Plates given to him by an Angel of God. A young school teacher by
 the name of Oliver Cowdery became convinced that this was God's work. He became the
 Prophet's scribe and was very involved in the restoration of the Gospel.
Oliver shared the good news of the Gospel with his family, including his brother, Warren
Cowdery who lived in Freedom, NY and was the town physician and postmaster. "He was
introduced to Mormonism by Oliver, who kept him abreast of the development of the
Church." In turn, Warren shared what he learned with his neighbors. William Hyde,
whose family lived next to the Cowderys, wrote that Warren “obtained from his brother,
Oliver at an early date some of the proof sheets to the Book of Mormon, some of which
we had the privilege of perusing, and we did not peruse any faster than we believed”
(Steele).
The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (or Mormon Church as it was called) was officially organized on the 6th of April 1830, with Joseph Smith as the Presiding Elder and Oliver Cowdery as its second. This exciting news certainly made its way to Oliver's brother in Freedom.
"In late October 1830, Oliver Cowdery, Peter Whitmer Jr., Ziba (Richard) Peterson, and Parley P. Pratt preached to a group of Seneca Indians living on the Cattaraugus Reservation, while passing through western New York en route to Missouri on the Lamanite mission" (Steele). At this time they left 2 Books of Mormon with the Indians. It is believed that it is one of these books which found their way to Freedom in the care of Wealtha Hatch.
Family records indicate that Aaron and Mehitable were baptized into this church in 1831. This is certainly possible. Others of the community had been baptized by this point. However we have no record of the exact date of the baptism in 1831. Some histories are specific in saying that Aaron was taught by Joseph Smith and Parley P. Pratt. We have no record of Joseph Smith being there in 1831, though he was there in 1834 when many more Saints were baptized.
Meanwhile the Gospel message was shared freely around Freedom, NY. "Early converts, like Warren Cowdery and Wealtha Hatch, shared their religious convictions with friends and family, allowing for the gospel message to grow in the community’s consciousness" (Steele).
On 9 March 1834 the Prophet, Joseph Smith, and Parley P. Pratt stopped in Freedom to enlist volunteers to march to Missouri to aid distressed Saints. (This camp would be called Zion's Camp). While they were in Freedom, they not only visited their fellow Saints, but preached the Restored Gospel to all. Joseph Smith and Parley P. Pratt moved on after only a few days, but more missionaries arrived to continue the preaching.
The County history printed in 1879 reads, "At the time mentioned, the quiet precincts of Fish Lake neighborhood [a community near Freedom] were invaded by Joe Smith, Sidney Rigdon, John Gould, and Parley Pratt. They made their headquarters at Rufus Metcalf’s and Dr. Warren Cowdery’s. Meetings were held, daily and nightly, in barns and dwelling-houses, and a prodigious excitement pervaded the minds of many people in that immediate vicinity. Among those residents who were conspicuous as believers … were Dr. Cowdery, Rufus Metcalf, Nehemiah Sparks, Orrin Cheney [Aaron's son], Hurd Cheney [Aaron's brother], Capt. Heman Hyde, Heman T. Hyde and wife, and Barber Lawson. As a result, 30 men and women were induced to join the Mormons, and emigrated with them to Kirtland, Ohio. Some came back and renounced their faith in Mormonism, while others continued with them to the end of their lives" (Everts).
Steele says that in the space of a few months in 1834-35 over 40 people were baptized into the Mormon Church. Because these members left other churches, the other churches were negatively affected. The Methodist Church especially was decimated and was never the same.
 At that time it was not uncommon for members of the Church to be re-baptized when they wanted to renew their covenants. The exact date of Aaron and Mehitable's original baptisms in 1831 have been lost, but the Ancestral file lists the following dates, which are probably re-baptism dates: Aaron on 5 April 1834 and Mehitable on 9 April 1834. Furthermore, Orson Pratt recorded that on 2 April 1834 they preached at the Fish Lake Schoolhouse and afterwards baptized four persons including Amasa and Olive Cheney, the children of Aaron Cheney (Steele).
At the Freedom [Mormon] conference in January 1835, where representatives from eleven western New York branches attended, [Presiding Elder] Warren [Cowdery] reported that the Freedom Branch had seventy-four members in good standing." Meanwhile the members continued to share the Gospel and more people were baptized including more Cheney family members (Steele).
Because so many community members joined the Church, the members were committed to the Gospel together. They helped and supported each other and were less likely to be persecuted for their faith. When the Saints chose to gather in Mormon centers like Kirtland and Nauvoo, these
community members often moved together in groups..
The Aaron Cheney family continued to live in Freedom, New York until 1835.
CHAPTER 3: Gathering to Kirtland
Meanwhile, the members of this new Church, wanted to live among others belonging to their new faith. In fact they were commanded to gather together. In 1835, the place to gather was Kirtland, Ohio, where Joseph Smith was living. Joseph Smith first arrived there in 1831 and found many converts. Soon many Saints were moving to Kirtland. Many families from Freedom, New York moved in 1834 and 1835. Aaron and his family made the 140 mile journey to Kirtland, Ohio in 1835.
At this time his oldest son, Selah (if he was alive), would have been 27 years old. Amasa F. Cheney was 26 years old; Orrin was 22; Selar was 20; Olive was 18; Aaron was 13; Elam was 10; and Ezekiel was 8 years old
 Since Elam was old enough now to be baptized, the ordinance was performed in Kirtland in 1835. He was baptized in the Chagrin River by Elder Dominicus Carter. The baptisms took place in "a large pool where the river was dammed to provide power for a mill" (Holzapeel,61). Elam was ten years old at the time.
Aaron Cheney purchased a farm two and one half miles southeast of the Kirtland Temple. Aaron put all his sons to work on the farm.
Aaron also built a foundry. A foundry is a factory where metal is melted down and poured into molds to make things. A number of men were employed there and large quantities of cast iron were produced. His son, Elam, learned to work at a forge here.
Aaron was a very knowledgeable man and is given credit for teaching his son, Elam, farm management, iron working, and how to build and run a grist mill among other things. Aaron would have taught all his sons as much as he could.
Because of all the Saints who were moving to the area, there was quite a lot of building going on. A Temple was being built and many small dwellings were being built near the Temple and also along the Chagrin River.
Aaron helped on the construction of the Kirtland Temple. Construction had begun in 1833 and by the time the Cheney's arrived the walls and tower were going up. “Almost all able-bodied men who were not away on missions worked on the temple” (Church, p. 163). “By November 1835 the exterior plastering commenced; crushed glassware was mixed with stucco to make the walls glisten...the interior was finished during February of 1836. The sisters made curtains and carpets” (Church, p. 164).
The completion of the Temple brought many blessings to the Saints. From 21 Jan to 1 May 1836 “Probably more Later-day Saints beheld visions and witnessed other unusual spiritual manifestations than during any other era in the History of the Church”(Church, 164). The Temple was dedicated on the 27th of March 1836 and many Spiritual outpouring were received.
Though most of the Saints were poor, the temple brought optimism and hope for the future of Zion. The Saints rejoiced in working hard for Zion.

CHAPTER 4: The Kirtland Camp
_________________________________
In the later half of the year 1837, many of the Saints began to be lifted up in pride. Many difficulties followed and many began to apostatize from the church. The Aaron Cheney family stayed firm in the faith.
In 1837 many apostates stirred the people of Kirtland up to extreme anger against the Saints. Hepzibah Richards, sister of the apostle, Willard Richards, wrote, “A dreadful spirit reigns in the breasts of those who are opposed to this Church. They are above and beneath whatever is laudable. Their leading object seems to be to get all the property of the Church for little or nothing, and drive them [the Saints] out of the place”(Church, p. 177).
Between November 1837 and June 1838, 200-300 Kirtland Saints left the Church. The mobs joined and threatened violence. An assassination plot was made against the life of Joseph Smith. He was warned of the plot and escaped from Kirtland altogether. He went to Jackson, Missouri where there was another gathering of Saints. This was in January of 1838.
That same month the lives of the members of the high council were also threatened and most of the faithful decided to follow their leader to Missouri. “Early in March the seventies began planning ways to help the poorest Saints move to Missouri. One of the presidents of the quorum, James Foster, had a vision of an orderly company of about five hundred Saints traveling to Missouri and camping by the way. Directed by vision and prophecy, the seventies drew up a constitution, formed a camp of those willing to abide by it, and designated leaders to preside over companies. Captains were to encourage their companies to keep the commandments and observe the Word of Wisdom.
Hepzibah Richards wrote, “I must give you some account of the plans and prospects of this Church relative to emigration. Many families have already left. Those that remain are going up [to Missouri] in camp. They design to start the first of May, but may not before the 10th or later. Probably and hundred and twenty-five families or more remain [in Kirtland]. They will go in large wagons covered [and] will take their clothing, beds, and cooking utensils and tent by the way . . . They will travel five days in a week; stop on Saturdays to bake and wash. Sabbath hold meeting. Will be eight or ten weeks on the road. They design to take along the poor and the lame, deeming it wrong to leave those who have a desire to go, but have no means. . The camp will move but slowly. The men will walk much of the way”(Holzapeel, 14).
“The trek was delayed for several weeks as the Saints struggled to settle their debts, sell their property, and purchase wagons, teams, and equipment. They finally left Rutland on 6 July 1838 with over five hundred Saints, 27 tents, 59 wagons, 97 horses, 22 oxen, 69 cows, and 1 bull” (Church, p. 179). The Kirtland Camp was the last group to leave Kirtland (Holzapeel, 14). The journey from Kirtland to Nauvoo was 900 miles long .
There were many difficulties along the trip. Many of the communities they passed through treated them badly. Meanwhile, “Accidents and illness constantly afflicted the pioneers. Some persons were crushed under wagon wheels; others succumbed to disease. They perspired by day and slept on cold and sometimes damp terrain by night. They forded streams, climbed up and down inclines, and followed rutted roads and trails, continually weakened by fatigue, a meager and changing diet, and polluted drinking water” (Church p. 179). John Smith, who was in the camp, wrote, “We are like the ancients wandering from place to place in the wilderness” (Holzapeel, 14).
After two months of travelling, Aaron Cheney and his family had to leave the Kirtland Camp. Aaron had become too ill to travel further. So on 11 Sept 1838 the family stopped in Decatur, Illinois. They rented a piece of land and, when Aaron was well enough, built a cabin and raised a crop.
The rest of the Camp moved on and reached the Mississippi in September. Word came that war had broken out in western Missouri between the Mormons and their enemies. Some of the Camp stopped there but most of the rest of the group pushed on and met the Prophet in Far West, Missouri.
That fall, great persecution prevailed in Missouri. Governor Boggs issued an extermination order on the 27th of October 1838. Mormon settlements were massacred. Homes were burned. And Far West was soon under siege. Joseph Smith was even imprisoned for several months. By February of 1839 a large-scale evacuation of the Saints from Missouri had begun. The Saints headed for Illinois. The people in Illinois were very sympathetic to the Saints.
At the October General Conference it was decided that Joseph Smith and Elias Higbee should journey to Washington D. C. and speak to the President of the United States (Martin Van Buren) about the persecutions of the Mormon People. They left Commerce, IL (Nauvoo) on 29 Oct 1839. Sydney Rigdon, Dr. Foster, and O. Porter Rockwell journeyed with them as far as Springfield, Illinois. Here one of them was injured and they all remained here. Joseph and Elias went on.
 Within the next day or two the Prophet traveled about 40 miles and reached Decater, IL. Here he spent the night at the home of Aaron Cheney (Nov 1839). The family gathered around to hear the Prophet speak. The Prophet and Elias left the next day and arrived at the capitol on 28 Nov 1839. Though they spoke with the President about the persecutions, the President was unsympathetic.

CHAPTER 5: NAUVOO
_____________________________
Perhaps this visit by the Prophet helped inspire Aaron Cheney and his family that it was time to continue their journey to gather with the Saints in Commerce, IL (soon to be renamed--Nauvoo). The journey from Decater was 130 miles. The soonest the family could go was the following spring.
So, they left in the spring of 1840. Aaron and his family traveled in the company of Nathan Cheney. Nathan Calhoon Cheney was the son of Ebenezer Cheney and Lydia Calhoon. He joined the Church while living in Freedom, NY. He and his young family came to Kirtland around the same time as Aaron Cheney's family. (Aaron and Nathan would have been sixth cousins. They were descended from two brothers who immigrated to America in the 17th century and settled in Roxbury, Massachusetts.)
After a period of trial and persecution Aaron Cheney and Nathan Cheney's families arrived in Nauvoo. The journey may have taken a few weeks to a month. They were probably there by summer at the latest. Once more the family enjoyed the society of fellow Saints. Elam, who was 15 that year, fondly remembered playing a game of ball with Joseph Smith.
Aaron Cheney is recorded as owning 40 acres on Lot 28 in Nauvoo. Catty Corner to Aaron and behind the "Nauvoo House" lived Aaron's brother Ephraim on Lot 32. Ephraim and Aaron had lived near each other in both Freedom and Kirtland and their families were very close
On the 6th of April 1841 the family was present when the cornerstone of the Nauvoo Temple was laid. It was a great moment for all the Saints. They were overjoyed at the prospect of building another Temple. While the temple was being built, the young men were given an opportunity to work out their tithing by working on the temple. "Each ward was assigned a particular day for working on the temple. Most able-bodied men in Nauvoo contributed work either in the quarry or on the temple, often donating a day in ten as tithing labor" (Church, 242). Aaron was one of those that worked on the Temple. His 16 year old son, Elam, worked as a carpenters helper on the Temple
The Nauvoo Charter became law in December of 1840. In February 1841, in accordance with the charter, a militia was organized and called the Nauvoo Legion. This militia was to help provide for the common defense of the people of Nauvoo and was technically part of the State militia. They were issues 3 cannons and various other armaments from the State. This helped to provide the Saints with a sense of security.
The militia included able-bodied males between eighteen and forty-five years of age. It was organized into two groups, infantry and cavalry. Each group was under a brigadier general and the entire body was under the command of Lieutenant General Joseph Smith. At its peak the Nauvoo Legion numbered three thousand men.
Aaron, who turned 54 in 1841, served in the Nauvoo Legion even though he was over age. He served under Captain Hosea Stout. (I assume that his service in the Legion was probably near the beginning, before there were sufficient younger men to serve.)
In 1843 Aaron's son, Elam, turned 18 and joined the Nauvoo Legion. Elam was in the Second Cohort (infantry), First Regiment, Artillery. He was a Private. The leaders of his unit were Colonel Titus Billings, Lt. Colonel John Scott, Major Hosea Stout, Quartermaster Sergeant (captain) William D. Pratt, and Sergeant Major (captain) Lewis Robinson. There were 30 commissioned officers and 235 non-commissioned officers and privates. Their unit had two of the Legion's three cannons, 63 swords and 6 rifles. Every other weapon had to be provided by the men themselves (Saunders). Historical evidence points to a poorly outfitted militia. They had a lot less arms than they were entitled to by law.
Parades and military demonstrations staged by the Legion drew considerable attention throughout western Illinois. One Latter-day Saint recalled, “Some of the most impressive moments of my life were, when I saw the 'Nauvoo Legion' on parade with the Prophet, then Gen. Joseph Smith, with his wife, Emma Hale Smith, on horseback at the head of the troops.”(Church, p. 244).
“The high-ranking officers probably appeared in colorful and flamboyant dress, while the enlisted men made shift with such homemade accouterments and clothing as they could piece together.” (Leonard, p. 113). The uniform usually included white breeches and a blue coat. Members of the Artillery units wore red sashes.
By the end of 1843, several companies of the Legion were kept in readiness to defend against mob attacks. The mobs had been increasing in violence toward the Saints. Unfortunately the fact that the Mormons had a militia group was taken as a threat by those who were not Mormons. Rumors and outright lies about the Mormons fanned the flames of prejudice against them. In 1844 the unrest escalated and eventually martial law was declared and the Nauvoo Legion was called out to maintain order within the city. In June of 1844, Joseph Smith and his brother, Hyrum, turned themselves over to stand trial in Carthage. Knowing that he would never be convicted on the trumped up charges, a mob broke into Carthage Jail and killed the Prophet and his brother. This occurred on 27 June 1844.
The death of their beloved Prophet caused great sorrow among the Saints. They had a short respite from persecution, as the mobs assumed that the Church would fall apart without the Prophet. However when the Church did not fall apart, persecution resumed. The charter for the Nauvoo Legion was repealed in January of 1845 and had no more legal authority
Meanwhile the Saints made plans to move out West, where they could live in peace. Supplies were gathered and wagons built.
The Saints did not want to leave Nauvoo, before the Temple was finished however. The promised blessings of the Temple were a great inspiration to the people. Among those blessings was the ability to be sealed to their families in the eternities. Marriages would last forever.
As part of the doctrine of Celestial Marriage, polygamy was introduced into the Church in 1841. At first, few men took plural wives, and all of them did so only after being commanded to do so. Aaron Cheney was one of those who took a second wife. He married a widow by the name of Sarah Griffith Pisel on 21 Jan 1846 in Nauvoo, Illinois. She was 55 at the time of this marriage.
Chapter 6: The Trek West
At first the Saints planned to leave in the Spring of 1846, but threats from the mobs forced them to leave early. The first Saints left Nauvoo in February of 1846. They crossed the frozen Mississippi River into Iowa and began a trek across Iowa. Groups continued to leave Nauvoo for several months. Aaron Cheney came among the main group of the Saints and probably camped at Mount Pisgah. His youngest son, Ezekiel, was married at Mount Pisgah on 11 May 1848.
 Mount Pisgah was originally founded as a camp in May of 1847 by Parley P. Pratt's group of Saints, who stopped to wait for Brigham Young's group to catch up with them. This site was 153 miles from Nauvoo and "served as a way station on the original Mormon Trail from 1846 until 1852. Between 1846 and 1852 more than 2,000 people made their homes here while thousands of others simply passed through on their way west" (Mount Pisgah).
The Saints made several camps in Iowa and on the West bank of the Missouri River near Council Bluffs, Iowa. The Saints remained there over the Winter of 1846-47. In 1847 the first wagon trains left and found their way to the Valley of the Great Salt Lake. Aaron's son, Elam, and his family left for the Valley in 1847 in the 3rd company of Jedediah M. Grant.
Many others needed more time to get ready to go. Aaron and his family were not able to leave until 1849. They stayed at the settlement called 'North Pigeon' in Harrison County along Pigeon Creek. (North Pigeon is not marked on the above map of Winter Quarters settlements but it was likely north of the Pigeon Tabernacle.) Aaron Cheney Sr. was appointed Bishop of this settlement on 19 July 1848 (Aaron and Mehitable). As Bishop Aaron would have been the lead citizen and organizer of this settlement.
Aaron (age 62) and his wives, Mehitable Wells (62) and Sarah Griffeth (58) made the journey across the plains in the George A. Smith/ Dan Jones Company in 1849. The company of 220 pioneers also included Aaron's youngest son, Ezekiel Wells Cheney (21) and his wife, Lucy E. Hardy Cheney (20), and their baby daughter Eliza Ann Cheney, Also in the company was (his 6th cousin) Elijah Cheney (age 63) and his wife Sarah Harmon Cheney (62) (Pioneer Overland Travel). They left Winter Quarters on 14 July 1849 and arrived in Salt Lake from 26 - 30 October 1849.
Aaron's sons, Amasa F. and Aaron Jr., settled in Iowa and did not go west with the rest of the Saints. Amasa F. stopped due to illness and eventually left the Church (probably joining with the Reorganized Church).
The Frontier Guardian printed on the 25th of July 1849 the following; "On Saturday, the 14th inst., about noon, the last wagons left Winter Quarters, and began to bend their way westward over the boundless plains that lie between us and the Valley of the Great Salt Lake. Slowly and majestically they moved along, displaying a column of upwards of three hundred wagons, cattle, sheep, hogs, horses, mules, chicken's, turkeys, geese, doves, goats, &c., &c., besides lots of men, women and children."
The first river the pioneers would cross on their long journey was the Elkhorn. The company crossed over on a ferry on the 18th of July. William Appleby who was in the company wrote, "Having got all our wagons and cattle safely over the River, the Camp moved forward about three miles, and encamped for the night where pasture was good and plenty. About twelve o'clock at night a general rush, or "stampede" was made by the Cattle, taking some fright. They passed the guards, and ran towards the River. Every man in camp was summoned to turn out, and with arms in their hands, assist in bringing them back. In about an hour or two, the Horesman [horsemen] had headed them, and we got them returned back to Camp, oxen, cows, etc. and placed an additional guard around them, We had the good fortune not to lose any of them."
Isaac Clark reported that on the 22nd of July "A Birth occurred while we were encamped at the Liberty Pole on the Platte [.] Sister Lucy [Elzada Hardy] Cheney—was delivered of a daughter [Elliza Ann]." This baby was Aaron's granddaughter, born to his son Ezekiel and his wife Lucy.
This was but one of the incidents that occurred during the companies trek to the Rocky mountains. Abigail Smith Abbot, who was in the company wrote, "We came in contact with many Indians, but were never harmed by them, for instead of fighting them every one gave them food." William Appleby reported that on August 26th the company met up with "A large company of Sioux Indians, with their mules, Ponies and horses. together with their Tents. were encamped on the South side of the Platte. We karaled [corralled] nearly opposite to them. Several of them both men and women mounted their ponies. together with their Chief, and came over to our Camps. They hoisted the white flag. and smoked the pipe of peace, and appeared friendly. The Camps traded with them. Beans. meat. bread. biscuits &c in exchange for Buffalo Robes, and Moccasins. They were the finest[,] noblest, best looking, and neatly dressed Indians I ever saw."
The pioneers received good news from Salt Lake as they traveled along. It seems that there was very little problems with crickets destroying crops that year and that the wheat and other crops in the Valley were all doing well. "The health of the citizens there was good, and great activity in business prevailed. About twelve or fifteen thousand California emigrants passed through the Valley, and about three thousand calculated to winter there." These California immigrants were part of the California Gold Rush. Since the more part of them were in a rush to get to the gold fields they sold many of their supplies in Salt Lake very cheaply so they could hurry on to the goldfields. This was quite a blessing to the Saints in Salt Lake (Frontier Guardian).
The letter also indicated that "George A's [George A. Smith's] and Ezra's [Ezra T. Benson's] company were all well, but getting along slowly on account of high water and constant rains" (Frontier Guardian). William Appleby mentioned the wet weather and muddy roads in his journal, but he also noted the good hunting they had along the way. Dan Jones reported in a letter, " Perhaps you are thinking that we are at the end of our journey by now; but the main causes which have kept us longer than some who went before us are that there were so many rainstorms on the first 300 miles of our journey that it was difficult to travel because the wheels of the covered wagons would often sink very deep in the mire, and also that after we came to the highland, the grazing was and still is very scarce for the animals."
On 16 September William Appleby reported, "Not a single death of man or beast has occurred among us, indeed we have been blessed, and reason to be thankful. we are now about six hundred miles from Winter Quarters. " On the 26th of September they camped at Independence Rock.
On the 2nd of October snow was flying and it was so cold that they crossed the Sweetwater River near South Pass across the ice. Some chickens and pigs froze to death. Women and Children slept in the wagons at night. On the 3rd a heavy snowstorm broke and 60 head of cattle were lost.
By the 7th the weather had warmed up and everyone did a lot better. There was still some rain and snow but it was not too bad. As they headed into the mountain passes towards the Valley they saw plenty of snow on the mountains.
The company found many graves as they made their way across the plains. Some were from companies ahead of them and many were from immigrants headed to the gold fields of California. Isaac Clark records that on the 7th of October they came across a grave of a mobocrat that had persecuted the saints by the name of Dodds.
Lucy M. Smith records, " Oct. 18th, we drove all day and Coral our wagons[.] the three fifties all together. We have a large pile of Sage brush in the center, then set it on fire[.] it lit the whole country around. Prest. Smith preached and related a Prophesy which he uttered in the Kirtland Temple, that the wild beasts should pick the old mobocrat Dr. Dodd's bones. Br. Elijah Cheena being in the Temple at the time that George A. Smith uttered that Prophesy, and also being at the grave with G.A. Smith, and seeing Dr. Dodds bones strewn about having been dug up and picked by the wolves, and his name plain to be seen on the board[.] br. Cheena [Cheney] arose and testified to the litteral fulfillment of that Prophesy. The old Dr. died with Cholera on the plains, while on his way to the gold regions."
The company arrived in Salt Lake from 26 to 30 October 1849.
In 1850 Aaron's daughter, Olive Mehitable Cheney McBride, made the trek across the plains with her husband James John McBride and her two sons; 2 year old Aaron and 9 month old Amos Orin.
Aaron Cheney Sr. received his Patriarchal Blessing under the hands of Patriarch John Smith. (There were two patriarchs by that name. Uncle John Smith was the Uncle of the Prophet Joseph Smith and served as Patriarch from 1 Jan 1849 until his death on 23 May 1843. Upon his death John Smith, the son of Hyrum Smith became the Patriarch and held that office from Feb 1855 until his death in 1911.) So at some time after 1 Jan 1849 Aaron received the following blessing :
May 5th A Blessing by John Smith, Patriarch, upon the head of Aaron Cheney son of Benjamin & Eunice, born July 14th 1787, Berwick, Maine _____ Aaron I lay my hands upon thy head & by the authority of the Holy Priesthood & in the name of Jesus Christ I seal a fathers blessing upon thee, thou art of the house of Jacob & a heir to the blessings which were sealed upon his sons & the priesthood which was sealed upon the sons of Joseph, for thou art of the blood of Ephraim, tis your privilege to receive the priesthood in fulness in due time & to come up to thy station thou must seek diligently for thou art appointed to be a counselor in the house of Israel & God shall give thee understanding in all difficult matters & thou shalt be able to counsel wisely & judge righteously, thou shalt be a president of a stake of Zion & thou shalt attend to all the duties & privileges of that office & thy name shall be respected among the saints because of thy wisdom; tis your privilege to preach the Gospel in any place that you desire to; tis thy lot to _____ thy fathers house clear back to Adam or to where they ____ in the Gospel & bring them up in the morning of the resurrection; thou shalt have an inheritance with thy brethren, a numerous posterity an honorable name among the saints forever; if your faith does not fail, you shall live till Zion is established in peace & not a dog to move his tongue against the saints, shall depart in peace 7 inherit eternal life; this is thy blessing sealed upon thee & thy posterity in common with thy companion, upon conditions of obedience, amen

The 1850 US Census finds Aaron and his two wives living in Weber county, Utah. This could possibly be in Ogden, Utah. Ogden is located at the confluence of the Ogden and the Weber rivers in northern Utah. It is about 40 miles north of Salt Lake City. It was first settled in 1847-48 very soon after the first pioneers arrived in the valley. It was no more than a small agricultural town at this time. The first stake in Ogden the Weber Stake was organized in 1851 with President Lorin Farr.(If Aaron was ever made a stake president it was not in this stake.)
In 1858 the United States Army under Col. Johnston was discovered to be marching towards the Salt Lake Valley. In response all northern Utah communities were evacuated in the spring of 1859. All inhabitants moved south to at least Provo.
The Saints feared being run out of their homes like they had so many times before. This time they decided to burn their homes rather than let the mobs steal them. Only a few remained in the northern settlements. These few were given instruction to burn their cities should the Army invade. The army finally negotiated a peaceful entrance into the valley, but the Saints did not return to their homes until the month after the Army proved its intention to abide by the treaty.
 The Saints did not move back into their homes and communities until June of 1859. Ogden was evacuated under the direction of its Mayor, and stake president, Lorrin Farr.
Aaron died in Ogden, Weber, Utah, on 18 September 1862 at the age of 75.
Mehitable died on 28 Nov 1869 in Grantsville, Tooele, Utah at the age of 82.
Sarah Griffith died on 24 Sept 1872 at the age of 82.
