# Emma Hannah Sinfield

_A story of [[Emma Hannah Sinfield (KWJC-G7W)|Emma Hannah Sinfield]]._

## The story as it was written

PIONEER
EMMA HANNAH SINFIELD WAS BORN IN BEDFORD, BEDFORDSHIRE, ENGLAND ON JAN 6, 1844. SHE WAS THE DAUGHTER OF SAMUEL SINFIELD WHO WAS BORN MARCH 8,1820, IN EVESHOLT BEDFORDSHIRE ENGLAND AND PHOEBE CROSS, BORN MAY 13, 1816 IN CONDICOTE, GLOUSTERSHIRE, ENGLAND.
BEDFORD HAD A CATHOLIC ABBEY WITH MANY LARGE BUILDINGS AND MANY ACRES OF PLANTINGS BEHIND A TALL BRICK WALL ENCLOSURE. THREE OR FOUR SMALL VILLAGES WERE LOCATED AT THE CORNERS OF THE ENCLOSURE, ONE OF WHICH WAS BEDFORD. [HALF THE ACREAGE IS NOW A DRIVE THROUGH WILD ANIMAL PARK BEHIND THOSE TALL BRICK WALLS. THE BUILDINGS ARE STILL BEING USED ALSO1992/R.G.BEZZANT].
HER FATHER AND MOTHER HAD MADE ALL PREPERATIONS TO LEAVE ENGLAND FOR AMERICA WHEN HER MOTHER DIED. THIS DID NOT CHANGE HER FATHERS’ PLANS. HE AND HIS TWO CHILDREN, ELIZA 8 AND EMMA 6, LEFT SOON AFTER FOR AMERICA. [ABOUT 1850]
THE ONLY SHIPS IN THOSE DAYS WERE SAILING VESSELS SO IT TOOK THEM SIX WEEKS TO CROSS THE OCEAN TO NEW ORLEANS. THEY THEN CAME UP THE RIVER BY RIVERBOAT TO COUNCIL BLUFFS.
HER FATHER GOT A CHANCE TO DRIVE A CHURCH TEAM ACROSS THE PLAINS. IN THAT WAY, HE AND HIS TWO CHILDREN REACHED UTAH. THE CHILDREN WALKED MOST OF THE WAY.AS PIONEERS WHEN THEY BECAME TIRED, THEIR FATHER WOULD SET THEM ON THE WAGON TO RIDE BUT THE CAPTAIN WOULD ORDER THEM PUT DOWN. THERE FEET BECAME SO SORE THAT THEY WERE FORCED TO CRAWL SOME TIMES.
THEY ENCOUNTERED GREAT HERDS OF BUFFALOES THAT MADE MUCH DUST AND DELAYED THE TRAVELERS UNTIL THE HERD PASSED ON.
AFTER ARRIVING IN UTAH, THEY LIVED SOME TIME IN THE OGDEN AREA, AT A PLACE CALLED OGDEN HOLE. FOR A FIRST HOME, THEIR FATHER DID AS MANY OTHERS HAD DONE, DIG A HOLE IN THE SIDE OF THE HILL, BOARDING UP THE FRONT WITH SLABS OF PINE AND COVERING THE ROOF WITH BRUSH AND CLAY TO STOP THE RAIN OR SNOW WATER. [THIS SERVED AS SHELTER UNTIL SAWMILLS COULD SAW TIMBER FOR BUILDING HOUSES OR ADOBE BRICKS COULD BE MADE AND LAID, OR STONE CUT AND LIME BURNED FOR MORTAR AND WALLS OF HOUSES ETC. LAID UP.]
ONE DAY, WHILE THEIR FATHER WAS AWAY, AN OLD INDIAN CAME TO THEIR HOME. EMMA RAN INSIDE AND PULLED THE LATCH STRING INSIDE. THE INDIAN WAS ANGRY AND BEAT UPON THE DOOR WITH HIS BUCKSKIN ROPE. AFTER AWHILE THE INDIAN LEFT. EMMA SAID SHE NEVER PRAYED SO HARD IN ALL HER LIFE.
AT THAT TIME HER SISTER, ELIZA, WAS SICK FOR A VERY LONG TIME WITH A FEVER THAT LEFT HER QUITE LAME.
WHILE LIVING IN OGDEN , THEY WENT WITHOUT BREAD FOR SIX WEEKS. AT THE HARVEST TIME, EMMA HELPED HER FATHER GLEAN WHEAT AND AT NIGHT HE WOULD BEAT THE HEADS OF WHEAT FREE AND SEPARATE THE GRAIN FROM THE CHAFF. THEY WOULD GRIND THE GRAIN IN THE COFFE MILL AND USE THE FLOUR TO MAKE PANCAKES. EMMA SAID IT WAS THE SWEETEST BREAD SHE HAD EVER TASTED.
EMMA WAS BAPTIZED 26 NOVEMBER, 1854. IN SALT LAKE CITY.
HER FATHER MARRIED A WIDOW, MISSES HANNAH MIDDLETON HAWKEY, WITH TWO CHILDREN. FROM THIS UNION THREE MORE CHILDREN WERE BORN.
AFTER THIS MARRIAGE, FAMILY LIFE FOR EMMA WAS NOT PLEASANT. EMMA LEFT THE HOME AT AGE 12 [1856] TO EARN HER OWN LIVING. SHE CONTINUED TO WORK OUT IN FAMILIES AS HELPER. AT THE TIME BRIGHAM YOUNG MARRIED AMELIA FOLSOM, SHE WAS THEIR HIRED GIRL IN THE FOLSOM HOME IN SALT LAKE CITY.
SHE TOLD OF THE MOVE SOUTH TO UTAH COUNTY WHEN JOHNSONS’ ARMY CAME TO SALT LAKE CITY TO DISCIPLINE THE SAINTS. THE PEOPLE LAID FIRE MATERIALS IN THEIR HOMES, ALREADY TO IGNITE IF THEIR HOMES WERE THREATENED.
AS A YOUNG WOMAN, EMMA WAS CONSIDERED VERY GOOD LOOKING AND SHE RETAINED MUCH OF LHER BEAUTY THROUGHOUT HER LIFE.
EMMA MARRIED, DANIEL MILEY SMITH ON 10 MAY ,1863. SHE WAS 19 YEARS OLD AND DANIEL WAS 25 YEARS OLD. THEY SETTLED IN PLEASANT GROVE UTAH IN 1864.
THEY WERE ENDOWED IN THE ENDOWMENT HOUSE IN SALT LAKE CITY ON 19 MARCH, 1864.
WHEN EMMA AND DANIEL CAME TO PLEASANT GROVE THEY BEGAN HOUSEKEEPING WITH VERY FEW ARTICLES OF FURNITURE AND KITCHEN WARE. SHE FIRST GATHERED THE HEADS OF CATTAIL RUSHES IN THE FALL FROM SWAMPY PLACES AND STRIPPED THE HEADS TO MAKE STUFFING FOR A MATTRESS. FOR MANY YEARS THE ONLY KIND OF MATTRESS THEY OWNED WAS A STRAW TICK. THE STRAW WAS REPLACED EACH FALL AT HARVEST TIME. [SOMETIMES THE SUPPORT FOR A STRAW TICK WAS A SERIES OF ROPES PASSING FROM SIDE TO SIDE OF A WOODEN BED FRAME. OVER TIME THE ROPES WOULD LOOSEN, SAG AND HAVE TO BE RE TIGHTENED…HENCE THE WISH FOR PEOPLE TO SLEEP TIGHT AND DON’T LET THE BED BUGS BITE]. EMMA BEING A GOOD HOUSEKEEPER FROM HER TRAINING AND A SEAMSTRESS MADE THEIR LITTLE HOME COZY AND COMFORTABLE.
THERE WAS LOTS OF WORK IN THE HOME. MOST OF THE FOOD HAD TO BE PREPARED IN THE HOME. FRUITS, VEGETABLES AND MEATS HAD TO BE PREPARED AND STORED FOR USE IN THE WINTER AS WELL AS THE SUMMER. EMMA WASHED, CARDED, SPUN AND WOVE OR KNITTED THE WOOL FOR CLOTHING AND STOCKINGS. SOAP HAD TO BE MADE OF WATER PASSED THROUGH WOOD ASHES AND OF BOILED FATS IN ORDER TO DO THE LAUNDRY. POTATOES HAD TO BE GRATED AND THE STARCH IN THEM EXTRACTED TO MAKE STARCH FOR LAUNDRY IRONING.
THE UNITED ORDER WAS INSTITUTED IN PLEASANT GROVE AND SHE AND DANIEL JOINED.
OVER TIME THEY HAD 12 CHILDREN, NOT ALL OF WHOM LIVED. THEY WERE; ELIZA KATE, DANIEL MILEY JR, FLORENCE ELLA, MELVIN, EARL CONRAD, ALVIRA, OLIVIA, HARRIET DEAN, BURDETTE, HETTY MAUD, PEARL ELIZABETH AND LAWRENCE BARRETT.
THEY EVENTUALLY BUILT A VERY LITTLE HOME AT ABOUT 3RD SOUTH AND 4TH EAST IN PLEASANT GROVE. THE WALLS MUST HAVE BULGED WITH THE LARGE FAMILY THEY HAD.
THEY HAD A SUBSTANTIAL ACREAGE AND RAISED THEIR OWN FOOD PLUS OPERATED A NURSERY, A CARRIAGE SHOP, MADE APPLE CIDER AND VINEGAR, AND SERVED AS AGENT FOR THE MCCORMICK LINE OF FARM MACHINERY.
SOME OF THE GRANDCHILDREN WERE BAPTIZED IN THE IRRIGATION DITCH THAT RAN BELOW THE SMITH HOME.[LURETHA SMITH FOR ONE].
HER THIRD SON, EARL CONRAD SMITH AND HIS WIFE ELIZABETH BAXTER SMITH PLUS AT LEAST TWO GIRLS [LURETHA AND CUMORAH SMITH] LIVED IN PART OF THE HOUSE UNTIL THEIR OWN HOUSE COULD BE BUILT ON A CORNER OF DANIELS PROPERTY AT 3RD SOUTH AND 3RD EAST. LURETHA TELLS OF SPENDING THE DAY WITH THE ROWDY BAXTER COUSINS AND ARRIVING HOME TO HAVE GRANDMA EMMA PROMPTLY GETTING THEM DOWN ON THEIR KNEES IN PRAYER TO KEEP ON THE RIGHT TRACK IN LIFE.
EMMA WAS ALWAYS FAITHFUL AND TRUE TO THE RELIGION SHE ACCEPTED AS A CHILD. SHE WAS A WONDERFUL HELPMATE TO HER HUSBAND, SHE STOOD BY HIM THROUGH SORROW, TROUBLE AND ADVERSITY. SHE WAS THE MOTHER OF ELEVEN CHILDREN; 6 GIRLS AND 5 BOYS. HER GREATEST DESIRE WAS TO SEE HER CHILDREN BECOME USEFUL GOD FEARING MEN AND WOMEN.
EMMA WAS ALWAYS READY AND WILLING TO ASSIST THE SICK AND AFFLICTED. MANY HOMES WERE BLESSED BY HER AID IN TIMES OF SICKESS AND DEATH.
EMMA WAS AN ACTIVE WORKER IN THE RELIEF SOCIETY. ALWAYS TRYING TO VISIT HER TEACHER DISTRICT EACH MONTH AND RECEIVED DONATIONS WHICH WERE LATER USED TO HELP SOME LESS FORTUNATE PERSON. EMMA WAS A WIDOW FOR 15 YEARS. ALL OF HER CHILDREN HAD THE OPPORTUNITY TO ATTEND SCHOOLS OF HIGHER LEARNING AND TWO OF HER SONS FILLED MISSIONS TO FOREIGN LANDS.
SHE DIED ON JAN 1, 1914 [1913?], AT PLEASANT GROVE, UTAH, 5 DAYS SHY OF HER 69TH BIRTHDAY AND IS BURIED IN THE PLEASANT GROVE CEMETERY.
