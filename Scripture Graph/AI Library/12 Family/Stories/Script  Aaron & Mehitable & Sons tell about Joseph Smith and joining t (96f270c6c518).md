# Script: Aaron & Mehitable & Sons tell about Joseph Smith and joining the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints

_A story of [[Mehitable Wells (KN6W-2R1)|Mehitable Wells]], [[Aaron Cheney (KNX4-J2Z)|Aaron Cheney]]._

## The story as it was written

AARON: I am Aaron Cheney. My wife Mehitable and I lived in a little town called Freedom, NY. Remember the name of that town! It is a fitting name, after all the sacrifices that were made in the Revolution to earn our freedom from kings and monarchs. My own father, Benjamin Cheney, and his brother Joseph, fought in the Revolutionary War as teenagers, as did my grandpa Joseph, as an older man.
Mehitable: My father Selah Wells and his brothers Joshua and David were also soldiers in the Revolution, and their families had to flee for safety from the British invasion of New York. My husband and I were born just a few years after the war was over, but for the adults who raised us, the war was fresh in their minds. There was even a neighbor of ours in Freedom, a Dutchman who had been a soldier, who every 4th of July would wake up all the neighbors by firing his old “Howling-piece” gun into the sky, with both arms high above the head, and shouting,
DUTCHMAN: “Hurrah for Washington, Gates, Putnam und Lee, und all de brave men who fought for Liberty!"[1]
Mehitable: Freedom was very, very precious to us. So, we liked the name of our town!
AARON: Little did we know, that we would gain so much more Freedom, through our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ and his restored church, which was introduced to us there.
While we lived there, I ran an iron foundry with my father and brothers. We had a large crew of men and turned out vast amounts of cast iron. Have you ever eaten anything from a Dutch oven? Dutch ovens are made of cast iron, and so were many other tools that pioneers needed in those days, so it was a useful profession and a reliable living. You can remember it this way: Aaron made iron.
On Sundays we would go to the Methodist Church in the neighboring town of Sandusky. I was even one of the church trustees, which meant that I was partly responsible for taking care of the church property and money. Mehitable and I had a big family, even though some of our babies had died, which was very sad. It helped us to go to church and to be with our good neighbors, but there were many questions about life and death and heaven that our church could not answer for us.
One of our neighbors[2] was named Warren Cowdery. He was the postmaster and doctor, and just about my same age. You might have heard about his brother, Oliver, who was one of the three witnesses of the Book of Mormon. Warren had heard about a prophet named Joseph Smith and the church from his brother. One time, Oliver even brought to town some giant proof sheet pages from when they were printing the first copies of the Book of Mormon. Some of the townsfolk read the pages. Warren was baptized along with some other families in town, but not us, not yet.
In 1834, just four years after the church was organized, our town had a very important visitor. It was Joseph Smith, the Prophet, along with his friend Parley P. Pratt. They were coming to visit the members in Freedom to ask for help. They needed recruits to march to Missouri in what was called Zion’s camp. While they were visiting, they also held meetings to preach the gospel to anyone who wanted to come learn what it was all about. Many people came to hear them speak, and more people joined the church. Although the Prophet only could stay for three days, he put other missionaries in charge of teaching and baptizing all those people who wanted to join Jesus’ Restored church. The missionaries gave talks in the Fish Lake schoolhouse and in people’s homes, since there wasn’t a church building yet. In just three months’ time, around 40 people were baptized, and a branch was formed. I was baptized on April 5, just a few weeks after the Prophet came, by Orson Pratt. I was 47 years old. Then that night was held a special meeting where they blessed the little children. Elder Pratt wrote the names of our family members in his journal.
Mehitable: Our grown children Amasa and Olive were baptized first, then their father three days later, then our son Orrin the next day, then I was baptized three days after that, on April 9. It was a busy week! Elder Pratt was called to be an apostle just a year later[3]. By January, there were enough members around our area that Freedom hosted a regional conference, with 11 other branches attending. The Freedom Branch reported 74 members! Section 106 in the Doctrine & Covenants talks more about this, and how Dr. Warren Cowdery was called to be our Presiding High Priest, kind of like a Stake President.
A historian in later years tried to angrily explain what had happened in our little town. (Sighing) He said that ___
ANGRY HISTORIAN:“our quiet little neighborhood had been invaded by Joe Smith, Sidney Rigdon, John Gould and Parley Pratt and meetings were held daily and nightly, in barns and dwelling-houses, and prodigious excitement pervaded the minds of many people”. Those who joined that church are “believers in humbug”. [4]
Mehitable: You can probably guess that it wasn’t very popular or even very acceptable to join this new church. People thought we were gullible and foolish, but they were wrong. Even though our lives haven’t been easy, our greatest joys and blessings came to us because of Joseph Smith and the Restored Church.
AARON: I know we made the right choice to join the church, but we decided to leave Freedom, New York. That next year we moved to Kirtland, Ohio, to gather with the Saints, and eventually to Nauvoo and farther. Our youngest sons, Elam and Ezekiel, will tell you what we were able to do there. Living in Nauvoo was splendid. Even though I was older than most, I joined the Nauvoo Legion for a short time. [5] My father and grandfather may have been privileged to serve under George Washington, but I marched with the Prophet Joseph Smith.
Mehitable: Aaron and I received our temple blessings in the Nauvoo temple and were sealed to our family, including our babies that had died, forever. Those promises are worth every lost friend, every lost job, every move or trek or hardship, to me.
ELAM: I’m Elam Cheney. When we moved to Kirtland, I was ten and my brother Ezekiel was seven. The two of us hadn’t been baptized yet. Our father bought a farm, built a house, and a new iron foundry. It was a busy place. The Saints in Kirtland were building the first temple in the Latter-Days, and the Cheney’s got to help, even though I was just a boy. Joseph Smith was there, too. One time he held a special meeting at the temple, which had a big room kind of like a chapel. All our family went to the meeting. While Joseph was speaking, I saw him look up as if he was looking at someone. I looked around and saw six personages, three on each side of the room. I noticed them because they were dressed so differently than what I was used to seeing. They had white moccasins on their feet and white caps on their heads. I turned to Mother and whispered to her to ask her who they were. To my surprise, she could not see them. I told her they were dressed in white.
Who do you think they were?
Later on, when I received my endowments as an adult in the next temple the Cheneys helped to build, the Nauvoo temple, I saw that the special temple clothing was the same as those angels were wearing that day when the prophet Joseph spoke, and this experience was a testimony of the true church that stayed with me through my whole life.
In 1906, when I was an old man, because I had known the prophet, my eyewitness testimony of Joseph Smith was printed in the Young Women’s magazine. I will tell you the same thing I told them.
“I saw him when we laid the cornerstone of the Nauvoo Temple. We lived there then and saw him every Sunday, more or less. I belonged to the Nauvoo Legion, and Joseph was the General. Brother Joseph was a man weighing about two hundred pounds, fair complexion, light brown hair. He was about six feet tall, sound bodied, very strong and quick—no breakage about his body. He most always wore a silk stock, and was smooth faced. He was very sympathetic and would talk to children and they liked him. He was honest, and was liked by everybody who knew him.”
EZEKIEL: Elam and I would know! We were some of the children he talked to. In fact, one of the most memorable days of my life was when we were living in Decatur, Illinois. Joseph Smith was on his way to Washington D.C., and he spent the night at our house! It gets even better than that—instead of staying in the house with the grown-ups, he played baseball with us! He watched us grow up. We were heartbroken along with the saints when he was killed.
ELAM: I heard him tell the people,
JOSEPH SMITH: “I am going like a lamb to the slaughter”
ELAM:…before he went to Carthage. It was a sad and confusing time, but the Lord had some answers for us soon.
EZEKIEL: My wife Lucy Hardy Cheney can tell you about that. She and her family knew Joseph and his wife Emma well.
