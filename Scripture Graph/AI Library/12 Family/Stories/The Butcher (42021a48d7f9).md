# The Butcher

_A story of [[Thomas Henry Stephens (KWNL-JWK)|Thomas Henry Stephens]]._

## The story as it was written

Excerpt from the book: Henefer Our Valley Home Compiled by Fannie J Richins and Maxine R Wright FHL Call No. 979.214 H2h
Stephens, Thomas Henry (son of Henry Stephens and Susan Ireland)
Born 20 Jan. 1839, Buringe, Buragfield, England.
Came to Utah 1 June 1864. Died 5 Aug. 1908, Salt Lake City, Utah.
Trade: Slaughterman, butcher. Buried Henefer, Utah.
Married Mary Jones (daughter of William Jones and Mary Shill) 25 Dec. 1865 by Charles
Richins at Echo, Utah.
She was born 16 June 1842, Brimpsfield, Gloucestershire, England. She sailed from Liverpool, England, 29 Apr. 1865 on ship Bellewood. Came to Utah 1865.
Died 12 July 1905.
Thomas H. Stephens was a butcher by trade in England. This training and experience proved valuable to him after he came to America. While crossing the plains he was selected to keep the wagon train supplied with fresh meat, such as deer, antelope and buffalo. After coming to Henefer Thomas owned and operated a slaughter house there. From this plant he furnished fresh dressed meat to the Union Pacific Commissary, while the railroad was under construction. Later he operated a retail meat market in Echo, Utah, and Evanston, Wyoming, furnishing meat for these shops from his plant in Henefer. He also supplied dressed meat to Beckworth and Lowder General Mercantile Store in Grass Creed where the U.P.R.R. mined coal; Fred, son of Thomas, drove a meat wagon from Henefer to Grass Creek three times each week to supply the demands of this western boom town, with fresh meat. Dressed meat in those days was delivered to the shop for 3 1/2 cents per lb.
Thomas and his sons, Fred and George, were more than generous with their meat, in the little settlement. In many cases, the poor were furnished with good fresh meat from the Stephens slaughter house.
William's (BOND) education was not wasted in Henefer because he became one of the first schoolteachers and postmaster and bookkeeper for Thomas H. Stephen's Butcher Shop. He checked all other accounts for business transactions.
Notes from the minutes of Council Meeting held at Henneferville between the years of 1867 and 1871. These minutes are all written in a beautiful English penmanship, not one of them is signed by a secretary.
Dec. 26, 1886: After some discussion in regard to the sale of the meetinghouse organ, it was moved and carried that the organ be sold to William Brewer for the sum of $40.00, and that the same terms be allowed him that he gained by purchasing a new one. It
was moved and carried that James Lythgoe, Thomas H. Stephens, James Fowler, John Paskett and Robert Jones be appointed as committeemen to purchase a new one.

Church Buildings:
In 1908 under the direction of Bishop Micah F. Harris and his counselors, William Richins and Alma E. Richins the old brick church was torn down anda new frame church building was commenced. The architect for the building was Mr. J. E. Allen from Provo, Utah.
Mr. F. LeRoy West of Morgan, Utah, was hired as building supervisor and carpenter.
The two carpenters under his supervision were William Ovard and Leslie G. Shill. Other labor was contributed by ward members as it was needed. The foundation for this building was made of cement manufactured at Devils Slide, Utah, and gravel hauled from a pit owned by Thomas H . Stephens.
Henefer Cemetery: The ground upon which the cemetery stands was contributed to the Henefer Ward by three men, all early pioneers, Charles Richins, John C. Paskett
and Thomas H . Stephens.
Mercantile Store: About 1907 or 1908 Mr. Cromar started construction on a new
store. It was a two-story frame building 36 by 60 feet in size. The ground on which it stands was owned by Thomas H . Stephens.
Henefer Amusement and Dramatic Co.: The Henefer Amusement and Dramatic Co. was incorporated
March 26, 1906 with the following elected directors; Alma E. Richins,
president; William F. Cromar, vice-president; John P. Stevens,
secretary; Thomas H. Stephens, treasurer; and Warwick Foster, business manager. Capitol stock to the amount of $3,000.00 was offered for sale at $1.00 per share. The purpose of the corporation was to procure sufficient funds to erect an amusement hall. The dire need for a suitable recreation center was recognized so the response
to the sale of the stock was stimulating. Every family purchased as many shares as their financial condition would allow.
