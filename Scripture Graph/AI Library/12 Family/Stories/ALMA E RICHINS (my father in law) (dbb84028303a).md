# ALMA E RICHINS (my father in law)

_A story of [[Alma Ether Richins (LLC5-YZB)|Alma Ether Richins]]._

## The story as it was written

It is April 1904 and I am attempting to the write the life of my Father in law, Alma E. Richins. I regret that I failed to ask the questions that I would now like to know the answers. I had plenty of opportunity that I could have asked him about his early life., but I failed to do so, and now I cannot prove anything about his early life. The stories I hear vary in many different ways, one saying he lived here and another there, but no doubt that he lived at many places, because he was such a small child when his mother died and later his father remarried, so I will not try to write about his childhood and I do not have a record of his life's work. I lack the ability to write all the good things he did in his lifetime, but I will write all the things I know about him. I can truthfully say that I consider him to be a very honest man and I loved and respected him as much as I did my own rather.
Alma Ether Richins, the second son of Edward and Caroline Ellen Tipper Richins was born 3 Dec. 1866, near Echo Utah. His mother died when he was about one month and fourteen days old. The first son Albert Edward was born in 1863 and only lived to be about one year old.
No doubt that Alma was moved from home to home and was loved by many people as he was taken care of as a child. His Father married Sarah Beard and he could have lived with them as they had twelve children, and I know that these brothers and sisters had a deep love for one another.
When he grew to manhood he fell in love with Emeline Hattie Richins and they were married on 16 May 1889 in the Logan Temple.
Their first home was a two room house (said to be the school house) on the property where the family home now stands. The first of their children was born in this house. I know Hillman and Jewell were because Violet Brewer Taylor told me she worked for Hattie when Jewell was born. To this couple 12 children were born. Ten sons and two daughters. Their names according to their birth Alma Marcus (died as a child) Archie Edward (died when about 6) Horace Hillman, Charles Jewell, Carl Ether, Clifford Seymores, Norman Tipper, Reed, Afton Edgar, Esther Ellen and Pearl Hattie. They also raised two nephews Glen and Dale, as their own children, giving them every advantage as they did their own.
Alma became interested in sheep and cattle and spent his lifetime at this type of work. He was active in the advancement of irrigation in Henefer. He was instrumental in erecting the Henefer Amusement Hall, and was appointed lst President of the building. A position he held throughout his life. We was President of the Henefer Pipe Line from the time of its completion until his death. Re was also a director of the Lake Side Grazing Association for several years.
Alma was very active in the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints and had a strong testimony of the Gospel. Re worked many years in the Y.M.M.IA. In May 25, 1901 he was ordained as a Counsler to Bishop Micah Harris and William Richins, and served for many years. Later he served as a Stake High Counsel man. He sent 4 sons on a mission and at the time of his death had one grandson in the Northern California Mission.
Alma was a very busy man. He had worked and accumulated land and sheep and cattle. He was a clear thinker and used good judgment on buying land. His work took him away from home a large part of the time but his good wife took care of the family. The boys grew and each helped their father with the farm work and all learned the trade of caring for sheep.
When I was a young girl I spent many hours at their home. I was closely associated with Esther and I can remember that he was always good natured and would let Esther drive the "old sports model Maxwell” around the meadow, and we all thought that was great. He always worked late into the night and his business kept him on the desert or reserve or farm work, but he was always whistling and happy with his work. His beloved wife. Hattie had been ill for some time and she died of 15 July 1936. This was a shock to all the family and community, but they were happy she did not have to suffer longer.
 After her death his daughter Pearl kept house for him until her marriage in 1950. She always kept the house nest and clean and cooked good meals and the family gathered here as they usually did and they tried to keep the good family spirit that had always been there. It was after Pearl's marriage that I really did get close to Grandpa. We would invite him to eat with us. Jewell would always be with him, so we tried to help him in every way. He was very independent and hated to bother anyone very much. He treated each daughter in law the same and never favored any one. We were always welcome there and sometimes we would listen to him play his violin or accordian, and he passed many long hours away with his music.
When he would eat with us he would always take time to thank me for tell me how good
something tasted. I remember he loved custard pie, sheep camp tapioca a certain jello salad or hot rolls. He would always say, “golly, that is good."
He was always very neat and fussy about his appearance and would always whistle while he was brushing his hair before he ate. Always had a special barber for his hair.
He worked hard all his life and as his health began to fail he finally leased his sheep to Hillman, Jewell and Norman. This is when they began the name of Richins Bros. Carl and Clifford were also in the sheep business and I think they had some of the sheep
He was very active up to the time of his death. He had planned to go to the desert
with his sons, but it rained so he thought he had better stay home. They had some sheep here that needed feeding so he helped Danny and David (grandsons) feed the sheep and he sat on the front porch waiting for his daughter Esther to come for him to take him to Hoytsville. I was painting my kitchen and had all the wall paper taken off the room when he came to me and said, “ I’ll seeing YOU,, Erma, goodnight.”
It was about seven thirty that night April 30, 1952,, that he died suddenly at the
home of his daughter, Esther.
It was a shock to his family and friends, but he had lived a good clean life and
and truly was a chosen person. He was buried in the Henefer Cemetery and lived to be 85 years old.
The last ten dollars he had in his pocket (he had tried to give it to me that morning) was given to me and I bought another revere ware pan, so that I could use each day and think of him as I used it.
