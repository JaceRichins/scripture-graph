# History of Aaron Cheney (1787-1862) & Mehitable Wells (1787-1869)

_A story of [[Mehitable Wells (KN6W-2R1)|Mehitable Wells]], [[Aaron Cheney (KNX4-J2Z)|Aaron Cheney]]._

## The story as it was written

Aaron Cheney (1787-1862)
& Mehitable Wells (1787-1869)
Aaron Cheney was born July 14, 1787 in Berwick, Maine. His parents were Benjamin Cheney and Eunice Hubbard. When Aaron was a small boy, his father moved to Granby, Vermont. When he was twenty-six or twenty-seven he met and married Mehitable Wells. Mehitable was born January 31, 1787. She was among the refugees who came from Long Island, New York to Guilford, Maine with her parents during the Revolutionary War.
She was frail but an industrious woman. Aaron was a prosperous farmer and later the owner of a foundry where a number of men were employed and where large quantities of cast iron were produced. About the time of his marriage he moved to the town of Marshfield, Vermont, and again in 1817 to Freedom, New York where his father’s family was located.
Aaron and Mehitable were religiously inclined people and were faithful members of the Methodist Church in the town where they lived. Aaron was chosen trustee in 1827 of the First Methodist Episcopal Church of Sandusky. By this time at least six of their children were born. They lived on a 113 acre farmland with a noticeable hill just east of Fish Lake (now referred to as Crystal Lake).1
One of the Cheney’s nearby neighbors was Mr. Warren A. Cowdery, one who had moved there around 1828 and talked a great deal about a new church and a new book of scripture that his brother had a central role in establishing. And on March 9, 1834 some friends of Warren A. Cowdery named Joseph Smith and Parley P. Pratt came to visit. Only five months prior, and 30 miles west in Perrysburg, NY, Joseph Smith had received a revelation from the Lord:
“Verily, thus saith the Lord unto you,… your families are well; they are in mine hands, and I will do with them as seemeth me good; for in me there is all power.
Therefore, follow me, and listen to the counsel which I shall give unto you.

As was promised, Joseph Smith was made mighty in testimony. According to his history, Joseph “preached to a great congregation; and at evening again preached to an overflowing house.”2 Given the remote nature and low population level of Freedom, NY, it seems very likely that Aaron Cheney and his family were among those who were present to hear the words of Joseph Smith.
Aher Joseph and Parley continued on to other nearby towns, other missionaries from The Church such as John Gould and Leonard Rich continued to preach to the people of Freedom. And on March 30th, Orson Pratt and John Murdock began preaching in the area. After spreading the news of the restored gospel in the nearby town of Centerville, they began on April 2nd teaching in the Fish Lake schoolhouse right next to where Aaron Cheney and Mehitable Wells’ family lived.
They opened the fourth chapter of Ephesians and taught about Jesus Christ calling prophets and apostles for the perfecting of the saints and the edifying of the body of Christ. The 48th chapter of the Book of Commandments (now D&C 45) was opened and they taught about the second coming of Jesus Christ, the signs of such times, and the urgent need to stand in holy places and gather to Zion.3
As promised to Joseph Smith, an effectual door was opened with the Cheney family. The very same day that Orson Pratt and John Murdock began teaching in the Fish Lake area, two of Aaron and Mehitable’s children chose to be baptized and confirmed: Amasa and Olive Cheney. In the coming days the preaching continued from house to house and on April 5th Aaron Cheney was baptized. Four days later Mehitable was baptized, and many other of their children were also baptized in the weeks and months that followed.4

Within just a few weeks of missionaries sharing the gospel in Freedom, NY, at least forty members had been baptized as members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in the Freedom Branch, “from which nucleus the light spread and souls were gathered into the fold in all the regions round”.
So successful were the missionary efforts that the local history 5 reported that “the Mormon excitement… split and disrupted the church to such an extent that it has since lived a precarious existence.
At the time mentioned, the quiet precincts of Fish Lake neighborhood were invaded by Joe Smith, Sidney Rigdon, John Gould, and Parley Pratt. Meetings were held, daily and nightly, in barns and dwelling-houses, and a prodigious excitement pervaded the minds of many people in that immediate vicinity.”6
In 1835 the Cheney family moved to Kirtland, Ohio to be with the saints. It was here that the remaining children in the family were baptized. By this time, both of Aaron’s parents had passed away and it remains unknown whether or not they joined the Church before that. The family purchased a farm which was located about two miles southeast of where the Kirtland Temple was built. Aaron also built a foundry with a blacksmith for handling iron. He also participated in the construction of the Temple.
At this time, notice was given that there was to be a meeting held in the temple. The whole Cheney family attended this meeting. The Prophet spoke in the meeting and Aaron’s young son, Elam, noticed that while he was speaking he looked up as if he was looking at someone. So Elam looked in that direction and saw six personages…three on each side of the room.
Their appearance attracted his attention as they were dressed so differently to what he was accustomed to seeing. They had white moccasins on their feet and white caps on their heads. He asked his mother, Mehitable, who they were and she said she could see no one. He told her they were dressed in white. He always remembered how they looked and how they were dressed.
The Cheney family eventually left Kirtland in July, 1838 in the company known as Kirtland Camp. Intending to join the saints in Missouri, but owing to sickness, they were not able to continue the journey. So they stopped temporarily at Decatur, Illinois. While detained to this place, they were to work and fence a piece of ground, build a small house, and raise a crop.
It was while they were living at Decatur, that Joseph, the Prophet, started on his memorable trip to Washington for the purpose of laying the grievances of the Saints before the President of the United States. As Joseph was passing through Decatur, he stopped and spent the night at the Cheney home. Being encouraged by the Prophet, Aaron Cheney and his family then continued their journey to Nauvoo as soon afterward as they could.

Aaron and Mehitable sealed in Nauvoo on 40 acres located on block 28. Aaron, and several of his children and siblings, helped in the building of the Nauvoo Temple. The family was present when the cornerstone of the Temple was laid on April 6, 1841. As mob violence increased and the saints were driven from their homes in Nauvoo, Aaron shared all the hardships endured by
the saints. He was at the meeting in Nauvoo when Governor Ford was present, during which the tragedy was being enacted at Carthage.
He was among those who left Nauvoo to accompany the bodies of the Prophet, and his brother Hyrum, back to the city. He was present at the funeral of the martyred brothers. He also attended the meeting when President Young took the stand and made manifest to the people that the twelve apostles were the ordained leaders of The Church.
With experienced blacksmiths and carpenters in the family, the Cheney family made their own wagons for the journey westward. Aaron and Mehitable, and most of their family members, came in the George A. Smith/Dan Jones Company in 1849.
Sadly, a number of Aaron and Mehitable’s children did not continue out west with them. About four, including Amasa who was their first son to get baptized in Freedom, stayed behind in Iowa and later separated from The Church. But Selar, Olive, Elam, and Ezekiel all had the faith to continue west with their parents. After arriving in Salt Lake City, they later moved to Ogden where Aaron died in 1862. The family soon afterward moved to Tooele County, Utah.
Although little is known about their family, it is surely true that Aaron Cheney and Mehitable Wells demonstrated great faith and endurance from the day they were introduced to the Restored Gospel by the Prophet Joseph Smith himself, to the day they died in Utah.
