# Basic Facts

_A story of [[Charles Wager Richins (KVP1-SVD)|Charles Wager Richins]]._

## The story as it was written

Charles Richins, son of Richard Richins and Sharlette Pricilla Wager Richins, was born Sheepcombe, Gloustershire, England, Aug. 17, 1828. Baptized by Charles Blackwell. Was married to Louisa Shell before coming to America.
 Crossed the Plains in John W. Young Company arriving in Salt Lake City Oct. 10, 1853. Lived in Salt Lake City for a number of years then moving to Henefer, Utah where he became the first Bishop of that ward. Acting as Bishop there for 25 years.
 Married Esther Ovard.
 While living at Henefer was interested in developing the coal industry from mines near Coalville, as also farming and stock raising.
 Married Agnes Mary Willmott in the Endowment House, Salt Lake City, April 11, 1878 by Wilford Woodruff.
 During the crusade against polygamists he was arrested at Pleasant Grove in the fall of 1887. Was never tried in court. For after one day's waiting in court at Provo by himself and wife, Agnes, also son, Jesse, and daughter, Eunice, ages about 6 and 8 who were held as witnesses, the case did not come up. The next day the witnesses did not appear so they could do nothing with the case. The $200 bond on Agnes Richins, as witness, was forfeited and so ended the case.
 In 1888 moved to Colonia Diaz, Old Mexico where he engaged in ranching, farming, and stock raising. Was a member of the bishopric there, and then ordained a Patriach a year or so before his death. Was the father of 30 children--15 boys and 15 girls. At the time of his death 18 of the children were living.
 Died of Brights Disease at Colonia Diaz, Mex. Aug 27, 1903.
 His four brothers came to America as follows:
 John Richins - Pleasant Grove, Utah
 Thomas Richins - " " "
 Edward Richins - Coalville, Utah
 George Richins - Canada
