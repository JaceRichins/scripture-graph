![[AI Library/12 Family/_media/KWJF-WJ6/35724422.jpg|"Family Coat of Arms"]]
_This was a Christmas present from my great-grandmother, Leora Cook Barrus, to her grandchildren for Christmas 1983. The design appears to be hand-drawn by her. It was accompanied by this letter:

"Family Coat of Arms"

  That's what we have always called it and for want of a better name we should probably continue. The original (silk embroidery on old country linen) measures 21 x 23 inches. It is ragged and stained but what else could we expect? It came to Utah with the John Pill family from the Isle of Guernsey in 1855. We do not know what warrior or knight was given this cognizance along with his right to bear arms. Library research has uncovered no clues.
  The College of Arms in London England has registered all English armorial bearings and associated genealogical data since 1484. In a letter from there we are told that "a gold chevron on a white background contravenes one of the fundamental rules of heraldry" and that probably "other tinctures were intended". Our design may have been altered or adapted for some special banner or ensign. It is a pleasing design and we may cherish it because it is a family keepsake. Perhaps sometime we may discover its origin.
  This copy of the embroidered piece is made on a piece of the linen brought across the ocean and plains by my great grandmother, Caroline Pill (Farnham) in 1855.

  Leora Cook Barrus
  December 1983_
