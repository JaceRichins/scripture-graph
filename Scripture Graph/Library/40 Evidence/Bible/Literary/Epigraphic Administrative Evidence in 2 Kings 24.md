# Epigraphic Administrative Evidence in 2 Kings 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
Babylonian administrative tablets name a figure conventionally read as Jehoiachin, king of Judah, together with his sons among recipients of oil rations. This independently supports Jehoiachin's presence in Babylon and the continued recognition of his royal identity after deportation.

**Does not establish:** The tablets do not establish the biblical deportation totals, the temple plunder details, divine judgment, or the chapter's theological interpretation.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The tablets attest Jehoiachin in Babylon but do not disclose the complete circumstances by which he arrived there.
- Administrative terminology and personal-name readings depend on specialist reconstruction of cuneiform texts.
- The evidence corroborates an individual and setting rather than the chapter's full deportation narrative.
