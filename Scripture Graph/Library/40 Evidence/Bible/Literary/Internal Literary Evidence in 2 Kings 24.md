# Internal Literary Evidence in 2 Kings 24

## Summary
<!-- SG:BEGIN summary -->
The juxtaposition of military actions, royal rebellions, and repeated divine-causation formulas is strong internal evidence that the narrator interprets Babylon's ascendancy as divine judgment operating through political events. It is not independent evidence that supernatural causation can be historically demonstrated.

**Does not establish:** It does not establish supernatural causation, the guilt of every person who suffered, or the historical precision of every chronological and numerical detail.

**Alternative explanations:**
- The divine-causation language may be a conventional theological idiom used to interpret otherwise explicable geopolitical events.
- The theological framing may reflect retrospective editorial interpretation after Judah's disasters.
- Political rebellion and Babylonian expansion provide ordinary historical mechanisms for the events described.
