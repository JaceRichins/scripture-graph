# Numbers 3 — Study Guide

Scripture: [[Numbers 3]] · My notes: [[Numbers 3 - My Notes]]

## Overview
<!-- SG:BEGIN overview -->
Numbers 3 organizes Israel's sanctuary personnel through genealogy, appointment, location, and responsibility. Aaron's surviving sons minister as priests, while the other Levites assist them and care for the tabernacle. The Levites are also taken in place of Israel's firstborn, a divine claim grounded in the deliverance from Egypt. The chapter concludes by redeeming the 273 firstborn who exceed the stated aggregate of 22,000 Levites. These arrangements connect [[Service]], [[Consecration]], family organization, and redemption without making every Levite a priest.
<!-- SG:END overview -->

## Structure & Setting
<!-- SG:BEGIN structure -->
The chapter proceeds through four major movements:

1. Aaron's priestly household and the deaths of Nadab and Abihu (vv. 1-4).
2. Appointment of the Levites and their substitution for Israel's firstborn (vv. 5-13).
3. Levitical genealogy, census, encampment, leaders, and clan duties (vv. 14-39).
4. Census of the firstborn and redemption of the excess 273 males (vv. 40-51).

Repeated divine-speech formulas mark major transitions, while recurring language about keeping the charge unites the chapter's assignments of sacred responsibility. This outline is analytical rather than an explicit division supplied by the text.
<!-- SG:END structure -->

## Doctrines & Principles
<!-- SG:BEGIN doctrines -->
The chapter associates belonging to God with assigned responsibility. Israel's firstborn belong to the LORD because of the deliverance from Egypt, and the Levites are accepted in their place (vv. 12-13). The Levites are declared to be the LORD's while also being given to Aaron and his sons for concrete service.

The chapter also distinguishes offices within the tribe of Levi. Aaron and his sons guard the priestly office, whereas the other Levites assist and keep custody of the tabernacle. This is relevant to [[Priesthood]] and, more cautiously, [[Aaronic Priesthood]], but later terminology should not erase the chapter's own distinction between priests and assisting Levites.

The substitution is corporate and cultic. Numbers 3 does not itself present a general theory of salvation or explicitly identify the arrangement as a prophecy of Jesus Christ or the Atonement.

## Language & Text
<!-- SG:BEGIN language -->
“Openeth the matrix” in verses 12 and 13 is archaic English for opening the womb. “Charge” refers contextually to entrusted duty or custody, and “service” includes practical care of the tabernacle and its equipment.

The KJV word “stranger” appears in warnings concerning approach to priestly and sanctuary functions. The context clearly concerns someone outside the authorized role, so the word should not automatically be read as a simple ethnic label. The exact excluded category, however, should not be claimed from the English wording alone without lexical evidence from the underlying Hebrew.
<!-- SG:END language -->

## Literary Features
<!-- SG:BEGIN literary -->
Genealogy, spatial arrangement, inventories, census, and financial accounting work together to portray differentiated responsibility around the sanctuary. Gershon camps west, Kohath south, Merari north, and Moses with Aaron and Aaron's sons east. Their assigned objects range from coverings and hangings to sanctuary furnishings and structural components. This organization supports a reading of guarded, ordered service, though the chapter does not assign symbolic meanings to the compass directions.

The closing redemption equation is exact: 22,273 firstborn minus the stated 22,000 Levites leaves 273, and 273 multiplied by five shekels yields 1,365 shekels. This internal coordination does not establish the historical accuracy of the census. A separate numerical tension must remain visible: the three clan totals add to 22,300 rather than the 22,000 reported in verse 39, and the chapter supplies no explicit reconciliation.
<!-- SG:END literary -->

## Questions Worth Studying
<!-- SG:BEGIN questions -->
1. Why does Numbers 3:1 introduce the generations of both Aaron and Moses when the following genealogy names only Aaron's sons?
2. What explanations have been proposed for the difference between the clan subtotal of 22,300 and the aggregate of 22,000, and which parts of those explanations go beyond the chapter?
3. How does counting males from one month old show that these censuses measure more than an active tabernacle workforce?
4. How do the duties in [[Numbers 4]] clarify the shorter clan assignments in Numbers 3?
5. What distinctions does the chapter maintain between priestly office, Levitical assistance, and authorized approach to the sanctuary?
6. Why is monetary redemption required specifically for the 273 firstborn who exceed the stated number of Levites?
<!-- SG:END questions -->
